# Possession Taxonomy — Top-Level Categories v0.2

*Stage 1 draft, revision 2 (session 1, 2026-10-17). Replaces v0.1 after blind placement round 2, the crosswalk regression and cold audit AU2 (see STATUS.md). Only the top level is fixed here. Each category's inner structure is researched separately in stage 2.*

## How to read this file

**What is in scope.** Every generic type of object a person can own and keep: worn, carried, furnished, used, ridden, lived in, grown, displayed, played, collected or held as data. Plants are in. Animals are out, but equipment for animals is in. Food and drink are out (the Culinary taxonomy covers them), except **alcoholic drinks in sealed containers**, which are kept as stock (B9). **Tobacco, cannabis and other products made to be smoked, vaped, chewed or snuffed** are in (B9).

**Levels in stage 1.** Domain, then Category. Domains are a reading aid that groups neighbouring categories. **Domain names describe most of their members, not all; they are never tests.** Placement is always to a category, by the placement key.

**The axis: a short status filter, then designed function.** Steps 1 to 7 first sort objects by status: outside scope, human health, real property, data only, living, natural specimen, or part of another object. From step 8 on, an object is filed only by its **primary designed function**: what it is made to do, or to be, for its owner. It is never filed by material, power source, technology, value, age, maker, brand, room or setting, installation, connectivity, market (domestic or commercial), the user's age, ability or gender, occasion, how it was acquired, its condition, or why the owner keeps it. Those are modifiers (Appendix A).

**The placement key is authoritative.** Category descriptions restate the key. Where they differ, the key wins.

**Shared rules for every category.**
- **S1 One home.** Every object type has one category. Run the placement key and stop at the first *yes*.
- **S1a Branches.** Branches inside a step are tried in the order printed; the first branch that fits wins. When a step's lead question is *yes* but no branch fits, the answer is *no*: continue to the next step.
- **S1b Notes bind.** A note in brackets inside a step is part of the key. It takes the objects it names out of that step and sends them to the step or category it names. Where a note and a lead question disagree, the note wins.
- **S2 Generic types.** A kind is a generic type of object a person can own. Models, cuts and designs are named forms under a kind. An individual item is never a kind: a one-of-a-kind object is filed under the generic type it belongs to (an original painting under "painting").
- **S3 Parts follow the whole.** A component, attachment or consumable **made to be fitted to or used up in one kind of object** goes to that object's category: a graphics card to Computers, a lens to Cameras, guitar strings to Music, printer ink to Computers, a child car seat to Vehicles. **Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts only when they are made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time and travel or stand with it**: a phone case, a guitar case or stand, a golf bag, a camera bag, a watch strap. **Never parts:** furniture, racks, shelves, hooks, hangers, boxes and other storage that hold several objects or stand in a room as furniture, whatever they are made to hold (B1; safes are B8); anything worn on the body (step 16); building products (step 3's note); anything for growing plants (C2). **Cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8, whatever they are used in.**
- **S4 Modifiers never split the top level.** See Appendix A.
- **S5 Test the object, not the motive.** A signed football is a football; a collected teapot is a teapot; an antique chair is a chair; a commemorative plate is a plate.
- **S6 Attestation.** Every name added later has an attested source.

## Categories

### A — Worn & Carried
- **A1 Clothing** — garments and wearable accessories of any material: everyday, traditional and subculture dress, underwear, uniforms, costume, team and fan jerseys, protective clothing, helmets and personal protective equipment.
- **A2 Footwear** — anything worn on the feet, except footwear made for a sport (E2).
- **A3 Jewellery** — ornaments worn on the body, hair or clothing, including pins, badges, patches and worn amulets.
- **A4 Bags & Accessories** — bags and luggage of every kind, wallets, umbrellas, non-corrective eyewear, hand fans, keyrings, fashion canes, baby carriers.
- **A5 Clocks & Watches** — timepieces of every kind, worn or not.

### B — Home
- **B1 Furniture & Storage** — pieces and containers to sit, lie, work at, display, store and organise things, indoor or outdoor, fitted or not, including mattresses.
- **B2 Soft Furnishings** — bedding, rugs and carpets, blinds and curtains, cushions, throws, table linen, towels.
- **B3 Lighting** — lamps, light fittings, bulbs, lanterns and torches.
- **B4 Decor** — ornamental objects that are not pictures, sculpture or lamps, decorative lighting, festival and party decorations, garden ornaments, flags and signs.
- **B5 Kitchen & Dining** — everything made to prepare, cook, cool, store or serve food and drink, powered or not, domestic or commercial.
- **B6 Cleaning & Laundry** — everything made to clean, wash or dry, cleaning products, waste bins.
- **B7 Climate, Water & Energy** — heating, cooling and air treatment, domestic water, baths and sanitaryware, home energy equipment.
- **B8 Home Safety & Security** — locks, safes, alarms, extinguishers, security cameras, child safety, pest control.
- **B9 Cellar & Smoking** — two independent parts: (a) alcoholic drinks in sealed containers; (b) tobacco and other smoking, vaping and chewing products and their implements.
- **B10 Animal Equipment** — housing, tack, carriers, feeding, care, veterinary and toys for animals, kept or wild.

### C — Making, Growing & Work
- **C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment** — hand and power tools, workshop machines, ladders, measuring and testing tools for building and trades, generators, tool storage.
- **C2 Garden & Grounds** — tools, machines and supplies for growing plants and keeping grounds and pools.
- **C3 Plants & Living Cultures** — living plants and fungi, fresh-cut flowers, seeds and bulbs, living cultures.
- **C4 Craft & Art Supplies** — tools, machines and materials for picture-making and for textile, paper, bead, clay and model crafts.
- **C5 Office & Stationery** — writing instruments, paper goods, desk accessories, calculators, office machines.
- **C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments** — instruments to observe, magnify or measure for study or science, and science-education kits.
- **C7 Farm & Forestry Equipment** — tractors and their implements, self-propelled farm and forestry machines, livestock handling and milking equipment.
- **C8 Building Materials & Hardware** — building products, hardware, electrical and plumbing supplies, household batteries, fuels, packing materials.

### D — Electronics
- **D1 Computers & Components** — computers, components, peripherals, storage, networking, point-of-sale equipment, loose electronic components.
- **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices** — phones and two-way radios, tablets, e-readers, smartwatches and wearables, GPS units and trackers, power banks.
- **D3 Audio & Video** — TVs, projectors, hi-fi, record players, radios, headphones, speakers for listening, smart speakers and hubs.
- **D4 Cameras & Photography** — cameras, lenses, photo lighting, tripods, film and darkroom equipment.
- **D5 Video Games** — consoles, handhelds, controllers, VR headsets, arcade machines, video games on any carrier.

### E — Play, Sport & Music
- **E1 Toys & Games** — toys, figures, dolls, models and kits, table, board, card and dice games, children's play equipment, gambling equipment.
- **E2 Sports, Fitness & Recreation** — equipment for sport, dance, exercise and outdoor activities, and garments and footwear made for a sport. Inner structure: by sport or activity first, then by equipment type.
- **E3 Musical Instruments & Music Gear** — instruments of every tradition, and equipment to amplify, perform and record music.
- **E4 Weapons & Armour** — weapons for fighting, self-defence or hunting, target firearms and bows, armour against weapons, ammunition.

### F — Culture, Records & Meaning
- **F1 Media & Publications** — published works on physical carriers: books, comics, magazines, maps and globes, sheet music, recordings, films.
- **F2 Art** — pictures and free-standing sculpture, including ornamental figurines, whatever the edition or price.
- **F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards** — physical money and bullion, stamps, medals, trophies and award plaques, tokens, non-game trading cards.
- **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects** — objects for religious practice and for the dead, worn or not, except dress and ornaments.
- **F5 Documents & Personal Records** — issued documents, access and payment cards, and personal records and keepsake papers.
- **F6 Natural History & Specimens** — natural objects kept as specimens: minerals, fossils, unset gemstones, shells, meteorites, biological specimens.

### G — Vehicles & Property
- **G1 Vehicles** — everything made mainly to transport people, animals or goods, with its fitted parts and electronics.
- **G2 Property & Structures** — land, whole buildings and permanent structures, fixed dwellings, burial plots.

### H — Body
- **H1 Personal Care & Beauty** — grooming, hygiene, cosmetic, fragrance and comfort tools, devices and products, and baby care.
- **H2 Health, Medical & Mobility** — medical devices and monitors, medicines, mobility and assistive aids, corrective eyewear, hearing aids, pregnancy and fertility, first aid.
- **H3 Adult Objects** — objects made for sexual stimulation or restraint during sex. Clinical, descriptive register.

### I — Digital
- **I1 Digital Goods** — files, licence entitlements and registered tokens owned by a person. Inner structure mirrors F1, F2, F5 and D5, plus data-only kinds (software, fonts, domain names, tokens); study it together with F1 and D5.

### X — Holding
- **X0 Unplaced** — review bin; must be empty before stage 2.
