# Stage 2 — Category structure memo (template)

One memo per top-level category: `framework/structure/<CODE>_<Name>.md`. The memo is research, not a decision. The owner approves a structure from it. Pilot: A1 Clothing and D1 Computers & Components. The categories, membership tests and scope are in the highest-numbered framework/CATEGORIES_v*.md; owner decisions are in BRIEF.md.

## 0. Category
Code, name, the membership test from the current CATEGORIES file, and the precedence sentences that touch it.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself
For each source type, give the top two or three levels of its division, with fetched URLs.
- Retailers and marketplaces in at least three regions.
- Enthusiast communities and forums, with their wikis and glossaries.
- Collectors and the resale market.
- Standards bodies and trade classifications.
- Museums and reference works.

Note where these sources agree, and where they split the same objects differently.

## 2. The first axis people choose by
What do owners and buyers decide first: form, function, fit or size, material, mechanism, scene, maker or performance tier? Give evidence: how navigation is ordered, filter order on major stores, and the questions buyers ask in community guides.

## 3. Candidate structures
Two or three candidate inner structures. For each:
- the levels, and the one stated axis per level;
- example sub-families and leaves, using 10 to 15 real kinds;
- where it breaks, with three or four edge objects.

## 4. Depth and size
- Rough count of generic kinds.
- Rough count of named forms: models, cuts and iconic designs.
- Where depth concentrates.
- Which leaves are broad, medium and narrow.

## 4b. Choice level
State where kinds should stop. **A kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.** Finer distinctions below that level are named forms or attributes, not separate kinds.

For example, frozen vegetables may be one kind with makers and tiers attached there, with peas and spinach as forms beneath it. Beef goes deeper, because buyers compare cut against cut and grade against grade.

Give evidence for the level you choose: retailer filter facets, review-site groupings, enthusiast buying guides, and where comparison and "best X" lists are written. The choice level may differ between sub-families of the same category.

## 5. Quality and tier
Is there a recognised tier ordering? Choose one:
- **spec-ranked:** a measurable spec sets the order, such as a GPU class or a camera sensor;
- **consensus-ranked:** a community or standard sets the order, such as keyboard switch tiers or watch movement grades;
- **preference:** no tier exists, only price band.

Give the source of any ordering, and say how it would appear as a modifier, never as a level.

## 6. Makers and brands
How central are makers? Choose one:
- **incidental:** brands are examples only;
- **identity:** the maker is part of what is sought, such as subculture labels or watch houses;
- **platform:** the maker sets compatibility, such as a camera mount or a CPU socket.

Recommend whether this category needs a maker ledger: maker, known-for kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country and era, each row attested.

## 7. Scenes, cultures and views
Name the subcultures, scenes, traditions or regions that cut across the category. For each, state whether it should be a generated view, a tag on named forms, or a named form itself. It is a named form only when the scene names a distinct form.

## 8. Order inside a leaf
Name the axis that orders kinds and named forms: canonical form first, spec order, or chronology. Give the reason.

## 9. Boundaries
For the three to five neighbouring categories, list the closest cross-boundary objects and the precedence sentence for each.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions
State the recommended structure and its maximum depth. List the owner decisions needed.
