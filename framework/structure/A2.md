# A2 Footwear — structure memo (research draft)

Stage 2 research memo for the owner. It describes how the world already divides footwear and recommends a structure with evidence. It is not a decision. Every claim about how a source divides the category cites a page that was fetched in this batch (S2_A2); the same URLs are in `verify/out/S2_A2_sources.tsv`. Where a fact was seen only in a search excerpt it is marked "(search excerpt only)". Framework: CATEGORIES_v0_6.md. Owner decisions: BRIEF.md.

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** A2 Footwear (domain A, Worn and Carried).

**Membership test (placement key step 17, first branch):** "footwear of any kind, including safety and steel-toe boots? A2 Footwear". Category description: "anything worn on the feet, except footwear made for a sport (E2)".

**Rules and notes that touch A2 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):**
- Step 17 note: "A garment, shoe or accessory with heating or lights stays here." A device whose main function is computing, communication, sensing or media goes to step 24.
- Step 17 note on sport: "Made for a sport means made for the demands of one sport, dance or outdoor activity and not suited to everyday wear: football boots, ski boots ..., climbing shoes and harnesses, ballet, tap and dance shoes, ..." go to step 26 (E2). "Running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers ... stay here."
- Step 17 branch order: footwear is tried before garments. Step 17 A1 branch lists "hosiery", so socks are A1.
- Step 17 A1 branch lists "motorcycle and cycling helmets and motorcycle riding gear" as protective clothing, but the first branch (footwear of any kind) is tried first, so motorcycle and riding boots are A2. Step 13 note: "riders' clothing and helmets are step 17".
- Step 2 (H2): braces, supports, "mobility and assistive aids" are H2; note: "An ordinary object made in an adapted form for a disabled user ... is filed as that object: 'adapted' is a modifier."
- S3 (parts follow the whole): a component, attachment or consumable made to be fitted to one kind of object goes to that object's category. Never parts: "racks, shelves, hooks, hangers, boxes" (B1), "shoe bags" (both in the B1 furniture-and-storage list), "cleaning products are B6".
- S4 and Appendix A: region, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user (baby, child, adult, gender, size, ability: adapted forms), setting, occasion, motive, material, condition and edition never split the top level.
- S2: a kind is a generic type; models, cuts and designs are named forms; an individual item is never a kind.

**Precedence sentences that already exist for A2 (restated, not new):** (1) Footwear of any kind is A2 unless it is made for a sport (E2). (2) Running shoes, hiking boots and general trainers stay A2. (3) Socks and hosiery are A1. (4) Shoe racks, shoe cabinets and shoe bags are B1. (5) Adapted ordinary shoes stay A2; footwear made to treat a condition is H2 (step 2).

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities and buying guides
- **Hockerty, dress-shoe guides** (https://www.hockerty.com/en-us/blog/types-of-dress-shoes): types are Oxford (closed lacing), Derby (open lacing), Monk strap (single or double), Brogue (full, semi, quarter: a perforation treatment "used on all of the dress shoe types"), Wingtip, Cap toe, Wholecut, Loafers (penny, tassel, horsebit), then Chukka, Chelsea and dress boots. Formality orders the types.
- **Hockerty, construction guide** (https://www.hockerty.com/en-us/blog/glued-blake-goodyear-shoe-construction): "Three methods account for nearly every leather shoe": glued (cemented), Blake (with Blake Rapid as a variant), Goodyear welt, compared on flexibility, weight, water resistance, resoleability and price tier (entry to mid, mid to premium, premium). Construction is the quality marker buyers are taught first.
- **r/goodyearwelt wiki** (https://www.reddit.com/r/goodyearwelt/wiki/index/): the fetched wiki index shows "an indexed collection of shoe photos organized by brand and model"; resource pages (seen in search excerpt only) cover construction, leather, care and repair. The community indexes by maker and model.
- **Styleforum** (https://www.styleforum.net/threads/how-would-you-rank-the-hierarchy-of-high-end-english-dress-shoe-brands-as-of-2024.768047/): members rank makers and in-house lines, for example "Crockett & Jones handgrade ... Crockett & Jones benchgrade", "Cheaney Imperial ... Cheaney mainline", with Edward Green and Gaziano & Girling at the top. A consensus maker hierarchy exists and is argued over.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market
- **StockX** (https://stockx.com/brands/nike?category=sneakers&product-line=dunk): filters in this order are category (Sneakers, Lifestyle), size, gender, **product line** (Dunk, Air Force, Air Max, Shox, Superstar, Wallabee and dozens more, even across brands), shoe height (low, high), **activity** (basketball, skateboarding), colour. - **GOAT** (https://www.goat.com/sneakers): browse by silhouette (Dunk), brand (Air Jordan, New Balance) and designer (Peter Moore, Tinker Hatfield). Collaborations are named "Partner x Brand Model 'Colourway'" (search excerpt of the home page; the sneaker page itself confirms the silhouette, brand and designer routes).
- **Wikipedia, Sneaker collecting** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sneaker_collecting): the vocabulary a collector uses: silhouette ("design of a sneaker"), colorway, OG ("retro re-release of an original design or colorway"), retro, Quickstrike (QS), reseller, NIB, VNDS ("very near deadstock"). Condition grades exist (NIB, VNDS) and sit on the item, not on the type. Collections are named by line: Air Jordans, Air Force Ones, Dunks, SB, Foamposites, Air Max, Yeezy.
- **Grailed** market tiers (Grails, Hype, Core, Sartorial) are a platform's own cultural tiers, not quality tiers.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications
- **HS 2022 chapter 64** (https://hsmate.com/en-us/hs-code/chapter-64): 6 headings, 25 six-digit codes. 6401 waterproof footwear with rubber or plastic soles and uppers; 6402 other rubber or plastic footwear (sports footwear such as ski boots split out at 6402.12); 6403 leather uppers (with a metal toe-cap at 6403.40); 6406 parts including removable insoles. The chapter divides by **outsole and upper material** plus a few tests (waterproof, ankle cover, toe-cap, sports). Material is a modifier here (Appendix A), so HS is useful only to confirm that safety toe-caps and sports footwear are recognised classes.
- **EN ISO 20345 safety footwear** (https://www.colbrook.co.uk/blog/latest-news-1/en-iso-20345-safety-footwear-standards-explained-sb-s1-s2-s3-s4-s5-23): six cumulative levels, SB, S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, all with a 200 J toe cap; S1 adds antistatic and closed heel energy absorption, S2 water penetration resistance, S3 a penetration-resistant midsole and cleated sole, S4 and S5 moulded waterproof footwear; additional marks P, HI, CI, WR, HRO, SRC, E. This is a spec-ranked ladder.
- **ISO 19407:2023 sizing** (https://www.iso.org/standard/83106.html): conversion tables for Mondopoint (foot length), European and UK systems plus China, Japan, Korea and the US; adults and children. Size is a modifier. ISO 19952:2025 (footwear vocabulary, ISO/TC 216) exists but was located in search only and not fetched.
- **Medicare therapeutic shoes** (https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52501): "depth shoe" (A5500: removable filler giving at least 3/16 inch extra depth, a minimum of three widths) and "custom-molded shoe" (A5501: built on a positive model of the foot) are defined classes, with inserts A5512 to A5514. This is the medical edge of A2.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works
- **Bata Shoe Museum** (https://batashoemuseum.ca/category/bsm-collection): about 15,000 artifacts over 4,500 years; footwear is read through culture, climate, religion, trade, gender and status, plus a companion collection of shoe-shaped ornamental objects (snuffboxes, hand-warmers). The museum organises by culture and period, which are modifiers here.
- **Wikipedia, Footwear** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footwear): Types: Boots (about 21), Shoes (athletic, ballet flats, boat, court/pumps, lace-up with derby, oxford, brogue, blucher, loafers, mules, monks and others), Sandals (flip-flops, slides, chappals), Slippers (closed, open), Specific footwear (ballet, climbing, cleats, ski boots, skates, steel-toe, surgical shoe, swim fins), Traditional footwear (by country), Socks.
- **Wikipedia, List of shoe styles** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_shoe_styles): about 136 first-level and about 190 total named styles in the main alphabetical list (more in the navbox groups), with groups Dress shoes, Other shoes, Wooden footwear, Boots (including a Military group), Folk footwear, Historical footwear, High heels and Shoe construction. Boots alone carry about 30 sub-styles (Chelsea, chukka, cowboy, engineer, wellington, and others).
- **Japanese Wikipedia, 履物** (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E5%B1%A5%E7%89%A9): the first split is open footwear (開放性履物: sandals, geta, which leave the instep open) versus closed footwear (閉塞性履物: shoes). A folklore classification adds strap-based footwear (geta, zori, waraji), covered footwear (straw boots, leather boots, tabi), auxiliary and special footwear.
- **Japanese Wikipedia, 靴** (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/靴): types by shape (short shoes versus boots, with heel-open or strap-only footwear classed as sandals, and indoor ones as slippers), by material, by use (business, casual, sports, rain, safety, riding, military), then period and ethnic shoes.

### 1.6 Makers
- **Red Wing Heritage** (https://www.redwingshoes.com/mens/heritage/, https://www.redwingberlin.com/en): the maker names models (Iron Ranger, Classic Moc, Classic Chelsea, Blacksmith, Weekender, Work Chukka, Logger, Engineer, Roughneck) and issues each in numbered styles by leather (Iron Ranger #8000 Oro Legacy, #8111 Amber Harness, #8085 Copper Rough and Tough). The Moc Toe 875 was introduced in 1953. Construction is Goodyear welt or stitchdown.
- **Dr. Martens** (https://www.drmartens.com/us/en/icons): four "icons" (1460 boot from 1960, 1461 shoe, 2976 Chelsea boot, Adrian tassel loafer), each issued in many material and edition variants.

### 1.7 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects differently
**Agree:** every retailer and reference keeps boots, sandals, sneakers or athletic shoes, and slippers or house shoes as separate nodes (Zalando, ABC-MART, Deichmann, Myntra, Wikipedia, Grailed). Sneakers are always a sibling of boots and never a child of them. Dress-shoe types (oxford, derby, monk, loafer) are named the same way in Hockerty, Wikipedia, Dr. Martens and ABC-MART.
**Split differently:**
1. **High-top sneakers:** Deichmann (Hohe Sneaker), ABC-MART (ハイカットスニーカー) and Grailed (Hi-Top Sneakers) keep them with sneakers; a pure shaft-height rule would call them boots.
2. **Mules and backless shoes:** Zalando has Mules as a node; Japanese Wikipedia classes a shoe with an open heel or heel strap only as a sandal.
3. **Toe-cap pattern versus lacing:** ABC-MART splits business shoes by toe-cap (straight tip, plain toe, wing tip, U-tip) while Hockerty and Wikipedia split by lacing system (oxford, derby) and treat broguing as decoration.
4. **Work footwear:** ABC-MART makes 作業靴 (work shoes) a top node and also lists ワークブーツ under Boots; Red Wing sells Heritage "work" boots as fashion. Certification (EN ISO 20345) separates the two.
5. **Gender:** Myntra, ABC-MART, Deichmann and Zalando all open with gender or age; Wikipedia's navbox has a "Women's" group. Gender is a user modifier.
6. **Material:** HS chapter 64 and Wikipedia's "Wooden footwear" group divide by material; retailers do not.
7. **Traditional footwear:** Wikipedia (Folk footwear, by country) and the Bata Shoe Museum (by culture) divide by region; Japanese sources divide by form (geta, zori, waraji).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence is consistent: buyers choose in this order.
1. **User and size (the gate).** Myntra's first filter is Men, Women, Boys, Girls; ABC-MART opens with 性別 (gender) and size in centimetres; Deichmann splits Damen, Herren, Kinder before any shoe type; Zalando has separate men's and women's trees; Grailed's footwear sizes are the main facet. Size is governed by ISO 19407 (https://www.iso.org/standard/83106.html). These are modifiers, so they become views and tags, not levels.
2. **Type or family.** After the gate every site lists type nodes (sneakers, boots, sandals, loafers, pumps). Myntra's category counts show type as the main navigation: Casual 71,095, Heels 63,529, Flip Flops 48,037.
3. **Maker.** Brand is the first "top filter" at Zalando UK (Brand, Size, Colour, Qualities, Price); Zappos' index carries many maker entries (Dansko, Frye, Hunter, Ugg, Sorel); ABC-MART has a brand tab with named series (Stan Smith, Superstar, Ultraboost for adidas; All Star, Jack Purcell, One Star for Converse); Mercado Libre's most-wanted searches are makers (Hush Puppies, Mizuno); StockX filters by product line before shoe height.
4. **Construction and quality (dress shoes and boots only).** Hockerty's guide is titled "How Your Dress Shoes Are Really Built"; Styleforum threads rank makers.
5. **Silhouette then colourway (sneaker collectors).** StockX lists product line, then colourway; GOAT browses silhouette, brand, designer.

The structural axis that matters most for A2 is therefore **form family** (type as the trade names it), with user and size as a gate and maker as the identity layer.

## 3. Candidate structures

Maximum depth for all three is the framework's four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form), or fewer where argued.

### Candidate A (recommended): form family, then kind, then named form
- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: footwear family, the form lineage the trade uses as its top split.** Six sub-families: (1) Sneakers and athletic-style shoes, (2) Shoes (closed, non-athletic), (3) Boots, (4) Sandals and open footwear, (5) Slippers and indoor footwear, (6) Work and safety footwear.
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis: closure or cut (lace-up, slip-on, strap, heel-led).** Used only inside Shoes and Boots, where one sub-family would otherwise hold more than about 12 kinds. Elsewhere the leaf level is skipped.
- **Level 3, kind. Axis: generic type a buyer can own and compare** (Oxford, Chelsea boot, flip-flop).
- **Level 4, named form. Axis: model or iconic design** (Iron Ranger, 1460, Air Force 1 Low).
- Example kinds (13): low-top sneaker; high-top sneaker; running shoe; skate shoe; Oxford; derby; monk strap; loafer; court shoe (pump); ballet flat; Chelsea boot; chukka boot; cowboy boot.
- **Where it breaks (edge objects):** (1) performance basketball shoes and spiked or cleated shoes, which the key sends to E2, sit next to lifestyle sneakers that share their silhouette; (2) the Chelsea "boot" that is ankle-high and worn as a dress shoe (boots or shoes?); (3) work boots without certification versus certified safety boots (Red Wing Heritage Iron Ranger versus an S3 boot); (4) mules and clogs, which the sources place in three different families; (5) the "slipper boot" and sheepskin boot (Zappos slipper boots, ABC-MART ボアブーツ) that work indoors and out.

### Candidate B: enclosure first (boot, closed shoe, open, indoor)
- **Level 1. Axis: how much of the foot and leg the upper encloses** (shaft above the ankle; closed below the ankle; open at heel, toe or instep; indoor only). Supported by the first split in Japanese Wikipedia (open versus closed) and the Wikipedia Footwear "Types" headings (Boots, Shoes, Sandals, Slippers).
- **Level 2. Axis: closure or cut.** **Level 3, kind; Level 4, named form.**
- Example kinds (12): Chelsea boot; chukka; lace-up boot; knee-high boot; low-top sneaker; high-top sneaker (as a boot); Oxford; loafer; court shoe; flip-flop; slide; slipper.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) high-top sneakers would become boots, against Deichmann, ABC-MART and Grailed, where they are a large share of the market (Grailed 65k hi-top versus 210k low-top listings); (2) mules and clogs (open heel, closed toe), which Japanese Wikipedia files with sandals and Zalando keeps apart; (3) espadrilles and Mary Janes that may or may not cover the ankle strap; (4) slippers, which are defined by indoor use, so the axis is not pure.

### Candidate C: function and occasion first (dress, casual, athletic, work, outdoor, weather, indoor)
- **Level 1. Axis: occasion or use.** Mirrors ABC-MART's top level (Business, Casual, Sports, Outdoor, Rain, Work) and the Japanese Wikipedia "by use" list.
- Example kinds (11): Oxford (dress); derby (dress/casual); Chelsea boot; running shoe; hiking boot; rain boot; safety boot; snow boot; nurse clog; slipper; sandal.
- **Where it breaks:** Appendix A makes occasion and setting modifiers, and S1 requires one home: a Chelsea boot is dress, casual and work at once (Red Wing Classic Chelsea, Dr. Martens 2976, Deichmann Chelsea Boots); a Dr. Martens 1460 is work, punk and fashion; one boot would need several homes.

**Recommendation:** Candidate A. It matches the top split in Zalando, ABC-MART, Deichmann, Myntra, Grailed and Wikipedia (sneakers beside boots beside sandals), keeps high-top sneakers with sneakers as the markets do, and avoids occasion as a level. Enclosure (Candidate B's axis) is kept as the axis for ordering and for the leaf level inside Boots.

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds (rough):** 100 to 140. Evidence: ABC-MART has about 70 category nodes under shoes; Wikipedia's Footwear page lists about 80 types in its Types headings; Wikipedia's list of shoe styles has about 136 first-level and about 190 total entries in its main list (more in its navbox groups), of which many are historical (poulaine, chopine) or regional (opanak, galesh); the worked tree above has 55 mainstream kinds. A first pass of about 120 kinds covers mainstream and most traditional types.

**Named forms (rough):** 1,500 to 3,000 for models and iconic designs. Evidence: the StockX Nike brand filter lists dozens of product lines for one maker; Red Wing's Heritage range alone shows about a dozen models; Dr. Martens sells four icons in dozens of variants. Colourways and collaborations multiply this to tens of thousands of style codes in the resale market (StockX and GOAT list each colourway as a product); those should not be named forms (see 4b and 7).

**Where depth concentrates:** (1) sneakers, where depth is almost all in named forms and variants; (2) boots, where makers' icon models and the work-boot canon sit; (3) dress shoes, where depth is in construction and last, which are attributes; (4) heeled women's shoes, where depth is in heel form, an attribute with ten values at Zalando; (5) traditional footwear, a long tail of narrow kinds.

**Leaf sizes:**
- Broad (many kinds or very many forms): Sneakers; Boots; Shoes (closed).
- Medium: Sandals and open footwear; Work and safety footwear.
- Narrow: Slippers and indoor footwear; traditional leaves (geta, zori, klompen, opanak) each hold one to three kinds.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. Finer distinctions are named forms or attributes.

| Sub-family | Kind stops at | Below it | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dress and closed shoes | Oxford, derby, monk strap, loafer, court shoe | broguing (full, semi, quarter), toe-cap (plain, cap, wing, wholecut) and last are attributes; "Longwing blucher" or "Dr. Martens 1461" are named forms | Hockerty types guide: broguing "can be used on all of the dress shoe types"; the quality comparison there is by construction (Blake, Goodyear), which is an attribute; ABC-MART splits by toe-cap, which suggests it as a second axis |
| Boots | Chelsea, chukka, lace-up, work, engineer, cowboy, combat, rain, snow... | maker icons (Iron Ranger, 1460, Moc Toe 875) | Zappos, ABC-MART and Wikipedia name these as types; Red Wing and Dr. Martens sell icons within the type; Styleforum and r/goodyearwelt index by maker and model |
| Sneakers | low-top, high-top, running, skate, slip-on, plimsoll, chunky | silhouette and model are named forms; **colourway, collaboration and retro year are not named forms** | StockX and GOAT: product line and silhouette first, colourway per listing; "Nike Dunk Low Retro Pink Donut" is a colourway of a product line; collaborations are priced by premium (255 percent) |
| Heeled shoes | court shoe, slingback, heeled sandal | heel form (stiletto, block, kitten, wedge, platform) is an attribute | Zalando offers heel form as a 10-value filter, not as a node |
| Sandals | flip-flop, slide, flat strap sandal, heeled sandal | Birkenstock-type footbed sandal (search excerpt only) is a named form | Myntra counts Flip Flops 48,037 separately from Sandals 32,640, so buyers treat them as distinct kinds |
| Work and safety | boot, shoe, wellington, clog (by form) | protection class (SB, S1 to S5) is a tier attribute, not a kind | EN ISO 20345 is a cumulative spec ladder printed on the shoe; buyers choose a level for a risk, then a form |
| Traditional | one kind per distinct construction (geta, zori, waraji, klompen, opanak, chappal) | regional variants are named forms | Japanese Wikipedia divides geta, zori, waraji as different constructions; Wikipedia lists them as separate styles |

The choice level is **deeper for boots and dress shoes** (buyers compare maker against maker and construction against construction) and **shallower for sandals, slippers and sneakers** (buyers compare silhouette and brand). Individual colourway pairs, graded condition (NIB, VNDS, Grailed's New/Never Worn to Very Worn) and sizes are never kinds.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Verdict: consensus-ranked for dress shoes and boots, spec-ranked for safety footwear, preference for sneakers, heels, sandals and slippers.** One answer is not honest for the whole category, so the tier type is given per sub-family. In every case it is a modifier or ledger field, never a level.

| Sub-family | Tier type | Source of the ordering | How it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Work and safety footwear | **spec-ranked** | EN ISO 20345: SB, S1, S2, S3, S4, S5 cumulative, with marks P, HI, CI, WR, HRO, SRC, E (https://www.colbrook.co.uk/blog/latest-news-1/en-iso-20345-safety-footwear-standards-explained-sb-s1-s2-s3-s4-s5-23) | attribute "protection class" on the kind or named form |
| Dress shoes, boots (welted) | **consensus-ranked**, with a spec-like sub-ladder | (a) Construction: cemented, Blake, Blake Rapid, Goodyear welt, ranked by resoleability and price tier (Hockerty, https://www.hockerty.com/en-us/blog/glued-blake-goodyear-shoe-construction). Other quality attributes that sources show but that are not tiers: upper material (Zalando lists about 25 upper materials; ABC-MART lists natural leather, synthetic leather, rubber, cotton and others), leather grade (shell cordovan, calfskin, suede, patent, nubuck, in the Wikipedia navbox), width (ABC-MART E-widths; Zappos width facets), and made-in country (Myntra country of origin; Red Wing "Made in USA").

## 6. Makers and brands

**Verdict: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought, in all three of the places where depth lives.
- Sneakers: StockX and GOAT browse brand and silhouette; collaborations are "Partner x Brand Model"; ABC-MART organises its brand tab by named series.
- Boots: Red Wing and Dr. Martens are bought for the maker (their own pages sell named icons); Alden and Viberg appear as makers in search excerpts of Styleforum and Grailed only; Red Wing issues its icons in numbered styles by leather.
- Dress shoes: Styleforum ranks makers and in-house lines; r/goodyearwelt indexes "by brand and model".

It is **not platform**: no maker sets compatibility with the footwear of another. Size systems are shared (ISO 19407). Maker widths and lasts differ, but that is fit data on the ledger.

**Recommendation: a maker ledger is needed.** Fields: maker; country; founded or era of the key models; known-for kinds; known-for named forms; scene tags (skate, workwear, punk, classic menswear, streetwear, outdoor); consensus tier with its source (dress shoes and boots only); in-house lines (handgrade, benchgrade, Imperial, Heritage, Originals); construction; price band; resale-market tier (dated, optional). Every row needs an attested source (S6).

**Two ledgers, not one.** (1) The **maker ledger** above. (2) A **variant ledger** for colourways, collaborations and retro releases of sneakers, keyed by the manufacturer style code (the code GOAT shows in its page addresses, for example IQ5495-005). The variant ledger is optional and sparse; it is not part of the tree. The owner's rule that individual items are not kinds supports this: a colourway is an edition of a named form (Appendix A lists edition as a modifier), and a pair is an item.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds:** canonical form first, then by shaft or heel height from low to high. Reason: buyers learn a canonical archetype (Oxford before wholecut; Chelsea before pull-on) and every source with a tree lists the generic type first. Height is a physical order that stays stable (Zalando lists Ankle boots before Boots; ABC-MART orders sneakers slip-on, low, high).

**Named forms:** chronology by year of introduction, oldest first. Reason: the makers attest dates (Dr. Martens 1460 in 1960; Red Wing 875 Moc Toe in 1953; Blake construction in 1856 per Hockerty), and collectors speak of "OG" and "retro" relative to the first release. Where no date is attested, alphabetical.

**Sub-families:** the order given in section 3 (Sneakers, Shoes, Boots, Sandals, Slippers, Work and safety) follows market volume (Grailed: sneakers first; Myntra: casual first) and can be reordered without structural effect.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A):** A2 Footwear splits into six sub-families by **form family**: Sneakers and athletic-style shoes; Shoes (closed, non-athletic); Boots; Sandals and open footwear; Slippers and indoor footwear; Work and safety footwear. Below each, **kinds** are generic types a buyer compares (Oxford, Chelsea boot, flip-flop). Below kinds, **named forms** are models and iconic designs (Iron Ranger, 1460, Air Force 1 Low). A **leaf** level (closure or cut) is used only inside Shoes and Boots, where one sub-family would otherwise hold over about 12 kinds. **Maximum depth below the category: three levels** (sub-family, kind, named form), with an optional fourth (leaf) in two sub-families only. This is one level less than the framework maximum, because the sources do not support a leaf level in Sneakers, Sandals, Slippers or Work.

**Everything that is not a level** is a tag, view or ledger:
- user (men, women, kids, baby), size system and width, colour, material, season, closure type, heel form, construction, safety class, region, scene, occasion, condition, edition;
- maker ledger and variant ledger (section 6);
- tier ordering as attributes: construction (cemented to Goodyear), safety class (SB to S5), maker consensus tier (dress shoes and boots).

**Choice level:** kinds stop at the generic type a buyer compares (Oxford, derby, Chelsea, chukka, flip-flop, low-top and high-top sneaker). Named forms are maker models and iconic designs. Colourways, collaborations, retro years, pair condition and sizes are not forms: they go to attributes, the variant ledger and views.

**Tier type:** consensus-ranked for dress shoes and boots (with construction as a spec-like attribute), spec-ranked for safety footwear, preference elsewhere. **Maker centrality:** identity; maker ledger and variant ledger required.

### Owner decisions needed
1. **Level 1 axis.** Approve "form family" (Candidate A) over enclosure (Candidate B) or occasion (Candidate C). The decisive evidence is that every major market keeps high-top sneakers with sneakers, and occasion is barred as a level.
2. **Slippers and indoor footwear.** Keep as a sixth sub-family (every source lists them) or file closed slippers under Shoes and open slippers under Sandals.
3. **Work and safety footwear.** Separate sub-family keyed on certification (EN ISO 20345 or equivalent marking) or a tag on boots and shoes. Certified-or-not is a spec test, not a use test.
4. **Dress-shoe kinds.** Oxford, derby, monk, loafer as kinds with broguing and toe-cap as attributes (Hockerty, Wikipedia), or toe-cap pattern as kinds (ABC-MART's straight tip, plain toe, wing tip, U-tip, swirl).
5. **Sneaker variants.** Confirm that colourways, collaborations and retro releases are a variant ledger keyed by style code, not named forms. Without this the data would hold tens of thousands of rows per maker.
6. **Traditional and historical footwear.** Include as ordinary kinds (geta, zori, klompen, opanak, chappal) with a region tag; decide whether purely historical styles (poulaine, chopine, caliga) are in scope as owned objects or only as museum references.
7. **Boundary calls** in section 9: overshoes and spats, waders, shoe horns, performance basketball shoes, care sprays.
8. **Mules and clogs.** One kind each under Shoes (Zalando) or files with sandals (Japanese Wikipedia rule).
9. **Minimalist and barefoot shoes.** Kind in Sneakers, or an attribute.
