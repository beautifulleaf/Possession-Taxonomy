Stage 2 research memo for the owner. It describes how the world already divides footwear and recommends a structure with evidence. It is not a decision. Every claim about how a source divides the category cites a page that was fetched in this batch (S2_A2); the same URLs are in `verify/out/S2_A2_sources.tsv`. Where a fact was seen only in a search excerpt it is marked "(search excerpt only)". A claim marked **†** still needs a fetched attestation; all of them are listed in section 10 under "Sourcing still needed". A place marked **OWNER:** is an owner decision that this memo does not take. Framework: CATEGORIES_v0_6.md. Owner decisions: BRIEF.md. Shared conventions: framework/structure/_conventions.md (C1 to C17, draft pending owner approval); this memo follows them.

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** A2 Footwear (domain A, Worn and Carried).

**Membership test (placement key step 17, first branch):** "footwear of any kind, including safety and steel-toe boots? A2 Footwear". Category description: "anything worn on the feet, except footwear made for a sport (E2)".

**Rules and notes that touch A2 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):**
- Step 17 note: "A garment, shoe or accessory with heating or lights stays here." A device whose main function is computing, communication, sensing or media goes to step 24.
- Step 17 note on sport: "Made for a sport means made for the demands of one sport, dance or outdoor activity and not suited to everyday wear: football boots, ski boots ..., climbing shoes and harnesses, ballet, tap and dance shoes, ..., equestrian riding hats ..." go to step 26 (E2). "Running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers ... stay here."
- Step 17 branch order: footwear is tried before garments. Step 17 A1 branch lists "hosiery", so socks are A1.
- Step 17 A1 branch lists "motorcycle and cycling helmets and motorcycle riding gear" as protective clothing, but the first branch (footwear of any kind) is tried first, so motorcycle boots are A2. Step 13 note: "riders' clothing and helmets are step 17".
- Step 2 (H2): braces, supports, "mobility and assistive aids" are H2; note: "An ordinary object made in an adapted form for a disabled user ... is filed as that object: 'adapted' is a modifier."
- Step 8 and S3 (parts follow the whole): a component, attachment or consumable made to be fitted to or used with one kind of object goes to that object's category. Never parts: "racks, shelves, hooks, hangers, boxes" (B1), "shoe bags" (both in the B1 furniture-and-storage list), anything worn on the body (step 17), and "cleaning products are B6".
- S4 and Appendix A: region, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user (baby, child, adult, gender, size, ability: adapted forms), setting (indoor, outdoor), market (domestic, commercial, professional), occasion, motive, material, condition and edition never split the top level.
- S2: a kind is a generic type; models, cuts and designs are named forms; an individual item is never a kind.

**Precedence sentences that already exist for A2 (restated, not new):** (1) Footwear of any kind is A2 unless it is made for a sport (E2). (2) Running shoes, hiking boots and general trainers stay A2. (3) Socks and hosiery are A1. (4) Shoe racks, shoe cabinets and shoe bags are B1. (5) Adapted ordinary shoes stay A2; footwear made to treat a condition is H2 (step 2). (6) Safety and steel-toe boots are A2 (step 17, first branch).

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications
- **HS 2022 chapter 64** (https://hsmate.com/en-us/hs-code/chapter-64): 6 headings, 25 six-digit codes. 6401 waterproof footwear with rubber or plastic soles and uppers; 6402 other rubber or plastic footwear (sports footwear such as ski boots split out at 6402.12); 6403 leather uppers (with a metal toe-cap at 6403.40); 6406 parts including removable insoles. The chapter divides by **outsole and upper material** plus a few tests (waterproof, ankle cover, toe-cap, sports). Material is a modifier here (Appendix A), so HS is useful only to confirm that safety toe-caps, sports footwear and removable insoles are recognised classes.
- **EN ISO 20345 safety footwear** (https://www.colbrook.co.uk/blog/latest-news-1/en-iso-20345-safety-footwear-standards-explained-sb-s1-s2-s3-s4-s5-23): six cumulative levels, SB, S1, S2, S3, S4, S5, all with a 200 J toe cap; S1 adds antistatic and closed heel energy absorption, S2 water penetration resistance, S3 a penetration-resistant midsole and cleated sole, S4 and S5 moulded waterproof footwear; additional marks P, HI, CI, WR, HRO, SRC, E. This is a spec-ranked ladder. It is read through a retailer blog, not the standard itself (†).
- **ISO 19407:2023 sizing** (https://www.iso.org/standard/83106.html): conversion tables for Mondopoint (foot length), European and UK systems plus China, Japan, Korea and the US; adults and children. Size is an attribute (C6). ISO 19952:2025 (footwear vocabulary, ISO/TC 216) exists but was located in search only and not fetched.
- **Medicare therapeutic shoes** (https://www.cms.gov/medicare-coverage-database/view/article.aspx?articleId=52501): "depth shoe" (A5500: removable filler giving at least 3/16 inch extra depth, a minimum of three widths) and "custom-molded shoe" (A5501: built on a positive model of the foot) are defined classes, with inserts A5512 to A5514. This is the medical edge of A2.

- **Japanese Wikipedia, 靴** (https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/靴): types by shape (short shoes versus boots, with heel-open or strap-only footwear classed as sandals, and indoor ones as slippers), by material, by use (business, casual, sports, rain, safety, riding, military), then period and ethnic shoes.

### 1.7 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects differently
**Agree:** every retailer and reference keeps boots, sandals, sneakers or athletic shoes, and slippers or house shoes as separate nodes (Zalando, ABC-MART, Deichmann, Myntra, Wikipedia, Grailed). Sneakers are always a sibling of boots and never a child of them. Dress-shoe types (oxford, derby, monk, loafer) are named the same way in Hockerty, Wikipedia, Dr. Martens and ABC-MART.
**Split differently:**
1. **High-top sneakers:** Deichmann (Hohe Sneaker), ABC-MART (ハイカットスニーカー) and Grailed (Hi-Top Sneakers) keep them with sneakers; a pure shaft-height rule would call them boots.
2. **Mules and backless shoes:** Zalando has Mules as a node; Japanese Wikipedia classes a shoe with an open heel or heel strap only as a sandal.
3. **Toe-cap pattern versus lacing:** ABC-MART splits business shoes by toe-cap (straight tip, plain toe, wing tip, U-tip) while Hockerty and Wikipedia split by lacing system (oxford, derby) and treat broguing as decoration.
4. **Work footwear:** ABC-MART makes 作業靴 (work shoes) a top node and also lists ワークブーツ under Boots; Red Wing sells Heritage "work" boots as fashion. Certification (EN ISO 20345) separates the two.
5. **Gender:** Myntra, ABC-MART, Deichmann and Zalando all open with gender or age; Wikipedia's navbox has a "Women's" group. Gender is a user modifier.
6. **Material:** HS chapter 64 and Wikipedia's "Wooden footwear" group divide by material; retailers do not.
7. **Traditional footwear:** Wikipedia (Folk footwear, by country) and the Bata Shoe Museum (by culture) divide by region; Japanese sources divide by form (geta, zori, waraji).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence is consistent: buyers choose in this order.
1. **User and size (the gate).** Myntra's first filter is Men, Women, Boys, Girls; ABC-MART opens with 性別 (gender) and size in centimetres; Deichmann splits Damen, Herren, Kinder before any shoe type; Zalando has separate men's and women's trees; Grailed's footwear sizes are the main facet. Size is governed by ISO 19407 (https://www.iso.org/standard/83106.html). Under C6, size and width are attributes with their standard, and user is a tag with generated department views; neither is a level.
2. **Type or family.** After the gate every site lists type nodes (sneakers, boots, sandals, loafers, pumps). Myntra's category counts show type as the main navigation: Casual 71,095, Heels 63,529, Flip Flops 48,037.
3. **Maker.** Brand is the first "top filter" at Zalando UK (Brand, Size, Colour, Qualities, Price); Zappos' index carries many maker entries (Dansko, Frye, Hunter, Ugg, Sorel); ABC-MART has a brand tab with named series (Stan Smith, Superstar, Ultraboost; All Star, Jack Purcell, One Star; the brand of each series is inferred from its position on the page, †); Mercado Libre's most-wanted searches are makers (Hush Puppies, Mizuno); StockX filters by product line before shoe height.
4. **Construction and quality (dress shoes and boots only).** Hockerty's guide is titled "How Your Dress Shoes Are Really Built"; Styleforum threads rank makers.
5. **Silhouette then colourway (sneaker collectors).** StockX lists product line, then colourway; GOAT browses silhouette, brand, designer.

The structural axis that matters most for A2 is therefore **form family** (type as the trade names it), with user and size as a gate and maker as the identity layer.

## 3. Candidate structures

Maximum depth for all three is the framework's four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; C5(a)), or fewer where argued. Variants are never a level (C3).

### Candidate A (recommended): form family, then kind, then named form
- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: footwear family, the form lineage the trade uses as its top split, tested on the object's construction.** Five form families: (1) Sneakers and athletic-style shoes, (2) Shoes (closed, non-athletic, structured outsole), (3) Boots (shaft above the ankle bone or at it, by canonical form), (4) Sandals and open footwear, (5) Slippers (soft, unstructured footwear without a rigid outsole). A sixth form family holds footwear worn over another shoe: (6) Over-footwear. The last sub-family is (7) Parts and accessories made for one pair of shoes (C5(d); the only sub-family exempt from the level-1 axis). There is no work and safety sub-family: certification is an attribute (`protection_class`) with a generated "Safety footwear" view (review F11). OWNER: confirm this level 1 (review question A2-1).
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis stated per sub-family (C5(c)).** Shoes: **closure and vamp cut** (fastened over a covered instep; slip-on over a covered instep; low-cut court and strap). Boots: **shaft height** by canonical form (Ankle; Mid; Calf; Knee and over-knee). Every other sub-family holds about 12 kinds or fewer and skips the leaf (`leaf = null`, C5(b)).
- **Level 3, kind. Axis: generic type a buyer can own and compare** (Oxford, Chelsea boot, flip-flop).
- **Level 4, named form. Axis: a specific design below the kind**, either maker-bound (Iron Ranger, 1460, Air Force 1 Low) or not maker-bound (penny loafer, klompen), recorded with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2(a)).
- Example kinds (13): low-top sneaker; high-top sneaker; running shoe; skate shoe; Oxford; derby; monk strap; loafer; court shoe (pump); ballet flat; Chelsea boot; chukka boot; cowboy boot.
- **Where it breaks (edge objects), and how the revised tree resolves each:** (1) performance basketball shoes and spiked or cleated shoes, which the key sends to E2, sit next to lifestyle sneakers that share their silhouette (resolved by the section 9 sentence; performance basketball and tennis shoes OWNER, section 10); (2) the Chelsea "boot" that is ankle-high and worn as a dress shoe (a boot by form: its shaft reaches the ankle; Boots > Ankle); (3) work boots without certification versus certified safety boots (both are filed by form; certification is `protection_class`); (4) mules and clogs, which the sources place in three different families (Shoes > Slip-on, with the tests in section 3 tree notes; OWNER, section 10); (5) the "slipper boot" and sheepskin boot (Zappos slipper boots, ABC-MART ボアブーツ) that work indoors and out (the slipper test is construction: a soft boot without a rigid outsole is a slipper boot; a sheepskin-lined boot on a moulded outsole is a boot; the Ugg Classic is OWNER, section 10).

### Candidate B: enclosure first (boot, closed shoe, open, indoor)
- **Level 1. Axis: how much of the foot and leg the upper encloses** (shaft above the ankle; closed below the ankle; open at heel, toe or instep; indoor only). Supported by the first split in Japanese Wikipedia (open versus closed) and the Wikipedia Footwear "Types" headings (Boots, Shoes, Sandals, Slippers).
- **Level 2. Axis: closure or cut.** **Level 3, kind; Level 4, named form.**
- Example kinds (12): Chelsea boot; chukka; lace-up boot; knee-high boot; low-top sneaker; high-top sneaker (as a boot); Oxford; loafer; court shoe; flip-flop; slide; slipper.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) high-top sneakers would become boots, against Deichmann, ABC-MART and Grailed, where they are a large share of the market (Grailed 65k hi-top versus 210k low-top listings); (2) mules and clogs (open heel, closed toe), which Japanese Wikipedia files with sandals and Zalando keeps apart; (3) espadrilles and Mary Janes that may or may not cover the ankle strap; (4) slippers, which are defined by indoor use, so the axis is not pure.

### Candidate C: function and occasion first (dress, casual, athletic, work, outdoor, weather, indoor)
- **Level 1. Axis: occasion or use.** Mirrors ABC-MART's top level (Business, Casual, Sports, Outdoor, Rain, Work) and the Japanese Wikipedia "by use" list.
- Example kinds (11): Oxford (dress); derby (dress/casual); Chelsea boot; running shoe; hiking boot; rain boot; safety boot; snow boot; nurse clog; slipper; sandal.
- **Where it breaks:** Appendix A makes occasion and setting modifiers, and S1 requires one home: a Chelsea boot is dress, casual and work at once (Red Wing Classic Chelsea, Dr. Martens 2976, Deichmann Chelsea Boots); a Dr. Martens 1460 is work, punk and fashion; one boot would need several homes.

**Recommendation:** Candidate A. It matches the top split in Zalando, ABC-MART, Deichmann, Myntra, Grailed and Wikipedia (sneakers beside boots beside sandals), keeps high-top sneakers with sneakers as the markets do, and avoids occasion, setting and market as levels. Shaft height (the measurable part of Candidate B's enclosure axis) is the leaf axis inside Boots only.

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds (rough):** 100 to 140. Evidence: ABC-MART has about 70 category nodes under shoes; Wikipedia's Footwear page lists about 80 types in its Types headings; Wikipedia's list of shoe styles has about 136 first-level and about 190 total entries in its main list (more in its navbox groups), of which many are historical (poulaine, chopine) or regional (opanak, galesh), and many regional ones become non-maker named forms under C2(c) and C11; the worked tree above has 61 mainstream kinds (including 2 over-footwear and 5 parts kinds). A first pass of about 110 kinds covers mainstream and most traditional types.

**Named forms (rough):** 1,500 to 3,000 for models and iconic designs, maker-bound and not. Evidence: the StockX Nike brand filter lists dozens of product lines for one maker; Red Wing's Heritage range alone shows about a dozen models; Dr. Martens sells four icons in dozens of variants. Colourways and collaborations multiply this to tens of thousands of style codes in the resale market (StockX and GOAT list each colourway as a product); those are variants (C3), not named forms (see 4b and 6).

**Where depth concentrates:** (1) sneakers, where depth is almost all in named forms and variants; (2) boots, where makers' icon models and the work-boot canon sit; (3) dress shoes, where depth is in construction and last, which are attributes; (4) heeled women's shoes, where depth is in heel form, an attribute with ten values at Zalando; (5) traditional footwear, a long tail of non-maker named forms with region tags and a few kinds.

**Leaf sizes:**
- Broad (many kinds or very many forms): Sneakers; Boots (Mid is the widest leaf, 8 kinds); Shoes (Slip-on is the widest leaf, 6 kinds).
- Medium: Sandals and open footwear.
- Narrow: Slippers; Over-footwear; Parts and accessories.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Verdict: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought, in all three of the places where depth lives.
- Sneakers: StockX and GOAT browse brand and silhouette; collaborations are "Partner x Brand Model"; ABC-MART organises its brand tab by named series.
- Boots: Red Wing and Dr. Martens are bought for the maker (their own pages sell named icons); Alden and Viberg appear as makers in search excerpts of Styleforum and Grailed only (†); Red Wing issues its icons in numbered styles by leather.
- Dress shoes: Styleforum ranks makers and in-house lines; r/goodyearwelt indexes "by brand and model".

It is **not platform**: no maker sets compatibility with the footwear of another. Size systems are shared (ISO 19407). Maker widths and lasts differ, but that is fit data. No compatibility ledger (C13) is needed.

**Recommendation: a maker ledger is needed, in the shared C1 schema.**
- *Core columns:* `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags` (C8), `price_band` (C4(d)), `sources` (at least one fetched URL, a second where available).
- *A2 extension column:* `construction_policy` (for example Goodyear welt or stitchdown throughout, as Red Wing states; cemented; mixed).
- *Separate tables, not columns:* **lines** (Crockett & Jones handgrade and benchgrade, Cheaney Imperial and mainline, Red Wing Heritage, Dr. Martens Made in England; with `built_by_maker_id` where a line is made by another maker); **tier entries** (C4(a), section 5); **named forms** (C2: `named_form_id`, `kind_id`, `maker_id` or empty, `maker_bound`, `first_year`, `region`, `scene_tags`, attributes, `sources`). **Variant ledger (C3, optional and sparse, not part of the tree):** `variant_id`, `named_form_id`, `variant_code` (the maker's style code, for example IQ5495-005 as GOAT shows it in its page addresses, or Red Wing's numbered style such as Iron Ranger #8111), `variant_type` (colourway; material; retro or re-issue; collaboration; limited edition), `partner` (for collaborations), `year`, `sources`. ## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds (C7):** canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states. Sneakers: by shoe height, then by activity (ABC-MART orders sneakers slip-on, low, high). Shoes: by formality within each leaf (Hockerty orders dress-shoe types by formality). Boots: by canonical shaft height within the height leaf, low to high. Sandals: by heel height, flat first. Reason: buyers learn a canonical archetype (Oxford before saddle shoe; Chelsea before ankle boot variants) and every source with a tree lists the generic type first.

**Named forms (C7):** by maker, in the order of the maker ledger's `name`; then by first-introduction year as attested (Dr. Martens 1460 in 1960; Red Wing Moc Toe 875 in 1953); then alphabetically where no year is attested. Named forms that are not maker-bound follow the maker-bound ones, by attested year, then alphabetically (C7 is silent on forms without a maker; this is the memo's reading). Collectors speak of "OG" and "retro" relative to the first release, which the year supports.

**Variants (C7):** by `variant_code` or year.

**Sub-families:** the order given in section 3 (Sneakers, Shoes, Boots, Sandals, Slippers, Over-footwear, Parts) follows market volume (Grailed: sneakers first; Myntra: casual first), with the parts sub-family last (C5(d)); it can be reordered without structural effect.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A):** A2 Footwear splits into five form-family sub-families tested on construction: Sneakers and athletic-style shoes; Shoes (closed, non-athletic); Boots; Sandals and open footwear; Slippers (soft, without a rigid outsole). A sixth form family holds Over-footwear, and the last sub-family holds Parts and accessories made for one pair of shoes (C5(d)). Safety footwear is not a sub-family: every safety boot, shoe, wellington and clog is filed by form, with the attribute `protection_class` and a generated view. Below each sub-family, **kinds** are generic types a buyer compares (Oxford, Chelsea boot, flip-flop). Below kinds, **named forms** are maker-bound models (Iron Ranger, 1460, Air Force 1 Low) or non-maker designs (penny loafer, klompen). A **leaf** level is used only inside Shoes (axis: closure and vamp cut) and Boots (axis: shaft height); elsewhere `leaf = null`. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form) in Shoes and Boots, three elsewhere (C5(a), (b)).

**Everything that is not a level** is an attribute, tag, view or ledger:
- attributes: size system and size, `width` (C6), colour, `upper_material`, season, closure details, heel form, `construction`, `protection_class`, `broguing`, `toe_pattern`, `shaft_height`, `gait_support`, heating and lights (C10);
- tags: user (men, women, kids, baby, adapted; C6), region (C2(c)), scene (C8), occasion, `occupation`, "historical";
- ledgers: maker ledger with lines, tier entries and named forms (C1), variant ledger (C3), alias table (C9);
- tier: tier entries on makers and lines for dress shoes and boots (C4(a)); spec ladders as attributes (C4(b)); resale labels as dated platform tags (C4(c)); price band in the domain-wide USD scheme (C4(d));
- not stored: condition grades of particular pairs (C16).

**Choice level:** kinds stop at the generic type a buyer compares (Oxford, derby, Chelsea, chukka, flip-flop, low-top and high-top sneaker). Named forms are maker models and iconic designs, maker-bound or not. Colourways, collaborations and retro years are variants; pair condition and sizes are not forms.

**Tier type:** consensus-ranked for dress shoes and boots (tier entries on makers and lines; construction as an attribute), spec-ranked for safety footwear (an attribute), preference elsewhere. **Maker centrality:** identity; maker ledger and variant ledger required.
