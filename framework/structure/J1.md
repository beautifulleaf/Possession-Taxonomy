## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J1 Meat & Poultry.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 3, first branch; K-47, K-52): *meat, poultry, game and other land animals eaten (frogs, insects), fresh, frozen, cured, smoked or cooked, including whole cuts, mince, offal, sausages, charcuterie, hams, jerky, and cooked, tinned and potted meats.* Category line: "meat, poultry, game and other land animals eaten (frogs, insects) as bought, fresh, frozen, cured, smoked or cooked, including charcuterie and hams, divided by species, cut and grade (for example USDA and JMGA grades, breed, feeding and ageing)."

**Rules and notes that touch J1:**
- Step 1 (OUT) note: "curing, smoking, ageing, fermenting, drying, salting, pickling, tinning, freezing or plain cooking of one main ingredient does not make a food prepared: … hams raw or cooked, sausages and charcuterie … are groceries". A prepared food is "made by cooking or combining ingredients into something eaten or drunk as it is" (dishes, ready meals, "dumplings and other prepared dishes") and is left to Culinary.
- Step 3 note: each grocery is "filed once, as the product as bought, divided by product and by the quality of the ingredient, and Culinary links to it". "Sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes and cooking fats are J5 whatever they are made from (… stock cubes, lard, dripping)". "Dried or cured meat and fish are groceries even when sold salted, roasted or as snacks." "Pasta and cooking sauces in jars, with or without meat, are sauces (J5)." "A set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value, and is OUT if that item is a prepared food." Food made for animals is B10, including "frozen feeder animals" and pet chews.
- Step 3, J2: shellfish and crustaceans sold alive, roe and caviar are J2. J3: eggs are J3. J5: "tofu, tempeh, seitan and other plant proteins and meat alternatives".
- Step 3 note, as amended in v0.7 (K-44, K-45, K-46, K-47, K-52): a spread, paste or dip eaten with other food (rillettes, liver pâté in a jar or tub) is J5, except milk spreads (J3); a pâté, terrine or galantine sold as a loaf or slice to be cut is J1 (meat); a sausage, loaf, patty, ball or other shaped product made mainly of one animal's meat, raw or cooked, seasoned but not coated (breaded or battered), filled (in pastry, as a dumpling or as a stuffed dish) or sauced, is J1; a whole cut cooked, cured, glazed or herb-rolled and sold to slice (glazed ham, porchetta) is J1; a mix of kinds from one category is filed under the kind that makes up half or more of its weight, otherwise under a final kind "mixed <class>", and a mix across categories by its item of greatest value; tinned and plain-cooked meat is J1 (cooked, tinned and potted meats), pastrami and corned brisket pieces included.
- Step 2 note: sports-nutrition and meal-replacement products in every form (including protein bars) are H2.
- Step 1 and the B10 memo: a living animal is OUT; live feeders are OUT.
- Framework rule S2 (generic types): models, cuts and designs are named forms under a kind (decided J-1: the named retail cut is the kind for meat, with national names and sub-cuts as named forms; section 4b). Rule S4 and Appendix A: region, era, price and tier, brand and maker, scene, condition, material and occasion are modifiers, never levels.
- Step 3 note and step 5 (spreads and pastes): "sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes and cooking fats are J5 whatever they are made from", and the J5 branch lists "sweet and savoury spreads, sauces, condiments, stocks and pastes from any source". This governs spreadable meat pastes (section 9, P8). The step 1 note makes "tinning" and "plain cooking of one main ingredient" grocery processes, so plain-cooked and tinned meat is J1 (section 3, J1-D).
- Domain-J conventions JC1 to JC7 (`CONVENTIONS_v1.md`; decided CV-40 to CV-44 and CV-46, with CV-15 and CV-42) and the J section of `_framework_conflicts.md`: binding rules in this memo. D4 R-0 applies: a rule of this memo that no decision covers and that was marked proposed is adopted as written.
- BRIEF.md (owner, 2026-10-17): groceries are "judged by the quality of the ingredient (for example high-quality meat: breed, cut, grade, origin, age)"; "Grocery products that the Culinary taxonomy also holds (… cured meats …) are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind."
- BRIEF.md, stage-2 owner rules (2026-10-17): depth and breadth are the point (no caps on makers, lines or named forms; makers, artisans, houses and schools in ledgers, not as kinds; scene tags where communities exist); one fetched page showing that a thing is or was made or sold proves it exists, and a second source or region is never a condition of inclusion.

### 1.5 Where the sources agree and where they split

**Agree.** (1) For raw meat the animal comes first, and then the cut: HS [S18], IMPS [S37], Interbev [S7], AHDB [S8], wholesalers [S14], Spanish shops [S21]. (2) Every national cut system rests on the same primal logic (US eight primals [S25]; Japanese 9, 13 or 15 parts [S5][S16]; French and British charts [S9][S32]) but the names and cutting lines differ. (3) Official grades are measured on the **carcass**, not on a retail cut: USDA's standard is titled and written for carcasses [S2]; Kobe's bylaws say "beef carcass grading is determined by yield and meat quality scores" [S31]; a Hanwoo restaurant says "one grade is stamped on the whole carcass" [S22]. (4) Cured meats and sausages are defined by process, not by species: HS 0210 [S18], IMPS 500/600/800 [S37], Leitsätze [S17], Japanese labelling [S15].

**Split.** (1) Species-first all the way down (Ocado puts Parma ham and salami inside pork [S35]) against process-first for made products (Japanese wholesaler "加工肉" [S14]; Spanish Jamonería and Charcutería [S21]; French Delicatessen [S28]; IMPS 500–800 [S37]). (2) Offal is its own group in HS 0206, IMPS 700, Interbev's "produits tripiers" poster and the Chinese by-products part [S18][S37][S7][S11], but a sub-category inside pork at Ocado [S35]. (3) D'Artagnan's single Type facet mixes species, breed, feeding, grade and product class [S12]; Crowd Cow splits beef by feeding first [S13]. (4) Sausages are grouped by process in Germany [S17], by composition and pork piece in Japan [S15], and by name and origin at retail [S1][S14][S28]. (5) The same name can mean different cuts: US and UK "brisket" differ [S32]; the Taiwanese guide puts 牛腩 under flank [S30], while another Taiwanese shop page (seen in a search excerpt only, not fetched) equates 牛腩 with brisket; the guide calls 翼板 flat iron [S30], and the same excerpt calls it chuck flap. (6) Ready-to-cook and ready-to-eat lines sit in the meat tree at retail [S1][S35][S36], but the framework sends them to Culinary (section 9).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Raw meat: the animal first, then the cut or use, then quality.**
- Navigation order: Ocado opens with species (Beef, Chicken, Pork, Lamb) and then use classes (steaks, mince, roasting joints, stewing) [S1][S35][S36]; Spanish shops open with ternera, cerdo, pollo, cordero [S21]; the Japanese wholesaler opens with 牛肉, 豚肉, 鶏肉, その他 [S14]; HS and IMPS open with species [S18][S37]; Interbev and AHDB publish per species [S7][S8].
- Filter order on specialist stores: D'Artagnan lists species/breed/grade ("Type") first, then Cut, then Bone/Skin, Fresh/Frozen, Cooking Method, Portion, Brand [S12]; Crowd Cow splits beef by **feeding** first (pasture-raised, grass-fed) and then by butcher's cut and cooking [S13]. These are quality-first stores, and both still use the animal as the first break.
- Buyer questions in guides: "cut, grade, marbling and packaging" [S23]; a Japanese guide teaches the position on the carcass first (four big blocks, then the fine cuts) [S16]; a Korean restaurant says cut, ageing and thickness outweigh the grade [S22]; a Chinese-language guide describes each cut by cooking function (grilling, braising, stewing, hotpot) [S30].

**Cured meats and sausages: the product type first, then the origin or sale name.** A buyer of ham asks first for the type (Ibérico, serrano, Parma, cooked) and then the seal colour [S4][S21]; German and Japanese rules define sausages by process class [S17][S15]; French retail separates saucissons, hams, dried meat, cured meat, rillettes and terrines [S28].

**Order of decisions I infer (estimate):** animal or product type → cut or use → quality scheme (grade, breed, feeding, ageing, sale name) → presentation (bone, skin, fresh or frozen, portion) → origin and maker. Quality is not a first split in the large mainstream tree [S1] but is the first split for premium Iberian ham (the seal colour is a legal feeding × breed class [S4]).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the fixed level names (category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form) and at most four levels below J1. Quality grade, breed, feeding, ageing, sale name, farming type, state (fresh or frozen), bone and skin, region and appellation are never levels (framework rule S4, Appendix A, C4(b)); they are attributes with a named standard, tags or ledger entries (sections 5 and 6).

### Candidate A — species first

- Level 1 sub-family (axis: **the animal**): Beef & veal; Pork; Lamb, mutton & goat; Poultry; Game; Other land animals.
- Level 2 leaf (axis: **product form**): fresh or frozen cuts; offal; mince; cured; sausages and charcuterie; cooked.
- Kinds: cuts, cured items, sausages; named forms below.
- Example (about 12 kinds): Beef > Fresh cuts > ribeye steak, striploin steak, tenderloin, brisket, short rib, shin; Beef > Mince > ground beef; Beef > Offal > tongue; Pork > Fresh cuts > belly, collar, loin chop; Pork > Cured > dry-cured ham, bacon; Pork > Sausages > fresh pork sausage.
- Evidence for: the large UK supermarket tree [S1][S35][S36]; HS Chapter 02 by species [S18]; the category line in CATEGORIES_v0_7 names "species, cut and grade".
- **Where it breaks:** (1) many sausage and cured products mix animals — the Japanese press-ham and sausage definitions allow pork, beef, poultry, horse, sheep, goat and rabbit together [S15] — so they need a sixth, non-species sub-family, and then level 1 has two axes; (2) a charcuterie board has no single animal; (3) duck rillettes and pork rillettes would sit in different sub-families although buyers compare them as one product [S28]; (4) Ibérico ham is bought by seal colour, not as "pork" [S4]; (5) the sources that treat cured and made products as a separate group [S14][S17][S21][S28][S37] are not honoured.

### Candidate B — state of preparation first, animal at the leaf for raw meat (decided, J-2)

- Level 1 sub-family (axis: **how the meat is prepared for sale**): J1-A Fresh & frozen meat (raw muscle, bone, offal, mince, shaped raw mince); J1-B Cured, smoked & dried whole-muscle meats; J1-C Sausages; J1-D Cooked, tinned & potted meats (plain-cooked meat; tinned meat; meat set in fat or jelly in a pot or jar; pâtés and terrines sold as a loaf or slice).
- Level 2 leaf (axis stated per sub-family): J1-A **the animal** (14 leaves, Veal and Goat included; offal sits under each animal, J-3); J1-B **cure class** (raw-cured; cooked-cured; dried and jerked; cured fat; pickled and fermented †); J1-C **process class** (fresh, to be cooked; dry-cured and fermented; scalded; cooked, liver and blood). J1-D has about 12 kinds at most and skips the leaf level (C5(b)); its kinds are ordered plain-cooked, then tinned and potted, then pâtés and terrines. If it passes about 12 kinds it takes those three groups as leaves (axis: how the cooked meat is kept or sold).
- Level 3 kind: the **named retail cut** (J1-A, under the kind rule below), the **type of cured piece** (J1-B), the **sausage type** (J1-C), the **product type** (J1-D). Level 4 named form: national or sub-cut names, protected names and traditional varieties.
- **Level-1 tie-break (first yes; C5(c)).** Try in this order. (1) Comminuted meat sold as a sausage (in a casing, or formed as a sausage), whether fresh, cured, scalded or cooked: J1-C. (2) Meat that is cooked, tinned, potted or made into a pâté or terrine, as a product that exists in that form (corned beef in a tin, luncheon meat, confit or brawn set in fat or jelly, sliced roast meat, a rotisserie chicken, a pâté loaf; a spreadable potted meat or rillettes is J5, P8; a plain or only seasoned shaped product of one meat sold cooked, such as a cooked meatball or patty, and a herb-rolled or glazed uncured roast sold to slice, such as porchetta, K-45): J1-D. (3) A whole muscle or piece that is cured, smoked, dried or pickled, raw or cured then cooked (dry-cured ham, bacon, cooked ham, pastrami): J1-B. (4) Everything else raw, chilled or frozen: J1-A. A tin, jar or pouch is a pack attribute when the same product is also sold unpacked (a cooked ham or a sausage in a tin keeps its home, JC4(c)); it makes a J1-D kind only when the product exists as tinned-cooked-in-the-pack or as a solid potted piece set in fat or jelly (JC4(b)). A tinned ready meal is prepared (step 1). The placement of tinned and plain-cooked meat in J1-D is decided (K-52); a cured piece that is cooked or glazed (glazed ham) stays in J1-B Cooked-cured (K-45).
- **Level-3 kind rule for J1-A (C5(c), review J1-3; decided J-1, J-3).** A kind is the animal and its named retail cut or joint (J-1: the named retail cut is the kind for meat). A cut enters as a kind on one fetched page that shows it sold (one-source rule); no minimum number of attested cuts is required. For a species whose cut kinds have not been harvested yet, the leaf holds the placeholder kind "<species>, whole or jointed", with the cut as the attribute `cut`; stage 3 adds the cut kinds from the cut catalogues (AHDB, Interbev, national charts), and the placeholder then stays only for the animal sold unjointed or a cut not yet a kind. A kind sold whole (suckling pig, suckling lamb) is a kind of its own. Primals, sides and carcasses sold as such stay kinds, listed after the retail cuts in the animal's leaf (J-3) and named "<animal> primal or side" (beef is attested [S25]; the others are added at harvest). Today beef (12 attested cuts and the primal or side), pork (8), chicken (whole bird, breast and leg), lamb (rack and leg) and venison (saddle and medallions) split by cut; goat, turkey, rabbit, hare, wild boar, horse, bison and each game bird hold the placeholder.
- Example kinds (about 15): J1-A Beef: ribeye steak, striploin steak, tenderloin, brisket, short rib, ground beef, tongue; J1-A Pork: belly, collar, loin chop, trotter; J1-A Duck & goose: duck breast, fatty liver; J1-A Wild boar: wild boar, whole or jointed; J1-B: dry-cured ham leg, cured pork shoulder, bacon, cooked ham; J1-C: dry-cured salami, scalded sausage; J1-D: pâté, terrine, corned beef in a tin †.
- Evidence for: HS separates raw (0201–0209) from salted, dried or smoked (0210) [S18]; IMPS puts fresh in series 100–400 and cured, dried, smoked, variety and sausage in 500–800 [S37]; the German Leitsätze group sausages and cured meats by process [S17]; Japan splits "Processed" from species in the wholesaler's list [S14] and defines ham, bacon and sausage by process [S15]; Spanish and French shops split Jamonería, Charcutería and Delicatessen from raw meat [S21][S28]. Species is kept as a required attribute (`animal`) on every kind in J1-B, J1-C and J1-D and gives a generated "by animal" view that restores the species-first path of [S1].
- **Where it breaks:** (1) offal appears in several leaves (beef tongue under Beef; pork trotter under Pork), although HS, IMPS, Interbev and the Chinese standards treat offal as its own group [S18][S37][S7][S11] — handled with a generated "offal and by-products" view and the decision J-3 (offal under each animal; C14: no category-level multi-use sub-family is used); (2) a cured-and-cooked item such as pastrami or corned beef brisket sits on the border of J1-B and J1-D: the tie-break above files it in J1-B Cooked-cured when sold as a piece, and the same meat sealed in a tin is the J1-D kind "corned beef in a tin" (K-52); (3) a minced-and-shaped raw product (burger, meatball, kebab) is raw in form but is treated as prepared by some rules [S17]: filed as the J1-A kind "shaped raw mince" if plain or only seasoned and as a J1-D kind when sold cooked (K-45); (4) fresh sausage has the same ingredients as mince: the test is the sausage form (tie-break step 1), so a loose or patted mince is J1-A and a sausage in a casing or formed as a sausage is J1-C.

### Candidate C — use or cooking method first

- Level 1 (axis: **how it is cooked**): Steaks and grilling; Roasting joints; Stewing and braising; Mince; Cured and cold cuts.
- Evidence for: Ocado's beef sub-categories [S36]; Interbev groups cuts by culinary destination and cooking type [S7]; D'Artagnan's Cooking Method facet [S12]; AHDB's per-cut cooking pages [S8].
- **Where it breaks:** (1) one cut has different uses in different countries: "bavette d'aloyau" is slow-cooked in the UK and flash-fried in France; chuck is a casserole cut in the UK and a grilled cut in France [S9]; (2) the Japanese guide rates the same cut for steak, barbecue, roast, sukiyaki and shabu-shabu at once [S5]; (3) cooking method, occasion and scene are modifiers (Appendix A), so they cannot be levels; (4) the same muscle (brisket) is a smoked barbecue cut, a braised joint and a corned meat. Rejected as a structure; kept as a generated view by cooking method and by cuisine (section 7).

**Modelling (C4, C16, CV-40, J1-O1).** (a) A measured or legal scale (USDA, JMGA, BMS, Hanwoo, GB/T, RD 4/2014, Reg. 543/2008) is an **attribute with its standard named** (C4(c) class (i)). The JC1 tables of CONVENTIONS_v1 hold it: a `standard` table (`standard_id`, `name`, `authority`, `steps`, `sources`), with values attached as (`subject_id`, `standard_id`, `value`); each scale is also registered once in the C4(c) scheme registry (class (i), with `kinds_served`). These tables replace the first-draft `grade_scheme` ledger. No grade value is stored for a particular lot or item (C16, CV-33). Decided J1-O1: a claimed grade is a value row on a maker line or a named form only when it holds for every object of that line or form (for example a line sold only at one grade); the first-draft `grade_claimed_by` field on a retail product is not used. (b) The Iberian sale name and the EU farming term are attributes of the same kind: they grade the same product and fix no different composition (JC4, CV-42); none of the J1 sale names is a kind (a ladder is an attribute; only a name that fixes a different composition or process is a kind, K-45 and JC4). (c) Retailer labels are `platform_tag` entries, not tiers (C4(c) class (vi)). (d) Designations (Kobe Beef, Prosciutto di Parma, the German protected-name meats listed in the Leitsätze annex [S17]) go in a `designation` table: `designation_id`, `name`, `scheme` (PDO, GI, national), `register_id`, `authority` (the consortium or association, never a maker), `sources`. Placement (JC1, CV-40): a designation that binds one kind is a non-maker named form of it carrying `designation_id` (Prosciutto di Parma under dry-cured ham); one that spans several kinds is a tag (Kobe Beef); a designation is a kind only under C11 (no J1 case). The written exception of CV-40 (wine and spirit designations are kinds) does not touch J1. (e) Tier entries (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) are used only for maker lines or named forms whose tier a source states, for example a producer's own named grade lines; they never go on a kind and a named form does not inherit a maker's tier. A tier scheme enters on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10); a second source or region only adds weight. The Snake River breed price tiers [S24] enter from their one fetched page as editorial class entries (C4(c) class (iii)); the Arcecarne "extra, select, top" grades [S20] are a shop's own label and enter as dated `platform_tag` entries, not tiers (class (vi)).

**Cross-scheme comparison is not valid.** Sources say an A5 and a "1++" or a USDA Prime are not equivalent [S6][S22]. Store each standard separately; do not store a "same as" table. A grade is assigned to the carcass [S2][S31][S22]; a retail product carries only the claim of its source carcass, which is not stored (C16).

**Price band (CV-15, JC2).** J1 carries the J unit band: `unit_price_band` in USD per kilogram, from a dated, sourced list price, with the bands set at the first data load; the C4(d) item band is not used in J1 (only J7 keeps an item band, CV-15). Per-kilo spread in the pages fetched, in the source currency: UK lean mince about £10.40 per kg and pork belly slices £9.00 per kg, Parma ham £37.50 to £57.14 per kg, and a 35-day-aged sirloin £85.71 per kg [S35][S36]; a 7.1 kg bellota ham for €389 is about €55 per kg (my arithmetic) [S28]. Conversion to USD needs a dated rate that is not in the sources †.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality (decided): identity, led by origin and scheme, not by the manufacturer name.** Buyers of premium meat seek a ranch, a farm system, an appellation or a ham house: Crowd Cow names the ranch on each product [S13], Snake River Farms sells purebred Kurobuta pork under its own name [S24] (its "Gold" and "Black" beef line names were seen in search excerpts only †), D'Artagnan sells under one brand [S12], Hawksmoor and Finnebrogue appear as maker names on UK lines [S35][S36], and the Japanese wholesaler lists prefecture-branded pork and chicken [S14]. For commodity cuts the maker is incidental. No platform dependence (no compatibility ledger, C13).

**Maker ledger.** The one uncapped domain ledger (C1, CV-5), because a quality-led buyer looks up the producer: ranches, farms, ham houses, smokehouses, butchers, packers and retail own-labels are ledger rows, each admitted on one fetched page, and none is a kind. `maker_class` keeps its C1 values (an appellation body or association is not a maker; it is the `authority` of a designation row, and "producer (farm or ranch)" is a supply role, not an identity class). One J extension column, `supply_role` (multi-valued, CV-41; the JC3 list plus `butcher`, J1-O3): farm or ranch; processor or packer; curer or smokehouse; importer or trader; retail own-label; butcher. Not maker columns: `farm_system` (grass-fed, pasture-raised, free range, organic are product attributes, section 3), `certifications` (attributes of the named form or product) and `scheme_ids` (derived from the named-form ledger). A bottled-by or packed-by fact on an own-label line uses C1's `built_by_maker_id`; "licensed by" and "rebrand of" go in the maker-relations table. Known-for named forms are not a ledger column (C1): derive them from the named-form ledger. Traditions of curers and breeders (for example the Iberian dehesa ham tradition, the Tajima cattle tradition †) are rows of the standing school table (C1, CV-3) where a fetched page names them; makers link by `school_id`. A maker line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form and a line spanning several kinds is a lines row (C2(d), R-18). Variants (C3, CV-8) are filled wherever a fetched source compares them (a ham house's cure-length editions or a ranch's seasonal releases, `variant_type` edition or vintage †); none is seeded now.

**Examples seen** (supply roles are my reading of each source's own description): ranches Little Belt Cattle Co. and Royal Ranch Farms [S13] (farm or ranch); Snake River Farms [S24] (farm or ranch); Canvin & Gunner, The Butcher's Board, Hawksmoor [S36] (processor or packer, or butcher where the page describes a butcher's counter); M&S "Heritage Gold" (retail own-label) [S35]; Finnebrogue, Golfera, Unearthed [S35] (processor or packer); Maximiliano Jabugo (ham house: curer or smokehouse) [S28]. The Consorzio del Prosciutto di Parma and the Kobe Beef Marketing and Distribution Promotion Association are designation authorities, not makers [S33][S34][S31].

**Kinds, named forms and makers (C2).** A named form is maker-bound only for a maker line (a "Gold" ribeye filet; Finnebrogue Naked sausage). A cut name (tomahawk), a regional name (entrecôte) and a protected name (Prosciutto di Parma) are `maker_bound: no`; the protected name also carries a `designation_id`. A line that spans several kinds is a C1 line row; a line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Axis: canonical form first, then position on the animal.** For J1-A leaves: whole-muscle steak and roast cuts first, ordered **front to back along the carcass** (chuck, rib, loin, round, then belly-side and lower cuts), then kinds sold whole (suckling pig), then primals or sides (J-3), then mince, then shaped raw mince, then offal last. Within the same position, steak cuts come before roasting joints. The tree of section 3 follows this order (beef: chuck roast, flat iron, ribeye, striploin, tenderloin, T-bone, top sirloin, rump cap, skirt, short rib, brisket, shin, then ground beef, shaped raw mince and tongue; pork: jowl, collar, loin chop, tenderloin, ribs, belly, hock, trotter, suckling pig, ground pork, shaped raw mince). Reason: every cut catalogue found is built in carcass order: the MAFF guide numbers chuck roll to knuckle [S5], Grassland's eight primals run chuck, rib, loin, round, flank, plate, brisket, shank [S25], Interbev has separate forequarter and hindquarter posters [S7], and the Japanese guide teaches four blocks (back, shoulder, belly, rump and leg) [S16]. Leaves are ordered by animal as printed (cattle, pigs, sheep and goats, poultry, then game, then other land animals). For J1-B: by cure class as in section 3, then, within a leaf, the canonical dry-cured ham first and the other pieces front to back (jowl, neck, shoulder, loin, belly, then cured beef). For J1-C: by process class (fresh, dry, scalded, cooked), then the canonical form of the class first. For J1-D: plain-cooked, then tinned and potted, then pâtés and terrines. The final mixed kind (K-46) is last in its sub-family. Named forms: C7 and CV-21: non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms by maker (ledger `name` order), first-introduction year, then alphabetically.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure: Candidate B** (J-2). J1 → sub-family (**how the meat is prepared for sale**: fresh & frozen; cured, smoked & dried; sausages; cooked, tinned & potted) → leaf (**the animal** in J1-A, with Veal its own leaf and offal under each animal (J-3); **cure class** in J1-B; **process class** in J1-C; none in J1-D) → kind (**the named retail cut** in J1-A (J-1), with primals, sides and carcasses sold as such listed after the retail cuts (J-3) and the placeholder "<species>, whole or jointed" only for a species whose cuts are not yet harvested; the type of cured piece in J1-B; the sausage type in J1-C; the product type in J1-D; one final "mixed <class>" kind per sub-family with `leaf = null` (K-46)) → named form (national and sub-cut names, protected names that bind one kind, varieties, maker lines). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category** (three in J1-D). Every quality statement (grade, marbling, sale name, farming term, breed, feeding, ageing, appellation) is an attribute with its standard named, a tag or a `designation` row, and never a level. Generated views: "by animal" (species-first across all sub-families), "offal and by-products" (across animals), "by tradition" and "by cooking style" (from Culinary links). Boundaries decided: tinned and plain-cooked meat is J1-D (K-52); a shaped product of one meat, raw or cooked, is J1 unless coated, filled or sauced, and a whole cut cooked, cured, glazed or herb-rolled and sold to slice is J1 (K-45); meat spreads in a jar or tub are J5 and loaf pâtés and terrines are J1-D (K-44); frogs, turtle and edible insects are J1 and snails are J2 (K-47). The 20-object check in section 3 gives each object one home under this structure (20 of 20).

**Choice level (decided):** the named retail cut for meat (J-1), lamb and venison cuts included as one fetched page shows them; the placeholder kind per species until its cuts are harvested; species × joint for chicken; the type of cured piece for cured meats; the sausage type for sausages. **Tier type:** spec-ranked carcass standards, rule-defined classes for Ibérico and EU poultry, preference for breed tiers and retailer labels; a scheme enters on one fetched source. **Maker centrality:** identity, origin- and scheme-led; one uncapped maker ledger with `supply_role` (butcher included), lines, relations and the standing school table.

**Former open questions, all decided** (the memo's item numbers; D3, "Memo owner items answered elsewhere", J1 line, and the J1-O lines):
1. First axis: state of preparation, with a generated by-animal view (J-2).
2. Kind against named form for cuts: the named retail cut is the kind for meat (J-1).
3. Standards and designations: `standard` and `designation` tables, one-kind designations named forms, multi-kind designations tags (CV-40); a claimed grade is a value row on a maker line or named form only when it holds for every object of it, never per lot (J1-O1, CV-33).
4. Price band: `unit_price_band`, USD per kilogram (CV-15).
5. Raw and shaped products: K-45.
6. Plain-cooked and tinned meat: J1-D (K-52).
7. Spreads and pastes: J5; loaf pâtés and terrines J1-D (K-44).
8. Veal: its own leaf (J-3).
9. Offal: under each animal, with a cross-species view (J-3).
10. Cut systems: one canonical list of cut kinds with `cut_system` tags and C9 alias and homonym rows (J1-O2).
11. Primals, sides and carcasses: kinds listed after the retail cuts (J-3).
