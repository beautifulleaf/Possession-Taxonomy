## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B7 Climate, Water & Energy (domain B, Home).

**Membership test (CATEGORIES_v0_7, key step 30, third branch):** made to heat, cool, ventilate, purify or humidify air; to heat, soften, filter or supply water for bathing and washing, or take away waste; or to generate, store or meter electricity for the home. Named kinds: heaters, fans and ceiling fans, air conditioners, heat pumps, dehumidifiers, air purifiers, thermostats, water heaters, softeners and filters, baths, showers, basins, toilets and bidets (fitted or freestanding), hot tubs and portable saunas, solar panels and inverters, home batteries and portable power stations, small wind turbines, energy meters.

**Notes that bind (S1b):** electric blankets and heated throws are B2. Tables with a built-in heater (kotatsu, korsi) are B1. Wall-box chargers for electric vehicles are G1 under S3. Step 4 sends a building's installed baths, showers, basins, toilets and hot tubs to B7, and fixed plumbing and wiring to C8. A connected version of a household object (smart thermostat) is filed by its task (step 24 note). Branch order in step 30 tries B5, B6, B7, B8, B3, B1 in that order, so a ceiling fan with a light is B7 (the B3 memo records the same). Cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8 whatever they are used in (S3). Pool cleaning equipment, pumps and test kits, and above-ground and inflatable pools, are C2 (step 29). Hot-water bottles, heat packs and hand warmers are H1 (step 18). UPSs, hardware wallets and power banks are D1 or D2 (step 24). **Portable generators are C1 (step 32 names them), not B7.** The key (v0_7) also settles: appliances that heat air or water (boilers, furnaces, stoves, underfloor heating kits, hot-water cylinders, solar thermal) are B7, while plumbing fittings, taps, valves, shower trays, enclosures and bath panels are C8 (B7-O1, K-186); a washbasin sold built into its cabinet is B7 (K-184); a smart radiator head follows the thermostat (B7); a powered hand-held or neck fan is B7 (K-133); a portable sauna is B7 and a sauna cabin that stands as a building is G2 (K-64); a portable power station is B7 with the home battery (K-157).

**Appendix A modifiers that apply and so may never be a level:** region and culture, era (antique, vintage, retro), price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, user (including adapted forms such as accessible baths), setting (indoor, outdoor, bathroom, camping), market (domestic, commercial), installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted, wall-hung), power source and technology (electric, gas, oil, solar, and by extension LED-style technology swaps), connectivity (smart), occasion, motive, origin, how acquired (kit, DIY), material, condition, edition. An efficiency grade is a measured attribute with its standard named, not a tier (C4(b)); the rule for the few cases where technology, mounting, region or material does define a kind is C10 to C12 and section 4b.

**Precedence sentences touching B7:** section 9. Every case that needed an owner answer is decided and carried by the key (v0_7): boilers, furnaces and stoves; underfloor heating kits; hot-water cylinders and solar thermal collectors; taps; air-quality monitors; shower trays and enclosures; pool heat pumps; the portable power station; the powered hand-held fan against A4; the washbasin sold built into a cabinet (B7-O1, K-133, K-147, K-157, K-176, K-184, K-186).

**Where they agree.** Everyone separates the three jobs somewhere: air (heat, cool, move, clean), water (heat, treat, bathe and dispose), electricity (generate, store, convert). Within air, the next cut is always the physical form or install position of the machine (wall, floor, ceiling, window, portable; radiator, fan heater, stove; desk, tower, ceiling fan). Within bathrooms the first cut is the fixture (bath, basin, toilet, shower).

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- Air conditioner versus heat pump: Kakaku tags an air conditioner "cooling-and-heating" or "cooling-only" [KA]; the customs heading 8415 treats reversible heat pumps as air conditioning machines while a separate subheading holds other heat pumps [T84]; ENERGY STAR lists "Heat Pumps" and "Room Air Conditioners" as separate product groups [ESF]; the EU puts air conditioners with comfort fans, and puts hydronic heat pumps with boilers under "Space Heaters" [EUL].
- Fans: Kakaku files fans apart from heaters and air conditioners [KF]; ENERGY STAR puts ceiling fans and ventilating fans under "Lighting & Fans" [ESF]; the EU calls them "Air Conditioners and Comfort Fans" [EUL].
- Heaters: Screwfix keeps radiators, central heating, fires and stoves, electric heating as separate departments [SX]; Kakaku puts portable fuel heaters together by fuel [KH]; Consumer Reports divides space heaters by mechanism (convection versus radiant) [CS].
- Water heaters: the EU regulates them (and storage tanks to 2,000 L) as one group [EUW]; Japan files them as housing equipment and divides by function (hot water only, bath with reheat, with space heating) and fuel [KW]; ENERGY STAR divides them by energy source (gas, heat pump, solar) [ESF].

### 1.3 Collectors, heritage and the resale market

- Period-bathroom retailers sell roll-top and cast-iron baths in traditional shapes with painted exteriors, and a "traditional" style line beside the modern line [VE] [VP].

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- EU ecodesign and energy-label portal, "Heating, Cooling and Ventilation": Air Conditioners and Comfort Fans, Local Space Heaters, Ventilation Units, Solid Fuel Boilers, Space Heaters, Water Heaters, Air Heating and Cooling Products [EUL]. Water heaters are in scope to 400 kW, with hot-water tanks to 2,000 L, and 60 percent of the 228 million EU units are dedicated water heaters and 40 percent combi heaters [EUW].
- ENERGY STAR residential groups: Water Heaters (gas, heat pump, solar); Heating & Cooling (boilers, furnaces, geothermal heat pumps, heat pumps, room air conditioners, smart thermostats); Lighting & Fans (ceiling fans, ventilating fans); Pools [ESF].
- NSF/ANSI water-treatment standards are divided by technology and claim: 42 aesthetic, 53 health effects, 44 softeners, 55 UV, 58 reverse osmosis, 62 distillation, 177 shower filters, 401 emerging contaminants. NSF states the numbers record the order of development "and are not a ranking or rating system" [NSF].
- Customs: HS 8415 splits air-conditioning machines by form (window, wall or split, self-contained) and by function (reversible heat pump, cooling only, fan-and-humidity without refrigeration) [T84]. HS 3922 splits plastic baths, shower-baths, sinks and washbasins from lavatory seats and covers and from bidets, pans and cisterns [T39]; customs thus divides sanitaryware first by material, a split Appendix A forbids as a level.

### 1.5 Review sites and maker catalogues

- Consumer Reports divides space heaters by mechanism: ceramic and fan-forced convection, oil-filled radiator, infrared radiant, micathermic panel [CS]; divides water heaters into storage tank, tankless and heat pump and ranks them by UEF [CW]. Wirecutter's air purifier test starts from airflow (CADR) [WC].
- Rheem (a maker catalogue) groups residential water heating as tank, indirect and buffer tanks, tankless, heat pump, point-of-use, with UEF stated on each model [RM].
- Choice (Australia) treats the home battery as a pair with solar panels and an inverter and compares capacity, inverter size, depth of discharge and cycle life [CH].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence from navigation, filter order and guides:
1. **The job comes first.** Retail and regulators separate heating, cooling, air cleaning, hot water, bathing and electricity before any other cut [SX] [VP] [AS] [EUL] [ESF]. A buyer who needs a warm room does not compare a boiler with a dehumidifier.
2. **Form or install position second.** Kakaku's air-conditioner facet leads with type (wall, floor, ceiling, window, portable) [KA]; its fan facet leads with style [KF]; Chinese guides lead with wall versus cabinet versus central [SH]; its water-heater guide leads with function type, then location, mounting, fuel and eco type [KW]; bathroom retailers lead with the fixture [VP].
3. **Capacity third, sized to the room or household.** Horsepower against room area [SH]; "go" number of hot-water output (5, 10, 16, 20, 24, 28; 16 to 24 is the family range) and tank litres [KW]; pints per day for dehumidifiers [DH]; maximum floor area for purifiers [KP]; kWh and inverter kW for batteries [CH]; watts per square foot for space heaters [CS].
4. **Efficiency grade fourth**, once form and size are fixed: UEF and first-hour rating for water heaters [CW]; SEER2, HSPF2, EER2 for US heat pumps and central air [EK]; APF energy grades 1 to 3 in China [SH] [ZH]; the multi-stage score in Japan [KA] [KW]; letter classes in the EU [EUW].
5. **Fuel and technology are tags in every source** (electric, gas, kerosene, LPG: [KH] [KW]). Where the technology fixes a different physical machine (heat pump versus resistance tank), buyers treat it as a type [CW]: rule T (C10), section 4b.

So the owner-facing first axis is **function (what the equipment does to air, water or electricity)**, then **device type (form and install position)**. Capacity, efficiency and fuel are specifications that sit on the kind as attributes (C6, C4(b)).

## 3. Candidate structures

Maximum depth for all three: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels below the category, C5(a)). Codes are numeric, `B7.<sub-family>.<leaf>` (review C-9).

### Candidate A (recommended): medium, then job, then device type

- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: what the equipment acts on** (room air, water, electricity). It restates the three clauses of the key and the EU and ENERGY STAR groupings [EUL] [ESF]. A closing fourth sub-family, B7.4 Parts and accessories made for one object, is added by CV-17; it is the only sub-family exempt from this axis.
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis, stated per sub-family (C5(c)):** B7.1 Room air: the job done to the air (heat, cool, move, treat, control). B7.2 Water: the job done to the water (heat, treat, bathe and take away waste). B7.3 Electricity: the job done to the electricity (generate, convert, store, meter). B7.4 Parts and accessories made for one object: the object served (C5(d)).
- **Level 3, kind. Axis stated per leaf (table below).** A kind is the level at which buyers compare quality or makers (C2(a), section 4b).
- **Level 4, named form. Axis: line, model or iconic design** (Kirigamine FL series, Rheem ProTerra, TOTO Washlet, Bramah-pattern closet). Each carries `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2(a)); a non-maker-bound regional or traditional form carries a `region` tag.
- Fuel, efficiency grade, capacity class, installation, setting, market, connectivity, material, era, maker are tags or attributes on the kind or named form (C6, C4(b)).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule (C2(a)):** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers. Beneath it are named forms and attributes. A named form is a specific design below the kind, maker-bound or not.

Evidence by leaf.
- **Air conditioners: stop at the form and install position.** Kakaku's first facet is wall, floor, ceiling, window, portable [KA]; Chinese guides stop at wall, cabinet, central [SH]; Home Depot sells all in one department [HD]. Under C12(a) these are kinds because the body differs. Buyers compare efficiency and capacity inside each form. Inverter versus fixed-speed, refrigerant, auto-clean and cold-region are attributes [KA] [SH]. "Heat pump" for an air-to-air unit is a synonym, not a kind (edge 1).
- **Space heaters: stop at the mechanism.** Consumer Reports gives ceramic/fan, oil-filled radiator, infrared and micathermic as the types and tests each [CS]. Kakaku's headline types (gas fan heater, kerosene fan heater, kerosene stove) are fuel plus mechanism [KH]; the fuel stays a tag. Kinds therefore are fan heater, convector heater, oil-filled radiator, radiant heater, infrared panel heater, and the room heaters whose flue changes the installation (flued stove, flued fire, flueless room heater); micathermic is a named form of the panel heater, not a kind. The plant kinds are boiler, furnace, hydronic and ground-source heat pump and underfloor heating kit (B7-O1). The stove, fire and flueless kinds depend on C10(b) and need a fetched comparison source †.
- **Water heaters: stop at storage, tankless, heat pump, solar, point-of-use (and the cylinder that has no heat source of its own).** Consumer Reports ranks UEF by these types (0.63 to 0.95 storage, 0.82 to 0.97 gas tankless, 3.3 to 4.1 heat pump) [CW]; Rheem's catalogue and ENERGY STAR divide the same way [RM] [ESF]. Gas versus electric inside a type is a tag. This is the case rule T(b) (C10) was written for: technology and physical form coincide and a fetched source ranks the types separately. It is therefore a kind (B-8, CV-24). Solar thermal collectors (B7-O1) sit in the same leaf as the solar system.
- **Air purifier: one kind.** Buyers compare CADR or floor area, filter grade, noise and filter cost [WC] [KP]; tower versus desktop and HEPA grade are attributes. Humidifier: one kind with mechanism (ultrasonic, evaporative, warm mist) as an attribute, because the guide that lists nine types [HS] groups them as one family and C10 makes technology an attribute (B-25). Dehumidifier: split into portable, mini and whole-house, because efficiency thresholds and install differ by class [DH].
- **Water treatment: stop at technology class.** NSF certifies softeners (44), UV (55), reverse osmosis (58), distillation (62), shower filters (177), and filter standards 42, 53, 401 as one adsorption/filtration group [NSF]; this is the C10(b) test (a fetched source tests the types separately). Certification marks and contaminant claims are attributes (`standard_met` with the NSF/ANSI standard named, CV-36; not a tier). Softener salt and cartridges are S3 consumables, one kind each, in the parts sub-family B7.4 (CV-17) †.
- **Sanitaryware: stop at the fixture with its flush or function type.** Retail leads with toilets, basins, baths, showers [VP] [HP]; museums separate water closet and toilet [SM]; bidet seats form a separate Japanese market with trademark names [KT]. Installation (wall-hung, close-coupled, back-to-wall, freestanding) is an attribute (C12); basin shape, tap holes, colour, depth are fields [VP]. Power source of a shower is an attribute (C10). Hot tubs stop at body and heating installation: hard-shell, inflatable and wood-fired, with construction material an attribute (B-26).
- **Home energy: stop at component class, with compatibility and chemistry as fields.** Solar retailers divide modules, inverters, storage, kits [AS] [SS]; guides compare capacity, inverter size, depth of discharge and cycle life on the battery [CH]. A rack and a wall battery are one body offered with different fixings, so form factor is an installation attribute (C12); hybrid and off-grid inverters are one kind with a type attribute.

The choice level is deeper (kinds by form) for heating, cooling, water heating and sanitaryware, where physical form changes the object, and shallower (one kind plus attributes) for purifiers, humidifiers and electricity components, where a single spec ladder carries the comparison.

**Technology, mounting, region and material at a glance.** *Attributes:* fuel; electric or gas; smart or plain; inverter or fixed-speed; mini-split, rack or wall mounting of a battery; hybrid or off-grid; shower power source; humidifier mechanism (B-25); hot-tub construction material (B-26). *Kinds by C10(b), C11 or C12:* heat-pump water heater, flued stove, flued fire, flueless room heater, the three hot-tub bodies, the five air-conditioner forms, the dehumidifier classes, the NSF treatment classes, warm-water toilet seat.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: attribute-ranked, with no tier entries now.** Every sub-family has a measured number, but the schemes are regional and not comparable. Under C4(b) a spec ladder measured on the object is an attribute with its standard named; it is never a tier entry. The attribute is stored as `scheme` plus `value` (and the standard), not as a normalised score (B7-O2: scheme plus value, never normalised across schemes). Each scheme is registered once in the scheme registry as class (i), a spec or certification measured on the object (C4(c)).
- Cooling and heat pumps: SEER2, EER2 and HSPF2 (US); ENERGY STAR sets pass lines (central AC at 15.2 SEER2; heat pumps 7.8 HSPF2 and 15.2 SEER2; cold-climate 8.5 HSPF2 for non-ducted split) [EK], which are the criteria of a `certification` mark; APF with national grades 1 to 3 and a "super level 1" label (China) [SH] [ZH]; Japan's multi-stage score 1 to 5 for the 2027 standard [KA]; EU letter classes [EUW].
- Water heaters: UEF and first-hour rating [CW]; Japan's annual hot-water efficiency and score [KW]; EU classes A+ to F for products, A+++ for packages with solar [EUW].
- Purifiers: CADR and floor area [WC] [KP]. Dehumidifiers: pints per day and efficiency per class [DH]. Batteries: kWh, kW, depth of discharge, cycle life, warranty [CH] [SS].
- Water treatment: NSF/ANSI certification claims; NSF says the standard numbers are not a ranking [NSF], so these are `standard_met` attributes (CV-36), not a tier order. Safety marks (UL, ETL, CSA [CS]; UL9540 [SS]) are `standard_met` too. Legal classes, such as ecodesign minimums and plug-in rules by country, go to the C17 ledger and never define a kind.
- Sanitaryware, hot tubs, heaters of the simple kind: preference and price band. Guarantee years (5 to 25 years or lifetime) is the only quality number retail prints [VP]; it is a warranty attribute.
- **Tier entries (C4(a)):** none stored for B7. A tier would be an entry on a maker, line or named form (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`), for example a reviewer score for a line, and enters on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10; a second source or region only adds weight); none is stored yet, and stage 3 enters one when a page supplies it, for example a Consumer Reports or Wirecutter rating of a line [CS] [CW] [WC] (class (iii)). A retailer's segment label would be a dated `platform_tag` (C4(c)).
- **Price band (C4(d)):** one domain-wide USD scheme (under 50, 50–150, 150–500, 500–1,500, 1,500–5,000, 5,000–15,000, over 15,000), derived from a dated, sourced list price in the retailer's currency converted at the date. A heating plant or home battery spans several bands; the band belongs to the line or named form, not the kind.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Choice: platform, with identity pockets.** Maker is a compatibility fact in energy and ducted HVAC, part of what is sought in heritage and Japanese sanitaryware, and incidental in small fans and portable heaters.

- *Platform:* batteries and inverters must match: Choice warns that components need to be compatible [CH]; the Solar Store sells a battery "fully compatible" with its own inverter series [SS]; Alpha Solar sells by brand partner [AS]. In split air conditioners, the outdoor and indoor units are matched, and the r/heatpumps wiki treats the OEM as the quality fact, tracing a dozen brands to five factories [RH].
- *Identity:* Japanese water heaters, toilet seats and purifiers are bought by maker (Noritz, Rinnai, Paloma, Daikin, Panasonic, Corona; TOTO, INAX/LIXIL, Panasonic, Toshiba) with maker features named in the guide [KW] [KT] [KP]; "Washlet" is TOTO's registered trademark used as the generic word [KT]. UK bathroom retail is heavy with heritage brands (Burlington, Armitage Shanks, Villeroy & Boch, Heritage), and 70+ brands are listed at HeatandPlumb [HP] [VP]. The Science Museum files by maker (Twyfords, Jennings) [SM]. Twyfords and Jennings are makers in the ledger, not named forms of the closet kind.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Follows C7. **Kinds:** canonical domestic form first, then the secondary key stated per leaf: ascending output or capacity (watts, horsepower, litres, pints per day, kWh) where the leaf is a size ladder; ascending complexity for the bathing leaf (fixture, then combined or powered fixtures). **Named forms (C7, CV-21):** non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms, by maker (the ledger's `name` order), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically. Maker ladders (`line_rank`) and spec ladders are generated views, not stored order. **Variants:** by `variant_code` or year. Generated views, such as "Period and heritage", may sort by date; the stored order is the C7 order.
- Kakaku lists the commonest form first: wall-mounted air conditioners are 2,575 of about 3,000 listed units, ahead of floor, ceiling, portable and window [KA]; fans likewise (desk 1573, stationary 1397, handheld 1041) [KF]. This supports "canonical form first", not an order by count.
- Within a kind, size ladders are universal and named by the sources: horsepower 1, 1.5, 2, 3, 5 [SH]; hot-water output 5 to 28 go [KW]; dehumidifier mini, portable 20 to 70, whole-house 70 to 150+ pints per day [DH]; battery 5 to 160 kWh in modules [SS].
- The museum orders closets and radiators by date and maker [SM] [VC]; that order is the generated heritage view, and the stored named-form order stays as C7 states it (non-maker forms first, then maker-bound forms by maker and year).
