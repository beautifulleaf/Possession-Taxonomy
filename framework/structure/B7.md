## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B7 Climate, Water & Energy (domain B, Home).

**Membership test (CATEGORIES_v0_6, key step 30, first branch):** made to heat, cool, ventilate, purify or humidify air; to heat, soften, filter or supply water for bathing and washing, or take away waste; or to generate, store or meter electricity for the home. Named kinds: heaters, fans and ceiling fans, air conditioners, heat pumps, dehumidifiers, air purifiers, thermostats, water heaters, softeners and filters, baths, showers, basins, toilets and bidets (fitted or freestanding), hot tubs and portable saunas, solar panels and inverters, home batteries, small wind turbines, energy meters.

**Notes that bind (S1b):** electric blankets and heated throws are B2. Tables with a built-in heater (kotatsu, korsi) are B1. Wall-box chargers for electric vehicles are G1 under S3. Step 4 sends a building's installed baths, showers, basins, toilets and hot tubs to B7, and fixed plumbing and wiring to C8. A connected version of a household object (smart thermostat) is filed by its task (step 24 note). Branch order in step 30 tries B5, B6, B7, B8, B3, B1 in that order, so a ceiling fan with a light is B7 (the B3 memo records the same). Cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8 whatever they are used in (S3). Pool cleaning equipment, pumps and test kits, and above-ground and inflatable pools, are C2 (step 29). Hot-water bottles, heat packs and hand warmers are H1 (step 18). UPSs, hardware wallets and power banks are D1 or D2 (step 24).

**Appendix A modifiers that apply and so may never be a level:** region and culture, era (antique, vintage, retro), price band and quality tier (this includes efficiency grade), brand and maker, scene and subculture, user (including adapted forms such as accessible baths), setting (indoor, outdoor, bathroom, camping), market (domestic, commercial), installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted, wall-hung), power source and technology (electric, gas, oil, solar, and by extension LED-style technology swaps), connectivity (smart), occasion, motive, origin, how acquired (kit, DIY), material, condition, edition.

**Precedence sentences touching B7:** section 9. Eight of the cases need owner confirmation because the present key is silent (boilers and stoves, taps, generators, jug filters, air-quality monitors, underfloor heating kits, shower trays and enclosures, pool heat pumps).

**Where they agree.** Everyone separates the three jobs somewhere: air (heat, cool, move, clean), water (heat, treat, bathe and dispose), electricity (generate, store, convert). Within air, the next cut is always the physical form or install position of the machine (wall, floor, ceiling, window, portable; radiator, fan heater, stove; desk, tower, ceiling fan). Within bathrooms the first cut is the fixture (bath, basin, toilet, shower).

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- Air conditioner versus heat pump: Kakaku tags an air conditioner "cooling-and-heating" or "cooling-only" [KA]; the customs heading 8415 treats reversible heat pumps as air conditioning machines while a separate subheading holds other heat pumps [T84]; ENERGY STAR lists "Heat Pumps" and "Room Air Conditioners" as separate product groups [ESF]; the EU puts air conditioners with comfort fans, and puts hydronic heat pumps with boilers under "Space Heaters" [EUL].
- Fans: Kakaku files fans apart from heaters and air conditioners [KF]; ENERGY STAR puts ceiling fans and ventilating fans under "Lighting & Fans" [ESF]; the EU calls them "Air Conditioners and Comfort Fans" [EUL].
- Heaters: Screwfix keeps radiators, central heating, fires and stoves, electric heating as separate departments [SX]; Kakaku puts portable fuel heaters together by fuel [KH]; Consumer Reports divides space heaters by mechanism (convection versus radiant) [CS].
- Water heaters: the EU regulates them (and storage tanks to 2,000 L) as one group [EUW]; Japan files them as housing equipment and divides by function (hot water only, bath with reheat, with space heating) and fuel [KW]; ENERGY STAR divides them by energy source (gas, heat pump, solar) [ESF].

### 1.3 Collectors, heritage and the resale market

- Period-bathroom retailers sell roll-top and cast-iron baths in traditional shapes with painted exteriors, and a "traditional" style line beside the modern line [VE] [VP].
- Reclaimed and reproduction cast-iron radiators are sold in three states: antique original, new reproduction, renovated to order, with stoves in the same shop [VC]. ### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- EU ecodesign and energy-label portal, "Heating, Cooling and Ventilation": Air Conditioners and Comfort Fans, Local Space Heaters, Ventilation Units, Solid Fuel Boilers, Space Heaters, Water Heaters, Air Heating and Cooling Products [EUL]. Water heaters are in scope to 400 kW, with hot-water tanks to 2,000 L, and 60 percent of the 228 million EU units are dedicated water heaters and 40 percent combi heaters [EUW].
- ENERGY STAR residential groups: Water Heaters (gas, heat pump, solar); Heating & Cooling (boilers, furnaces, geothermal heat pumps, heat pumps, room air conditioners, smart thermostats); Lighting & Fans (ceiling fans, ventilating fans); Pools [ESF].
- NSF/ANSI water-treatment standards are divided by technology and claim: 42 aesthetic, 53 health effects, 44 softeners, 55 UV, 58 reverse osmosis, 62 distillation, 177 shower filters, 401 emerging contaminants. NSF states the numbers record the order of development "and are not a ranking or rating system" [NSF].
- Customs: HS 8415 splits air-conditioning machines by form (window, wall or split, self-contained) and by function (reversible heat pump, cooling only, fan-and-humidity without refrigeration) [T84]. HS 3922 splits plastic baths, shower-baths, sinks and washbasins from lavatory seats and covers and from bidets, pans and cisterns [T39]; customs thus divides sanitaryware first by material, a split Appendix A forbids as a level.

### 1.5 Review sites and maker catalogues

- Consumer Reports divides space heaters by mechanism: ceramic and fan-forced convection, oil-filled radiator, infrared radiant, micathermic panel [CS]; divides water heaters into storage tank, tankless and heat pump and ranks them by UEF [CW]. ## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence from navigation, filter order and guides:
1. **The job comes first.** Retail and regulators separate heating, cooling, air cleaning, hot water, bathing and electricity before any other cut [SX] [VP] [AS] [EUL] [ESF]. A buyer who needs a warm room does not compare a boiler with a dehumidifier.
2. **Form or install position second.** Kakaku's air-conditioner facet leads with type (wall, floor, ceiling, window, portable) [KA]; its fan facet leads with style [KF]; Chinese guides lead with wall versus cabinet versus central [SH]; its water-heater guide leads with function type, then location, mounting, fuel and eco type [KW]; bathroom retailers lead with the fixture [VP].
3. **Capacity third, sized to the room or household.** Horsepower against room area [SH]; "go" number of hot-water output (5, 10, 16, 20, 24, 28; 16 to 24 is the family range) and tank litres [KW]; pints per day for dehumidifiers [DH]; maximum floor area for purifiers [KP]; kWh and inverter kW for batteries [CH]; watts per square foot for space heaters [CS].
4. **Efficiency grade fourth**, once form and size are fixed: UEF and first-hour rating for water heaters [CW]; SEER2, HSPF2, EER2 for US heat pumps and central air [EK]; APF energy grades 1 to 3 in China [SH] [ZH]; the multi-stage score in Japan [KA] [KW]; letter classes in the EU [EUW].
5. **Fuel and technology are tags in every source** (electric, gas, kerosene, LPG: [KH] [KW]). Where the technology fixes a different physical machine (heat pump versus resistance tank), buyers treat it as a type [CW]: see section 4b.

So the owner-facing first axis is **function (what the equipment does to air, water or electricity)**, then **device type (form and install position)**. Capacity, efficiency and fuel are specifications that sit on the kind.

## 3. Candidate structures

Maximum depth for all three: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels below the category).

### Candidate A (recommended): medium, then job, then device type

- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: what the equipment acts on** (room air, water, electricity). It restates the three clauses of the key and the EU and ENERGY STAR groupings [EUL] [ESF].
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis: the job done to that thing** (heat, cool, move air, treat air, control; heat water, treat water, bathe and dispose; generate, convert, store, meter).
- **Level 3, kind. Axis: device type, meaning physical form and install position at the point where buyers compare** (wall split, window, portable; storage, tankless, heat pump; bath, basin, toilet).
- **Level 4, named form. Axis: line, model or iconic design** (Kirigamine FL series, Rheem ProTerra, TOTO Washlet, Bramah-pattern closet).
- Fuel, efficiency grade, capacity class, installation, setting, market, connectivity, material, era, maker are tags or fields on kind or named form.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. *A reversible wall split.* It is an air conditioner (cooling leaf) and an air-to-air heat pump. Rule: file by the medium delivered at the output and by the buyer's name for it; here "air conditioner", with the heating mode as a tag [KA]. A hydronic heat pump (water out) goes to Heating.
2. *A combi boiler.* It heats radiators and taps. Key says "primary designed function"; a combi boiler is filed in Heating, and the EU rates the hot-water function separately [EUL]. A hot-water cylinder is Water heating.
3. *A Dyson Purifier Hot+Cool.* Purifier, fan and heater in one body; it tops Kakaku's heater and fan rankings [KH] [KF] and is sold in Nojima's purifier list [NJ]. Rule: primary function from the maker's own name for it ("purifier"), tag the others.
4. *A hybrid inverter with built-in battery.* Inverter or battery? Rule: if sold and rated by kWh storage, battery; if rated by kW conversion, inverter.
5. *A towel radiator.* Heater (B7) or towel rack (B2/B1)? Retail files it under radiators [BH] [VP], so B7.

### Candidate B: job-first, flat sub-families (seasonal-appliance model)

- Level 1, sub-family: the job (Space heating; Cooling; Air movement; Air treatment; Water heating; Water treatment; Bathing and sanitary; Generation; Storage; Controls and metering), nine to ten sub-families.
- Level 2, leaf: device type. Level 3, kind: sub-type or fuel. Level 4, named form.
- Mirrors Kakaku and Nojima, where the aircon, heater, fan and purifier categories sit side by side [KA] [KH] [KF] [NJ], and the Bauhaus and Screwfix departments [BH] [SX].
- Example kinds: panel radiator, fan heater, split air conditioner, ceiling fan, air purifier, tankless water heater, softener, toilet, hot tub, solar panel, home battery.
- Breaks: (1) it needs ten sub-families and the level-3 axis is mixed (type in some, fuel in others); (2) thermostats and heat pumps belong to two jobs; (3) the sub-family list grows with every new product (Balkonkraftwerke, bidet seats); (4) nothing groups the three plain-language clauses of the key, so audit disagreements between the key and the tree are harder to see.

### Candidate C: technology-first

- Level 1: heat pump, resistive electric, combustion, solar, passive. Level 2: output. Level 3: device.
- Mirrors the ecodesign debate and the r/heatpumps wiki [RH] [WH].
- Breaks: power source and technology is an Appendix A modifier; a heat pump is an air conditioner, a water heater, a hydronic boiler and a dryer; a fan has no technology branch. Rejected for that reason.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds:** about 110 to 150 (est.). The tree above has 60 attested; the rest are the same pattern: further bath forms are named forms not kinds, further toilet types (squat, pour-flush, urinal, smart toilet), further fans (pedestal, bladeless, window fan), further heaters (patio heater, underfloor heating kit, wall-hung electric fire, gas fire), further water-treatment kinds (water jug filter, tap-mounted filter), electricity kinds (power meter, smart meter display, solar charge kit).
- **Named forms:** models run to thousands in volume (Kakaku lists about 3,000 air conditioners across five types [KA]; BAUHAUS has 5,061 infrared panels, 4,998 of them picture-motif prints [BH]). Recommended capture: lines and iconic designs only, about 200 to 400 (est.), such as the Kirigamine FL, Dyson Hot+Cool, Rheem ProTerra, Sanden SanCO2, Eco Cute, TOTO Washlet, Bramah closet and the roll-top bath. A print motif or a colour is never a named form.
- **Where depth concentrates:** heating (22 or more kinds, many historic), sanitaryware and bathing (about 28 kinds with a long tail of period forms), and home storage and inverters (spec and platform depth rather than kind depth).
- **Broad leaves (many kinds):** Heating, Bathing and sanitary. **Medium:** Cooling, Air movement and ventilation, Air treatment, Water heating, Water treatment, Generation. **Narrow:** Climate control, Conversion, Storage, Metering.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers. Beneath it are named forms and attributes.

Evidence by leaf.
- **Air conditioners: stop at the form and install position.** Kakaku's first facet is wall, floor, ceiling, window, portable [KA]; Chinese guides stop at wall, cabinet, central [SH]; Home Depot sells all in one department [HD]. Buyers compare efficiency and capacity inside each form. Inverter versus fixed-speed, refrigerant, auto-clean and cold-region are attributes [KA] [SH].
- **Space heaters: stop at the mechanism.** Consumer Reports gives ceramic/fan, oil-filled radiator, infrared and micathermic as the types and tests each [CS]. Kakaku's headline types (gas fan heater, kerosene fan heater, kerosene stove) are fuel plus mechanism [KH]; the fuel stays a tag.
- **Water heaters: stop at storage, tankless, heat pump, solar, point-of-use.** Consumer Reports ranks UEF by these types (0.63 to 0.95 storage, 0.82 to 0.97 gas tankless, 3.3 to 4.1 heat pump) [CW]; Rheem's catalogue and ENERGY STAR divide the same way [RM] [ESF]. Gas versus electric inside a type is a tag. This is the one place technology and physical form coincide; see open question 2.
- **Air purifier: one kind.** Buyers compare CADR or floor area, filter grade, noise and filter cost [WC] [KP]; tower versus desktop and HEPA grade are attributes. Humidifier: one kind with mechanism (ultrasonic, evaporative, warm mist) as an attribute, because the guide that lists nine types [HS] groups them as one family; owner may prefer to promote the mechanism to kinds (open question 6). Dehumidifier: split into portable, mini and whole-house, because efficiency thresholds and install differ by class [DH].
- **Water treatment: stop at technology class.** NSF certifies softeners (44), UV (55), reverse osmosis (58), distillation (62), shower filters (177), and filter standards 42, 53, 401 as one adsorption/filtration group [NSF]. Certification marks and contaminant claims are attributes.
- **Sanitaryware: stop at the fixture with its flush or function type.** Retail leads with toilets, basins, baths, showers [VP] [HP]; museums separate water closet and toilet [SM]; bidet seats form a separate Japanese market with trademark names [KT]. Installation (wall-hung, close-coupled, back-to-wall, freestanding) is a tag; basin shape, tap holes, colour, depth are fields [VP].
- **Home energy: stop at component class, with compatibility and chemistry as fields.** Solar retailers divide modules, inverters, storage, kits [AS] [SS]; guides compare capacity, inverter size, depth of discharge and cycle life on the battery [CH].

The choice level is deeper (kinds by form) for heating, cooling, water heating and sanitaryware, where physical form changes the object, and shallower (one kind plus fields) for purifiers, humidifiers and electricity components, where a single spec ladder carries the comparison.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: spec-ranked.** Every sub-family has a measured number, but the schemes are regional and not comparable.
- Cooling and heat pumps: SEER2, EER2 and HSPF2 (US); ENERGY STAR sets pass lines (central AC at 15.2 SEER2; heat pumps 7.8 HSPF2 and 15.2 SEER2; cold-climate 8.5 HSPF2 for non-ducted split) [EK]; APF with national grades 1 to 3 and a "super level 1" tier (China) [SH] [ZH]; Japan's multi-stage score 1 to 5 for the 2027 standard [KA]; EU letter classes [EUW].
- Water heaters: UEF and first-hour rating [CW]; Japan's annual hot-water efficiency and score [KW]; EU classes A+ to F for products, A+++ for packages with solar [EUW].
- Purifiers: CADR and floor area [WC] [KP]. Dehumidifiers: pints per day and efficiency per class [DH]. Batteries: kWh, kW, depth of discharge, cycle life, warranty [CH] [SS].
- Water treatment: NSF/ANSI certification claims; NSF says the standard numbers are not a ranking [NSF], so these are certificate attributes, not a tier order.
- Sanitaryware, hot tubs, heaters of the simple kind: preference and price band. Guarantee years (5 to 25 years or lifetime) is the only quality number retail prints [VP].

**How it appears:** as modifier fields on the kind or named form (efficiency scheme and value, capacity class, certification marks), never as a level. Because the schemes differ by region and are not directly comparable even across generations (SEER versus SEER2), store scheme name with the value rather than a normalised score.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Choice: platform, with identity pockets.** Maker is a compatibility fact in energy and ducted HVAC, part of what is sought in heritage and Japanese sanitaryware, and incidental in small fans and portable heaters.

- *Platform:* batteries and inverters must match: Choice warns that components need to be compatible [CH]; the Solar Store sells a battery "fully compatible" with its own inverter series [SS]; Alpha Solar sells by brand partner [AS]. In split air conditioners, the outdoor and indoor units are matched, and the r/heatpumps wiki treats the OEM as the quality fact, tracing a dozen brands to five factories [RH].
- *Identity:* Japanese water heaters, toilet seats and purifiers are bought by maker (Noritz, Rinnai, Paloma, Daikin, Panasonic, Corona; TOTO, INAX/LIXIL, Panasonic, Toshiba) with maker features named in the guide [KW] [KT] [KP]; "Washlet" is TOTO's registered trademark used as the generic word [KT]. UK bathroom retail is heavy with heritage brands (Burlington, Armitage Shanks, Villeroy & Boch, Heritage), and 70+ brands are listed at HeatandPlumb [HP] [VP]. The Science Museum files by maker (Twyfords, Jennings) [SM].
- *Incidental:* Kakaku's heater and fan lists show a long tail of small makers [KH] [KF]; Consumer Reports names mass-market makers (Honeywell, Lasko, Vornado) as examples [CS].

**Maker ledger recommended: yes.** Rows with maker, known-for kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country and era. Priority rows: Daikin, Mitsubishi Electric, Panasonic, Midea, Gree, Rheem, Rinnai, Noritz, Stiebel Eltron, Sanden, TOTO, LIXIL/INAX, Villeroy & Boch, Twyfords, Armitage Shanks, Burlington, Dyson, Trina, Deye, Anker Solix, Zendure, RUiXU, Vestwoods. OEM-to-brand lineage (RH) should be a field "made by" on the ledger because it is the fact the community cares about.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** Three sub-families (Room air, Water, Electricity), twelve leaves (Heating; Cooling; Air movement and ventilation; Air treatment; Climate control; Water heating; Water treatment; Bathing and sanitary; Generation; Conversion; Storage; Metering), device-type kinds at the physical-form level, named forms for lines and iconic designs. **Maximum depth four levels below the category** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Efficiency grade, capacity class, fuel, installation, certification, maker, era, scene and market are tags or fields. A maker ledger with a "made by" OEM field is recommended. Tier type: spec-ranked, regional schemes stored as scheme plus value. Maker centrality: platform, with identity pockets.

**Evidence limits.** Thin: saunas, small wind turbines, energy meters (no fetched retailer lists them as a leaf); the DIY solar forum was seen in search excerpts only; Home Depot returned no sub-tree; French and Spanish retail and Indian water-heater markets were not fetched.

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. Approve three sub-families and twelve leaves (Candidate A), or choose the flatter Candidate B.
2. Heat-pump water heater: the key's Appendix A lists "power source and technology" as a modifier, yet buyers and regulators rank heat-pump heaters against tank and tankless as a type (UEF 3.3 to 4.1 against 0.63 to 0.97) [CW]. Recommended: keep it a kind, because the form (compressor unit with tank or split outdoor unit) differs; fuel (gas, electric, kerosene) stays a tag.
3. Reversible air conditioner versus heat pump: confirm the rule "file by the medium delivered at the output": air-to-air is an air conditioner, water-out is a heating kind.
4. Objects the key does not name, with proposed homes in B7: boilers and furnaces (Heating); wood stoves and fires (Heating; fixed chimney work is C8); underfloor heating kits (Heating); hot-water cylinders and solar thermal collectors (Water heating); jug and tap filters (B5 or B7: recommend jug and bottle to B5, plumbed to B7); portable generators and power stations (Electricity, or D2: recommend a rule by use, mains backup to B7); air-quality monitors (Air treatment or C6: recommend C6 weather and science instruments); taps (C8); shower trays, enclosures, bath panels (C8 or B7); pool heat pumps (C2).
5. Whether efficiency schemes should be normalised for comparison or kept as scheme plus value (recommended: scheme plus value).
6. Whether humidifier mechanism (ultrasonic, evaporative, warm mist) should be promoted from attribute to kind.
7. Named-form policy: lines and iconic designs only; no print motifs, colours or individual model numbers unless attested as a distinct line.
8. Hot tub construction material as kind (acrylic, inflatable, wooden and so on): confirm that this is "material is the form".
9. Generated views: "Period and heritage" and "Off-grid and DIY" wanted?

**Framework conflicts found, for the next revision (not applied).**
- Step 30's B7 branch does not name boilers, furnaces, stoves, fireplaces, underfloor heating, taps, shower trays, generators or jug filters (decision 4).
- S3 "Never parts" sends building products to their own category, which puts plumbed radiator valves in C8, while thermostats are B7; a smart radiator head is a thermostat. A sentence is needed.
- Appendix A treats technology as a modifier but the heat-pump water heater is ranked as a type (decision 2).
