## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B4 Decor, in domain B Home.

**Membership test (placement key step 30, last branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** made to **decorate** a room, surface or garden and not a picture, sculpture or lamp: vases, decorative bowls and objects, picture frames, wall and dresser mirrors, candles and home fragrance (including incense), decorative lighting, seasonal, festival and party decorations, Christmas crackers, party poppers, sparklers and fireworks, artificial and dried flowers, garden ornaments, statues and gnomes, sundials, wind chimes, flags, windsocks and signs (including illuminated and shop signs), decorative replica weapons. Branch B4 is tried last, after B5, B6, B7, B8, B3, B1 and B2.

- Step 14 note: decorations for any festival, religious or secular (Christmas trees and ornaments; Easter, Diwali, Halloween and Hina-matsuri displays) are B4 unless made for use in worship (menorah, diya, Advent wreath, home nativity set are F4). ### 1.2 Enthusiast and maker sources
- **Fragrance wheels.** The Edwards wheel (S5) has four families (Floral, Amber, Woody, Fresh) and 14 subfamilies. CandleScience (S4), a candle-maker supplier, uses ten families (Spicy, Gourmand, Amber, Woody, Aromatic, Floral, Citrus, Marine/Ozonic, Green, Fruity) and defines "kindred" and "complementary" neighbours on the wheel. The market report taxonomy (S23) uses eight fragrance types; Nippon Kodo's store (S22) uses ten for incense (Aromatic, Fruity, Citrus, Fresh, Floral, Herbal, Spicy, Perfumy, Sweet, Woody). **The sources agree that scent is a facet that cuts across product forms and disagree on how many families there are (4, 8 or 10).**
- **Incense makers.** Nippon Kodo (S21) describes forms by burn behaviour: stick (mild, private room), cone (intensive, short, larger room), coil (about two hours). Its store (S22) navigates by form (short, regular, long, bamboo stick; cone; coil; shaped; powder; smudge sticks and resins; incense matches), with burners, holders and plates as separate kinds, plus fragrance family and season facets.
- **Wind chimes (S34).** The enthusiast quality dimension is tuning (major or minor pentatonic, Gregorian, 432 Hz, Japanese yo and in scales); untuned decorative chimes ring random intervals; the guide tells buyers to check tubes with a tuner.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications
- **HS heading 9505 (S16, S36)** collects festive, carnival and entertainment articles, split at subheading level by occasion (950510 Christmas articles, with glass and non-glass lines; 950590 other). The WCO notes list garlands, lanterns, tree decorations, artificial Christmas trees, nativity scenes, crackers, stockings, streamers and confetti, and **exclude** candles (34.06), textile flags and bunting (63.07), lighting strings and electric garlands (94.05), natural trees, tree stands, statues for places of worship, and utilitarian articles that merely carry a festive motif (tableware, linen).
- **US customs ruling N125135 (S17)** puts tealights in 3406 candles, a ceramic Santa figurine in 6913 ornamental ceramics, and a candle holder in 9405 non-electrical lighting fittings; a festive motif does not make a utilitarian article a festive article. This is direct evidence that candle, candle holder and ornament are three different things to the trade.
- **Result for B4:** the trade has no heading for "decor". B4 is a retailer concept, bundled by the framework's function test. The trade splits it by material and function (candles, festive articles, lighting, textile flags, ceramic ornaments).
- **Market research (S23, S24)** segments home fragrance by product type (candles, air fresheners, reed and electric diffusers, essential oils, sachets, wax melts, room sprays, incense, potpourri) and separately by fragrance type, ingredient, packaging and channel; candles by product (container, pillar, other), wax (paraffin, soy, blended, beeswax, palm) and category (mass versus premium). Form, wax and tier are three separate axes.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence from navigation order:
- IKEA, John Lewis, Westwing, Pepperfry, Rakuten and 1stDibs all open with **object form** (S1, S2, S6, S7, S8, S26). Filters follow in the order colour, material, style, price (S7, S35).
- Target puts **Type** first and then Brand, Price, then Fragrance Type and Scent Type, Color and Holiday (S3). Scent comes after the form choice.
- Wayfair's vase facets begin with size, colour and material, then Type and Shape (S35): buyers narrow by fit to the space first and by type second.
- Occasion comes first only where the retailer sells only occasions (Home Depot seasonal S11, Matsuya S25, Old World Christmas S10).
- Collectors start with **maker and period** (S26 creator and period facets; S27 series, era and condition).
- For flags the first choice is **whose flag** (S33) and for signs it is **material and type** (S20).

Reading: owners and buyers choose first by **what the object is** (form), then by fit (size, colour, material), then by scent or style; maker and era are the first choices only for collectors and for luxury fragrance (S28).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the category as B4 and divide below it. The framework allows sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels).

### Candidate A (recommended): ornamental family, then generic type, then kind, then named form
- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: the family of ornamental object, meaning what the thing is as a display object** (vessel, mirror or frame, candle, scent, temporary display, artificial flora, garden ornament, flag or sign, decorative light). This is the axis the generalist retailers use for their top tabs (S1, S2, S6, S7).
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis: stated for each sub-family in the table below** (C5(c)). A sub-family with about 12 kinds or fewer skips the leaf and stores `leaf = null` (C5(b)): B4.6, B4.7 and B4.9.
- **Level 3, kind. Axis: the form a buyer compares at** (see 4b), such as pillar candle, standing mirror, ginger jar.
- **Level 4, named form: a specific design below a kind** (C2). Each carries `maker_bound: yes | no`. Maker-bound: Diptyque Baies, Voluspa Baltic Amber. Not maker-bound: meiping, trumeau, hina doll set, koinobori (all with a `region` or `culture` tag and a generated "by tradition" view).
- **Codes** are numbers only, `B4.<sub-family>.<leaf>` (review C-9): B4.3.1 is Candles.
- **Never levels:** scent family, wax, material, size, colour, style, occasion, region or tradition, period, maker, price band. A tier is a tier entry on a maker, line or named form (C4(a)). Condition is a modifier in Appendix A but is not stored (C16).

### Candidate B: sensory or display effect first (sight, flame, scent, sound, movement, message)
- **Level 1 axis: the effect the object has on the room** (static form, flame and light, scent, sound, wind movement, message).
- Sub-families: Static form (vases, bowls, objects, mirrors); Flame (candles); Scent (diffusers, incense); Sound (wind chimes); Wind movement (spinners, windsocks, weathervanes, flags); Message (signs, flags).
- Gains: it explains why incense, candles and chimes feel like their own products, and why a buyer of one does not compare it to a vase.
- **Breaks:** a scented candle has flame, light and scent; a mirror has reflection and light; a flag has wind movement and message; a wind spinner has movement and ornament. No retailer uses this axis (S1, S2, S6, S7), so a person looking for a vase would not look under Static form. Edge objects: scented candle, flag, wind spinner, snow globe.

### Candidate C: occasion or placement first (seasonal, wall, table, floor, garden)
- **Level 1 axis: when or where it is displayed.**
- This is what Home Depot, Matsuya, Old World Christmas and HS 9505 do for seasonal goods (S10, S11, S16, S25), and what Pepperfry's room-wise guide does for everything else (S6).

In practice a lantern, string light, wreath, garland or bauble serves several occasions; a mirror is wall or floor; a candle holder is table or wall. Customs shows the same failure: a festive motif does not change what the article is (S17, S36).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Overall: preference, with a price band** (C4(d): the domain-wide USD bands, derived from a dated, sourced list price). Pepperfry's budget filters (S6) are a retailer scheme and are not used as the band.

**Exceptions:**
- **Home fragrance and candles: preference with a maker price band.** One luxury-candle retailer's blog places houses in tiers: Voluspa owns the 30 to 50 USD tier, Diptyque and Trudon set the luxury template, Jo Malone, Byredo and Le Labo are premium benchmarks (S28). The market report splits candles into mass and premium categories (S24). Review sites compare single product lines at cost per ounce and wax type (S29). The claim rests on one ranking source (S28), so it is not called a consensus ranking, and no tier is stored as category-wide. S28 may enter the data as tier entries on makers and lines (scheme: that retailer's tiers; source URL; date), which C4(e) allows with one fetched source; a second source from another region is still needed †. The measurable specs (fragrance-oil load, burn hours, wax) exist but are not a standard ladder.
- **Wind chimes:** tuning is checkable with a tuner but labels such as Gregorian or 432 Hz are used loosely (S34); store as an attribute (scale, tuned or untuned), not a tier.
- **Flags:** manufacturers sell material grades (standard nylon, polyester, reinforced, commercial quality, S33); store as material and grade attributes, not tiers (C4(b)).
- **Collectible ornaments and signs:** condition codes and era (S27, S20) are condition, which Appendix A treats as a modifier. Under C16 a condition grade of one particular ornament is not stored. The code scheme (MIB, SDB, DB, NT, NB) may be recorded as a scheme the secondary market uses, never as values for items.

**How tier appears:** tier entries (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) on a maker, a line or a named form, never on a kind (C4(a)); a `price_band` on the maker row. Platform segment labels, if ever used, are a dated `platform_tag` (C4(c)). Never a level.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity** in fragrance (the house is what is sought: S28), in art glass and studio ceramics (1stDibs creators facet S26, SSENSE designers S9) and in series ornaments (Hallmark, S27). **Incidental** for flags (S33), garden ornaments (S14), signs, and generic vases, mirrors and frames. No platform dependence is expected. Candle holders and warmers may fit by standard size, not by maker †; if a size standard is recorded it goes in the compatibility ledger (C13), never as a level.

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger** on the shared core schema (C1), scoped to fragrance houses and candle makers (a 60-plus brand shelf in one specialist, S28), art-glass and studio-ceramic makers (S26), ornament makers (S27) and incense houses (S22).
- *Core columns:* `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class`; `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `known_for_kinds`; `scene_tags` (empty unless a sourced vocabulary exists: C8); `price_band`; `sources` (at least one fetched URL). No extension columns are needed.
- *Separate tables:* **lines and collections** (a candle line such as Baies or Baltic Amber that spans candle, diffuser and spray; an ornament series such as Hallmark Keepsake), with `built_by_maker_id` where a licensee makes it; **tier entries** (section 5); **named forms**, which carry the maker and `maker_bound: yes`; **variants** (C3), used only if the owner wants ornament codes (QX numbers, S27) or candle scent and colour versions.
- *Kinds do not carry a maker.* Known-for named forms are derived from the named-form ledger, not stored in a column (C1).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Following C7: **kinds go canonical form first, then the secondary key that the sub-family states; named forms go by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested; variants by `variant_code` or year.**
- Candles: container candle first (the form named first in the market segmentation, S24), then ascending size: tealight, votive, taper, pillar.
- Incense: stick first, then by ascending burn length: cone, coil (S21); the burner follows the incense.
- Mirrors: wall mirror first, then floor (leaner), standing (cheval), table (dresser): from the wall to the floor to the table (S19, S30).
- Other leaves: canonical form first (the first kind in the tree above), then alphabetical until a secondary key is sourced.
- Vase silhouettes carry lineage (bronze-derived gu, zun, hu versus the plum-blossom meiping) and period (S18), but no dates are attested in the sources, so the named forms go alphabetically; lineage stays a note until years are fetched †.
