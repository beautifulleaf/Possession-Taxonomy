## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B4 Decor, in domain B Home.

**Membership test (placement key step 30, last branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md):** made to **decorate** a room, surface or garden and not a picture, sculpture or lamp: vases, decorative bowls and objects, picture frames, wall and dresser mirrors, candles and home fragrance (including incense), decorative lighting, seasonal, festival and party decorations, Christmas crackers, party poppers, sparklers and fireworks, artificial and dried flowers, preserved and artificial plants, garden ornaments, statues and gnomes, sundials, wind chimes, flags, windsocks and signs (including illuminated and shop signs), decorative replica weapons. Branch B4 is tried last, after B5, B6, B7, B8, B3, B1 and B2. The branch notes of the key add: an essential oil sold for a diffuser or fragrance burner is B4 unless sold to treat a condition (H2); wax warmers and electric diffusers are B4; a flagpole made for a flag follows the flag (S3, B4); regulatory and safety signs are C8; candle-making supplies are C4.

### 1.2 Enthusiast and maker sources
- **Fragrance wheels.** The Edwards wheel (S5) has four families (Floral, Amber, Woody, Fresh) and 14 subfamilies. CandleScience (S4), a candle-maker supplier, uses ten families (Spicy, Gourmand, Amber, Woody, Aromatic, Floral, Citrus, Marine/Ozonic, Green, Fruity) and defines "kindred" and "complementary" neighbours on the wheel. The market report taxonomy (S23) uses eight fragrance types; Nippon Kodo's store (S22) uses ten for incense (Aromatic, Fruity, Citrus, Fresh, Floral, Herbal, Spicy, Perfumy, Sweet, Woody). **The sources agree that scent is a facet that cuts across product forms and disagree on how many families there are (4, 8 or 10).**
- **Incense makers.** Nippon Kodo (S21) describes forms by burn behaviour: stick (mild, private room), cone (intensive, short, larger room), coil (about two hours). Its store (S22) navigates by form (short, regular, long, bamboo stick; cone; coil; shaped; powder; smudge sticks and resins; incense matches), with burners, holders and plates as separate kinds, plus fragrance family and season facets.
- **Wind chimes (S34).** The enthusiast quality dimension is tuning (major or minor pentatonic, Gregorian, 432 Hz, Japanese yo and in scales); untuned decorative chimes ring random intervals; the guide tells buyers to check tubes with a tuner.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications
- **HS heading 9505 (S16, S36)** collects festive, carnival and entertainment articles, split at subheading level by occasion (950510 Christmas articles, with glass and non-glass lines; 950590 other). The WCO notes list garlands, lanterns, tree decorations, artificial Christmas trees, nativity scenes, crackers, stockings, streamers and confetti, and **exclude** candles (34.06), textile flags and bunting (63.07), lighting strings and electric garlands (94.05), natural trees, tree stands, statues for places of worship, and utilitarian articles that merely carry a festive motif (tableware, linen).
- **US customs ruling N125135 (S17)** puts tealights in 3406 candles, a ceramic Santa figurine in 6913 ornamental ceramics, and a candle holder in 9405 non-electrical lighting fittings; a festive motif does not make a utilitarian article a festive article. This is direct evidence that candle, candle holder and ornament are three different things to the trade.
- **Result for B4:** the trade has no heading for "decor". B4 is a retailer concept, bundled by the framework's function test. The trade splits it by material and function (candles, festive articles, lighting, textile flags, ceramic ornaments).
- **Market research (S23, S24)** segments home fragrance by product type (candles, air fresheners, reed and electric diffusers, essential oils, sachets, wax melts, room sprays, incense, potpourri) and separately by fragrance type, ingredient, packaging and channel; candles by product (container, pillar, other), wax (paraffin, soy, blended, beeswax, palm) and category (mass versus premium). Form, wax and tier are three separate axes.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence from navigation order:
- IKEA, John Lewis, Westwing, Pepperfry, Rakuten and 1stDibs all open with **object form** (S1, S2, S6, S7, S8, S26). Filters follow in the order colour, material, style, price (S7, S35).
- Target puts **Type** first and then Brand, Price, then Fragrance Type and Scent Type, Color and Holiday (S3). Scent comes after the form choice.
- Wayfair's vase facets begin with size, colour and material, then Type and Shape (S35): buyers narrow by fit to the space first and by type second.
- Occasion comes first only where the retailer sells only occasions (Home Depot seasonal S11, Matsuya S25, Old World Christmas S10).
- Collectors start with **maker and period** (S26 creator and period facets; S27 series, era and condition).
- For flags the first choice is **whose flag** (S33) and for signs it is **material and type** (S20).

Reading: owners and buyers choose first by **what the object is** (form), then by fit (size, colour, material), then by scent or style; maker and era are the first choices only for collectors and for luxury fragrance (S28).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the category as B4 and divide below it. The framework allows sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels).

### Candidate A (recommended; adopted, B-5): ornamental family, then generic type, then kind, then named form
- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: the family of ornamental object, meaning what the thing is as a display object** (vessel, mirror or frame, candle, scent, temporary display, artificial flora, garden ornament, flag or sign, decorative light): nine ornamental families (B-5), closed by the parts sub-family B4.10 (C5(d), CV-17), the only sub-family exempt from this axis. This is the axis the generalist retailers use for their top tabs (S1, S2, S6, S7).
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis: stated for each sub-family in the table below** (C5(c)). A sub-family with about 12 kinds or fewer may skip the leaf and store `leaf = null` (C5(b), CV-16): B4.6, B4.7, B4.9 and B4.10.
- **Level 3, kind. Axis: the form a buyer compares at** (see 4b), such as pillar candle, standing mirror, ginger jar.
- **Level 4, named form: a specific design below a kind** (C2). Each carries `maker_bound: yes | no`. Maker-bound: Diptyque Baies, Voluspa Baltic Amber. Not maker-bound: meiping, trumeau, hina doll set, koinobori (all with a `region` or `culture` tag and a generated "by tradition" view). Large sets of named forms (flag designs, and ornament series or candle lines if they grow large) live in registers linked to their kind, uncapped (CV-45, C2(g)); named forms have no cap (CV-7).
- **Codes** are numbers only, `B4.<sub-family>.<leaf>` (review C-9): B4.3.1 is Candles.
- **Never levels:** scent family, wax, material, size, colour, style, occasion, region or tradition, period, maker, price band. A tier is a tier entry on a maker, line or named form (C4(a)). Condition is a modifier in Appendix A but is not stored (C16).

### Candidate B: sensory or display effect first (sight, flame, scent, sound, movement, message)
- **Level 1 axis: the effect the object has on the room** (static form, flame and light, scent, sound, wind movement, message).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Overall: preference, with a price band** (C4(d), CV-13, CV-14: the domain-wide USD bands, derived from a dated, sourced list price; a band is never a tier). Pepperfry's budget filters (S6) are a retailer scheme and are not used as the band.

**Exceptions:**
- **Home fragrance and candles: preference with a maker price band, and a tier scheme from the one fetched ranking.** One luxury-candle retailer's blog places houses in tiers: Voluspa owns the 30 to 50 USD tier, Diptyque and Trudon set the luxury template, Jo Malone, Byredo and Le Labo are premium benchmarks (S28). The market report splits candles into mass and premium categories (S24). Review sites compare single product lines at cost per ounce and wax type (S29). The claim rests on one ranking source (S28), which is an editorial or community class scheme (C4(c) class iii), so it is not called a consensus ranking and no tier is stored as category-wide. S28 enters the data on that one fetched page as a tier scheme (scheme: that retailer's tiers; source URL; date) with tier entries on makers and lines (CV-10, C4(e)). No second source or region is sought: a second source would only add weight and feed the computed `disputed` flag (B-34). The measurable specs (fragrance-oil load, burn hours, wax) exist but are not a standard ladder; they are attributes.
- **Wind chimes:** tuning is checkable with a tuner but labels such as Gregorian or 432 Hz are used loosely (S34); store as an attribute (scale, tuned or untuned), not a tier.
- **Flags:** manufacturers sell material grades (standard nylon, polyester, reinforced, commercial quality, S33); store as material and grade attributes, not tiers (C4(b)).
- **Collectible ornaments and signs:** condition codes and era (S27, S20) are condition, which Appendix A treats as a modifier. Under C16 a condition grade of one particular ornament is never stored. The code scheme (MIB, SDB, DB, NT, NB) is recorded once in the grading-scale registry (CV-34: `scale_type` condition grade, steps in order, `kinds_served`), never as values for items. A maker's own order of its ornament series (first in series, club exclusive) is `line_rank` in the lines table, not a tier on a kind.

**How tier appears:** tier entries (`subject_id`, `subject_type`, `scheme_id`, `value`, `rank`, `direction`, `scope`, `source_url`, `date`, `disputed`) on a maker, a line or a named form, never on a kind (C4(a)); a `price_band` on the maker row. Platform segment labels, if ever used, are a dated `platform_tag` (C4(c) class vi). Never a level.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity** in fragrance (the house is what is sought: S28), in art glass and studio ceramics (1stDibs creators facet S26, SSENSE designers S9) and in series ornaments (Hallmark, S27). **Incidental** for flags (S33), garden ornaments (S14), signs, and generic vases, mirrors and frames. No platform dependence is expected. Candle holders and warmers may fit by standard size, not by maker †; if a size standard is recorded it goes in the compatibility ledger (C13, CV-29), never as a level.

**Decided: yes, a maker ledger** on the domain-wide ledger (C1, CV-1, CV-2), with no cap and no scope limit (CV-5, CV-7). Identity makers (fragrance houses and candle makers, a 60-plus brand shelf in one specialist, S28; art-glass and studio-ceramic makers, S26; ornament makers, S27; incense houses, S22) are harvested first as an ordering rule only; flag makers (S33), garden-ornament makers (S14), sign makers, neon fabricators, mirror and frame makers and retail own-labels (IKEA, John Lewis: class "retail own-label") are ledger rows too.

No extension columns are needed.
- *Separate tables:* **lines and collections** (a candle line such as Baies or Baltic Amber that spans candle, diffuser and spray; an ornament series such as Hallmark Keepsake), with `line_rank` and `built_by_maker_id` where a licensee makes it; **tier entries** (section 5); **named forms**, which carry the maker and `maker_bound: yes`; **variants** (C3, CV-8), filled for ornament codes (QX numbers, S27) and for candle colour and scent versions where a community compares them; the **maker-relations table** (including `composed by` for the perfumer of a scent, CV-3, and OEM or licence relations); the standing **school table** for traditions of makers, linked by `school_id`; and the **alias and homonym table** (C9, CV-23).

- *Lines against named forms (C2(d)):* a candle line spanning candle, diffuser and spray is a lines row; Baies as a scent inside the container candle kind is a maker-bound named form. Fragrances keep named forms (CV-46).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Following C7 as amended by CV-20 and CV-21: **kinds go canonical form first, then the secondary key that the sub-family states; named forms go non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year and then alphabetically, then maker-bound named forms by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically; variants by `variant_code` or year.** Maker ladders (`line_rank`), newest-first and similar orders are generated views, not stored order.
- Candles: container candle first (the form named first in the market segmentation, S24), then ascending size: tealight, votive, taper, pillar.
- Incense: stick first, then by ascending burn length: cone, coil (S21); the burner follows the incense, then the smudge bundle.
- Mirrors: wall mirror first, then floor (leaner), standing (cheval), table (dresser): from the wall to the floor to the table (S19, S30).
- Other leaves: canonical form first (the first kind in the tree above), then alphabetical until a secondary key is sourced.
- Vase silhouettes carry lineage (bronze-derived gu, zun, hu versus the plum-blossom meiping) and period (S18), but no dates are attested in the sources, so the non-maker named forms go alphabetically; lineage stays a note until years are fetched †.
- Flag designs sit in the flag register, ordered alphabetically until a secondary key is sourced.
