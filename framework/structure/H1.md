## 0. Category

**Code and name:** H1 Personal Care & Beauty.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 18): *made to be used on the body for grooming, hygiene, cosmetics, fragrance, or bodily comfort and soothing: grooming tools and powered tools (hair dryers, shavers, electric toothbrushes), grooming mirrors, bathroom and body scales, cosmetics attached to the body, wigs and hairpieces, hair ties and clips, sponges and pumice, massagers, hot-water bottles, heat packs and hand warmers, pacifiers and teethers, baby baths, changing mats, potties and toilet-training seats.* Category list line: "grooming, hygiene, cosmetic, fragrance and comfort tools, devices and products, and baby care."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name H1:**
- Step 18 notes: towels of every kind are B2; massagers made to treat a condition, such as TENS units, are H2.
- Step 2 note (H2): massagers for comfort are H1; infant formula is J3; breast pumps and incontinence products are H2.
- Step 7 note (F6): sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for a use are H1; cleaning sponges and scourers are B6.
- Step 17 note (A-domain): wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties, plain clips and bands are H1; hair ornaments are A3 (so jewelled combs, hairpins and clips are A3).
- Step 1: baby-food meals are prepared food (Culinary); the baby items in step 18 are the only baby goods H1 names.
- C4 memo: a brush made for painting or glazing is C4; a brush made for applying makeup is H1.
- Step 30 (B4 branch) and B4 memo: wall and dresser mirrors, candles and home fragrance including incense are B4; fragrance made to be used on the body and grooming mirrors are H1.
- Step 27 (B9): nicotine, cannabis and CBD products are B9 even when sold as a cosmetic or supplement.
- S3 (parts follow the whole: a razor blade or toothbrush head follows its razor or toothbrush, and both are H1; cleaning products are B6), S5 (a collected perfume bottle is a perfume bottle), S6 (attestation).
- Appendix A: user (baby, child, adult, gender, ability), price band and tier, brand and maker, region and culture, scene, power source, material, market (domestic, professional) and installation are modifiers.
- **BRIEF.md conflict to note:** the owner decision "baby and child goods have no category of their own; each goes to its functional home" and the placement key's naming of pacifiers, baby baths, changing mats and potties in H1 are consistent only if baby goods are placed by function and "baby" is a user tag. ### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Fragrance.** The Perfume Society lists eight families: Fresh, Floral, Floriental, Ambrée, Gourmand, Chypre, Woody, Fougère [S23]. A fragrance guide credits the Société Française des Parfumeurs (1984) with seven groups (citrus, floral, fougère, chypre, woody, oriental, aromatic) plus 18 listed families or "facets" such as aldehydic, leather, marine, powdery, green [S24]. Fragrantica's groups page uses chypre, floral, leather, woody with compound sub-groups such as floral aldehyde, floral aquatic, woody chypre [S30].
- **Concentration.** Buying guides and a retailer give five or six labelled classes, with ranges that disagree (section 5) [S9][S25][S29][S26].
- **Shaving.** An enthusiast guide gives six types: double-edge (DE), single-edge (SE), straight, shavette, cartridge, electric, separated by "how many edges meet your skin, how the blade is held, and how much skill and upkeep each asks" [S33]. Wikipedia's razor article lists straight, single-edge safety, double-edge safety, cartridge, disposable, electric [S31].
- **Hair tools.** Wikipedia's hair-iron article gives three kinds (curling, straightening, crimping), with barrel diameter, plate material and handle type as attributes [S34].

- **FDA cosmetic categories** (US listing codes): 01 baby products (shampoos; lotions, oils, powders, creams; wipes), 02 bath preparations, 03 eye makeup (brow pencils, eyeliners, shadows, removers, false eyelashes, mascaras, lash and brow adhesives and serums), 04 children's eye makeup, 05 fragrance (colognes and toilet waters; perfumes; powders; other), 06 hair preparations non-colouring, 07 hair colouring, 08 makeup not eye (blushers, face powders, foundations, body paints, lipsticks and glosses), then a skin-care group (moisturizing, night, paste masks, skin fresheners; body and hand), 15 suntan, 16 tattoo, 17 other; a leave-on or rinse-off split sits below [S20]. ### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:** (1) The first split is the part of the body or the purpose served: skin, make-up, hair, fragrance, body, shaving, mouth (Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA, EU recital 7, HS 3303–3307). (2) Product type comes next (Produktart; cleansers, treatments, moisturizers; shampoo, conditioner). (3) Skin type, hair type, colour and scent note are facets, not levels. (4) Fragrance is divided in three independent ways: by family (scent), by concentration class (strength) and by user or segment (women's, men's, unisex, niche).
**Split:**
- **Tools.** Douglas files a flat iron in Hair and tools in Gesicht ("Beauty Tools & Zubehör") and brushes in Make-up (Pinsel) [S2][S3][S4]; Olive Young keeps "Skincare Tools" under Skincare [S14]; @cosme gathers beauty goods and appliances as one category beside the product categories [S19]; the standards cover only preparations [S20][S21][S22].
- **Sun care.** Top-level in @cosme (801) and FDA (15); a leaf inside Skincare at Olive Young; "Sonne & Schutz" inside Gesicht and a body-care item at Douglas [S4][S5][S14][S15][S20].
- **Fragrance by user.** Douglas splits by Damen, Herren, Unisex [S1]; Fragrances of the World keeps gender as a field of the entry [S28]; @cosme has none [S18].
- **Dermocosmetics.** Douglas puts pharmacy-brand skincare in Gesundheit & Wellness with filters Wirkungsbereich and Beschwerden and also flags products "Dermakosmetik" inside Gesicht [S4][S7]; Boots keeps CeraVe beside mass labels [S11]. It is a channel, not a product type.
- **Soap.** The EU lists toilet soaps as cosmetics [S21]; HS excludes soap from chapter 33 (heading 3401, note 1) [S22].
- **Baby.** FDA has its own baby category [S20]; no retail page fetched has a baby department in beauty, so baby goods are found by function.
- **Concentration percentages** differ by source (section 5).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Area or purpose first, then product type, then personal attributes.**
- *Navigation order.* Every store and standard fetched opens with the area or purpose: Douglas (Parfum, Make-up, Gesicht, Körper, Haare, Gesundheit) [S1]; Boots (Makeup, Skincare) [S11]; @cosme (skincare, UV, makeup, base makeup, fragrance, hair, body and oral) [S15]; FDA (baby, bath, eye makeup, fragrance, hair, makeup, skin care, suntan) [S20]; HS (perfumes, beauty and skin, hair, oral, shaving and deodorants) [S22]; EU recital 7 [S21].
- *Filter order.* At Douglas the filters run Preis, Produktart (product type), Marke (maker), Für Wen (user), then the area's own attribute: Duftnote for fragrance, Farbe, Deckkraft and Finish for make-up, Haartyp and Haarfarbe for hair, Hauttyp for face and body [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5]. Olive Young puts Skin Concern, Skin Type, SPF, Formulation and Format after the type tabs [S14].
- *What buyers decide first inside an area.* Skin care: skin type or concern, then the routine step (Boots' "10-step K-Beauty regime"; Douglas's guide "Gesichtspflege: Diese Reihenfolge ist die richtige" [S8][S11]). Make-up: face area, then colour and finish [S2][S12]. Hair: hair type and colour, then job (wash, treat, style) [S3]. Fragrance: scent family or note, then concentration and house; the Perfume Society advises learning the families first, "just as you would if you were learning about wine" [S23]. Shaving: the razor mechanism (how many edges, how held, how much upkeep) [S33].
- *Maker first in two places.* Fragrance (Douglas has a separate niche view and Fragrantica is organised by house) [S10][S30] and Korean skin care (Olive Young's brand facet lists counts by label) [S14].
- *Cross-cutting views.* User (men's, women's, unisex, baby) [S1][S6], Korean origin [S8][S12], dermocosmetic channel [S4][S7], and "natural" make-up [S2] each run across every area. They are tags and views.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the fixed level names of C5: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (at most four levels below H1).

### Candidate A (recommended): sub-family by area or purpose served; leaf by the job or method inside it

- **Level 1, sub-family:** the part of the body or purpose the object serves. Ten sub-families (the tenth is the closing parts sub-family that C5(d) and CV-17 give every category that receives S3 parts):
  1. Skin care (leave-on and rinse-off care for any area of skin, face or body; sun care and skin repellents are one leaf, H-6, K-138)
  2. Makeup
  3. Hair care and styling
  4. Fragrance (fine fragrance and its other formats; the owner's deepest area, kept in named forms and ledgers, section 6)
  5. Shaving and hair removal
  6. Body, bath and hygiene (includes intimate and menstrual hygiene, toileting and nappy changing equipment and nappies, baby wipes with the user tag baby)
  7. Oral care
  8. Grooming implements, mirrors and scales (the C14(c) multi-use sub-family: implements, cases and chairs that serve no single area)
  9. Comfort, warmth and massage (includes pacifiers and teethers)
  10. Parts and accessories made for one <object> (cartridges, blades, foils, heads, guards and attachments; leaf axis = object served)
- **Level 2, leaf: one axis per sub-family, stated in the sub-family's definition** (C5c). In the five area sub-families that have leaves (Skin care, Makeup, Hair care and styling, Shaving and hair removal, Body, bath and hygiene) the axis has two ordered parts: preparations are divided by the job (or, in Makeup, the area coloured; in Shaving, the method), and one final leaf holds the tools and devices (in Body, bath and hygiene, the equipment) of that area, manual or powered, with power source as an attribute (H-7, C10). Fragrance, Oral care, Grooming implements and Comfort have no leaf level (12 kinds or fewer, C5b: leaf = null); Parts is the only sub-family exempt from the level-1 axis (C5d) and its leaf axis is the object served.

- **Level 3, kind:** the generic type where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). **Level 4, named form:** a line, model, iconic product or scent (Gillette Mach3, ghd gold styler, Chanel No. 5, Creed Aventus), with `maker_bound` yes or no (C2). A fragrance's strength label or edition is a C3 variant below the named form, never a level.
- **Attributes, never levels:** skin type, hair type, colour, shade, finish, scent family and notes (fragrance strength is a C3 variant, not an attribute: H-4), SPF and PA grade (`standard_met`, CV-36), volume (and price per 100 ml, derived), formulation (cream, gel, fluid), format (stick, pad, pump, cushion, mist, wipe, roll-on, balm), user (women, men, unisex, couples, baby, child, teen, adult, bariatric, adapted; R-20), body area (face, body, hands, eye, lip), material, power source, `disposable`, region, segment-like labels (platform tags only).
- **Placement lines that fix the leaf axes (H-4, H-6, H-7, CV-17, K-33, K-129, K-132, K-138, K-190):**
  - Skin care holds every skin-care product for any area. Body lotion, hand cream, foot cream and eye cream are the kinds cream or lotion with `area` as the attribute. Body sunscreen and face sunscreen are one kind. Massage oil and body oil are one kind in Moisturising, cotton pads a kind in Skin tools and devices (K-33), and skin repellent a kind in Sun care and skin protection (K-138).
  - A product that leaves visible colour, or that is applied to prepare or fix makeup (primer, setting spray), is Makeup; a product with no colour is Skin care, whatever it claims. Lash and brow serums are Makeup > Brows and lashes (FDA 03 [S20]). Kumkum, sindoor and other powder or paint applied to the skin are H1 (K-129): the kind skin paint or powder in Makeup > Body paint and skin powders, kumkum and sindoor as non-maker named forms with culture = South Asia (C2c, R-19); a stick-on bindi is A3.
  - Eyelash curlers and makeup brushes are "Makeup tools and devices". Hair clippers, beard trimmers and body groomers are "Electric shaving and trimming". Tweezers are "Depilation and epilation". Nail clippers, files, manicure sets, toiletry bags and make-up bags (K-132) and barber chairs (K-190) are sub-family 8; massage chairs are sub-family 9 (K-190). Nail polish and artificial nails are Makeup > Nails. Hot brushes, hair dryers, irons, brushes, combs and plain ties and clips are "Hair tools". Blades, cartridges, foils, heads, guards and attachments made for one razor, shaver, clipper, toothbrush or dryer are sub-family 10 (S3, CV-17); the shaving brush is not made for one object and stays in Blade shaving.
  - Fragrance (H-4): one kind per format; the scent (house plus name) is a maker-bound named form; a flanker is its own named form linked to the original by `flanker_of`; the printed strength label is a C3 variant with `variant_type` = concentration.
  - Format (stick, pad, cushion, mist, wipe, roll-on, balm) is an attribute, with Olive Young's vocabulary [S14]. The one exception is masks, where the product compared is the mask type (sheet, sleeping; Douglas Tuchmaske [S8]). A deodorant wipe is a deodorant with format = wipe.

### Candidate B: split preparations from implements first

- **Level 1:** the nature of the object: Preparations (cosmetics and toiletries), Implements and devices (tools, appliances, mirrors, scales), Hairpieces. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** product or tool type.
- *Support:* the standards cover only preparations (FDA, EU, HS 33) [S20][S21][S22]; @cosme keeps beauty goods and appliances as one category [S19]; the regulatory difference (ingredient listing and safety assessment) is real.
- *Where it breaks:* (1) a flat iron sits beside hair spray in the Douglas Hair department [S3] but is far from it here; (2) an electric toothbrush and its paste are one use; (3) a sunscreen stick against a facial roller both belong to a skin routine; (4) a set that holds a brush and a palette; (5) a razor and shaving cream in one wet-shaving kit [S33]. Retail evidence in four of the five regions is area-first, so this candidate adds a cut no buyer navigates by. It remains a usable generated view ("durable" against "used-up").

### Candidate C: function or routine step across the whole body

- **Level 1:** cleanse, treat and care, protect, colour, scent, remove hair, comfort. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** type.
- *Support:* routine order in skin care (Boots' "10-step" regime; Douglas's "right order" guide [S8][S11]); the EU definition lists cleaning, perfuming, changing appearance, protecting, keeping in good condition, correcting odours [S21].
- *Where it breaks:* (1) shampoo and face wash both cleanse but live in different shops and buyer decisions; (2) a tinted sunscreen both protects and colours; (3) a lip balm treats, protects and may colour; (4) fragrance and deodorant both address smell. It cannot place tools (a hair dryer cleans nothing). No fetched source navigates this way except for skin care.

**Choice:** Candidate A. It matches the navigation of Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA and HS, and it carries tools by the area they serve, as Douglas does for hair and brushes.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: preference (price band), with measured attributes and non-standard label classes. No consensus-ranked tier was found for any sub-family.**

- **Measured spec ladders (attributes with a named standard, C4b):** SPF (Olive Young's SPF facet reads SPF 30+ and SPF 50+ [S14]) and PA grade (printed "SPF50+ PA++++" in a Douglas title [S8]). † The test standard for each (ISO 24444 for SPF and ISO 24442 for UVA by reviewer knowledge; the Japanese PA grade standard) was not in a fetched page and must be sourced before data entry.
- **Legal and regulatory class goes in the C17 ledger, not on the tree and not in attributes.** Cosmetic, quasi-drug and OTC-drug status of a sunscreen, anti-dandruff or anti-blemish product differs by jurisdiction (US sunscreens are OTC drugs, Japan has a quasi-drug class, the EU treats sunscreens as cosmetics: reviewer knowledge) †. Each row needs a fetched source. Customs headings (HS 33) are evidence only and are not stored [S22].
- **Label classes with no common standard (concentration variants, not tiers):** fragrance strength is a C3 variant with `variant_type` = concentration (H-4). Four fetched sources give four different ranges:

- **Segment labels are platform tags, not tiers (C4c):** Douglas's "Nischendüfte" view [S10] and "Dermakosmetik" flag [S4][S7]; Boots's "premium beauty" [S11]. Store as dated `platform_tag` on makers, lines or named forms.
- **Fragrance families:** three systems, none ranked: Edwards (four to five families; 1983 or 1992 depending on the article) [S26][S27][S28], the SFP (seven groups, 1984) [S24], the Perfume Society (eight) [S23]. Decided (H-5): the one controlled list for H1 and B4 home fragrance is the Michael Edwards Fragrance Wheel [S27]; `scent_family` takes its families and sub-families (Floral, Amber, Woody, Aromatic Fougère, Fresh, with the 2008 additions Woods and Fruity [S27]). The SFP and Perfume Society families are kept as source labels in the C9 alias table, mapped to the nearest Edwards family where a fetched page shows the mapping, never as a second list.
- **Price band (C4d):** one USD scheme, computed from the dated USD list price of the reference size; price per 100 ml is a derived attribute and volume is an attribute (C6). The price spread is large, shown here in the retailer's own currency as evidence: a Douglas Chloé eau de parfum at 30 ml, 36.99 EUR online price (1,233 EUR per litre), against Kilian The Liquors Angels' Share parfum at 50 ml, 195 EUR (3,900 EUR per litre) [S1]. The conversion to USD bands needs a dated rate. Where a scent is sold in several strengths, each concentration variant carries its own dated list price and the named form takes the band of its cheapest sourced variant (the plan rule of C4(d) applied by analogy; default, section 12).
- **Modifier route:** `tier` entries are written on makers, lines or named forms only, with scheme, value, source and date (C4a). Nothing is written on a kind. A maker's own range level, if one is found, is a tier entry with `scheme` = the maker's name; a reviewer's grade enters on one fetched source, and a second source or region only adds weight (C4(e), CV-12). None is in the data yet.
- **Razors, hair tools, comfort goods:** no ranking source fetched. Professional against consumer is the Appendix A market modifier.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity, with platform pockets.**
- *Identity (fragrance):* the house and the name are what is sought; Fragrance databases are keyed by brand, perfumer and date [S28]; Fragrantica lists houses [S30]; Douglas runs a separate niche view and brand pages [S1][S10]. The same scent is sold under several strength labels [S9][S29]. Fragrance is the owner's emphasis area, so the house, the perfumer, the bottle designer and the scent are all recorded (below).
- *Identity (skin care, makeup):* Olive Young's brand facet is the main way into Korean skin care [S14]; Douglas product titles lead with maker and line (for example "Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun") [S8].
- *Platform (shaving):* cartridge systems are tied to their handles (Gillette Mach3 and Fusion, including battery-powered variants) and the Schick Injector uses its own narrow blades [S32]. Whether double-edge blades are a shared standard needs its own source †. Toothbrush-head, shaver-foil and clipper-guard platforms were not fetched †.
- *Incidental:* body wash, toilet soap bar, hot-water bottle, massager, pacifier.

**Maker ledger: yes (C1).** One maker row per company, with the C1 core columns and `maker_class` from the C1 list. H1 adds no maker column of its own (X1). In particular:
- **`segment` is dropped** (H1-7): it repeated `maker_class`, C4c platform tags and C8 scenes. Its values map as follows. Designer or fashion house: `maker_class` = costume or fashion brand, or licensee or diffusion where a fetched source says so (Jean Paul Gaultier, Max Mara [S1]; Hugo Boss, Burberry, Calvin Klein [S30] are named as fashion houses selling fragrance; whether each label is made under licence is not stated in the fetched pages †). Niche: a dated `platform_tag` (Douglas Nischendüfte [S10]), and a C8 scene tag "niche perfumery" once a definition page is fetched †. Gulf or Arabian and natural or clean: held-back scene tags (section 7). Mass retail: nothing (the price band covers it). Dermocosmetic: a dated `platform_tag` (Douglas Dermakosmetik [S4][S7]). Professional salon: the Appendix A market modifier on lines.
- `parent_id` for corporate group (Fragrances of the World records the corporate group [S28]).
- Retail own-label rows: Douglas Collection (brand page named in navigation [S1]), Boots No7 [S12], with `maker_class` = retail own-label.
- **Lines against named forms (C1, C2(d), CV-46, R-18, R-9):** a maker's line spanning several kinds is a lines row with `maker_id` and the kinds it spans (Olaplex No. 4 and No. 6 across shampoo and leave-in); a line may group scents inside one kind when the maker sells them as one collection (Kilian The Liquors [S1]; R-9); a product or line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun and Round Lab Birch Juice sunscreen, each a sunscreen). Fragrances keep named forms in every case (C2(e)). Whether used-up toiletry lines (shampoo, toothpaste, deodorant) are the "everyday consumable" lines of C2(e) is not settled by CV-46; this memo applies R-18's reading (section 12, applied by default).
- *Fragrance depth: ledgers and named forms (H-4, CV-2, CV-3, CV-7, R-24; owner emphasis).* None of the following has a cap; each enters on one fetched page showing it made or sold.
  - **Houses** are maker rows with `maker_class` from the C1 list: house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; costume or fashion brand; licensee or diffusion; retail own-label; artisan or atelier (small-batch, attar and oud makers). `parent_id` records the corporate group [S28]; `licensor_id` and the "licensed by" relation record fashion-house licences (whether each label is licensed is still †, section 10).
  - **Perfumers (noses)** are maker rows with `maker_class` = perfumer or composer; the "composed by" relation of the maker-relations table links a perfumer to a scent, with `scope` and `years`. `perfumer_id` is therefore not a named-form column.
  - **Bottle designers** are maker rows with `maker_class` = designer; the "designed by" relation (R-24) links one to a named form. `bottle_designer` is therefore not a named-form column.
  - **Schools** (traditions such as the Grasse tradition or Gulf attar and oud houses †) go in the standing school table (C1); makers link by `school_id`.
  - **Scents** are maker-bound named forms of the kind fine fragrance (or of another format kind) with the C2 named-form columns: `maker_id`, `first_year`, `region`, `scene_tags`, `sources`, plus the controlled `scent_family` (H-5). Fragrances of the World keeps brand, corporate group, perfumer, date, country, bottle designer and family for each entry [S28] and is the first seeding catalogue.
  - **Flankers** are their own maker-bound named forms; `flanker_of` (an H1 fragrance extension column pointing at the original named form) records the link. The same scent in several strengths is one named form with concentration variants, never a set of flankers.
  - **Variants (C3):** `variant_type` = concentration (label as printed), edition, limited edition, vintage (reformulation), retro or re-issue, collaboration, version, regional model; each with `year` and `sources`. Volume and bottle size are attributes.
  - **Collections** that group scents are lines rows.
  - **Seeding order (owner: use existing catalogues first):** a source census of perfume databases, perfumer registers and house catalogues precedes harvest; Fragrances of the World [S28] and Fragrantica [S30] are the two fetched so far; the rest are found by the census (section 10, †).
- *Compatibility ledger (C13):* cartridge systems and single-edge injector blades, with `owner` = maker [S32]. Toothbrush heads, shaver foils and heads, and clipper guards are also maker platforms, and the double-edge blade gets a `standard_ref` only if a source shows a shared standard; all three need fetched pages first †. They are Parts kinds (sub-family 10); the `platform_type` values they need (razor cartridge system, toothbrush-head system, shaver-foil system) are not in the C13 list and are added only when a fetched page shows them (provisional).
- Legal classes (cosmetic, quasi-drug or OTC-drug status) and declared conformity are never maker columns; they go to the C17 ledger or to the named form (section 5).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the axis the sub-family states** (C7).
- Skin care: routine order (cleanse, tone, treat, moisturise, protect), the order in which buyers are taught to apply [S8][S11].
- Makeup: face-area order as the stores print it: face makeup, eye makeup, lips, nails [S12]; Douglas's Teint, Augen, Augenbrauen, Lippen, Nägel, Pinsel [S2].
- Fragrance: kinds by format, fine fragrance first; concentration variants strongest to lightest as Douglas orders them ("from the highest to the lightest") [S9], a generated view (C7 stores variants by `variant_code` or year); a flanker follows its original in the generated view.

Reason: retail and guide orders already follow these keys, and no tier order exists to sort by (section 5).

## 9. Boundaries

Each precedence sentence below is decided (the key step or the decision id is given). Legal class always goes to the C17 ledger and never moves an object.

**H1 / H2 Health, Medical & Mobility.** Closest objects: acne cleanser and medicated shampoo, sunscreen, dermocosmetic cream, TENS and comfort massagers, contact lenses and lens solution, incontinence and period products, condoms, supplements sold for skin, hair or nails, wigs and breast forms. The EU test is purpose: a product placed on the body "exclusively or mainly" to clean, perfume, change appearance, protect, keep in good condition or correct odours is a cosmetic [S21].
*Precedence (K-31, step 2 of the key): a product is H2 when its labelled main purpose is to treat, relieve or prevent a named condition, or when it contains an active ingredient sold only as a medicine in every sourced jurisdiction. Cosmetic purposes (clean, perfume, change appearance, protect, keep in good condition, correct body odour) are H1, sunscreens and anti-dandruff and anti-blemish products included. Legal status goes to C17 and does not move the object; a pharmacy channel or the label "dermocosmetic" does not move it [S4][S7]. Medicines are H2 by labelled use class, prescription or not (H-2, H-8).* A heat wrap with a stated pain claim follows the same sentence. Contact lens solution follows the lens (S3, K-11) to H2, though HS 33.07 note 4 lists it with toilet preparations [S22]. Contact lenses of every kind are H2, and coloured non-corrective lenses are H2 with `corrective: no` (K-35), not H1 and not A3. **Supplements sold for skin, hair or nails, including collagen drinks, are H2 (step 2, K-36).** Condoms, dental dams, incontinence products, nursing pads, nipple cream, teething gel, delay spray and stoma articles are H2; breast forms and packers are H2; wigs are H1; period underwear is A1; pelvic-floor trainers, vacuum devices, ED rings and dilators are H2 when the maker states a medical purpose, otherwise H3 (K-33, H-14). Lubricants, massage oil, anal douches, intimate washes, menstrual products, nappies, baby wipes and cotton pads are H1 (K-33).

**H1 / H3 Adult Objects.** Closest objects: powered massagers, wand-type massagers, personal lubricants, anal douches, massage oil, intimate oils.
*Precedence (K-32, step 2): a powered massager is H3 when its maker names sexual use; H2 when made to treat a condition (TENS, EMS units sold for pain); H1 for comfort, percussion and neck massagers included. The test rests on what the maker says on the object or its packaging; a specialist retailer's listing is not a fact on the object and is not used. Step 9 is tried before step 18, so an object made for sexual stimulation is H3 and not H1. Personal lubricant, massage oil and anal douche are H1 (K-33); an oil sold as an intimate oil is H3 (K-165).*

**H1 / B4 Decor and F4.** Closest objects: room spray, incense (agarbatti sits in HS 33.07 [S22]), scented candles, essential oils, lit makeup mirror, dresser mirror.
*Precedence: fragrance made to be worn on the body is H1; a product made to scent a room (spray, plug-in, diffuser), incense and candles are B4 (B4-O3); an essential oil sold for a diffuser or fragrance burner is B4 unless sold to treat a condition (H2), and wax warmers and electric diffusers are B4 (K-183); incense and incense burners made for a rite are F4. A grooming or lit makeup mirror is H1; a wall or dresser mirror is B4 (B4 memo). The fragrance-family list is shared with B4 (H-5).*

**H1 / A1, A3, A4 (worn and carried).** Closest objects: wigs, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties and plain clips, jewelled combs and hairpins, bindis, toiletry bags, period underwear.
*Precedence: wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties and plain clips and bands are H1 (step 17 note); a decorative or jewelled hair comb, pin or clip is A3; a stick-on bindi is A3, while kumkum and other powder or paint applied to the skin is H1 (K-129); a case made for the small items of one task follows the task, so a toiletry bag and a make-up bag are H1 and not A4 (K-132, in sub-family 8); period underwear is a garment and A1.*

**H1 / B1, B2, B6, B7 (furniture, cleaning, linen, bathroom).** Closest objects: bar soap, body sponge and loofah, towel, bath, bath salts, barber chair, massage chair, examination table.
*Precedence: soap, bath and shower preparations, bath salts and sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for the body are H1 (the EU lists toilet soaps and bath preparations as cosmetics [S21]); laundry, dish and cleaning soaps and cleaning sponges are B6; towels of every kind are B2; bathtubs and sanitaryware are B7. Barber chairs and massage chairs are H1 (K-190); hospital beds, examination tables and dentist chairs are H2.*

**H1 / B10 Animal Equipment.** Closest objects: pet shampoo and grooming brushes. HS 33.07 note 4 lists "animal toilet preparations" with human cosmetics [S22].
*Precedence: grooming goods made for an animal are B10; the same goods made for a person are H1, whatever the trade heading.*

**H1 / B8 Security and pest control.** Closest objects: insect repellent, repellent sprays and coils.
*Precedence (K-138): repellent applied to the skin is H1; sprays, coils and traps for rooms are B8.*

**H1 / B9 Smoking & Vaping.** Closest objects: CBD balms and lotions, cannabis-infused skin care.
*Precedence (K-165): any cannabinoid consumer product, balm and lotion included, is B9; skin care without a cannabinoid is H1; intimate oils are H3.*

**Makeup / Skin care (hybrids, H1-6).** Closest objects: BB or CC cream, tinted moisturiser, tinted sunscreen, tinted lip balm, primer, setting spray, lash and brow serum.
*Precedence: a product that leaves visible colour, or that is applied to prepare or fix makeup (primer, setting spray), is Makeup; a product with no colour is Skin care, whatever it claims. Lash and brow serums are Makeup > Brows and lashes (FDA 03 [S20]). Within H1 this decides the leaf, not the category.*

**Sets and kits.** *S3a (K-1): a set of objects of one kind is filed as that kind. A mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed. A kit sold, standardised and compared as one object (the key names the manicure set) is a kind: the manicure set is a kind in sub-family 8, and the wet-shaving starter kit a kind in Blade shaving, provisional until attested.*

**H1 / B9, H3, B5 (edge pairs).** *Nicotine, cannabis and CBD products are B9 even when sold as cosmetics or supplements (B9 memo); intimate-hygiene washes are H1 (EU "external intimate hygiene" [S21]) and objects made for sexual stimulation are H3; baby bottles and feeding gear are B5, breast pumps are H2, and pacifiers, teethers, baby baths, changing mats and potties are H1 (the key).*

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure (decided in S2U_H1, on the owner's delegation).** H1 → sub-family by the area or purpose served (ten: Skin care; Makeup; Hair care and styling; Fragrance; Shaving and hair removal; Body, bath and hygiene; Oral care; Grooming implements, mirrors and scales; Comfort, warmth and massage; and the closing Parts and accessories made for one <object>) → leaf by job or method, per sub-family, with one final "tools and devices" (or preparations or equipment) leaf in the five area sub-families that have leaves; Fragrance, Oral care, Grooming and Comfort have no leaf level (12 kinds or fewer, C5b), and the Parts leaf axis is the object served → kind at the retail product-type level (in Fragrance, the format) → named form for lines, models, iconic products and scents, maker-bound or not → C3 variants (fragrance strength and edition) and ledgers off the tree. **Maximum depth four levels below the category; most leaves need three** (leaf, kind, optional named form), and four sub-families need two. Fragrance is kept deep in ledgers and named forms: houses, perfumers, bottle designers and schools in the maker and school tables; every scent and flanker a named form; strength, edition and reformulation variants; no cap anywhere.
