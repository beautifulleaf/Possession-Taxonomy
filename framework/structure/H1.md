## 0. Category

**Code and name:** H1 Personal Care & Beauty.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 18): *made to be used on the body for grooming, hygiene, cosmetics, fragrance, or bodily comfort and soothing: grooming tools and powered tools (hair dryers, shavers, electric toothbrushes), grooming mirrors, bathroom and body scales, cosmetics attached to the body, wigs and hairpieces, hair ties and clips, sponges and pumice, massagers, hot-water bottles, heat packs and hand warmers, pacifiers and teethers, baby baths, changing mats, potties and toilet-training seats.* Category list line: "grooming, hygiene, cosmetic, fragrance and comfort tools, devices and products, and baby care."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name H1:**
- Step 18 notes: towels of every kind are B2; massagers made to treat a condition, such as TENS units, are H2.
- Step 2 note (H2): massagers for comfort are H1; infant formula is J3; breast pumps and incontinence products are H2.
- Step 7 note (F6): sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for a use are H1; cleaning sponges and scourers are B6.
- Step 17 note (A-domain): wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties, plain clips and bands are H1; hair ornaments are A3 (so jewelled combs, hairpins and clips are A3).
- Step 1: baby-food meals are prepared food (Culinary); the baby items in step 18 are the only baby goods H1 names.

- Step 30 (B4 branch) and B4 memo: wall and dresser mirrors, candles and home fragrance including incense are B4; fragrance made to be used on the body and grooming mirrors are H1.
- Step 27 (B9): nicotine, cannabis and CBD products are B9 even when sold as a cosmetic or supplement.
- S3 (parts follow the whole: a razor blade or toothbrush head follows its razor or toothbrush, and both are H1; cleaning products are B6), S5 (a collected perfume bottle is a perfume bottle), S6 (attestation).
- Appendix A: user (baby, child, adult, gender, ability), price band and tier, brand and maker, region and culture, scene, power source, material, market (domestic, professional) and installation are modifiers.
- **BRIEF.md conflict to note:** the owner decision "baby and child goods have no category of their own; each goes to its functional home" and the placement key's naming of pacifiers, baby baths, changing mats and potties in H1 are consistent only if baby goods are placed by function and "baby" is a user tag. ### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Fragrance.** The Perfume Society lists eight families: Fresh, Floral, Floriental, Ambrée, Gourmand, Chypre, Woody, Fougère [S23]. A fragrance guide credits the Société Française des Parfumeurs (1984) with seven groups (citrus, floral, fougère, chypre, woody, oriental, aromatic) plus 18 listed families or "facets" such as aldehydic, leather, marine, powdery, green [S24]. Fragrantica's groups page uses chypre, floral, leather, woody with compound sub-groups such as floral aldehyde, floral aquatic, woody chypre [S30].
- **Concentration.** Buying guides and a retailer give five or six labelled classes, with ranges that disagree (section 5) [S9][S25][S29][S26].
- **Shaving.** An enthusiast guide gives six types: double-edge (DE), single-edge (SE), straight, shavette, cartridge, electric, separated by "how many edges meet your skin, how the blade is held, and how much skill and upkeep each asks" [S33]. Wikipedia's razor article lists straight, single-edge safety, double-edge safety, cartridge, disposable, electric [S31].
- **Hair tools.** Wikipedia's hair-iron article gives three kinds (curling, straightening, crimping), with barrel diameter, plate material and handle type as attributes [S34].

- **FDA cosmetic categories** (US listing codes): 01 baby products (shampoos; lotions, oils, powders, creams; wipes), 02 bath preparations, 03 eye makeup (brow pencils, eyeliners, shadows, removers, false eyelashes, mascaras, lash and brow adhesives and serums), 04 children's eye makeup, 05 fragrance (colognes and toilet waters; perfumes; powders; other), 06 hair preparations non-colouring, 07 hair colouring, 08 makeup not eye (blushers, face powders, foundations, body paints, lipsticks and glosses), then a skin-care group (moisturizing, night, paste masks, skin fresheners; body and hand), 15 suntan, 16 tattoo, 17 other; a leave-on or rinse-off split sits below [S20]. ### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:** (1) The first split is the part of the body or the purpose served: skin, make-up, hair, fragrance, body, shaving, mouth (Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA, EU recital 7, HS 3303–3307). (2) Product type comes next (Produktart; cleansers, treatments, moisturizers; shampoo, conditioner). (3) Skin type, hair type, colour and scent note are facets, not levels. (4) Fragrance is divided in three independent ways: by family (scent), by concentration class (strength) and by user or segment (women's, men's, unisex, niche).
**Split:**
- **Tools.** Douglas files a flat iron in Hair and tools in Gesicht ("Beauty Tools & Zubehör") and brushes in Make-up (Pinsel) [S2][S3][S4]; Olive Young keeps "Skincare Tools" under Skincare [S14]; @cosme gathers beauty goods and appliances as one category beside the product categories [S19]; the standards cover only preparations [S20][S21][S22].
- **Sun care.** Top-level in @cosme (801) and FDA (15); a leaf inside Skincare at Olive Young; "Sonne & Schutz" inside Gesicht and a body-care item at Douglas [S4][S5][S14][S15][S20].
- **Fragrance by user.** Douglas splits by Damen, Herren, Unisex [S1]; Fragrances of the World keeps gender as a field of the entry [S28]; @cosme has none [S18].
- **Dermocosmetics.** Douglas puts pharmacy-brand skincare in Gesundheit & Wellness with filters Wirkungsbereich and Beschwerden and also flags products "Dermakosmetik" inside Gesicht [S4][S7]; Boots keeps CeraVe beside mass labels [S11]. It is a channel, not a product type.
- **Soap.** The EU lists toilet soaps as cosmetics [S21]; HS excludes soap from chapter 33 (heading 3401, note 1) [S22].
- **Baby.** FDA has its own baby category [S20]; no retail page fetched has a baby department in beauty, so baby goods are found by function.
- **Concentration percentages** differ by source (section 5).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Area or purpose first, then product type, then personal attributes.**
- *Navigation order.* Every store and standard fetched opens with the area or purpose: Douglas (Parfum, Make-up, Gesicht, Körper, Haare, Gesundheit) [S1]; Boots (Makeup, Skincare) [S11]; @cosme (skincare, UV, makeup, base makeup, fragrance, hair, body and oral) [S15]; FDA (baby, bath, eye makeup, fragrance, hair, makeup, skin care, suntan) [S20]; HS (perfumes, beauty and skin, hair, oral, shaving and deodorants) [S22]; EU recital 7 [S21].
- *Filter order.* At Douglas the filters run Preis, Produktart (product type), Marke (maker), Für Wen (user), then the area's own attribute: Duftnote for fragrance, Farbe, Deckkraft and Finish for make-up, Haartyp and Haarfarbe for hair, Hauttyp for face and body [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5]. Olive Young puts Skin Concern, Skin Type, SPF, Formulation and Format after the type tabs [S14].
- *What buyers decide first inside an area.* Skin care: skin type or concern, then the routine step (Boots' "10-step K-Beauty regime"; Douglas's guide "Gesichtspflege: Diese Reihenfolge ist die richtige" [S8][S11]). Make-up: face area, then colour and finish [S2][S12]. Hair: hair type and colour, then job (wash, treat, style) [S3]. Fragrance: scent family or note, then concentration and house; the Perfume Society advises learning the families first, "just as you would if you were learning about wine" [S23]. Shaving: the razor mechanism (how many edges, how held, how much upkeep) [S33].
- *Maker first in two places.* Fragrance (Douglas has a separate niche view and Fragrantica is organised by house) [S10][S30] and Korean skin care (Olive Young's brand facet lists counts by label) [S14].
- *Cross-cutting views.* User (men's, women's, unisex, baby) [S1][S6], Korean origin [S8][S12], dermocosmetic channel [S4][S7], and "natural" make-up [S2] each run across every area. They are tags and views.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the fixed level names of C5: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (at most four levels below H1).

### Candidate A (recommended): sub-family by area or purpose served; leaf by the job or method inside it

- **Level 1, sub-family:** the part of the body or purpose the object serves. Nine sub-families:
  1. Skin care (leave-on and rinse-off care for any area of skin, face or body)
  2. Makeup
  3. Hair care and styling
  4. Fragrance
  5. Shaving and hair removal
  6. Body, bath and hygiene (includes toileting and nappy changing equipment, baby wipes with the user tag baby)
  7. Oral care
  8. Grooming implements, mirrors and scales (the C14(c) multi-use sub-family: implements that serve no single area)
  9. Comfort, warmth and massage (includes pacifiers and teethers)
- **Level 2, leaf: one axis per sub-family, stated in the sub-family's definition** (C5c). In the six area sub-families the axis has two ordered parts: preparations are divided by the job (or, in Makeup, the area coloured; in Shaving, the method), and one final leaf holds the tools and devices of that area, manual or powered, with power source as an attribute (H1-3, C10).

- **Level 3, kind:** the generic type where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). **Level 4, named form:** a line, model or iconic product (Gillette Mach3, ghd gold styler, Chanel No. 5, Creed Aventus), with `maker_bound` yes or no (C2).
- **Attributes, never levels:** skin type, hair type, colour, shade, finish, scent family and notes, SPF and PA grade, volume (and price per 100 ml, derived), formulation (cream, gel, fluid), format (stick, pad, pump, cushion, mist, wipe, roll-on, balm), user (women, men, unisex, couples, baby, child, bariatric, adapted), body area (face, body, hands, eye, lip), material, power source, `disposable`, region, segment-like labels (platform tags only).
- **Placement lines that fix the leaf axes (H1-3, H1-4, H1-6):**
  - Skin care holds every skin-care product for any area. Body lotion, hand cream, foot cream and eye cream are the kinds cream or lotion with `area` as the attribute. Body sunscreen and face sunscreen are one kind.
  - A product that leaves visible colour, or that is applied to prepare or fix makeup (primer, setting spray), is Makeup; a product with no colour is Skin care, whatever it claims. Lash and brow serums are Makeup > Brows and lashes (FDA 03 [S20]).
  - Eyelash curlers and makeup brushes are "Makeup tools and devices". Hair clippers, beard trimmers and body groomers are "Electric shaving and trimming". Tweezers are "Depilation and epilation". Nail clippers, files and manicure sets are sub-family 8. Nail polish and artificial nails are Makeup > Nails. Hot brushes, hair dryers, irons, brushes, combs and plain ties and clips are "Hair tools".
  - Format (stick, pad, cushion, mist, wipe, roll-on, balm) is an attribute, with Olive Young's vocabulary [S14]. The one exception is masks, where the product compared is the mask type (sheet, sleeping; Douglas Tuchmaske [S8]).

### Candidate B: split preparations from implements first

- **Level 1:** the nature of the object: Preparations (cosmetics and toiletries), Implements and devices (tools, appliances, mirrors, scales), Hairpieces. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** product or tool type.
- *Support:* the standards cover only preparations (FDA, EU, HS 33) [S20][S21][S22]; @cosme keeps beauty goods and appliances as one category [S19]; the regulatory difference (ingredient listing and safety assessment) is real.
- *Where it breaks:* (1) a flat iron sits beside hair spray in the Douglas Hair department [S3] but is far from it here; (2) an electric toothbrush and its paste are one use; (3) a sunscreen stick against a facial roller both belong to a skin routine; (4) a set that holds a brush and a palette; (5) a razor and shaving cream in one wet-shaving kit [S33]. Retail evidence in four of the five regions is area-first, so this candidate adds a cut no buyer navigates by. It remains a usable generated view ("durable" against "used-up").

### Candidate C: function or routine step across the whole body

- **Level 1:** cleanse, treat and care, protect, colour, scent, remove hair, comfort. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** type.
- *Support:* routine order in skin care (Boots' "10-step" regime; Douglas's "right order" guide [S8][S11]); the EU definition lists cleaning, perfuming, changing appearance, protecting, keeping in good condition, correcting odours [S21].
- *Where it breaks:* (1) shampoo and face wash both cleanse but live in different shops and buyer decisions; (2) a tinted sunscreen both protects and colours; (3) a lip balm treats, protects and may colour; (4) fragrance and deodorant both address smell. It cannot place tools (a hair dryer cleans nothing). No fetched source navigates this way except for skin care.

**Choice:** Candidate A. It matches the navigation of Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA and HS, and it carries tools by the area they serve, as Douglas does for hair and brushes.

## 9. Boundaries

**H1 / H2 Health, Medical & Mobility.** Closest objects: acne cleanser and medicated shampoo, sunscreen, dermocosmetic cream, TENS and comfort massagers, contact lens solution, incontinence and period products, condoms, supplements sold for skin, hair or nails. The EU test is purpose: a product placed on the body "exclusively or mainly" to clean, perfume, change appearance, protect, keep in good condition or correct odours is a cosmetic [S21].
*Precedence (memo's current text): a skin, hair, nail or oral product sold to clean, protect, perfume, colour or improve the look of the body is H1, sunscreens included; a product sold to treat or diagnose a named condition, or licensed as a medicine, is H2. A pharmacy channel or the label "dermocosmetic" does not move it [S4][S7]. OWNER: question 12. The domain-H review (X3, H1/H2 sentence) would replace "licensed as a medicine" with: "a product is H2 when its labelled main purpose is to treat, relieve or prevent a named condition, or when it contains an active ingredient sold only as a medicine in every sourced jurisdiction; cosmetic purposes are H1, sunscreens and anti-dandruff and anti-blemish products included; legal status goes to C17 and does not move the object". Whichever wording is chosen, legal class is recorded in the C17 ledger (section 5).* A massager made for comfort is H1 and one made to treat a condition is H2 (the key). Contact lens solution follows the lens (S3) to H2, though HS 33.07 note 4 lists it with toilet preparations [S22]. **Supplements sold for skin, hair or nails, including collagen drinks, are H2 (step 2).** Condoms and other contraceptives are H2 (the key). Coloured non-corrective contact lenses are not an H1 kind in this memo; OWNER: question 15 (H2 with `corrective: no`, H1, or A3).

**H1 / H3 Adult Objects.** Closest objects: powered massagers, wand-type massagers, personal lubricants, anal douches, massage oil.
*Precedence: step 9 is tried before step 18, so an object made for sexual stimulation is H3 and not H1. OWNER: question 13 (massagers). The H3 memo's ordered test would be printed here and in H3: (1) if the maker's own description or packaging names sexual use, or the maker sells it in its adult range, H3; (2) if the maker is silent and a specialist adult retailer files it among sex toys, H3; (3) otherwise H1 for comfort or H2 for treating a condition (X4). Lubricant, massage oil and anal douche are proposed here as H1 kinds (Intimate and menstrual hygiene; Skin care), OWNER: question 5.*

**H1 / B4 Decor.** Closest objects: room spray, incense (agarbatti sits in HS 33.07 [S22]), scented candles, lit makeup mirror, dresser mirror.
*Precedence: fragrance made to be worn on the body is H1; fragrance made to scent a room, incense and candles are B4. A grooming or lit makeup mirror is H1; a wall or dresser mirror is B4 (B4 memo).*

**H1 / A1, A3, A4 (worn and carried).** Closest objects: wigs, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties and plain clips, jewelled combs and hairpins, toiletry bags, period underwear.
*Precedence: wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties and plain clips and bands are H1 (step 17 note); a decorative or jewelled hair comb, pin or clip is A3; a toiletry bag is A4 (step 17 is tried before step 18, A4 memo); period underwear is a garment and A1.*

**H1 / B6, B2, B7 (cleaning, linen, bathroom).** Closest objects: bar soap, body sponge and loofah, towel, bath, bath salts.
*Precedence: soap, bath and shower preparations, bath salts and sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for the body are H1 (the EU lists toilet soaps and bath preparations as cosmetics [S21]); laundry, dish and cleaning soaps and cleaning sponges are B6; towels of every kind are B2; bathtubs and sanitaryware are B7.*

**H1 / B10 Animal Equipment.** Closest objects: pet shampoo and grooming brushes. HS 33.07 note 4 lists "animal toilet preparations" with human cosmetics [S22].
*Precedence: grooming goods made for an animal are B10; the same goods made for a person are H1, whatever the trade heading.*

**Makeup / Skin care (hybrids, H1-6).** Closest objects: BB or CC cream, tinted moisturiser, tinted sunscreen, tinted lip balm, primer, setting spray, lash and brow serum.
*Precedence: a product that leaves visible colour, or that is applied to prepare or fix makeup (primer, setting spray), is Makeup; a product with no colour is Skin care, whatever it claims. Lash and brow serums are Makeup > Brows and lashes (FDA 03 [S20]). Within H1 this decides the leaf, not the category.*

**Sets and kits.** *A set of objects of one kind is filed as that kind; a mixed set by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed; a kit sold, standardised and compared as one object (manicure set, wet-shaving starter kit if sourced) would be a kind of its own. This wording is the proposed general rule (X11); the framework gives the set rule for groceries only. OWNER: question 4.*

**H1 / B9, H3, B5 (edge pairs).** *Nicotine, cannabis and CBD products are B9 even when sold as cosmetics or supplements (B9 memo); intimate-hygiene washes are H1 (EU "external intimate hygiene" [S21]) and objects made for sexual stimulation are H3; baby bottles and feeding gear are B5, breast pumps are H2, and pacifiers, teethers, baby baths, changing mats and potties are H1 (the key).*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A. H1 → sub-family by the area or purpose served (nine) → leaf by job or method, per sub-family, with one final "tools and devices" (or preparations or equipment) leaf in the six area sub-families (skipped in three sub-families of about 12 kinds or fewer) → kind at the retail product-type level (strength class for fragrance: OWNER question 1) → named form for lines, models and iconic products. **Maximum depth four levels below the category; most leaves need three** (leaf, kind, optional named form), and three sub-families need two. Sub-families 8 and 9 rest on the key alone and are provisional until sourced (H1-8).

**Modifiers as attributes or views, never levels:** body area, skin type, hair type, colour and shade, finish, scent family and notes, SPF and PA, volume and price per 100 ml, formulation and format, user (women, men, unisex, couples, baby, child, bariatric, adapted: one list for domain H, no leaf name carries a user word), power source (hair dryer, toothbrush, shaver, hot brush), `disposable`, region, scene, price band, tier entries, channel (dermocosmetic, professional). Legal class goes to the C17 ledger.

**Shared conventions applied.** C1 maker ledger with no H1 column of its own; `segment` dropped (section 6). C2 kind against named form, with `maker_bound` yes for Mach3, ghd gold styler and Aventus and no for the generic cuts; named-form test: a non-maker named form is a design many makers make that buyers ask for by name, and a measuring mode, load or size class, fitting variant, active ingredient or material is an attribute; formats are attributes (H1-5). C3 variant ledger: not used for volume; used for fragrance strength only if the owner takes question 1 option (b). C4 tier entries only on makers, lines and named forms; SPF and PA as attributes with their standards named †; segment labels as platform tags; price band in USD. C5 fixed names, one stated leaf axis per sub-family (section 3). The optional "parts and accessories" sub-family is not used in this memo's recommendation: S3 sends blades, cartridges and brush heads to the razor or toothbrush kind or its leaf; OWNER: question 11. C6 size and user as attributes and tags. C7 order (section 8). C8 scenes (section 7). C9 aliases (section 7). C10 power source is an attribute except where the key names the powered form (electric toothbrush, shaver, hair dryer) or the form changes (electric shaver against cartridge razor); the retail-facet test applies to every facet. C11 regional names are not kinds where a generic kind covers them. C12 mounting does not apply. C13 compatibility ledger for cartridge and injector platforms; other platforms only with sources †. C14(c) sub-family 8 is the multi-use sub-family. C16 no individual-item facts. C17 ledger for every legal class.
