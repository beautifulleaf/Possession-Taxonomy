## 0. Category

**Code and name:** H1 Personal Care & Beauty.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 18): *made to be used on the body for grooming, hygiene, cosmetics, fragrance, or bodily comfort and soothing: grooming tools and powered tools (hair dryers, shavers, electric toothbrushes), grooming mirrors, bathroom and body scales, cosmetics attached to the body, wigs and hairpieces, hair ties and clips, sponges and pumice, massagers, hot-water bottles, heat packs and hand warmers, pacifiers and teethers, baby baths, changing mats, potties and toilet-training seats.* Category list line: "grooming, hygiene, cosmetic, fragrance and comfort tools, devices and products, and baby care."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name H1:**
- Step 18 notes: towels of every kind are B2; massagers made to treat a condition, such as TENS units, are H2.
- Step 2 note (H2): massagers for comfort are H1; infant formula is J3; breast pumps and incontinence products are H2.
- Step 7 note (F6): sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for a use are H1; cleaning sponges and scourers are B6.
- Step 17 note (A-domain): wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties, plain clips and bands are H1; hair ornaments are A3 (so jewelled combs, hairpins and clips are A3).
- Step 1: baby-food meals are prepared food (Culinary); the baby items in step 18 are the only baby goods H1 names.

- Step 27 (B9): nicotine, cannabis and CBD products are B9 even when sold as a cosmetic or supplement.
- S3 (parts follow the whole: a razor blade or toothbrush head follows its razor or toothbrush, and both are H1; cleaning products are B6), S5 (a collected perfume bottle is a perfume bottle), S6 (attestation).
- Appendix A: user (baby, child, adult, gender, ability), price band and tier, brand and maker, region and culture, scene, power source, material, market (domestic, professional) and installation are modifiers.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Fragrance.** The Perfume Society lists eight families: Fresh, Floral, Floriental, Ambrée, Gourmand, Chypre, Woody, Fougère [S23]. A fragrance guide credits the Société Française des Parfumeurs (1984) with seven groups (citrus, floral, fougère, chypre, woody, oriental, aromatic) plus 18 listed families or "facets" such as aldehydic, leather, marine, powdery, green [S24]. Fragrantica's groups page uses chypre, floral, leather, woody with compound sub-groups such as floral aldehyde, floral aquatic, woody chypre [S30].
- **Concentration.** Buying guides and a retailer give five or six labelled classes, with ranges that disagree (section 5) [S9][S25][S29][S26].
- **Shaving.** An enthusiast guide gives six types: double-edge (DE), single-edge (SE), straight, shavette, cartridge, electric, separated by "how many edges meet your skin, how the blade is held, and how much skill and upkeep each asks" [S33]. Wikipedia's razor article lists straight, single-edge safety, double-edge safety, cartridge, disposable, electric [S31].
- **Hair tools.** Wikipedia's hair-iron article gives three kinds (curling, straightening, crimping), with barrel diameter, plate material and handle type as attributes [S34].

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009** defines a cosmetic product by purpose: placed in contact with external parts of the body or the teeth and mouth "exclusively or mainly" to clean, perfume, change appearance, protect, keep in good condition or correct body odours; recital 7 lists skin creams, face masks, tinted bases, make-up powders, toilet soaps, deodorant soaps, perfumes, toilet waters and eau de Cologne, bath and shower products, depilatories, deodorants, hair colorants and waving and straightening products, hair-cleansing, conditioning and hairdressing products, shaving products, make-up and removers, lip products, tooth and mouth care, nail care and make-up, external intimate hygiene, sunbathing and tanning products, skin-whitening and anti-wrinkle products [S21].
- **FDA cosmetic categories** (US listing codes): 01 baby products (shampoos; lotions, oils, powders, creams; wipes), 02 bath preparations, 03 eye makeup (brow pencils, eyeliners, shadows, removers, false eyelashes, mascaras, lash and brow adhesives and serums), 04 children's eye makeup, 05 fragrance (colognes and toilet waters; perfumes; powders; other), 06 hair preparations non-colouring, 07 hair colouring, 08 makeup not eye (blushers, face powders, foundations, body paints, lipsticks and glosses), then a skin-care group (moisturizing, night, paste masks, skin fresheners; body and hand), 15 suntan, 16 tattoo, 17 other; a leave-on or rinse-off split sits below [S20]. Categories 09 to 14 were cut from the fetched excerpt.
- **HS chapter 33** (WCO 2002 text): 33.03 perfumes and toilet waters; 33.04 beauty, make-up and skin care including sun preparations and manicure; 33.05 hair preparations; 33.06 oral and dental hygiene; 33.07 pre-shave, shaving, after-shave, deodorants, bath preparations, depilatories, room deodorisers. Note 4 adds animal toilet preparations and perfumed papers [S22]. Tools are not in this chapter; the standards describe preparations only.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:** (1) The first split is the part of the body or the purpose served: skin, make-up, hair, fragrance, body, shaving, mouth (Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA, EU recital 7, HS 3303–3307). (2) Product type comes next (Produktart; cleansers, treatments, moisturizers; shampoo, conditioner). (3) Skin type, hair type, colour and scent note are facets, not levels. (4) Fragrance is divided in three independent ways: by family (scent), by concentration class (strength) and by user or segment (women's, men's, unisex, niche).
**Split:**
- **Tools.** Douglas files a flat iron in Hair and tools in Gesicht ("Beauty Tools & Zubehör") and brushes in Make-up (Pinsel) [S2][S3][S4]; Olive Young keeps "Skincare Tools" under Skincare [S14]; @cosme gathers beauty goods and appliances as one category beside the product categories [S19]; the standards cover only preparations [S20][S21][S22].
- **Sun care.** Top-level in @cosme (801) and FDA (15); a leaf inside Skincare at Olive Young; "Sonne & Schutz" inside Gesicht and a body-care item at Douglas [S4][S5][S14][S15][S20].
- **Fragrance by user.** Douglas splits by Damen, Herren, Unisex [S1]; Fragrances of the World keeps gender as a field of the entry [S28]; @cosme has none [S18].
- **Dermocosmetics.** Douglas puts pharmacy-brand skincare in Gesundheit & Wellness with filters Wirkungsbereich and Beschwerden and also flags products "Dermakosmetik" inside Gesicht [S4][S7]; Boots keeps CeraVe beside mass labels [S11]. It is a channel, not a product type.
- **Soap.** The EU lists toilet soaps as cosmetics [S21]; HS excludes soap from chapter 33 (heading 3401, note 1) [S22].
- **Baby.** FDA has its own baby category [S20]; no retail page fetched has a baby department in beauty, so baby goods are found by function.
- **Concentration percentages** differ by source (section 5).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Area or purpose first, then product type, then personal attributes.**
- *Navigation order.* Every store and standard fetched opens with the area or purpose: Douglas (Parfum, Make-up, Gesicht, Körper, Haare, Gesundheit) [S1]; Boots (Makeup, Skincare) [S11]; @cosme (skincare, UV, makeup, base makeup, fragrance, hair, body and oral) [S15]; FDA (baby, bath, eye makeup, fragrance, hair, makeup, skin care, suntan) [S20]; HS (perfumes, beauty and skin, hair, oral, shaving and deodorants) [S22]; EU recital 7 [S21].
- *Filter order.* At Douglas the filters run Preis, Produktart (product type), Marke (maker), Für Wen (user), then the area's own attribute: Duftnote for fragrance, Farbe, Deckkraft and Finish for make-up, Haartyp and Haarfarbe for hair, Hauttyp for face and body [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5]. Olive Young puts Skin Concern, Skin Type, SPF, Formulation and Format after the type tabs [S14].
- *What buyers decide first inside an area.* Skin care: skin type or concern, then the routine step (Boots' "10-step K-Beauty regime"; Douglas's guide "Gesichtspflege: Diese Reihenfolge ist die richtige" [S8][S11]). Make-up: face area, then colour and finish [S2][S12]. Hair: hair type and colour, then job (wash, treat, style) [S3]. Fragrance: scent family or note, then concentration and house; the Perfume Society advises learning the families first, "just as you would if you were learning about wine" [S23]. Shaving: the razor mechanism (how many edges, how held, how much upkeep) [S33].
- *Maker first in two places.* Fragrance (Douglas has a separate niche view and Fragrantica is organised by house) [S10][S30] and Korean skin care (Olive Young's brand facet lists counts by label) [S14].
- *Cross-cutting views.* User (men's, women's, unisex, baby) [S1][S6], Korean origin [S8][S12], dermocosmetic channel [S4][S7], and "natural" make-up [S2] each run across every area. They are tags and views.

### Candidate B: split preparations from implements first

- **Level 1:** the nature of the object: Preparations (cosmetics and toiletries), Implements and devices (tools, appliances, mirrors, scales), Hairpieces. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** product or tool type.
- *Support:* the standards cover only preparations (FDA, EU, HS 33) [S20][S21][S22]; @cosme keeps beauty goods and appliances as one category [S19]; the regulatory difference (ingredient listing and safety assessment) is real.
- *Where it breaks:* (1) a flat iron sits beside hair spray in the Douglas Hair department [S3] but is far from it here; (2) an electric toothbrush and its paste are one use; (3) a sunscreen stick against a facial roller both belong to a skin routine; (4) a set that holds a brush and a palette; (5) a razor and shaving cream in one wet-shaving kit [S33]. Retail evidence in four of the five regions is area-first, so this candidate adds a cut no buyer navigates by. It remains a usable generated view ("durable" against "used-up").

### Candidate C: function or routine step across the whole body

- **Level 1:** cleanse, treat and care, protect, colour, scent, remove hair, comfort. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** type.
- *Support:* routine order in skin care (Boots' "10-step" regime; Douglas's "right order" guide [S8][S11]); the EU definition lists cleaning, perfuming, changing appearance, protecting, keeping in good condition, correcting odours [S21].
- *Where it breaks:* (1) shampoo and face wash both cleanse but live in different shops and buyer decisions; (2) a tinted sunscreen both protects and colours; (3) a lip balm treats, protects and may colour; (4) fragrance and deodorant both address smell. It cannot place tools (a hair dryer cleans nothing). No fetched source navigates this way except for skin care.

**Choice:** Candidate A. It matches the navigation of Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA and HS, and it carries tools by the area they serve, as Douglas does for hair and brushes.

**Choice level differs between sub-families:** fragrance is the one place where the kind is a strength label, not a function, because the label changes what is sold and priced (section 5 notes the labels are not standardised). In every other sub-family the kind is a function or mechanism.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: preference (price band), with measured attributes and non-standard label classes. No consensus-ranked tier was found for any sub-family.**

- **Measured spec ladders (attributes with a named standard, C4b):** SPF (Olive Young's SPF facet reads SPF 30+ and SPF 50+ [S14]) and PA grade (printed "SPF50+ PA++++" in a Douglas title [S8]). The test standard for each (ISO or national) was not in a fetched page and must be sourced before data entry.
- **Label classes with no common standard (attributes, not tiers):** fragrance strength. Five fetched sources give five different ranges:

| Class | Douglas [S9] | Fourth Sense [S25] | The Perfume Shop [S29] | Wikipedia [S26] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Extrait or parfum | EdP Intense over 20 % | extrait 35–45 %; parfum 25–35 % | (not in fetched excerpt) | parfum or extrait 15–30 % (IFRA typically about 20 %) |
| Eau de parfum | 10–14 % | 15–25 % | 15–20 % | — |
| Eau de toilette | 6–10 % | 5–15 % | 5–15 % (the quoted expert says up to 18 %) | — |
| Eau de cologne | 3–5 % | 2–4 % | 2–4 % | — |
| Eau fraîche | 1–3 % | 1–3 % | 1–3 % | — |

  Wikipedia states that "various sources differ considerably in their definitions of perfume types" [S26]. The data should store the class label, the source's own range per row, and no computed tier.
- **Segment labels are platform tags, not tiers (C4c):** Douglas's "Nischendüfte" view [S10] and "Dermakosmetik" flag [S4][S7]; Boots's "premium beauty" [S11]. Store as dated `platform_tag` on makers, lines or named forms.
- **Fragrance families:** three systems, none ranked: Edwards (four to five families; 1983 or 1992 depending on the article) [S26][S27][S28], the SFP (seven groups, 1984) [S24], the Perfume Society (eight) [S23]. One controlled list is needed, shared with B4 home fragrance (B4 memo recommends the Edwards wheel).
- **Price band (C4d):** one USD scheme from dated list prices. Price spread is large: a Douglas Chloé eau de parfum at 30 ml, 36.99 EUR online price (1,233 EUR per litre), against Kilian The Liquors Angels' Share parfum at 50 ml, 195 EUR (3,900 EUR per litre) [S1]. Price per 100 ml should be derived and shown, because volume is an attribute.
- **Modifier route:** `tier` entries are written on makers, lines or named forms only, with scheme, value, source and date (C4a). Nothing is written on a kind.
- **Razors, hair tools, comfort goods:** no ranking source fetched. Professional against consumer is the Appendix A market modifier.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity, with platform pockets.**
- *Identity (fragrance):* the house and the name are what is sought; Fragrance databases are keyed by brand, perfumer and date [S28]; Fragrantica lists houses [S30]; Douglas runs a separate niche view and brand pages [S1][S10]. The same scent is sold under several strength labels [S9][S29].
- *Identity (skin care, makeup):* Olive Young's brand facet is the main way into Korean skin care [S14]; Douglas product titles lead with maker and line (for example "Beauty of Joseon Relief Sun") [S8].
- *Platform (shaving):* cartridge systems are tied to their handles (Gillette Mach3 and Fusion, including battery-powered variants) and the Schick Injector uses its own narrow blades [S32]. Whether double-edge blades are a shared standard needs its own source. Toothbrush-head platforms were not fetched.
- *Incidental:* body wash, toilet soap bar, hot-water bottle, massager, pacifier.

**Maker ledger: yes (C1).** Core columns as in C1. Extensions for H1:
- `segment` (designer or fashion house, niche, Gulf or Arabian, mass retail, natural or clean, dermocosmetic, professional salon), each with a fetched source for the label: the Douglas niche view [S10], the Fragrantica list mixing fashion, niche, Gulf and mass houses [S30], the Douglas Dermakosmetik flag [S4][S7].
- `parent_id` for corporate group (Fragrances of the World records the corporate group [S28]).
- Fashion houses sell fragrance under their own names (Jean Paul Gaultier, Max Mara [S1]; Hugo Boss, Burberry, Calvin Klein [S30]); whether a given label is made under licence is not stated in the fetched pages and needs a source for `maker_class` = licensee.
- Retail own-label rows: Douglas Collection (brand page named in navigation [S1]), Boots No7 [S12].
- *Named-form extension (fragrance only):* `perfumer`, `year`, `scent_family` (controlled list), `bottle_designer` (all fields kept by Fragrances of the World [S28]); the strength class is the kind, and sister strengths of the same scent share a `scent_id`.
- *Compatibility ledger (C13):* cartridge systems and single-edge injector blades, with `owner` = maker [S32]; add toothbrush-head and any other platform only with a fetched source.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the axis the sub-family states** (C7).
- Skin care: routine order (cleanse, tone, treat, moisturise, protect), the order in which buyers are taught to apply [S8][S11].
- Makeup: face-area order as the stores print it: face makeup, eye makeup, lips, nails [S12]; Douglas's Teint, Augen, Augenbrauen, Lippen, Nägel, Pinsel [S2].
- Fragrance: strongest strength class to lightest, as Douglas orders them ("from the highest to the lightest") [S9].
- Razors: the order Wikipedia gives (straight, single-edge, double-edge, cartridge, disposable, electric) [S31], which also runs from open blade to powered; hair irons: curling, straightening, crimping [S34].
- Named forms: by maker (ledger `name`), then first-introduction year as attested (Fragrances of the World keeps a date for each perfume [S28]), then alphabetical.
Reason: retail and guide orders already follow these keys, and no tier order exists to sort by (section 5).

## 9. Boundaries

**H1 / H2 Health, Medical & Mobility.** Closest objects: acne cleanser and medicated shampoo, sunscreen, dermocosmetic cream, TENS and comfort massagers, contact lens solution, incontinence and period products. The EU test is purpose: a product placed on the body "exclusively or mainly" to clean, perfume, change appearance, protect, keep in good condition or correct odours is a cosmetic [S21].
*Precedence: a skin, hair, nail or oral product sold to clean, protect, perfume, colour or improve the look of the body is H1, sunscreens included; a product sold to treat or diagnose a named condition, or licensed as a medicine, is H2. A pharmacy channel or the label "dermocosmetic" does not move it [S4][S7]. A massager made for comfort is H1 and one made to treat a condition is H2 (the key). Contact lens solution follows the lens (S3) to H2, though HS 33.07 note 4 lists it with toilet preparations [S22].*

**H1 / B4 Decor.** Closest objects: room spray, incense (agarbatti sits in HS 33.07 [S22]), scented candles, lit makeup mirror, dresser mirror.
*Precedence: fragrance made to be worn on the body is H1; fragrance made to scent a room, incense and candles are B4. A grooming or lit makeup mirror is H1; a wall or dresser mirror is B4 (B4 memo).*

**H1 / A1, A3, A4 (worn and carried).** Closest objects: wigs, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties and plain clips, jewelled combs and hairpins, toiletry bags.
*Precedence: wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties and plain clips and bands are H1 (step 17 note); a decorative or jewelled hair comb, pin or clip is A3; a toiletry bag is A4 (step 17 is tried before step 18, A4 memo).*

**H1 / B6, B2, B7 (cleaning, linen, bathroom).** Closest objects: bar soap, body sponge and loofah, towel, bath, bath salts.
*Precedence: soap, bath and shower preparations, bath salts and sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for the body are H1 (the EU lists toilet soaps and bath preparations as cosmetics [S21]); laundry, dish and cleaning soaps and cleaning sponges are B6; towels of every kind are B2; bathtubs and sanitaryware are B7.*

**H1 / B10 Animal Equipment.** Closest objects: pet shampoo and grooming brushes. HS 33.07 note 4 lists "animal toilet preparations" with human cosmetics [S22].
*Precedence: grooming goods made for an animal are B10; the same goods made for a person are H1, whatever the trade heading.*

**H1 / B9, H3, B5 (edge pairs).** *Nicotine, cannabis and CBD products are B9 even when sold as cosmetics or supplements (B9 memo); intimate-hygiene washes are H1 (EU "external intimate hygiene" [S21]) and objects made for sexual stimulation are H3; baby bottles and feeding gear are B5, breast pumps are H2, and pacifiers, teethers, baby baths, changing mats and potties are H1 (the key).*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A. H1 → sub-family by the area or purpose served (nine) → leaf by job or method, per sub-family (skipped in three sub-families of about 12 kinds or fewer) → kind at the retail product-type level (strength class for fragrance) → named form for lines, models and iconic products. **Maximum depth four levels below the category; most leaves need three** (leaf, kind, optional named form), and three sub-families need two.

**Modifiers as attributes or views, never levels:** skin type, hair type, colour and shade, finish, scent family and notes, SPF and PA, volume, formulation and format, user (women, men, unisex, baby, child), area (face or body), power source (hair dryer, toothbrush, shaver), region, segment, scene, price band, tier entries, channel (dermocosmetic, professional).

**Shared conventions applied.** C1 maker ledger with H1 extensions (section 6); C2 kind against named form, with `maker_bound` yes for Mach3, ghd gold styler, and Aventus and no for the generic cuts; C3 variant ledger used for fragrance volume and limited editions only if an owner scene needs it; C4 tier entries only on makers, lines and named forms; SPF and PA as attributes; segment labels as platform tags; C5 fixed names, leaf axes in section 3; the optional "parts and accessories" sub-family is not needed, because S3 sends blades, cartridges and brush heads to the razor or toothbrush kind or its leaf; C6 size and user as attributes and tags; C7 order (section 8); C8 scenes (section 7); C9 aliases (section 7); C10 power source is an attribute except where the key names the powered form (electric toothbrush, shaver, hair dryer) or the form changes (electric shaver against cartridge razor); C11 regional names are kinds only where no generic kind covers them (toner pad, sleeping mask, sun stick are generic descriptions with a region tag); C12 mounting does not apply; C13 compatibility ledger for cartridge and injector platforms.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. **Fragrance kind.** Strength classes as kinds (recommended: retailers and FDA treat them as product types and they change the product and price), or one kind "fine fragrance" with strength as an attribute (simpler, matches the fact that the percentage ranges are not standardised, section 5).
2. **Sun care.** A leaf in Skin care (recommended, as Olive Young) or its own sub-family (as @cosme top level 801 and FDA 15).
3. **Tools.** By the area they serve (recommended, as Douglas does for hair and brushes) or one tools sub-family (as @cosme 808).
4. **Sets and kits** (Douglas "Sets", Boots "value packs and bundles", @cosme kits). Recommend an attribute listing contents, filed by the principal item; the framework gives that rule for groceries only.
5. **Items the key does not name:** nappies and diapers, menstrual products, baby wipes, contact lens solution, cotton pads, deodorant wipes. Default proposed: H1 for hygiene and wipes, H2 for incontinence and lens solution.
6. **Product-level depth.** The fetched evidence suggests tens of thousands of skincare and makeup products and 17,000 perfumes. Recommend named forms only for iconic or attested lines and models, with an owner-chosen cut-off for fragrance.
7. **Strength percentages.** Store the label and each source's range (recommended), or adopt one scheme such as IFRA's roughly 20 % for parfum [S26].
8. **Baby goods.** Confirm that "baby" is a user tag, and that pacifiers, teethers, baby baths, changing mats and potties sit in Comfort and Body, bath and hygiene by function (BRIEF.md against the key's listing).
9. **Scope of daily consumables.** BRIEF.md names consumables "kept or collected rather than used up" (perfume collections). Step 18 sends all toiletries and cosmetics to H1. Confirm that everyday toiletries (toothpaste, shampoo, soap) are in scope.
10. **Fragrance family list.** One controlled list for H1 and B4: Edwards wheel (recommended in the B4 memo), SFP seven groups, or the Perfume Society eight [S23][S24][S27].
