## 0. Category

**Code and name:** H1 Personal Care & Beauty.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 18): *made to be used on the body for grooming, hygiene, cosmetics, fragrance, or bodily comfort and soothing: grooming tools and powered tools (hair dryers, shavers, electric toothbrushes), grooming mirrors, bathroom and body scales, cosmetics attached to the body, wigs and hairpieces, hair ties and clips, sponges and pumice, massagers, hot-water bottles, heat packs and hand warmers, pacifiers and teethers, baby baths, changing mats, potties and toilet-training seats.* Category list line: "grooming, hygiene, cosmetic, fragrance and comfort tools, devices and products, and baby care."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name H1:**
- Step 18 notes: towels of every kind are B2; massagers made to treat a condition, such as TENS units, are H2.
- Step 2 note (H2): massagers for comfort are H1; infant formula is J3; breast pumps and incontinence products are H2.
- Step 7 note (F6): sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for a use are H1; cleaning sponges and scourers are B6.
- Step 17 note (A-domain): wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties, plain clips and bands are H1; hair ornaments are A3 (so jewelled combs, hairpins and clips are A3).
- Step 1: baby-food meals are prepared food (Culinary); the baby items in step 18 are the only baby goods H1 names.
- C4 memo: a brush made for painting or glazing is C4; a brush made for applying makeup is H1.
- Step 30 (B4 branch) and B4 memo: wall and dresser mirrors, candles and home fragrance including incense are B4; fragrance made to be used on the body and grooming mirrors are H1.
- Step 27 (B9): nicotine, cannabis and CBD products are B9 even when sold as a cosmetic or supplement.
- S3 (parts follow the whole: a razor blade or toothbrush head follows its razor or toothbrush, and both are H1; cleaning products are B6), S5 (a collected perfume bottle is a perfume bottle), S6 (attestation).
- Appendix A: user (baby, child, adult, gender, ability), price band and tier, brand and maker, region and culture, scene, power source, material, market (domestic, professional) and installation are modifiers.
- **BRIEF.md conflict to note:** the owner decision "baby and child goods have no category of their own; each goes to its functional home" and the placement key's naming of pacifiers, baby baths, changing mats and potties in H1 are consistent only if baby goods are placed by function and "baby" is a user tag. ### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Fragrance.** The Perfume Society (UK) lists eight families: Fresh, Floral, Floriental, Ambrée, Gourmand, Chypre, Woody, Fougère [S23]. A French buying guide credits the Société Française des Parfumeurs (1984) with seven groups (citrus, floral, fougère, chypre, woody, oriental, aromatic) plus 15 secondary families or "facets" such as aldehydic, leather, marine, powdery, green [S24]. Fragrantica's groups page uses chypre, floral, leather, woody with compound sub-groups such as floral aldehyde, floral aquatic, woody chypre [S30].
- **Concentration.** Buying guides and a retailer give five or six labelled classes, with ranges that disagree (section 5) [S9][S25][S29][S26].
- **Shaving.** An enthusiast guide gives six types: double-edge (DE), single-edge (SE), straight, shavette, cartridge, electric, separated by "how many edges meet your skin, how the blade is held, and how much skill and upkeep each asks" [S33]. Wikipedia's razor article lists straight, single-edge safety, double-edge safety, cartridge, disposable, electric [S31].
- **Hair tools.** Wikipedia's hair-iron article gives three kinds (curling, straightening, crimping), with barrel diameter, plate material and handle type as attributes [S34].

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **EU Cosmetics Regulation 1223/2009** defines a cosmetic product by purpose: placed in contact with external parts of the body or the teeth and mouth "exclusively or mainly" to clean, perfume, change appearance, protect, keep in good condition or correct body odours; recital 7 lists skin creams, face masks, tinted bases, make-up powders, toilet soaps, deodorant soaps, perfumes, toilet waters and eau de Cologne, bath and shower products, depilatories, deodorants, hair colorants and waving and straightening products, hair-cleansing, conditioning and hairdressing products, shaving products, make-up and removers, lip products, tooth and mouth care, nail care and make-up, external intimate hygiene, sunbathing and tanning products, skin-whitening and anti-wrinkle products [S21].
- **FDA cosmetic categories** (US listing codes): 01 baby products (shampoos; lotions, oils, powders, creams; wipes), 02 bath preparations, 03 eye makeup (brow pencils, eyeliners, shadows, removers, false eyelashes, mascaras, lash and brow adhesives and serums), 04 children's eye makeup, 05 fragrance (colognes and toilet waters; perfumes; powders; other), 06 hair preparations non-colouring, 07 hair colouring, 08 makeup not eye (blushers, face powders, foundations, body paints, lipsticks and glosses), then a skin-care group (moisturizing, night, paste masks, skin fresheners; body and hand), 15 suntan, 16 tattoo, 17 other; a leave-on or rinse-off split sits below [S20]. Categories 09 to 14 were cut from the fetched excerpt.
- **HS chapter 33** (WCO 2002 text): 33.03 perfumes and toilet waters; 33.04 beauty, make-up and skin care including sun preparations and manicure; 33.05 hair preparations; 33.06 oral and dental hygiene; 33.07 pre-shave, shaving, after-shave, deodorants, bath preparations, depilatories, room deodorisers. Note 4 adds animal toilet preparations and perfumed papers [S22]. Tools are not in this chapter; the standards describe preparations only.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:** (1) The first split is the part of the body or the purpose served: skin, make-up, hair, fragrance, body, shaving, mouth (Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA, EU recital 7, HS 3303–3307). (2) Product type comes next (Produktart; cleansers, treatments, moisturizers; shampoo, conditioner). (3) Skin type, hair type, colour and scent note are facets, not levels. (4) Fragrance is divided in three independent ways: by family (scent), by concentration class (strength) and by user or segment (women's, men's, unisex, niche).
**Split:**
- **Tools.** Douglas files a flat iron in Hair and tools in Gesicht ("Beauty Tools & Zubehör") and brushes in Make-up (Pinsel) [S2][S3][S4]; Olive Young keeps "Skincare Tools" under Skincare [S14]; @cosme gathers beauty goods and appliances as one category beside the product categories [S19]; the standards cover only preparations [S20][S21][S22].
- **Sun care.** Top-level in @cosme (801) and FDA (15); a leaf inside Skincare at Olive Young; "Sonne & Schutz" inside Gesicht and a body-care item at Douglas [S4][S5][S14][S15][S20].
- **Fragrance by user.** Douglas splits by Damen, Herren, Unisex [S1]; Fragrances of the World keeps gender as a field of the entry [S28]; @cosme has none [S18].
- **Dermocosmetics.** Douglas puts pharmacy-brand skincare in Gesundheit & Wellness with filters Wirkungsbereich and Beschwerden and also flags products "Dermakosmetik" inside Gesicht [S4][S7]; Boots keeps CeraVe beside mass labels [S11]. It is a channel, not a product type.
- **Soap.** The EU lists toilet soaps as cosmetics [S21]; HS excludes soap from chapter 33 (heading 3401, note 1) [S22].
- **Baby.** FDA has its own baby category [S20]; no retail page fetched has a baby department in beauty, so baby goods are found by function.
- **Concentration percentages** differ by source (section 5).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Area or purpose first, then product type, then personal attributes.**
- *Navigation order.* Every store and standard fetched opens with the area or purpose: Douglas (Parfum, Make-up, Gesicht, Körper, Haare, Gesundheit) [S1]; Boots (Makeup, Skincare) [S11]; @cosme (skincare, UV, makeup, base makeup, fragrance, hair, body and oral) [S15]; FDA (baby, bath, eye makeup, fragrance, hair, makeup, skin care, suntan) [S20]; HS (perfumes, beauty and skin, hair, oral, shaving and deodorants) [S22]; EU recital 7 [S21].
- *Filter order.* At Douglas the filters run Preis, Produktart (product type), Marke (maker), Für Wen (user), then the area's own attribute: Duftnote for fragrance, Farbe, Deckkraft and Finish for make-up, Haartyp and Haarfarbe for hair, Hauttyp for face and body [S1][S2][S3][S4][S5]. Olive Young puts Skin Concern, Skin Type, SPF, Formulation and Format after the type tabs [S14].
- *What buyers decide first inside an area.* Skin care: skin type or concern, then the routine step (Boots' "10-step K-Beauty regime"; Douglas's guide "Gesichtspflege: Diese Reihenfolge ist die richtige" [S8][S11]). Make-up: face area, then colour and finish [S2][S12]. Hair: hair type and colour, then job (wash, treat, style) [S3]. Fragrance: scent family or note, then concentration and house; the Perfume Society advises learning the families first, "just as you would if you were learning about wine" [S23]. Shaving: the razor mechanism (how many edges, how held, how much upkeep) [S33].
- *Maker first in two places.* Fragrance (Douglas has a separate niche view and Fragrantica is organised by house) [S10][S30] and Korean skin care (Olive Young's brand facet lists counts by label) [S14].
- *Cross-cutting views.* User (men's, women's, unisex, baby) [S1][S6], Korean origin [S8][S12], dermocosmetic channel [S4][S7], and "natural" make-up [S2] each run across every area. They are tags and views.

### Candidate B: split preparations from implements first

- **Level 1:** the nature of the object: Preparations (cosmetics and toiletries), Implements and devices (tools, appliances, mirrors, scales), Hairpieces. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** product or tool type.
- *Support:* the standards cover only preparations (FDA, EU, HS 33) [S20][S21][S22]; @cosme keeps beauty goods and appliances as one category [S19]; the regulatory difference (ingredient listing and safety assessment) is real.
- *Where it breaks:* (1) a flat iron sits beside hair spray in the Douglas Hair department [S3] but is far from it here; (2) an electric toothbrush and its paste are one use; (3) a sunscreen stick against a facial roller both belong to a skin routine; (4) a set that holds a brush and a palette; (5) a razor and shaving cream in one wet-shaving kit [S33]. Retail evidence in four of the five regions is area-first, so this candidate adds a cut no buyer navigates by. It remains a usable generated view ("durable" against "used-up").

### Candidate C: function or routine step across the whole body

- **Level 1:** cleanse, treat and care, protect, colour, scent, remove hair, comfort. **Level 2:** area. **Level 3:** type.
- *Support:* routine order in skin care (Boots' "10-step" regime; Douglas's "right order" guide [S8][S11]); the EU definition lists cleaning, perfuming, changing appearance, protecting, keeping in good condition, correcting odours [S21].
- *Where it breaks:* (1) shampoo and face wash both cleanse but live in different shops and buyer decisions; (2) a tinted sunscreen both protects and colours; (3) a lip balm treats, protects and may colour; (4) fragrance and deodorant both address smell. It cannot place tools (a hair dryer cleans nothing). No fetched source navigates this way except for skin care.

**Choice:** Candidate A. It matches the navigation of Douglas, Boots, @cosme, FDA and HS, and it carries tools by the area they serve, as Douglas does for hair and brushes.
