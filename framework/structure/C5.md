## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C5 Office & Stationery.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 31): *made for writing, paper and desk work: pens, pencils, highlighters, paper goods, notebooks, diaries and planners, calendars, greeting cards, stickers, gift wrap, desk accessories and organisers, letter trays and filing supplies, rulers and postal scales, calculators, office machines whose main function is not printing from or scanning to a computer (copiers, shredders, laminators, label makers, binding machines), connected or not.* Category list line: "writing instruments, paper goods, desk accessories, calculators, office machines."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name C5:**
- Step 31 note: printers, scanners and multifunction printers are D1.
- Step 28 note (C4): "pencils, pens and highlighters made for writing are C5"; inks, pastels, coloured pencils, crayons and markers made for making pictures, sketchbooks and scrapbooking supplies are C4.
- Step 19 note (F5): blank notebooks, diaries, greeting cards and calendars are C5; blank scrapbook albums are C4; written diaries, letters and picture postcards used or not are F5. Appendix A: being written, drawn or recorded on is not condition; used paper goods become records (step 19).
- Step 23 note (C6): rulers and postal scales are C5; kitchen scales B5, bathroom scales H1, luggage scales A4, building measuring tools C1, sewing tape measures C4.
- Step 24 (D1): printers, scanners, cash registers and card readers, point-of-sale terminals, hardware keys are D1; step 24 (D2): electronic organisers, e-readers and tablets are D2.
- Step 30 comes before step 31: desks, filing cabinets, shelves, boxes, magazine racks and lap desks are B1 (furniture and storage), lamps are B3, picture frames and decorations are B4.
- Step 32: scissors-class hand tools are C1; adhesives, fasteners, packing materials are C8. These steps come after step 31, so a paper-and-desk object that fits step 31 stops there.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): ink cartridges, converters, refills, nibs and leads, made to be used up in or fitted to one kind of object, go with the pen or pencil, so they land in C5. Cases are parts only when they hold one object at a time. Printer ink is D1.
- S5 and Appendix A: a collected pen is a pen; era (vintage), brand, price band and quality tier, material, region, scene, user (school), setting (office), market (professional), edition (limited) are modifiers.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

All of these are retailer- or maker-run guides; no forum wiki was fetched (gap, section 1.6).
- **Fountain pen buying guides** break the choice into the same few attributes in the same order: filling system (cartridge, converter, piston, vacuum, eyedropper) [S18][S6], nib size and nib shape [S19], nib material and body material [S4][S27], price band [S27], and paper and ink compatibility [S22][S20]. The JetPens filter list for fountain pens carries the same attributes as facets [S4].
- **Nib width is not a standard.** A medium nib can be "an entirely different width" between makers, and Japanese nibs run about one size finer than Western ones [S19][S27]. The JetPens table gives Western EF/F/M/B as about 0.5/0.6/0.75/0.9 mm and Japanese about 0.3/0.45/0.6 mm [S19].
- **Ink** is described by colorant (dye-based or pigment-based), wetness, saturation, shading, sheen and water resistance [S22].
- **Paper** is described by weight (gsm), size standard (ISO 216 A and B series, JIS B, Semi B5), ruling (blank, lined, dot grid, graph), and fountain-pen friendliness [S21][S20][S33]. Tomoe River is 52 gsm, Stalogy 80 gsm, Kokuyo Zarazara 90 gsm [S20][S25].
- **Planners and notebook systems.** Hobonichi Techo, Traveler's Notebook (cover plus refills), Midori MD, Kokuyo Jibun Techo and Stalogy are each sold as a named system with accessories and covers [S25][S32][S28].
- **Japanese stationery stores** are described in three classes: lifestyle stores (Loft, Hands, MUJI), specialist stores (Itoya Ginza, mt lab) and online [S29].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- **Collectors Weekly** splits vintage pens first by **Types** (ballpoint, dip, fountain, gold, sterling), which mixes mechanism and material, then by **Brands** (Aurora, Cross, Montblanc, Montegrappa, OMAS, Parker, Pelikan, Pilot, Sailor, Sheaffer, Wahl Eversharp, Waterman, Visconti), then lists Related items (ink bottles, ink blotters, inkwells, pencils) [S10]. The essay on the page names three main types (dip, fountain, ballpoint) and narrates eras: innovation to the 1960s, then fashion and limited editions [S10].
- **Resale and consignment.** A Toronto consignment dealer's Montblanc guide organises the market by **production era** (pre-war, celluloid, precious resin, 14K to 18K nib around 1991), by **model number** (the 149 has a no. 9 nib, the 146 a no. 6 nib; the numbers encode line, filling mechanism and nib size) and by **limited editions** (Patron of the Arts, Writers Edition); value drivers are condition, completeness and edition number [S26].
- **Pen-cyclopedias** (PenHero) work at maker and model-year grain: Sheaffer model taxonomies by year, Snorkel nib codes, Conklin filling-system eras [S35].
- **Societies:** Pen Collectors of America [S36] and the Writing Equipment Society (UK) [S37] cover "fountain pens and other writing instruments", with reference libraries, repair classes and shows; WES also runs a service to find new homes for collections [S37].
- Collectors Weekly also lists desk and office antiques (typewriters, calculators, slide rules, staplers, paperweights, rulers, wax seals), but that list was seen only in a search excerpt, so it is not counted.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **UNSPSC** puts "Office Equipment, Accessories and Supplies" as segment 44 (family 4412 Office supplies; class 441219 Ink and lead refills) and "Paper Materials and Products" as a different segment in the raw-materials block [S34]. Pens, paper and machines therefore sit in different commodity blocks; UNSPSC divides by commodity type, not by use.
- **ISO 216** (A and B paper series) and **JIS** B sizes are the paper-size standards buyers actually filter by [S21]. JetPens' own size facet adds a coarse Small/Medium/Large beside A4, A5, B5, Semi B5 [S3].
- **Harmonized System heading 9608 "Pens, pencils"** exists (Chapter 96) [S13]; the fetched page gave only the heading title, so the subheading split is not reported. ISO standards for ballpoint and rollerball pens were seen only in a search excerpt.
- **Japanese trade classes** (OfficeJapan) are the finest-grained trade list found: about 150 classes, including premium writing instruments as its own class [S7].

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **Wikipedia, Office supplies** divides by "type of product and general use": blank paper (sizes, thicknesses), containers (binders, envelopes, boxes, desk organisers), writing pads and books (notebooks, composition books, legal pads), writing utensils and corrections [S11].
- **Wikipedia, Fountain pen** divides nibs (round sizes plus stub, italic, oblique, music, flex, hooded, inlaid), filling mechanisms (eyedropper, lever, piston, vacuum, Snorkel, cartridge and converter), cartridge standards (international versus proprietary) and eras [S12]. **List of pen types, brands and companies** lists types and brands A–Z with country templates [S23].
- **Smithsonian "Carbons to Computers"** treats office equipment through desks, filing systems and the typewriter, and compares typewriter holdouts with fountain-pen users [S24]. Japanese Wikipedia files 文房具 (stationery) under 事務用品 (office supplies) with paper as the largest subcategory [S8].

### 1.6 Where sources agree, where they split, and gaps

Agree: pens, pencils, paper goods, desk items, filing and calculators/machines are separate families everywhere; the pen choice is made by mechanism first and by nib, filling and material second; paper is described by size, weight and ruling.
Split: whether tier/style/origin are navigation (Ryman, JetPens) or attributes (Officeworks, OfficeJapan); whether calculators and label makers are "supplies" or "machines"; whether Types mix mechanism and material (Collectors Weekly) or keep mechanism only (Wikipedia, OfficeJapan).
Gaps: no fetched source from China, Korea, France or Italy (strong stationery markets: Korean and Chinese retailers, Clairefontaine/Rhodia, Italian pen houses); no forum wiki (Fountain Pen Network, Reddit); no museum other than the Smithsonian page; calculators and office machines have retail evidence only; amazon.de returned no category tree and one tariff page failed.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Short answer: form or mechanism first, then a small fixed set of specs, then the maker or system.** The evidence differs by family.

- **Pens: mechanism, then filling, nib and material, then price.** JetPens' first split under Pens is by writing mechanism (fountain, ballpoint, gel, rollerball, brush, dip, marker, highlighter) [S2]. Inside fountain pens the retailers then offer, in this order: use/tier collections, filling mechanism [S6], and facets for nib size, nib material, body material [S4]. The beginner guides rank the questions the same way: filling system, nib width, material and price band [S27][S18][S19]. Cult Pens' FAQ answers "which nib" with "medium is the safest" and "which paper" with named brands, so for first-time buyers the choice is nib and paper after the pen type [S5].
- **Paper goods: form, then size, then ruling and weight.** JetPens separates Planners, Notebooks, Sticky Notes, Binders, Loose Leaf, Memo Notepads [S3], then offers Binding Type, Paper Size and Sheet Style as the first notebook browse links and Paper Weight as a facet [S33]. Planners add Planner Layout (daily, weekly horizontal and vertical, monthly) and Dated [S32]. Japanese stationery guides put the **paper stock** (Tomoe River, MD, Stalogy) and the **system** (Hobonichi, Traveler's) ahead of the cover [S25][S20].
- **Desk items and machines: function.** Officeworks, Ryman and Staples list them by what they do (stapler, hole punch, trimmer, laminator, shredder, label maker) [S14][S15][S16]. Staples' buying text states the machine function and then the spec (cross-cut or micro-cut versus strip-cut shredders; basic, printing, scientific, graphing calculators) [S16].
- **Calculators: capability class first** (scientific, desktop, financial, graphing) at Officeworks [S30] and (basic, printing, scientific, graphing) at Staples [S16]. The class is the thing a student or accountant buys by.
- **Maker or tier first** only appears as navigation in one retailer's style split (Ryman Essentials / Premium / Fashion / Japanese-inspired) [S17] and in collector sites, where Brands is the second level after Types [S10]. In the collector market the maker is part of the thing sought (section 6).

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: C5 holds the whole category; named forms are models and lines; the modifiers listed in section 0 never become levels.

### Candidate A (recommended): role at the desk, then form family, then kind, then named form

Levels, one axis each:
1. **Sub-family**: the role the object plays in writing and desk work: *puts marks on a surface* (C5.1), *carries marks* (C5.2), *holds, organises and handles paper at the desk* (C5.3), *calculates or processes paper* (C5.4).
2. **Leaf**: the mechanism or physical form family that sets how it does that job (nib pens, ball pens, bound books, diaries and calendars, desk organisers, calculators, document machines).
3. **Kind**: a generic type a person owns (fountain pen, notebook, stapler, shredder).
4. **Named form**: a model, line or iconic design (Lamy Safari, Hobonichi Techo, Traveler's Notebook).

Where Candidate A breaks (edge objects):
- **Brush pen**: a pen by mechanism, but sold for drawing and lettering (JetPens lists Brush, Calligraphy, and Comic & Manga pens as pen types [S2], Goulet lists brush pens under pens [S6]); the framework sends art markers and brushes made for picture-making to C4.
- **Traveler's notebook / Hobonichi cover**: a cover plus refills is both a notebook and a system, and the cover is an accessory of several systems (an "Accessories for the Hobonichi Techo" collection [S1]; Kokuyo Jibun Techo index tabs [S32]; TRAVELER'S notebook refills [S33]). Candidate A files the system as a kind and the refill books as kinds of notebook.
- **Himekuri sticky-note calendar** [S32]: calendar by use, sticky notes by form; Candidate A files it under diaries and calendars (designed function).
- **Pen case, pencil case**: a one-pen sleeve is a part of the pen (framework rule S3); a multi-pen pencil case is a bag-like object that Officeworks and Ryman keep in stationery [S14][S17]; step 17 "a bag ... of any kind" may claim it (section 9).

Levels: (1) leaf = the 30-odd retailer product families (Pens, Pencils, Notebooks, Calculators, Staplers, Laminating, Binding, Labels ...) [S14]; (2) kind; (3) named form. One axis: "product family as shopped".
Examples (10–15 leaves): Pens, Markers & Highlighters, Pencils, Notebooks & Journals, Diaries & Planners, Adhesive Notes, Calculators, Document Filing, Binders, Staplers, Hole Punches, Laminating, Binding, Labels & Label Makers, Rulers.
Breaks: (a) 30 leaves with no grouping force the owner and any reader to scan a long flat list; (b) "Pens" mixes fountain pens (with ink, nibs and converters as their own sub-universe) with disposable gel pens, so the leaf is too broad where collector depth is needed; (c) ink, refills and nibs have no natural home (Ryman files ink under "Premium Ink, Refills & Accessories" [S17], JetPens as pen parts [S18][S22]); (d) it follows a single retailer's choices, and the other four retailers differ on where calculators and label makers go [S14][S15][S16].

### Candidate C: market tier or style first (Ryman style)

Levels: (1) tier or style (Essentials, Premium, Fashion, Japanese-inspired, School, Gifts) [S17]; (2) family; (3) kind; (4) named form.
Breaks: it uses region, tier, scene and user as levels, which Appendix A forbids; a Lamy safari, a Hobonichi Techo and a Kokuyo Campus notebook each fit two or three tier headings; a scientific calculator is "School" or "Office" depending on the buyer; one object has several homes, which violates framework rule S1 (one home). Kept only as evidence that these are real browse axes, to be offered as generated views.

## 4. Depth and size

These are estimates from the retailer counts; JetPens' counts are product lines and variants (colours, nib sizes), not models.

- **Generic kinds**: about 110–140 in total (the 55-kind tree above plus pen accessories, further pencils and markers, more desk and filing items, copiers, fax, time recorders and check writers, more card and stamp kinds). Evidence for the order of magnitude: Officeworks' office-supply families number about 30 [S14], OfficeJapan's trade classes about 150 [S7], JetPens' fountain pen "types" 18 [S4] and its pen types 13 [S2].
- **Named forms**: probably 1,000–2,500 notable models and lines (my estimate): roughly 500–800 pen lines including vintage, 150–250 ink lines, 150–250 notebook, paper-stock and planner lines, 100–200 calculators, 100–200 machines and desk items. Evidence: JetPens shows 2,958 fountain-pen items, 10,372 pen items, 1,394 notebooks and 736 planners [S4][S2][S33][S32]; Collectors Weekly names 13 brand pages and PenHero documents model-year taxonomies for single makers [S10][S35].
- **Where depth concentrates**: (1) fountain pens and their inks (collector depth, model and maker driven), (2) notebooks, planners and paper stocks (Japanese system forms), (3) vintage pens by model and era, (4) calculators by model. Everything in C5.3 and the machines of C5.4 is shallow.
- **Broad leaves** (many kinds or many named forms): Nib pens, Ball and gel pens, Bound books, Diaries and planners. **Medium**: Pencils, Porous-tip pens, Loose paper, Desk organisers, Paper-handling tools, Filing, Calculators. **Narrow**: Measuring and geometry, Cards and wrap, Document machines (five to eight kinds, little brand comparison).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Verdict: mixed, with one dominant type per sub-family.**

- **Pens (C5.1): consensus-ranked at best.** No measurable spec sets the order. What exists is a community and retailer ladder by price and nib material: JetPens' budget guide steps through under 20 USD (Platinum Preppy, Pilot Kakuno, Kaweco Perkeo), 20–50 (LAMY safari, TWSBI ECO), 50–100 ("all about style", better build), 100–200, and 200+ (Sailor Pro Gear Slim) [S27]. Cult Pens ties steel nibs to sub-100 GBP pens and gold nibs above, then says "don't get hung up on it", so nib material is a spec that correlates with tier without ranking by itself [S5]. Retailer collections name the rungs: entry-level gold-nib picks and "Showstopper" pens [S6]; Beginner, Luxury, Disposable [S4]; Essentials versus Premium [S17]; and the Japanese trade has a class "premium writing instruments" 高級筆記具 [S7]. Collectors add the luxury end: Montblanc left the mid-market in 1977 and sits "exclusively at the luxury end" [S26].
- **Paper (C5.2): consensus-ranked on top of a measurable spec.** Weight (gsm), size standard and ruling are specs [S21][S20]. Quality is named by consensus: MD Paper and Tomoe River are "the gold standards" for fountain pens [S25]; Rhodia and Clairefontaine are the usual favourites [S5]. "Fountain-pen friendly" is the property JetPens' ink and pen guides tell buyers to look for [S22][S20].
- **Calculators and machines (C5.4): spec-ranked by class and capacity.** Basic < printing < scientific < graphing is a capability ladder [S16][S30]; shredders are compared by cut type (strip, cross-cut, micro-cut) [S16]. Sheet capacity and speed are measurable but were not shown as filters in the fetched pages.
- **Desk accessories and filing (C5.3): preference.** No tier ordering is visible; Ryman's Premium line exists but is a price/style label [S17].

**How it appears as a modifier, never a level:** a `tier` modifier with the values entry / mid / upper / luxury on kinds and named forms, set from the source ladders above (price band is a separate modifier); `nib_material` (steel, 14K gold, 18K gold, other) as an attribute; `paper_weight_gsm` and `paper_stock` as attributes; for calculators and shredders the class and cut type are already the kind or attribute. A ledger row carries the tier with its source.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity for pens, inks, notebooks and planners; platform for cartridge, refill and cover systems; incidental for desk items and most machines.**

- **Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought: JetPens' fountain-pen page has a Brand facet and maker pages for dozens of makers [S4]; Collectors Weekly's second level is the brand [S10]; the Journal Shop and LUIBAN shop by maker (Hobonichi, Midori, Life, Tsubame, Stalogy, MD Paper) [S25][S28]; the collector market prices by maker, era, model number and edition [S26].
- **Platform.** (a) *Cartridge and converter standards*: most European brands use the "standard international" cartridge, others are proprietary, so a converter or cartridge fits some pens and not others [S12]. (b) *Refill and cover systems*: JetPens has an "Accessories for the Hobonichi Techo" collection [S1], sells Hobonichi covers and Kokuyo Jibun Techo index tabs as system parts [S32], and sells TRAVELER'S notebook refills by number and size [S33]. (c) *Paper size* (A5, B6, Semi B5) decides whether a refill fits a cover [S21].
- **Incidental.** Desk items and machines are listed by type in every fetched retailer tree [S14][S15][S16]; no fetched page shows buyers choosing them by maker.

**Maker ledger: yes.** One row per maker or paper/ink house: maker, known-for kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country, era (founding and relevant periods), platform note (cartridge standard, refill system), and the attestation URL. Starting rows with attested facts: Montblanc (Hamburg, German, founded 1906 per [S26]; luxury; Meisterstuck 149 and 146 [S26]); Pelikan (German; 1929 first modern screw piston-filling pen [S12]); Pilot, Platinum, Namiki, Ohto, Pentel (Japan [S23]); Parker, Sheaffer, Waterman (collector brands [S10]); LAMY, TWSBI, Kaweco (entry to mid tier [S27]); Tomoegawa (Tomoe River paper, founded 1914 [S20]); Sonic Co., Osaka (Stalogy [S25]); Midori, Hobonichi, Kokuyo, Maruman, Leuchtturm1917 (planner and notebook lines [S32][S33]). A second small table, a **compatibility ledger**, would record cartridge standard (international or proprietary) and refill-system size per maker and line.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Kinds**: canonical everyday form first, then specialist forms by increasing specialisation. Within Nib pens: fountain pen, then dip pen. Within Calculators: basic, printing, scientific, graphing, financial, then abacus (spec ladder, then historical forms).
- **Named forms**: by maker (alphabetical), then by year of introduction (chronology). Reason: both retailers (JetPens "Newest", Brand facet) and collector sources (PenHero model-year taxonomies, Montblanc eras) lean on maker and date [S2][S4][S35][S26]. Where a model line has colourways or year variants, those are editions (modifier), not further named forms.
- **Reason for canonical-first**: the mixed tier evidence in section 5 gives no single spec to sort kinds, and canonical-first matches how every retailer in section 1.1 lists the commonest type first.
- **Attribute values** are controlled lists: nib size (EF, F, M, B, plus stub, italic, flex, oblique, music) [S12][S19]; filling system (cartridge, converter, piston, vacuum, eyedropper, lever, button, Snorkel and other vintage systems) [S18][S12]; paper size (ISO A, ISO B, JIS B, Semi B5, Letter) [S21]; ruling (blank, lined, dot grid, graph) [S33].

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** Category C5, then four sub-families (writing and marking instruments; paper goods; desk accessories and filing; calculators and office machines) by role at the desk, then leaves by mechanism or form (about 15), then generic kinds (about 110–140), then named forms. **Maximum depth four levels below the category** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Everything else is an attribute, tag, view or ledger:
- *Attributes (controlled lists)*: nib size and shape, nib material, filling system, ink composition, body material, paper size, paper weight, paper stock, ruling, binding, planner layout, dated, cut type, sheet capacity.
- *Tags and modifiers*: tier, price band, era, region, scene, user, setting, market, edition, condition, motive.
- *Ledgers*: maker ledger (identity) and compatibility ledger (cartridge and refill platforms).
- *Views to generate*: Japanese stationery, fountain-pen kit, planner systems, vintage pens by era, luxury and gifting, school, office machines.

**Choice level**: fountain pen, ballpoint, gel, rollerball, wooden and mechanical pencil, notebook, diary/planner and bottled ink are single kinds with attributes; calculators are kinds by capability class; desk items by generic type.

**Owner decisions needed (all recommendations are mine, for approval):**
1. **Dip pens and brush pens.** Keep all pens in C5 by mechanism (simple), or split writing from drawing (comic nibs, brush pens, drawing inks to C4, as P1 proposes)? Recommend the P1 split.
2. **Pencil cases and multi-pen rolls.** C5 (as retailers shelve them) or A4 (step 17, a bag)? Recommend an added note: a pencil case is a desk and stationery item, which would change step 17's reading.
3. **Magazine holders, document trays, bookends.** Confirm P4: desk-top organisers C5, free-standing racks B1.
4. **Hand tools in the office.** Staplers, hole punches, trimmers and tape dispensers C5 (recommended); scissors and craft knives C1 or C5?
5. **Adhesives and tape.** Glue sticks, correction tape, tape dispensers: C5 (recommended) or C8?
6. **Washi tape, stickers, origami and scrapbook paper.** Stickers are named in step 31; washi tape and origami paper sit in paper-craft supplies (C4). Confirm.
7. **Copy paper and printer paper.** C5 paper goods (recommended) or D1 as printer consumables (rule S3)? Printer ink is D1.
8. **Phone-driven label printers, digital pens, reusable smart notebooks.** D1/D2 or C5 (step 31 says "connected or not")?
9. **Calligraphy (shodo) sets, abacus, slide rule.** C5 writing instruments and calculating devices (recommended), or C4/E1/C6?
10. **Greeting cards, gift wrap and postal scale** are named by the framework but no fetched retailer tree shows them; they need attested sources (rule S6) before they enter data.
11. **Notebook systems.** Are Hobonichi Techo and TRAVELER'S notebook named forms of "diary/planner" and "refillable notebook system" (recommended), or kinds of their own?
12. **Tier values.** Approve entry / mid / upper / luxury as the `tier` list for pens and paper, with price band kept separate.
13. **Attribute lists.** Confirm that filling system, nib size and nib material are controlled attributes and not kinds.
