## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C5 Office & Stationery.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 31): *made for writing, paper and desk work: pens, pencils, highlighters, paper goods, notebooks, diaries and planners, calendars, greeting cards, stickers, gift wrap, desk accessories and organisers, letter trays and filing supplies, rulers and postal scales, calculators, office machines whose main function is not printing from or scanning to a computer (copiers, shredders, laminators, label makers, binding machines), connected or not.* Category list line: "writing instruments, paper goods, desk accessories, calculators, office machines."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name C5:**
- Step 31 note: printers, scanners and multifunction printers are D1.
- Step 28 note (C4): "pencils, pens and highlighters made for writing are C5"; inks, pastels, coloured pencils, crayons and markers made for making pictures, sketchbooks and scrapbooking supplies are C4. File by the maker's first-named use: handwriting, lettering, calligraphy and shodo C5; drawing, comics and painting C4; both named, C5 (K-170). A cutting tool, adhesive, stamp or ink pad is filed by the material or work it is made for; office stamps are C5 and decorative stamps C4; washi tape and origami paper are C4; general household scissors are C5 (K-171).
- Step 19 note (F5): blank notebooks, diaries, greeting cards and calendars are C5; blank scrapbook albums are C4; written diaries, letters and picture postcards used or not are F5. Appendix A: being written, drawn or recorded on is not condition; used paper goods become records (step 19).
- Step 23 note (C6): rulers and postal scales are C5; kitchen scales B5, bathroom scales H1, luggage scales A4, building measuring tools C1, sewing tape measures C4. A scale goes with its task (K-167). A compass for navigation is E2; a drawing compass is C5 (C6-O2).
- Step 24 (D1): printers, scanners, cash registers and card readers, point-of-sale terminals, hardware keys are D1; step 24 (D2): electronic organisers, e-readers and tablets are D2. Step 24 closing note (K-158): copy and printer paper, label makers and label printers of any connection, and reusable paper notebooks are C5; an electronic writing tablet is D2. A digital pen that writes in ink on paper and records it is C5; a stylus made for one tablet follows the tablet (C5-O1, S3).
- Step 30 comes before step 31: desks, filing cabinets, shelves, boxes, magazine racks and lap desks are B1 (furniture and storage), lamps are B3, picture frames and decorations are B4. Bookends are B1 (K-191); desk-top trays and holders are C5 and free-standing racks B1 (C-17).
- Step 31 note (K-193): envelopes, padded envelopes and mailing tubes for letters and post are C5; parcel tape, bubble wrap, stretch film, void fill and cardboard boxes sold flat for packing or moving are C8; boxes made for storing things are B1. Step 17 note (K-132): a case made for the small items of one task follows the task, so the pencil case is C5.
- Step 32: scissors-class hand tools are C1; adhesives, fasteners, packing materials are C8. These steps come after step 31, so a paper-and-desk object that fits step 31 stops there.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): ink cartridges, converters, refills, nibs and leads, made to be used up in or fitted to one kind of object, go with the pen or pencil, so they land in C5, in the closing sub-family C5.5 "Parts and accessories made for one object" (convention C5(d)). Covers, refill books and index tabs made for one planner or notebook system, and staples, follow their object in the same way. Cases are parts only when they hold one object at a time. Printer ink is D1.
- Convention C16 and BRIEF: individual items are out; the condition, completeness or edition number of one pen are not stored.
- S5 and Appendix A: a collected pen is a pen; era (vintage), brand, price band and quality tier, material, region, scene, user (a tag from the C6 list), setting (school, office), market (professional), edition (limited) are modifiers.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

All of these are retailer- or maker-run guides; no forum wiki was fetched (gap, section 1.6).
- **Fountain pen buying guides** break the choice into the same few attributes in the same order: filling system (cartridge, converter, piston, vacuum, eyedropper) [S18][S6], nib size and nib shape [S19], nib material and body material [S4][S27], price band [S27], and paper and ink compatibility [S22][S20]. The JetPens filter list for fountain pens carries the same attributes as facets [S4].
- **Nib width is not a standard.** A medium nib can be "an entirely different width" between makers, and Japanese nibs run about one size finer than Western ones [S19][S27]. The JetPens table gives Western EF/F/M/B as about 0.5/0.6/0.75/0.9 mm and Japanese about 0.3/0.45/0.6 mm [S19].
- **Ink** is described by colorant (dye-based or pigment-based), wetness, saturation, shading, sheen and water resistance [S22].
- **Paper** is described by weight (gsm), size standard (ISO 216 A and B series, JIS B, Semi B5), ruling (blank, lined, dot grid, graph), and fountain-pen friendliness [S21][S20][S33]. Tomoe River is 52 gsm, Stalogy 80 gsm, Kokuyo Zarazara 90 gsm [S20][S25].
- **Planners and notebook systems.** Hobonichi Techo, Traveler's Notebook (cover plus refills), Midori MD, Kokuyo Jibun Techo and Stalogy are each sold as a named system with accessories and covers [S25][S32][S28].
- **Japanese stationery stores** are described in three classes: lifestyle stores (Loft, Hands, MUJI), specialist stores (Itoya Ginza, mt lab) and online [S29].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- **Collectors Weekly** splits vintage pens first by **Types** (ballpoint, dip, fountain, gold, sterling), which mixes mechanism and material, then by **Brands** (Aurora, Cross, Montblanc, Montegrappa, OMAS, Parker, Pelikan, Pilot, Sailor, Sheaffer, Wahl Eversharp, Waterman, Visconti), then lists Related items (ink bottles, ink blotters, inkwells, pencils) [S10]. The essay on the page names three main types (dip, fountain, ballpoint) and narrates eras: innovation to the 1960s, then fashion and limited editions [S10].
- **Resale and consignment.** A Toronto consignment dealer's Montblanc guide organises the market by **production era** (pre-war, celluloid, precious resin, 14K to 18K nib around 1991), by **model number** (the 149 has a no. 9 nib, the 146 a no. 6 nib; the numbers encode line, filling mechanism and nib size) and by **limited editions** (Patron of the Arts, Writers Edition); value drivers are condition, completeness and edition number [S26].
- **Pen-cyclopedias** (PenHero) work at maker and model-year grain: Sheaffer model taxonomies by year, Snorkel nib codes, Conklin filling-system eras [S35].
- **Societies:** Pen Collectors of America [S36] and the Writing Equipment Society (UK) [S37] cover "fountain pens and other writing instruments", with reference libraries, repair classes and shows; WES also runs a service to find new homes for collections [S37].
- Collectors Weekly also lists desk and office antiques (typewriters, calculators, slide rules, staplers, paperweights, rulers, wax seals), but that list was seen only in a search excerpt, so it is not counted.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **UNSPSC** puts "Office Equipment, Accessories and Supplies" as segment 44 (family 4412 Office supplies; class 441219 Ink and lead refills) and "Paper Materials and Products" as a different segment in the raw-materials block [S34]. Pens, paper and machines therefore sit in different commodity blocks; UNSPSC divides by commodity type, not by use.
- **ISO 216** (A and B paper series) and **JIS** B sizes are the paper-size standards buyers actually filter by [S21]. JetPens' own size facet adds a coarse Small/Medium/Large beside A4, A5, B5, Semi B5 [S3].
- **Harmonized System heading 9608 "Pens, pencils"** exists (Chapter 96) [S13]; the fetched page gave only the heading title, so the subheading split is not reported. ISO standards for ballpoint and rollerball pens were seen only in a search excerpt.
- **Japanese trade classes** (OfficeJapan) are the finest-grained trade list found: about 150 classes, including premium writing instruments as its own class [S7].

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **Wikipedia, Office supplies** divides by "type of product and general use": blank paper (sizes, thicknesses), containers (binders, envelopes, boxes, desk organisers), writing pads and books (notebooks, composition books, legal pads), writing utensils and corrections [S11].
- **Wikipedia, Fountain pen** divides nibs (round sizes plus stub, italic, oblique, music, flex, hooded, inlaid), filling mechanisms (eyedropper, lever, piston, vacuum, Snorkel, cartridge and converter), cartridge standards (international versus proprietary) and eras [S12]. **List of pen types, brands and companies** lists types and brands A–Z with country templates [S23].
- **Smithsonian "Carbons to Computers"** treats office equipment through desks, filing systems and the typewriter, and compares typewriter holdouts with fountain-pen users [S24]. Japanese Wikipedia files 文房具 (stationery) under 事務用品 (office supplies) with paper as the largest subcategory [S8].

### 1.6 Where sources agree, where they split, and gaps

Agree: pens, pencils, paper goods, desk items, filing and calculators/machines are separate families everywhere; the pen choice is made by mechanism first and by nib, filling and material second; paper is described by size, weight and ruling.
Split: whether tier/style/origin are navigation (Ryman, JetPens) or attributes (Officeworks, OfficeJapan); whether calculators and label makers are "supplies" or "machines"; whether Types mix mechanism and material (Collectors Weekly) or keep mechanism only (Wikipedia, OfficeJapan).
Gaps: no fetched source from China, Korea, France or Italy (strong stationery markets: Korean and Chinese retailers, Clairefontaine/Rhodia, Italian pen houses); no forum wiki (Fountain Pen Network, Reddit); no museum other than the Smithsonian page; calculators and office machines have retail evidence only; amazon.de returned no category tree and one tariff page failed.

**Catalogues to use first in stage 3 (owner rule: existing catalogues before blind search).** The source census for C5 starts from catalogues already seen here: the Wikipedia list of pen types, brands and companies [S23], PenHero's maker and model-year taxonomies [S35], the Collectors Weekly brand pages [S10], the JetPens brand facets and product lists [S2][S4][S32][S33], the OfficeJapan class list [S7] and the society pages [S36][S37]. ## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Short answer: form or mechanism first, then a small fixed set of specs, then the maker or system.** The evidence differs by family.

- **Pens: mechanism, then filling, nib and material, then price.** JetPens' first split under Pens is by writing mechanism (fountain, ballpoint, gel, rollerball, brush, dip, marker, highlighter) [S2]. Inside fountain pens the retailers then offer, in this order: use/tier collections, filling mechanism [S6], and facets for nib size, nib material, body material [S4]. The beginner guides rank the questions the same way: filling system, nib width, material and price band [S27][S18][S19]. Cult Pens' FAQ answers "which nib" with "medium is the safest" and "which paper" with named brands, so for first-time buyers the choice is nib and paper after the pen type [S5].
- **Paper goods: form, then size, then ruling and weight.** JetPens separates Planners, Notebooks, Sticky Notes, Binders, Loose Leaf, Memo Notepads [S3], then offers Binding Type, Paper Size and Sheet Style as the first notebook browse links and Paper Weight as a facet [S33]. Planners add Planner Layout (daily, weekly horizontal and vertical, monthly) and Dated [S32]. Japanese stationery guides put the **paper stock** (Tomoe River, MD, Stalogy) and the **system** (Hobonichi, Traveler's) ahead of the cover [S25][S20].
- **Desk items and machines: function.** Officeworks, Ryman and Staples list them by what they do (stapler, hole punch, trimmer, laminator, shredder, label maker) [S14][S15][S16]. Staples' buying text states the machine function and then the spec (cross-cut or micro-cut versus strip-cut shredders; basic, printing, scientific, graphing calculators) [S16].
- **Calculators: capability class first** (scientific, desktop, financial, graphing) at Officeworks [S30] and (basic, printing, scientific, graphing) at Staples [S16]. The class is the thing a student or accountant buys by.
- **Maker or tier first** only appears as navigation in one retailer's style split (Ryman Essentials / Premium / Fashion / Japanese-inspired) [S17] and in collector sites, where Brands is the second level after Types [S10]. In the collector market the maker is part of the thing sought (section 6).

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: C5 holds the whole category; named forms are models and lines; the modifiers listed in section 0 never become levels.

### Candidate A (decided, C-8): role at the desk, then form family, then kind, then named form

Levels (C5(a): category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; four levels below the category). **The axis of each level, stated per sub-family (C5(c)):**
1. **Sub-family**: the role the object plays in writing and desk work.
   - **C5.1 Writing, marking & correcting instruments**: makes, corrects or maintains marks on a surface. *Leaf axis: the marking or unmarking mechanism* (liquid ink through a nib; viscous or gel ink through a ball; ink through a fibre, felt or brush tip; ink carried from a separate stone or well by a hair brush; solid core; removing, covering or sharpening).
   - **C5.2 Paper goods**: carries marks. *Leaf axis: what the sheets are made to be* (a bound book for notes; a time-ruled record; a loose sheet, a pad or a cover for posting sheets; a printed or decorated sheet). Tie-break: a bound book ruled by date is a diary or planner (designed function: it keeps time).
   - **C5.3 Desk accessories & filing**: holds, organises and handles paper at the desk. *Leaf axis: the job done at the desk* (hold things, display on a board, handle sheets, fasten or stick, file, measure).
   - **C5.4 Calculators & office machines**: calculates or processes paper. *Leaf axis: what the device does* (calculate; act on paper or labels). Calculators order by capability class, the one place where the class is the kind.
   - **C5.5 Parts and accessories made for one object** (C5(d), rule S3; consumables used up in one object, such as ink and refills, are parts under S3; the only sub-family exempt from the level-1 axis). *Leaf axis: the object served.*
2. **Leaf**: the mechanism or form family set by the sub-family's axis.
3. **Kind**: a generic type a person owns (fountain pen, notebook, stapler, shredder).
4. **Named form**: a model or iconic design (Lamy Safari, Hobonichi Techo, Traveler's Notebook). Each carries `maker_bound` (C2a): **yes** for every named form below except *soroban* (**no**, non-maker, tagged region Japan; C11).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Verdict: mixed, with one dominant type per sub-family.**

Rules applied (conventions C4 and C16). (a) A `tier` never sits on a kind. It sits on a maker, a line or a named form, as a tier entry (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`), with a computed `disputed` flag where entries conflict. (b) A measured spec ladder is an attribute with its standard named. (c) A retailer's or platform's own segment labels are a dated `platform_tag`, not a tier. (d) Price band is the domain-wide USD scheme, computed from a dated, sourced list price. (e) A tier scheme enters the data on one fetched source (C4(e), owner one-source rule); a second source or region adds weight (it feeds `disputed`) and is never a condition of entry. Every scheme is registered once with its scale class (C4(c)). (C16) No value is stored for an individual item (condition, completeness, edition number).

- **Pens (C5.1): consensus-ranked at best.** No measurable spec sets the order, and the price ladder is not a tier.
  - JetPens' budget guide steps through under 20 USD (Platinum Preppy, Pilot Kakuno, Kaweco Perkeo), 20–50 (LAMY safari, TWSBI ECO), 50–100, 100–200, and 200+ (Sailor Pro Gear Slim) [S27]. That is a retailer's price guide. It becomes a price band on each named form, computed from a dated list price (C4d), and not a tier.
  - Cult Pens ties steel nibs to pens below about 100 GBP and gold nibs above, then says "don't get hung up on it" [S5]. Nib material is an attribute (`nib_material`; C4b).
  - Retailer collections name rungs: Beginner, Luxury, Disposable [S4]; entry-level gold-nib picks and "Showstopper" [S6]; Essentials versus Premium [S17]. These are `platform_tag` values (C4c). The Japanese trade class "premium writing instruments" 高級筆記具 [S7] is a trade-list class, recorded the same way.
  - The one maker-level position in the evidence: Montblanc left the mid-market in 1977 and sits "exclusively at the luxury end" [S26]. It is a tier entry on the maker (scheme: market position, scale class (iii) editorial or community; one collector guide admits it, C4(e)); a second source adds weight only.
  - Decided (C-9, Q13c): a pen tier only where a non-price source exists (a maker's own line position, a consensus list, or a spec class). Otherwise a pen carries a price band and attributes only. The earlier entry / mid / upper / luxury list stays withdrawn because it was read off price. Tier entries are per source with the computed `disputed` flag (CV-10); none sits on a kind.
- **Paper (C5.2): consensus-ranked on top of a measurable spec.** Weight (gsm), size standard and ruling are attributes [S21][S20]. Consensus: MD Paper and Tomoe River are "the gold standards" for fountain pens [S25], recordable as tier entries on those named forms (scheme: fountain-pen paper consensus, scale class (iii); one UK retailer source admits each entry, C4(e)); Rhodia and Clairefontaine are the usual favourites [S5] and enter the same way on their own sources. A second source adds weight only. "Fountain-pen friendly" is the property JetPens' ink and pen guides tell buyers to look for [S22][S20]; it is kept as a note on the named form with its source, not as a measured attribute.
- **Calculators and machines (C5.4): spec-ranked by class and capacity.** Basic < printing < scientific < graphing is a capability ladder [S16][S30]; the class is the kind, so no tier entry is needed. Shredders are compared by cut type (strip, cross-cut, micro-cut), an attribute [S16]. Sheet capacity and speed are measurable but were not shown as filters in the fetched pages.
- **Desk accessories and filing (C5.3): preference.** No tier ordering is visible; Ryman's Premium line is a platform_tag [S17].

**How it appears, never as a level and never on a kind:** price band (C4d, scale class (v)) and `platform_tag` (class (vi)) on named forms; tier entries on makers, lines and named forms only where one fetched source meets (e) and a non-price source exists for a pen (C-9); `nib_material`, `paper_weight_gsm`, `paper_stock` and cut type as attributes. A ledger row carries each tier entry with its source.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity for pens, inks, notebooks and planners; platform for cartridge, refill and cover systems; incidental for desk items and most machines.**

- **Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought: JetPens' fountain-pen page has a Brand facet and maker pages for dozens of makers [S4]; Collectors Weekly's second level is the brand [S10]; the Journal Shop and LUIBAN shop by maker (Hobonichi, Midori, Life, Tsubame, Stalogy, MD Paper) [S25][S28]; the collector market prices by maker, era, model number and edition [S26].
- **Platform.** (a) *Cartridge and converter standards*: most European brands use the "standard international" cartridge, others are proprietary, so a converter or cartridge fits some pens and not others [S12]. (b) *Refill and cover systems*: JetPens has an "Accessories for the Hobonichi Techo" collection [S1], sells Hobonichi covers and Kokuyo Jibun Techo index tabs as system parts [S32], and sells TRAVELER'S notebook refills by number and size [S33]. (c) *Paper size* (A5, B6, Semi B5) decides whether a refill fits a cover [S21].
- **Incidental.** Desk items and machines are listed by type in every fetched retailer tree [S14][S15][S16]; no fetched page shows buyers choosing them by maker.

**Maker ledger: one domain-wide ledger, CONVENTIONS_v1 C1 schema (CV-1, CV-2).** C5 makers are rows of the one ledger, never kinds. Core columns: `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class` (multi-valued), `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `licensor_id`, `authority_id`, `scene_tags` (via the C8 tag table), `price_band`, `sources`. `known_for_kinds` is not stored; it is derived from the named-form ledger. Eras are carried by `founded`, `ended` and `status`. Platforms are not maker columns: they go to the compatibility ledger. Tier is not a maker column: it is a tier entry (section 5). Lines and collections are rows of the lines table with `line_rank` where the maker orders its lines (Montblanc Meisterstuck; a group such as Pilot Iroshizuku or Kokuyo Campus); a line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (C2(d)). The maker-relations table records OEM, supplier and licence links, and `composed by` is unused in C5; a relation enters on an attested page only. The school table is standing (CV-3); no school row is attested in the fetched C5 pages, so none is seeded, and a tradition (for example a calligraphy school) enters when a fetched page names it. `maker_class` values come from the C1 list (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; artisan or atelier for pen makers and turners; supplier for a paper maker such as Tomoegawa; retail own-label for stationers' own brands); they are not yet assigned and need each maker's fetched page †.

**Admission and growth (CV-5; owner depth-and-breadth and one-source rules).** One fetched page showing that a maker makes or sold pens, inks, paper goods, desk items or machines admits the maker; there is no cap. The ledger is seeded from the existing catalogues named in section 1.6 (the Wikipedia pen list [S23], PenHero [S35], Collectors Weekly [S10], the JetPens brand facets [S4][S33]) and grows with the harvest. Identity makers (pens, inks, notebooks, planners) come first only as a harvest order. Pen houses, pen-makers, ink makers, paper mills and notebook houses are recorded here, not as extra kinds.

Starting rows with attested facts: Montblanc (Hamburg, German, founded 1906 per [S26]; Meisterstuck 149 and 146 [S26]); Pelikan (German; 1929 first modern screw piston-filling pen [S12]); Pilot, Platinum, Namiki, Ohto, Pentel (Japan [S23]); Parker, Sheaffer, Waterman (collector brands [S10]); LAMY, TWSBI, Kaweco (named in the entry and mid-price guide [S27]); Tomoegawa (Tomoe River paper, founded 1914 [S20]); Sonic Co., Osaka (Stalogy [S25]); Midori, Hobonichi, Kokuyo, Maruman, Leuchtturm1917 (planner and notebook lines [S32][S33]). These are the first rows, not a limit.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Kinds** (convention C7): canonical everyday form first, then the secondary key that each sub-family states. C5.1: increasing specialisation (fountain pen, then dip pen; wooden pencil, then mechanical pencil). C5.2: simplest binding or form first (notebook, composition book, refillable notebook system). C5.3: commonest desk use first. C5.4: spec ladder, then historical forms (basic, printing, scientific, graphing, financial, then abacus). C5.5: the parts of the commonest object served first (fountain pens, then ball and gel pens, then pencils).
- **Named forms** (convention C7, amended by CV-21): non-maker named forms first (soroban), by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms, by maker (in ledger `name` order), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested. Reason: both retailers (JetPens "Newest", Brand facet) and collector sources (PenHero model-year taxonomies, Montblanc eras) lean on maker and date [S2][S4][S35][S26]. **Variants** (colourways, year versions, limited editions) go by `variant_code` or year; they are never further named forms (C3). Maker ladders (`line_rank`), newest-first and benchmark orders are generated views, not stored order.
- **Reason for canonical-first**: the mixed tier evidence in section 5 gives no single spec to sort kinds, and canonical-first matches how every retailer in section 1.1 lists the commonest type first.
- **Attribute values** are controlled lists: nib size (EF, F, M, B, plus stub, italic, flex, oblique, music) [S12][S19]; filling system (cartridge, converter, piston, vacuum, eyedropper, lever, button, Snorkel and other vintage systems) [S18][S12]; `paper_size` (ISO A, ISO B, JIS B, Semi B5, Letter) with its standard named (ISO 216, JIS) [S21], the only form in which size is stored (C6); ruling (blank, lined, dot grid, graph) [S33]; `drive` (manual, electric) where a kind has both versions (sharpener, stapler, label maker, typewriter); `connection` (standalone, computer, phone) on the label maker; `recording` (yes or no) on ballpoint and rollerball pens, for the digital pen; `reusable` (yes or no) on the notebook; `standard_met` where a maker names a design or voluntary standard (none attested yet †).
- **Alias and homonym table** (convention C9, decision CV-23): columns `term`, `language`, `script`, `target_id`, `relation`, `sources`; labels in English with the native-script label. ## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure.** Candidate A is decided (C-8): C5 Office & Stationery is divided by the role the object plays in writing and desk work into five sub-families (C5.1 Writing, marking & correcting instruments; C5.2 Paper goods; C5.3 Desk accessories & filing; C5.4 Calculators & office machines; C5.5 Parts and accessories made for one object, the closing parts sub-family of C5(d)), each with its leaf axis stated in section 3 (mechanism or form for C5.1 and C5.2, the job done at the desk for C5.3, what the device does for C5.4, the object served for C5.5), then 24 leaves in the worked tree, then generic kinds (87 in the worked tree, about 110–140 expected at first harvest, not a cap), then named forms, at most four levels below the category with the named form counted. Choice level: fountain pen, ballpoint, gel, rollerball, wooden and mechanical pencil, notebook, diary / planner and bottled ink are single kinds with attributes (filling system, nib size, shape and material, ink composition, body material, `paper_size` with its standard, paper weight and stock, ruling, binding, planner layout, dated, format, `drive`, `connection`, `recording`, `reusable`, cut type, sheet capacity); calculators are kinds by capability class; desk items by generic type; Hobonichi Techo and TRAVELER's notebook are maker-bound named forms. Pens, nibs, brushes and inks are filed by the maker's first-named use (K-170), so a drawing-only brush pen is C4; scissors, glue sticks, desk tape, office stamps and ink pads, pencil cases, envelopes and mailing tubes, copy paper, label printers of any connection and digital pens are C5; bookends and free-standing racks are B1, washi tape and decorative stamps C4, printers D1. Everything else is a tag, view or ledger: price band (C4(d)) and `platform_tag`; tier entries only on makers, lines and named forms and, for pens, only with a non-price source (C-9); the one maker ledger with its lines, relations and standing school tables, uncapped and seeded from the existing catalogues; the C13 compatibility ledger; the C9 alias table; the variant ledger, filled wherever collectors compare variants; proposed scene tags (Japanese stationery, fountain-pen hobby, vintage pen collecting, planner and journaling, calligraphy, typewriter and office antiques), each held until its definition source is fetched; generated views for Japanese stationery, the fountain-pen kit, planner systems, vintage pens by era, luxury and gifting, school and office machines.

**Remaining risks.**
1. *Key conflict (S3b, K-9) against step 31:* the S3b holder sentence says an album, binder, stock book, box or tray made to hold several objects is B1 when empty, while step 31 names "letter trays and filing supplies" as C5. The memo keeps ring binders, lever arch files, box files, letter trays and desk-top holders in C5 (step 31, C-17) and reads the S3b sentence as covering albums and collection holders. No decision settles this; the key should say so (section 12).
2. *Products in two categories:* brush pen, dip pen, nib and fineliner have products in C5 and in C4, set by each maker's first-named use (K-170); a placer needs the maker's own page, and a maker that names no use is unplaced until one is found.
3. *Universal stylus:* a stylus made for one tablet follows the tablet (C5-O1, S3), but no decision places a universal capacitive stylus, which JetPens lists as a pen type [S2]; it is left unplaced in C5 (section 12).
4. *Level provisional until attested (C11, CV-27):* sticky-note calendar (himekuri) and name seal (hanko) are kinds named generically with the Japanese word as an alias; if no fetched seller in two regions gives each its own category, or a generic kind (calendar, office stamp) covers its form, each becomes a non-maker named form under that kind. It stays in, in the same leaf.
5. *Attribute against kind (provisional until attested):* the digital pen (`recording`) and the reusable notebook (`reusable`) are attributes under C10; each becomes a kind if a fetched comparison source ranks it apart from the ordinary pen or notebook.
6. *Evidence gaps:* no fetched source from China, Korea, France or Italy; no forum wiki; calculators and office machines rest on retail evidence only; the pen choice level rests on twelve JetPens pages plus Goulet, Cult Pens and one resale guide (review F26). None changes a home.
7. *C13 values and kind names:* "cartridge standard" and "refill and cover system" are not in C13's listed `platform_type` values (the list says "include"); the conventions file should list them at its next revision. C5.5 now carries the convention's name "Parts and accessories made for one object", and consumables used up in one object (ink, refills, lead) are parts under S3.
8. *Scene tags wait on definition sources.* All six proposed tags are held back until each has one (C8).

**Attestation list (†, attested in the stage-3 harvest; no separate sourcing pass; one fetched page showing the item is or was made or sold admits it).**
- Kinds named only by the framework key, a decision or a class label: greeting card, gift wrap, postal scale; correction tape and correction fluid (only the family "correction" is shown); whiteboard (only the class "boards"); paper clip, bulldog clip, drawing pin (only the family "Pins/Clips"); glue stick; desk tape; staple; single-pen sleeve; calligraphy brush, ink stick, inkstone and calligraphy set (only the class 書道用品 is shown); copy and printer paper; padded envelope; mailing tube; slide rule; label tape for label makers.
- Pencil lead grades and lead diameters (not attested in a fetched page).
- The owner or standard of the "standard international" cartridge (CP-INT), and region, introduction year and status of every platform row.
- A definition source for each proposed scene tag (convention C8).
- `maker_class` for every starting maker row.
- Source census and gaps (review F26): one non-US pen retailer with a facet list and one community wiki or forum guide (Fountain Pen Network, Reddit) to test the choice level; calculator and typewriter catalogues; China, Korea, France and Italy; the structure need not change.
- Each brush pen's first-named use, from maker pages, product by product (K-170).
- Decisions provisional until attested: the kind level of himekuri and hanko (C11); the digital pen and the reusable notebook as attributes (C10).
