# D3 Audio & Video — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md. Owner emphasis for D3: TVs (panel technologies), projectors, hi-fi components, turntables, headphones and in-ear monitors (audiophile tier lists and measurement sites), speakers and smart speakers, with spec-ranked versus consensus-ranked tiers kept off the tree. Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_D3_sources.tsv (35 rows); the short keys S01 to S35 below point to them, in that order (full URLs in the key table at the end of section 1). Call count: 12 searches, 36 fetches (one fetch, Best Buy, returned only a country splash and is logged in verify/BLOCKED_HOSTS.txt). Evidence comes from retailers and price sites in the US, UK, Germany/Switzerland, Japan, Korea and China, from enthusiast ranking and measurement sites, from magazines, from a trade classification (HTS), from reference works and from a museum catalogue.*

**One-line result.** Divide D3 by the role a device plays in a picture-and-sound setup (seven sub-families, in signal order), then by device type as shops name it (leaf), then by the one physical or technological class buyers actually compare inside that type (kind), then by model or series (named form). Keep panel marketing names, throw ratio details, driver technology, drive type, connectivity and price as attributes; keep quality in a tier ledger with a stated scale and source; keep makers in a maker ledger with an ecosystem column.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D3 Audio & Video (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, third branch).** "A **TV, projector, hi-fi, record player, radio, clock radio, headphones or earbuds, speakers for listening, smart speaker or display, voice assistant, smart-home hub, karaoke machine**." Category line: "TVs, projectors, hi-fi, record players, radios, headphones, speakers for listening, smart speakers and hubs."

**Rules and notes that already touch D3.**
- Step 8 and S3 (parts follow the whole): a part, attachment or accessory made for one kind of object goes to that object's category, and step 8 is tried before step 24. So a turntable cartridge, headphone ear pads, a projector screen made for projectors, or a TV remote go to D3 with the object. Cases and stands made to hold one object at a time are parts; furniture, racks and shelves that hold several objects are B1 (a TV cabinet is B1).
- Step 22 note (E3): "Headphones, turntables and speakers are D3 unless sold as studio, stage or DJ equipment. Karaoke machines are D3." Studio, stage and PA speakers, studio headphones, DJ turntables and controllers, mixers, audio interfaces and music microphones are E3.
- Step 24 first branch (D1): "computer speakers are D3"; monitors (screens for computers) are D1; microphones made for speech, calls or streaming are D1; cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices (audio, HDMI, USB) are D1, "whatever device they serve".
- Step 16 note (A5): "Smart displays and clock radios are D3." Sunrise lamps are B3; radio-controlled clocks are A5.
- Step 24 closing note: a connected version of a household object (smart lock, thermostat, bulb, plug, camera, robot vacuum) is filed by its task at step 30; connectivity is a modifier. Baby monitors are B8.
- Step 5 note: software built into a device is part of the device; a separately paid licence, unlock or subscription is I1. Subscriptions to music, video and audiobook catalogues are I3.
- Step 21 (F1): records, CDs, tapes, films and discs are carriers (F1); blank recording media are F1. A record player is D3, a record is F1.
- Step 2 (H2): hearing aids are H2. Step 17 (A1): ear defenders and other hearing protection worn against a hazard are A1.
- Appendix A modifiers that bite here: brand and maker, price band and quality tier, era (vintage, retro), setting (indoor, outdoor, travel), market (domestic, commercial, professional), installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted), power source and technology, connectivity (smart, connected), user, motive.

**Gaps in the key that this research hit (details in section 9).** Portable music players (digital audio players) are named in neither D2 nor D3. Disc players, streaming and set-top boxes, DACs, headphone amplifiers and projection screens are not named in D3 (they are covered only by "hi-fi", by "TV" or by S3). Gaming headsets, Bluetooth call headsets, hearing-assist earbuds and TV mounts have no explicit home.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

All claims cite the fetched pages by key. "Top two or three levels" are given where the page shows them; several shops publish only a flat list of children.

**Where the retail trees agree.** Seven or eight device types recur in every region: televisions, projectors, headphones/earphones, speakers, amplifiers/receivers, turntables/record players, disc and streaming players, radios, and all-in-one systems (S01, S02, S12, S13, S14, S26). The type is the top node of a shop category almost everywhere.

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- Soundbars sit under Home cinema (Currys S13), under TV Sound Solutions (Crutchfield S01) or have an entry of their own beside HIFI/AV speakers (Danawa S14).
- AV receivers: HiFi-Regler files them under TV & Video (S26, path tv-video/av-receiver), Danawa gives them an entry of their own (S14), Crutchfield groups receivers and amplifiers together (S01).
- Headphone amplifiers and DACs: Crutchfield links headphone amps from its Headphones department (S28) while DACs sit in Home audio (S01); Danawa pairs them as "DAC/amp" (S14); Stereophile rates them inside the headphone section (S29); ASR gives them device types of their own (S08).
- The digital audio player is a PC product at Kakaku (S06); MediaMarkt has a "Portable audio" child (S02); Danawa has an "audio/player" entry in sound appliances (S14).
- Headphone categories are multi-axis lists: Crutchfield's thirteen children mix feature (noise-canceling), connection (wired, wireless, true wireless), use (sports, studio, gaming) and form (on-ear, over-the-ear) (S28), as do Yodobashi's (S31).
- The Currys soundbar "Type" facet puts channel layout (2.1, 3.1, 5.1), connection (Bluetooth, wireless, wired), mounting (wall mounted), format (Dolby Atmos) and bundle (with subwoofer) in one list, so one soundbar appears under many entries (S13). This is the clearest evidence that these are attributes, not kinds.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, wikis and glossaries

- **r/headphones wiki (S11).** Backing (open, closed) first, then form factors (circumaural, supra-aural, universal IEM, custom IEM), then transducer technology (planar magnetic and others), each with example models (Grado SR60i, Sennheiser HD600, Audeze LCD2; Audio-Technica M50, Fostex TH900; Bowers & Wilkins P5, Koss Porta Pro; UERM, JH Audio JH13).
- **Wikipedia Headphones (S10).** Form factor (circumaural, supra-aural, earbud, in-ear) first; then open/semi-open/closed back; then wired versus wireless and true wireless stereo; then transducer types (moving-coil, electrostatic, electret, planar magnetic, balanced armature, others).
- **Zhihu, China (S15).** An electro-acoustics professional divides first by wearing style (in-ear, semi-in-ear, flat-head earbud, ear-hook, over-ear and on-ear, bone conduction), then by driver principle (dynamic, balanced armature, hybrid "圈铁", electrostatic, planar, electret), then by enclosure (closed, open, semi-open). The same three axes as the English sources in a different order of emphasis; it also notes that one generic mould is reused across brands (Sennheiser MX375 style earbuds).
- **Audio Science Review (S08).** Its equipment list uses a device-type vocabulary for electronics: ADC/Soundcard, CD Player, DAC (Dedicated), Headphone Amp (Dongle), Headphone AMP, DAC+Headphone AMP, Power AMP, Amplifier, AVR/AVP (Pre Pro); each row carries one measured number (SINAD) and a Recommended yes/no.
- **Wikipedia High fidelity (S19).** Separates (preamplifier, power amplifier, integrated amplifier = both, receiver = with tuner, monoblock) versus integrated "mini", "midi", "lifestyle" or music-centre systems; audiophiles prefer separates, often each from a different maker.
- **HiFi.de (S25).** Topics HiFi Elektronik (Vollverstaerker, Endstufe, Plattenspieler, Streaming-Verstaerker), AV-Receiver, Gaming; an editorial note that integrated devices are the trend and separates are "the next level".

### 1.3 Reviewers, magazines and rankers (tier sources)

- **Stereophile Recommended Components (S03, S29, S34).** Category pages (digital processors, preamplifiers, power amplifiers, integrated amplifiers, loudspeakers, subwoofers, headphones and accessories) each carry Class A, B, C, D; A+ exists for hi-res digital, turntables, phono preamps and headphones. Loudspeakers are sub-grouped inside a class by bass reach: A (Full Range), A (Restricted Extreme LF), B (Full Range), B (Restricted LF), C (Restricted LF). The class "is based on performance, including on the test bench", prices "are not explicitly taken into account", and a $$$ mark flags value.
- **Crinacle IEM list (S04).** Rank S- to F, with a separate Tone Grade and Technical Grade, a value-star column, price, signature, and a driver-setup string (for example "2EST 4BA 1DD"). It is a named individual's list with a notes-and-disclaimers header.
- **RTINGS (S05, S32).** Measured test benches. TV family tree: two roots, LED(LCD) with VA and IPS(ADS), and OLED with WOLED and QD-OLED; Mini LED and QLED are described as marketing terms, with maker names (Samsung QLED/Neo QLED, LG QNED, Sony Triluminos, Hisense ULED, TCL QLED) mapped to wide-gamut technology. Projector picks carry Projector Type (Standard, Short, Ultra Short Throw), Native Resolution (1080p, pixel-shift 4K) and Light Source (laser, LED), and are grouped by price and use.
- **Hifiklubben (S27).** TV screen types as buying choices (LED, QLED, Neo QLED, Mini-LED, OLED, QD-OLED, MicroLED) each with an experience-level word from "Basis" to "Absolute Spitzenklasse".

### 1.4 Collectors, museums and the resale side

- **Science Museum Group (S20).** Object types radiograms, radios, record players, gramophones and radiogramophones sit under category "Sound Reproduction"; maker (Braun, Decca), place and date are facets, not tree levels (Braun Atelier 11 by Dieter Rams, Decca Decola).
- **Resale and auction hosts.** No resale or auction page was fetched in this batch (call cap), so the collector view of hi-fi comes from the magazine and enthusiast sources above; a vintage-audio and resale pass is left open.

### 1.5 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Harmonized System / US HTS (S09).** Heading 8518 holds loudspeakers (single in an enclosure, multiple in one enclosure), headphones and earphones, audio-frequency amplifiers and sound amplifier sets; 8519 record players (with or without loudspeaker); 8521 video players and recorders; 8522 pickup cartridges as parts; 8527 radio receivers; 8528 monitors, projectors and television receivers. So the trade view divides by function (sound out, record playing, video, radio, display) and puts microphones with loudspeakers in one heading.
- **NAICS 334310-11 via siccode.com (S33).** Turntables, CD players, amplifiers, speakers, headphones, microphones, mixers, audio interfaces and studio monitors in one industry, with no consumer/professional split. Weak source (secondary aggregator).
- **IEC 60268 (sound system equipment)** was found by search (it covers measurement methods for the whole field) but not fetched, so it is only a lead for anchoring a spec-ranked ladder (open item).

### 1.6 Agreement and disagreement in one view

Agree: the function-first device type is the common top node; headphones split by form first; separates sit beside integrated systems; TVs split by display technology; projectors split by throw geometry and light source; the tier comes from a ranking source, never from the tree.

Disagree: where to put soundbars, AV receivers, DACs and headphone amplifiers, DAPs and smart speakers; whether wireless is a type or an attribute; whether "studio" or "gaming" is a category; whether installation forms (in-wall, outdoor) are kinds.
