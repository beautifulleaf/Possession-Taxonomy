## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B8 Home Safety & Security, in domain B Home.

**Membership test (placement key step 30, fourth branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** made to **secure or protect** a home, its people and its contents, to hold doors and windows, or to keep pests out: locks and safes for any contents (locks made for a bicycle or vehicle are G1 under S3), smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms, fire extinguishers, security cameras, doorbells and alarm systems, baby monitors, stair gates and child-safety locks, letterboxes and parcel boxes, door stops, pest traps, swatters, zappers, poisons and repellents, mosquito nets and coil holders. Branch B8 is tried after B5, B6 and B7 and before B3, B1, B2 and B4.

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch B8:**
- S3: locks made for a bicycle or vehicle are G1; safes are never "parts" (so a safe is B8 and not B1, "whatever they are made to hold").
- S3: cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8, whatever they are used in.
- Step 10 note: tools made to kill pests are B8; slaughter and stunning tools for livestock are C7; pepper spray, stun guns and tasers are E4.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object (smart lock, alarm, camera, doorbell) is filed by its task at step 30, so it is B8; connectivity is a modifier. Baby monitors are B8.
- Step 32 (C8): hinges and fittings, plumbing and electrical supplies, fencing and windows and doors are building hardware, tried after B8.
- Step 30 (B1): cots, high chairs and playpens, lockers and cabinets are furniture and storage; B1 is tried after B8.
- Step 2 (H2): first-aid kits and monitors of an illness or pregnancy are H2.
- Step 5 (I1, I3): software, accounts and subscriptions are I; a cloud video plan is a subscription.

### 1.4 Standards bodies, certifiers and trade classifications
- **Safe standards (S9, S28, S37, S31).** EN 1143-1 resistance grades 0 to XIII (safes grade 0 to X), each about 50% stronger than the last, with a required EN 1300 lock class (A to D); EN 14450 S1 and S2 for lower-risk "security cabinets"; EN 1047-1 and EN 15659 for fire; UL 687 TL-15, TL-30, TL-30x6, TRTL-30x6, TRTL-60x6, TXTL-60x6 for burglary and UL fire classes (350 with 0.5 to 4 hours). China grades anti-theft safes A1, A2, B1, B2, B3, C (A1: 15 minutes; C: 60 minutes with explosives, at least 450 kg). Belgian dealer pairs each grade with an indicative insurable cash value (S9).
- **Lock and cylinder standards (S8, S24, S25, S23, S32).** US ANSI/BHMA Grades 1 to 3 (cycles, strikes, weight) with BHMA letter grades AAA, AA, A (S8). UK BS 3621 for mortice deadlocks and night latches on timber doors; TS 007 stars (3-star cylinder, or 1-star cylinder with a 2-star handle) and Sold Secure SS 312 Diamond for euro cylinders on uPVC and composite doors (S24). Padlocks: CEN grades 1 to 6 versus Sold Secure Bronze, Silver, Gold, Diamond, with an approximate equivalence CEN 3 to 6 (S25). Sold Secure rates by product category: Domestic Safes, Key Safes, Safes EN 14450 S2, Lock Cylinders, Padlocks & Fittings, Smart Locks (maximum Gold), Firearms Cabinets, Ground Anchors, Security Chains, Audible Accessory Alarms, Personal Attack Alarms, Domestic Buildings, plus cycle, motorcycle and caravan categories (S23). China's mandatory GB 21556.2-2025 (from 1 March 2026) rates whole locks A/B/C with C needing 10 minutes of technical-opening resistance and 100,000 cycles, and adds anti-electromagnetic, local-encryption and 3D-liveness requirements for smart locks (S32; consumer-media source).
- **Domestic fire detection (S10, S11).** BS 5839-6:2019 describes a home system by two independent codes: Grade (A, C, D1, D2, F1, F2, the power and control engineering) and Category (LD1 to LD3 for life protection, PD1 and PD2 for property protection, the coverage). Products are marked to BS EN 14604. Extinguishers are identified by agent type, fire class (A B C D K in UL/NFPA; A B C F in UK) and a numeric rating (S15, S34).
- **Trade classifications (S19, S20, S21).** The baby-proofing market is segmented into Safety Gates (28.4%), Cabinet & Drawer Locks (18.7%), Corner & Edge Guards (14.2%), Outlet Covers (11.8%), Door Locks & Stops (10.6%), Window Guards (8.5%), Others (stove knob covers, furniture straps, toilet locks, pool barriers) (S19). A UK pest-control supplier that belongs to the BPCA divides by target animal first: Bird, Rodent, Insect, Animal; and within rodent: traps, poison, bait stations, proofing, repellers; within insect: public-use versus professional-use insecticides, traps, electric fly killers, repellers, insect screens (S20). The Japanese reference page divides insecticides by formulation (aerosol, oil, emulsion, powder, bait, fumigant, vaporiser) and lists household kinds as aerosol, mosquito coil, electric mosquito vaporiser (liquid and mat), bait, trap, with market shares by maker (Earth about 40%, Kincho about 25%, Fumakilla about 15%) (S21).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split
- **Agree:** locks, safes, alarms and cameras are separate object groups; fire safety (extinguishers, blankets, smoke and CO alarms), child safety and pest control are each their own group in every region fetched (Safelincs, OBI, Home Depot Canada, IKEA, PestFix, Ace, Japanese guides). Mechanism (pin tumbler and so on) is an attribute, not a type, in enthusiast sources (S7, S38).
- **Split on alarms:** Toolstation and OBI group burglar and fire alarms differently; Screwfix may put fire alarms in Safety & Workwear and burglar alarms in Electrical (unverified, search excerpt only) (S3, S26). Secom treats the fire alarm as a home-security product (S12).
- **Split on child safety:** a lock for a cabinet is a "child safety lock" at OBI, IKEA and Home Depot Canada (S26, S18, S6), but a "cabinet lock" or "latch" at general hardware stores; Wikipedia files "Child safety lock" among locks (S38).
- **Split on pests:** supplier trees go pest-first (S20); consumer sources go product form first (S21, S22). Ace carries both as facets (Product Form and Pest Type).
- **Split on safes:** German and Belgian sources lead with the resistance grade (S28, S9); UK and US consumer sources lead with size and protection against (S4, S5); Sold Secure rates domestic safes by Bronze to Gold (S23).
- **Split on security lights and fencing:** Screwfix lists Security Lights and Garage Security in Security (S1), Japan lists fence guards and gravel (S14); the framework files lights in B3 and gravel and fencing elsewhere (see section 9).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Buyers first choose the hazard they are guarding against, then the kind of device.** Evidence:
- Every retailer fetched has separate entry points for "Security" (break-in), fire safety, child safety and pest control, and only inside "Security" does it show the object types (padlocks, safes, door locks, CCTV, alarms). OBI puts Kindersicherheit and Brandschutz as siblings of Tresore and Alarmanlagen (S26); Home Depot Canada puts Child Safety beside Safes and Padlocks (S6); Safelincs and Secom run fire safety on its own (S33, S12); pest suppliers and Ace run pest control on its own (S20, S22).
- Inside break-in protection the first visible choice is the object form: Screwfix, Toolstation, OBI and Safe.co.uk all list padlocks, door locks, safes, cameras, alarms as peers (S1, S3, S26, S4).
- Inside each form the second choice is the **protection grade the buyer needs**, set by their insurer: UK buyers are told to match BS 3621, TS 007 3-star, CEN 3 or Sold Secure Bronze to Diamond to the value of contents (S24, S25); German and Belgian buyers match EN 1143-1 grade to insured cash value (S9, S28); US buyers read ANSI Grade 2 as the minimum for exterior doors (S8). Home Depot's safe facets lead with "Protection against" and "Lock type" before size (S5).
- For fire, alarm and extinguisher buyers choose a **type by sensing or agent** (optical versus heat; water mist versus CO2 versus wet chemical) and then a **rating or grade** (S11, S15, S34, S10).
- For child safety the first question is where the hazard is (stairs, cabinets, sockets, corners, windows): CR's gate guide leads with placement (top of stairs needs a hardware-mounted gate) (S16); the market report segments by hazard site (S19).
- For pests consumers go by pest and product form together: Ace's two facets are Product Form and Pest Type (S22); Cainz and the Japanese reference go by deterrence principle or formulation (S13, S21).

**Therefore:** the first axis is "what the object defends against" (intruders and theft, fire and gas, harm to young children, pests); the second is "what kind of device it is"; the third is "the form at which grade or maker is compared". Material, mechanism, maker, price and setting come after.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework limit of four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form).

### Candidate A (recommended): hazard, then device type
- **Level 1, sub-family, axis: the hazard the object defends against.** B8.1 Break-in & theft; B8.2 Fire, smoke & gas; B8.3 Young-child hazards; B8.4 Pests.
- **Level 2, leaf, axis: the device's job (what kind of device it is).** Under B8.1: Door locks; Window & door restraints; Padlocks, chains & hasps; Safes & secure storage; Intruder alarms & sensors; Security cameras & video doorbells; Mail & parcel boxes. Under B8.2: Smoke, heat & gas alarms; Fire extinguishers; Fire blankets. Under B8.3: Gates & barriers; Latches & locks for cabinets and appliances; Edge, socket & knob guards; Window & door guards; Baby monitors. Under B8.4: Traps; Baits & poisons; Sprays, foggers & vaporisers; Repellent devices; Zappers & swatters; Nets & screens.
- **Level 3, kind, axis: the form at which buyers compare grade or maker** (section 4b). **Level 4, named form: a model, series or iconic design.**
- 10 to 15 example kinds: mortice deadlock, night latch, euro cylinder, multipoint lock, smart door lock (B8.1 door locks); open-shackle padlock, closed-shackle padlock (padlocks); home safe, burglary-resistant safe, key safe (safes); fixed security camera, video doorbell (cameras); smoke alarm, heat alarm, CO alarm, water-mist extinguisher, wet-chemical extinguisher (B8.2); hardware-mounted gate, pressure-mounted gate (B8.3); snap mouse trap, mosquito coil, bed mosquito net (B8.4).
- **Where it breaks:** (1) a fire-resistant document chest protects against fire but is a safe, so it sits in B8.1 Safes with fire rating as a tier tag, not in B8.2; (2) a cabinet latch is a lock for child hazards, so the leaf (latch) decides, not the word "lock"; (3) a baby monitor watches a child and is a camera-like device, so it sits under B8.3 even though it is functionally a camera; (4) a door stop prevents damage and finger-trapping (IKEA files it under child safety, S18) but the framework lists it with security; it has no hazard home (section 10).

### Candidate B: device type first, flat (retailer style)
- **Level 1 axis: kind of object.** Locks & latches; Safes & secure storage; Alarms & detectors; Cameras & doorbells; Fire-fighting equipment; Child-safety products; Pest-control products; Mail & door furniture. (This is how Screwfix, Toolstation and OBI organise their Security departments, S1, S3, S26.)
- **Level 2: leaf by form; level 3: kind; level 4: named form.** Eight sub-families instead of four, no extra level.
- 10 to 15 example kinds: as in A.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) the axis is mixed: five sub-families are named for what the object is, two for who it protects (child) and what it kills (pest), so "child safety lock" has two homes; (2) smoke alarms and burglar alarms share "Alarms & detectors" although no buyer shops them together and their grading (BS 5839-6, EN 14604) differs from burglar-alarm grades; (3) fire blankets and extinguishers would be a sub-family of two leaves, while CO alarms (gas) have no sibling; (4) Screwfix and Toolstation themselves disagree on where fire alarms live (S1, S3).

### Candidate C: protective action
- **Level 1 axis: how the object protects.** Close & lock; Store & hide; Detect & sound; Watch & record; Suppress fire; Block & restrain; Kill & repel.
- **Levels 2 to 4:** leaf by device; kind; named form.
- 10 to 15 example kinds: as in A.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) a child cabinet latch is "close & lock" and "block & restrain" at once; (2) a smoke alarm "detects & sounds" while a burglar alarm also does, so the two merge; (3) a safe is "store & hide" and "close & lock"; (4) no retailer, standard or enthusiast source fetched uses this axis, so its buyer-facing evidence is nil.

**Comparison.** A matches the way retailers, standards bodies and fire-safety sites already split the departments, keeps one axis per level, and sends each ambiguous object to one home by its leaf. B is closest to Screwfix and Toolstation menus but has a mixed axis. C is original and has no source behind it. The owner may still prefer B if one flat "kind of object" menu is wanted.

The choice level differs by leaf. Locks and padlocks stop at the form at which a numeric grade is attached. Safes stop at the protective job. Fire alarms and extinguishers stop at the sensing or agent type because the fire classes each type can fight differ. Pest products stop at delivery method, and consumer brand lines (named forms) sit below it. Counter-evidence: Locks Direct recommends padlocks by grade and by application (shed, container, warehouse), not by shackle design; but it still sells "closed shackle" as its own collection and insurers name the design (S25), so closed shackle stays a kind.
