## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B8 Home Safety & Security, in domain B Home.

**Membership test (placement key step 30, fourth branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** made to **secure or protect** a home, its people and its contents, to hold doors and windows, or to keep pests out: locks and safes for any contents (locks made for a bicycle or vehicle are G1 under S3), smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms, fire extinguishers, security cameras, doorbells and alarm systems, baby monitors, stair gates and child-safety locks, letterboxes and parcel boxes, door stops, pest traps, swatters, zappers, poisons and repellents, mosquito nets and coil holders. Branch B8 is tried after B5, B6 and B7 and before B3, B1, B2 and B4.

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch B8:**
- S3: locks made for a bicycle or vehicle are G1; safes are never "parts" (so a safe is B8 and not B1, "whatever they are made to hold").
- S3: cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8, whatever they are used in.
- Step 4 note: fencing, windows and doors are C8 building products; "installed machines, lamps, safes and alarms keep their own category".
- Step 10 note: tools made to kill pests are B8; slaughter and stunning tools for livestock are C7; pepper spray, stun guns and tasers are E4.
- Step 13 (B10) is tried before step 30: an object made for keeping or caring for animals is B10.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object (smart lock, alarm, camera, doorbell) is filed by its task at step 30, so it is B8; connectivity is a modifier. Baby monitors are B8.
- Step 32 (C8): hinges and fittings, plumbing and electrical supplies, fencing and windows and doors are building hardware, tried after B8.
- Step 30 (B1): cots, high chairs and playpens, lockers and cabinets are furniture and storage; B1 is tried after B8.
- Step 2 (H2): first-aid kits and monitors of an illness or pregnancy are H2.
- Step 5 (I1, I3): software, accounts and subscriptions are I; a cloud video plan is a subscription.
- Appendix A: user (baby, child, adult), installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted), power source and technology, connectivity and setting are modifiers, never divisions.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split
- **Agree:** locks, safes, alarms and cameras are separate object groups; fire safety (extinguishers, blankets, smoke and CO alarms), child safety and pest control are each their own group in every region fetched (Safelincs, OBI, Home Depot Canada, IKEA, PestFix, Ace, Japanese guides). Mechanism (pin tumbler and so on) is an attribute, not a type, in enthusiast sources (S7, S38).
- **Split on alarms:** Toolstation and OBI group burglar and fire alarms differently; Screwfix may put fire alarms in Safety & Workwear and burglar alarms in Electrical (unverified, search excerpt only †) (S3, S26). Secom treats the fire alarm as a home-security product (S12).
- **Split on child safety:** a lock for a cabinet is a "child safety lock" at OBI, IKEA and Home Depot Canada (S26, S18, S6), but a "cabinet lock" or "latch" at general hardware stores; Wikipedia files "Child safety lock" among locks (S38). Retailers label the group by the user (child), but the objects are filed by the injury they prevent (section 2).
- **Split on pests:** supplier trees go pest-first (S20); consumer sources go product form first (S21, S22). Ace carries both as facets (Product Form and Pest Type).
- **Split on safes:** German and Belgian sources lead with the resistance grade (S28, S9); UK and US consumer sources lead with size and protection against (S4, S5); Sold Secure rates domestic safes by Bronze to Gold (S23).
- **Split on security lights and fencing:** Screwfix lists Security Lights and Garage Security in Security (S1), Japan lists fence guards and gravel (S14); the framework files lights in B3 and gravel and fencing elsewhere (see section 9).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Buyers first choose the hazard they are guarding against, then the kind of device.** Evidence:
- Every retailer fetched has separate entry points for "Security" (break-in), fire safety, child safety and pest control, and only inside "Security" does it show the object types (padlocks, safes, door locks, CCTV, alarms). OBI puts Kindersicherheit and Brandschutz as siblings of Tresore and Alarmanlagen (S26); Home Depot Canada puts Child Safety beside Safes and Padlocks (S6); Safelincs and Secom run fire safety on its own (S33, S12); pest suppliers and Ace run pest control on its own (S20, S22).
- Inside break-in protection the first visible choice is the object form: Screwfix, Toolstation, OBI and Safe.co.uk all list padlocks, door locks, safes, cameras, alarms as peers (S1, S3, S26, S4).
- Inside each form the second choice is the **protection grade the buyer needs**, set by their insurer: UK buyers are told to match BS 3621, TS 007 3-star, CEN 3 or Sold Secure Bronze to Diamond to the value of contents (S24, S25); German and Belgian buyers match EN 1143-1 grade to insured cash value (S9, S28); US buyers read ANSI Grade 2 as the minimum for exterior doors (S8). Home Depot's safe facets lead with "Protection against" and "Lock type" before size (S5).
- For fire, alarm and extinguisher buyers choose a **type by sensing or agent** (optical versus heat; water mist versus CO2 versus wet chemical) and then a **rating or grade** (S11, S15, S34, S10).
- For household injury (the "child safety" group of retailers) the first question is where the hazard is (stairs, cabinets, sockets, corners, windows): CR's gate guide leads with placement (top of stairs needs a hardware-mounted gate) (S16); the market report segments by hazard site (S19). Retailers name the group after the user, but the user is a modifier (Appendix A; BRIEF: baby and child goods have no category of their own), so the hazard (falls, entrapment, crushing, electric shock, burns) is the axis and "child" is a user tag.
- For pests consumers go by pest and product form together: Ace's two facets are Product Form and Pest Type (S22); Cainz and the Japanese reference go by deterrence principle or formulation (S13, S21).

**Therefore:** the first axis is "what the object defends against" (intruders, theft and attack; fire and gas; household injury; pests); the second is "what kind of device it is"; the third is "the form at which grade or maker is compared". Material, mechanism, maker, price, user and setting come after.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework limit of four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; C5(a)).

### Candidate A (recommended): hazard, then device type
- **Level 1, sub-family, axis: the hazard the object defends against.** B8.1 Break-in, theft & attack; B8.2 Fire, smoke & gas; B8.3 Household injury hazards; B8.4 Pests. OWNER: Q-B8 (hazard-first A or object-type B; and the rename of "Young-child hazards" to "Household injury hazards", with child as a user tag).
- **Level 2, leaf, axis (stated per sub-family, C5(c)):** B8.1 by device job (lock a door, restrain a door or window, lock a loose item, store securely, detect and sound, watch and answer the door, receive mail). B8.2 by device job (detect, suppress). B8.3 by the site of the injury hazard and the device job there (stairs and openings, cabinets and furniture, edges, sockets and knobs, windows and doors, watching a room). B8.4 by control method (trap, bait, spray or vaporise, repel, kill on contact, exclude).
- **Level 3, kind, axis: the form at which buyers compare grade or maker** (section 4b). **Level 4, named form: a model, line member or iconic design** (C2).
- 10 to 15 example kinds: night latch, euro cylinder, mortice deadlock, multipoint lock (door locks); closed-shackle padlock, ground anchor (padlocks and anchors); safe, key safe, firearms cabinet (secure storage); video doorbell (cameras and door entry); smoke alarm, CO alarm, wet-chemical extinguisher (B8.2); hardware-mounted gate, door stop (B8.3); snap trap, mosquito coil, bed mosquito net (B8.4).
- **Where it breaks:** (1) a fire-resistant document box protects against fire but is secure storage, so it sits in B8.1 Safes & secure storage with fire class as an attribute, not in B8.2; the rule is "secure storage is filed in B8.1 whatever it resists"; (2) a cabinet latch is a lock against a household injury, so the leaf (latch) decides, not the word "lock"; (3) a baby monitor watches a person in a room: it has its own leaf B8.3.5 Room monitors (it warns a carer, as a smoke alarm warns; it does not stop the injury itself); (4) doorbells and letterboxes are named by the key but defend against a hazard only weakly (screening callers, mail theft); they sit in B8.1 by that reading.

### Candidate B: device type first, flat (retailer style)
- **Level 1 axis: kind of object.** Locks & latches; Safes & secure storage; Alarms & detectors; Cameras & doorbells; Fire-fighting equipment; Child-safety products; Pest-control products; Mail & door furniture. (This is how Screwfix, Toolstation and OBI organise their Security departments, S1, S3, S26.)
- **Level 2: leaf by form; level 3: kind; level 4: named form.** Eight sub-families instead of four, no extra level.
- 10 to 15 example kinds: as in A.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) the axis is mixed: five sub-families are named for what the object is, two for who it protects (child, a modifier under Appendix A) and what it kills (pest), so "child safety lock" has two homes; (2) smoke alarms and burglar alarms share "Alarms & detectors" although no buyer shops them together and their grading (BS 5839-6, EN 14604) differs from burglar-alarm grades; (3) fire blankets and extinguishers would be a sub-family of two leaves, while CO alarms (gas) have no sibling; (4) Screwfix and Toolstation themselves disagree on where fire alarms live (S1, S3).

### Candidate C: protective action
- **Level 1 axis: how the object protects.** Close & lock; Store & hide; Detect & sound; Watch & record; Suppress fire; Block & restrain; Kill & repel.
- **Levels 2 to 4:** leaf by device; kind; named form.
- 10 to 15 example kinds: as in A.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) a child cabinet latch is "close & lock" and "block & restrain" at once; (2) a smoke alarm "detects & sounds" while a burglar alarm also does, so the two merge; (3) a safe is "store & hide" and "close & lock"; (4) no retailer, standard or enthusiast source fetched uses this axis, so its buyer-facing evidence is nil.

**Comparison.** A matches the way retailers, standards bodies and fire-safety sites already split the departments, keeps one axis per level, and sends each ambiguous object to one home by its leaf. B is closest to Screwfix and Toolstation menus but has a mixed axis. C is original and has no source behind it. The owner may still prefer B if one flat "kind of object" menu is wanted (OWNER: Q-B8).

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (rough):** B8.1 about 50 (door locks 10, restraints 4, padlocks, chains and anchors 7, secure storage 7, alarms and sensors 6, cameras and door entry 9, mail 3); B8.2 about 14 (alarms 5, suppression 9); B8.3 about 16; B8.4 about 22. Total about 100, plus a tail of 20 to 40 niche kinds (for example hotel in-room safe, wasp trap, finger-pinch guard) that stay unattested until fetched. The merge of safe kinds (B8-3) and the removal of technology kinds (B8-2) lower the count; the injury and pest leaves gain attested kinds (B8-5). The worked tree in section 10 lists 80 kinds.

The choice level differs by leaf. Locks and padlocks stop at the form at which a numeric grade is attached. Safes stop at the form (cabinet, portable box, key box, cash box, gun cabinet); grade, fire class and installation are attributes of the one kind "safe". Fire alarms and extinguishers stop at the sensing or agent type because the fire classes each type can fight differ. Pest products stop at delivery method, and consumer brand lines (named forms) sit below it. Counter-evidence: Locks Direct recommends padlocks by grade and by application (shed, container, warehouse), not by shackle design; but it still sells "closed shackle" as its own collection and insurers name the design (S25), so closed shackle stays a kind.

**Not named forms (C2(b)):** "5-lever" is a spec (lever count) on a mortice lock; EN, UL, TS, CEN and Sold Secure grades are attributes or certifications; colours and finishes are variants (C3).

**Regional names (rule R, C11):** 補助錠 (hojo-jō, "supplementary lock") names an added lock on a door or sash, a form that generic kinds already cover. It is not a kind: it goes in the alias table (C9) with two targets, window lock and rim lock, tagged region Japan. 金庫 = safe; キーボックス = key safe; Möbeltresor = safe (installation: furniture); Wandtresor = safe (installation: wall); Waffenschrank = firearms cabinet.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Central for locks and cylinders (identity), platform for connected cameras, doorbells and interlinked alarms, incidental elsewhere.**
- **Identity:** enthusiasts and buyers name high-security makers (Abloy, Medeco, Mul-T-Lock, ASSA, Schlage in Lockwiki's table, S7; Squire, Mul-T-Lock in padlock grades, S25); collectors organise by maker marks and patent locks (Chubb, Bramah, Hobbs, S38, S39). Safe makers are a buyer cue (Chubbsafes, Burg-Wächter, Format, S9, S27, S28) but the grade attributes carry the quality claim.
- **Platform:** smart-camera and doorbell lines are sold as ecosystems with cloud plans (Ring, Nest, Eufy, Wyze, S29, S30); smoke alarms are sold by "product range" with a "Connection" facet (standalone, radio-interlink, wired), which implies same-range interlinking (S11; not tested here †); Japanese vaporisers and refills are device-specific (S21).
- **Incidental:** child gates, extinguishers, pest products, letterboxes. Japanese pest products are an exception: three makers hold about 80% of the market (S21 †), so brand lines matter as named forms.

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger, in the shared schema (C1).** Estimated 80 to 150 rows, each attested.
- *Core columns:* `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class` (house; independent; retail own-label; licensee; supplier, such as a cylinder maker behind a lock brand), `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id` (for example a lock group), `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags` (C8, with `core` or `adjacent`), `price_band` (C4(d)), `sources`.
- *Extension column:* `certifications` (Sold Secure, EN 14604 and similar approvals held by the maker's products), as A5 does.
- *Lines table (C1):* Chubbsafes Vega, Burg-Wächter Pure-Safe, Format Antares and Wega (S9, S27, S28); Aico Ei140e and Ei3000 series (S11); Squire SS50 series (S25). A line that spans several kinds (an alarm series with smoke, heat and CO models) is one line row; each member is a named form under its own kind.
- *Tier entries (C4(a)):* only the reviewer scores in section 5. The old column "tier or grade ceiling" is dropped: grades are attributes of named forms, and a maker's ceiling is derived from them.
- *Compatibility ledger (C13), not a tag or a level:* camera and doorbell ecosystems (Ring, Nest, Eufy, Wyze; owner = maker; S29, S30); smoke-alarm interlink ranges (S11 †); vaporiser refill systems, liquid and mat (S21); cylinder keyway profiles (Lockwiki's identification table, S7); socket standards served by outlet covers (†). Named forms carry `platform`, refills and parts carry `fits`.
- *Named forms (C2):* each with `maker_bound`. Maker-bound: ASSA dp4400, Medeco Duracam (S7); Bramah lock, Chubb detector lock (S38); Squire SS50CS, Mul-T-Lock NE12SB (S25); Master Lock 5400, Burg-Wächter Key Safe 30 SB (S26); FireAngel ST-622 (S11); Kincho swirl coil, Earth Nomat, Vape, Combat, Gokiburi Hoihoi (S21; OWNER: Q-X10 consumable brand lines as named forms or ledger only). Not maker-bound: Banbury lock (S38); Cooper vane (S38; maker binding to check †).
- *Variants (C3):* none proposed now; open only if an owner-emphasis scene (antique locks) needs depth.
- *Scene tags (C8):* locksport (definition source Lockwiki, S7), antique lock collecting (S39, S40). "Prepper" is held back: no definition source was fetched.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds (C7):** canonical form first, then the secondary key that each sub-family states.
- B8.1 Break-in, theft & attack: by how commonly the form is fitted or bought in a home, as the retail trees list them (S1, S2, S3), then specialist forms. Door locks: night latch, rim lock, euro cylinder, mortice deadlock, mortice sashlock, deadbolt, knob or lever lockset, multipoint lock, patio door lock.
- B8.2 Fire, smoke & gas: by the order a home fits them (smoke alarm first, S12), then by the fire classes covered; extinguishers by agent from general (water, water mist, foam) to special (CO2, dry powder, wet chemical), then extinguisher spray and fire blanket.
- B8.3 Household injury hazards: by the share of the market segment (S19: gates, cabinet and drawer locks, edge guards, outlet covers, door stops, window guards).
- B8.4 Pests: by control method as listed by the supplier (S20), from capture to exclusion.

**Named forms (C7):** by maker, in the order of the maker ledger's `name`; then by first-introduction year as attested; then alphabetically where no year is attested. This replaces the earlier "ascending grade" order (review C-10). The ascending-grade reading (CEN 3 to CEN 6 padlocks, EN 1143-1 grade 0 to V safes), which buyers use to meet an insurer's requirement (S9, S25), becomes the generated insurer-approved view (section 7).

**Variants (C7):** by `variant_code` or year.

## 9. Boundaries

1. **B8 versus G1 (vehicles).** Framework: locks made for a bicycle or vehicle are G1 under S3. Sold Secure rates pedal-cycle, motorcycle, caravan and wheel locks as their own categories (S23). *A lock, chain or anchor made and sold for a bicycle, motorbike, caravan or car is G1; a padlock or chain sold for gates, sheds and general use is B8 even if it carries a cycle rating; ground anchors and security posts for a garage floor are B8 (B8.1.3).* A stroller insect net made for one pram follows the pram (G1) under S3. OWNER: Q12 (confirm this cut).
2. **B8 versus E4 (weapons).** Personal attack alarms are rated by Sold Secure (S23). *A device that only makes noise to deter attack is B8 (B8.1.5); a device that sprays, shocks or strikes is E4; a safe or cabinet made to hold firearms is B8 (safes for any contents) and the firearm is E4.* The review settles the gun cabinet as B8 (review C-7 row 6). OWNER: Q2 (personal attack alarm B8 or E4).
3. **B8 versus D4 and D1 (cameras and recorders).** Security cameras, video doorbells and baby monitors are B8 by framework. *A camera made to watch and record a place for protection, including a video doorbell and floodlight camera, is B8.1.6; a baby or room monitor is B8.3.5; a camera made for making pictures is D4; a recorder or network video recorder sold only to run security cameras follows the cameras under S3 and is B8, while a general network drive is D1.* Cloud video storage plans are I3 subscriptions. OWNER: Q5 (NVR and DVR B8 or D1).
4. **B8 versus B3 (lighting).** Screwfix files security lights under Security (S1). *A light, with or without a motion sensor, is B3; adding a camera makes a floodlight camera B8.* Settled in review C-7 row 7; listed for one-answer confirmation (OWNER: boundary settlements).
5. **B8 versus B1 (furniture and storage).** Lockers and filing cabinets appear next to safes at Screwfix and Safe.co.uk (S1, S4). *A lockable locker, cabinet or filing cabinet is B1; a safe, cash box, firearms cabinet or fire-resistant document box made to resist forced entry or fire is B8; a key safe (an outdoor key box or lock box) and a key cabinet (a lockable cabinet fitted with key hooks, sold among security goods, S1, S14) are B8.* (This replaces the earlier rule that a key cabinet is B8 only with a security rating; review "objects that would split": lock box vs key cabinet.) ## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A** (OWNER: Q-B8). Level 1 sub-family by the hazard defended against (Break-in, theft & attack; Fire, smoke & gas; Household injury hazards; Pests); level 2 leaf by the device job, with the leaf axis stated per sub-family; level 3 kind at the level where grade or maker is compared; level 4 named forms. Maximum depth four levels below the category. Grades are attributes with their standard named, pass/fail marks are certifications, and tier entries exist only as reviewer scores on named forms; one shared price-band scheme. A maker ledger of 80 to 150 rows in the C1 schema (identity for locks), with a compatibility ledger for connected devices and refills and a jurisdiction ledger for legal classes. Views: insurer-approved, jurisdiction-rule, historical, deterrence-principle, baby and child safety.
