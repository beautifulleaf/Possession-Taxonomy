## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C8 Building Materials & Hardware.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 32, branch 2): *a material, building product, hardware or supply: timber, bricks, roofing, windows, doors, stairs, flooring, tiles, wallpaper, paint, adhesives, fasteners, hinges and fittings, plumbing and electrical supplies, mains cables, extension leads, plugs, fuses, household batteries, lubricants, fuels and fuel cans, firewood, matches, firelighters, and long utility and candle lighters (pocket lighters are B9), packing materials, fencing, acoustic panels.* Category line: "building products, hardware, electrical and plumbing supplies, household batteries, fuels, packing materials."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name C8:**
- Step 4 note (G2): a building is filed as a whole; its elements and products, installed or not, are filed by what they are: structural, envelope and finishing products (bricks, timber, roofing, windows, doors, stairs, flooring, tiles, wallpaper), fixed plumbing and wiring, fencing and acoustic panels are C8. Kitchen and wardrobe cabinetry is B1; carpets, blinds and curtains are B2; baths, showers, basins, toilets and hot tubs are B7; installed machines, lamps, safes and alarms keep their own category. Greenhouses and polytunnels are C2.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): building products are never parts; cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8, whatever they are used in.
- Step 7 note (F6): a natural material sold for a use is filed by that use; firewood is C8; garden stone is C2.
- Step 27 note (B9): long utility and candle lighters, matches and firelighters are C8; pocket lighters are B9.
- Step 28 (C4): paints and glues made for model-making and picture-making are C4; step 29 (C2): garden stone and artificial turf are C2.
- Step 24 (D1): cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices (USB, HDMI, audio, charging) are D1; step 30 B7 note: wall-box chargers for electric vehicles are G1.
- Step 30 B8: locks and safes, smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms, letterboxes and parcel boxes, door stops are B8; B4 holds signs and house numbers.
- Step 32 branch 1 (C1): tools, ladders, tool boxes, and by S3 the batteries, bits and blades made for tools stay in C1.

**Collectors and the resale market.** Evidence is thin. **Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **UNSPSC** [S18, S19, S35, S36]: building materials and hardware are split across segments. Segment 30 (structures, building and construction) has 13 families: Structural components and basic shapes; Concrete and cement and plaster; Roads and landscape; Structural building products; Insulation; Exterior finishing materials; Interior finishing materials; Doors and windows and glass; Plumbing fixtures; Construction and maintenance support equipment; Portable structure components; Underground mining structures; Structural materials [S18]. Segment 31 (manufacturing components and supplies) holds Hardware (31160000), Rope and chain and cable and wire and strap, Adhesives and sealants (31200000), Paints and primers and finishes (31210000), Gaskets, Seals, Fabricated pipe assemblies [S36], and its description names fasteners, industrial adhesives, sealants and solvents, paints, primers and finishes [S19]. Segment 15 holds Fuels, Gaseous fuels and additives, Lubricants and oils and greases and anti-corrosives [S35]. Segment 30's description includes "electrical and plumbing fixtures" [S19].
- **Uniclass (UK, NBS) Pr Products table, v1.43** [S20]: 17 groups: Pr_15 Preparatory; Pr_20 Structure and general; Pr_25 Skin; Pr_30 Opening; Pr_35 Covering and finishing; Pr_40 Signage, sanitary fittings and FF&E; Pr_45 Flora and fauna; Pr_50 Fabric accessory; Pr_55 Manufacturing process; Pr_60 Services source; Pr_65 distribution; Pr_70 outlet; Pr_75 control; Pr_80 general; Pr_82 Vehicle; Pr_85 Process engineering; Pr_90 Soft FM. The first axis is the building element or service function the product performs.
- **Battery designation (IEC 60086, ANSI C18)** [S15, S16]: the IEC code is a letter/number sequence for number of cells, chemistry letter (none zinc-carbon, L alkaline, C lithium manganese dioxide, S silver oxide, P zinc-air), shape (R round, F flat, P other), size code (R6 = AA, R03 = AAA, R14 = C, R20 = D; button cells and lithium-ion round cells use diameter and height codes) and modifiers. Size and chemistry are separate parts of the same designation. The ANSI system has its own letter suffixes. Common names (AA, C, D) are older than both [S15, S16].
- **Plug and socket types** [S21, S38]: 15 lettered types (A to O) assigned by the US Department of Commerce and carried by the IEC to group plugs by rough mechanical compatibility; the letters are arbitrary, and each type maps to national standards (A NEMA 1-15; C Europlug CEE 7/16; D BS 546; E CEE 7/5-7/6; F Schuko CEE 7/3-7/4; G BS 1363; I AS/NZS 3112; J SN 441011; K Danish; L CEI 23-50) [S21, S38].
- **Fastener standards** [S17, S33, S37]: ISO 898-1:2013 specifies bolts, screws and studs "with specified property classes", coarse and fine thread, under ISO/TC 2/SC 11 [S33]; screw drives are catalogued with ANSI and ISO type letters and bit size codes (Phillips PH0–4, Pozidriv PZ0–5) [S17].
- **Paint: EN 13300** [S31]: interior wall and ceiling paints are classified by end use, binder type (acrylic, vinyl, alkyd, epoxy, hydraulic lime, silicate), gloss, largest grain size, wet-scrub resistance, hiding power and cleanability, each as a set of classes [S31].
- **Fuel: EN 228** [S34] and GOV.UK [S25]: EN 228:2025 "Automotive fuels — Unleaded petrol — Requirements and test methods" (ICS 75.160.20, liquid fuels) and the E10 explainer exist; only the titles loaded, so grade detail is not attested here.

**Museums and reference works.** Wikipedia's battery-size list [S15] and screw-drive list [S17] are the working reference works; they divide batteries by shape (cylindrical, button/coin and lithium-ion among others) and screw drives by drive family (cruciform, multiple-square, pentalobular, tamper-resistant and others). No museum collection page for building materials was fetched.

**Where the sources agree.** (1) Every retailer and classification keeps fasteners as one family of its own, split by fastener type (screw, nail, bolt and nut, rivet, anchor) [S2, S5, S12, S14, S27]. (2) Plumbing, electrical and paint/adhesive supplies are separate families from structural materials in every source [S3, S5–S9, S18, S36]. (3) Batteries and fuels are never filed with building elements: they sit with electrical (Bunnings, Toolstation) or outdoor/BBQ and heating, or with fuels in UNSPSC [S3, S10, S11, S29, S30, S35]. (4) Where a standard gives a designation (battery size, plug type, property class, gloss class), buyers use it as the primary description [S11, S16, S21, S31, S37].

**Where they split the same objects differently.** (1) Taps and valves: with plumbing at Toolstation and Komeri [S7, S26], with sanitary fixtures in UNSPSC segment 30 "Plumbing fixtures" [S18]. (2) Screws: by application at Hornbach and Toolstation [S5, S13], by drive/thread/use at Leroy Merlin [S14], by head or drive in standards [S17]. (3) Hardware: Bunnings keeps Door Hardware and General Hardware in Building & Hardware [S1]; Hornbach's URL tree puts fixings under Eisenwaren (ironmongery) [S12]; UNSPSC puts "Hardware" under manufacturing components, away from building products [S36]. (4) Safety devices: Toolstation lists carbon-monoxide detectors and EV charging under electrical [S6]; the framework files these as B8 and G1. (5) Adhesives sit in an "Adhesives & Sealants" department of their own at Toolstation [S9], while tile adhesive and grout are grouped with tiling cutters and tools under Painting & Decorating [S8]: the same product is filed by chemistry in one place and by job in another.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence on what buyers decide first.**
1. **The job or system the product serves, then the product family.** Every department tree starts from the part of the build or repair: Bunnings Building & Hardware by element/material [S1]; Toolstation by Screws & Fixings, Electrical, Plumbing, Painting & Decorating, Adhesives & Sealants [S5–S9]; Komeri by building element [S26]; Uniclass by structure, skin, opening, covering, services source/distribution/outlet/control [S20]; UNSPSC by structure, finishing, hardware, adhesives, paints, fuels [S18, S35, S36].
2. **Inside fasteners, the application (what it fastens into) beats the drive or material.** Hornbach lists screws by application (wood, stainless, thread, drywall, sheet metal, self-drilling, concrete) [S13]; Toolstation by use (wood, decking and roofing, drywall, masonry) [S5]; Leroy Merlin by use ("pour votre chantier": terrasse, bardage, charpente, placo) [S14]. Diameter (M3–M14), length, drive and material (A2) are filters [S13, S14, S17].
3. **For batteries, the standard size class comes first, chemistry second.** Toolstation's Type facet lists AA, C, CR123 and the chemistry facet is separate [S11]; Bunnings' first cut is rechargeable versus non-rechargeable, but its titles carry the size [S10]; the IEC code separates chemistry letter from size code [S16]; flashlight buyers ask which cell the light takes (18650, 21700, CR123A) [S22].
4. **For paint, the paint type by surface and use, then the finish class.** Toolstation's menu is Emulsion, Masonry, Fence, Spray, Radiator, Tile, Floor, Furniture, Anti-mould and room [S8]; the standard classifies by end use, binder and classes for gloss, grain size, scrub resistance and hiding power [S31].
5. **For fuels, the form it is burned in** (gas bottle, logs, coal, charcoal, pellets, firelighter) and then use and size [S28–S30].
6. **For electrical and plumbing, the standard or joint system.** Plugs by lettered type (G, F, I) [S21, S38]; plumbing fittings by joining method (compression, push-fit, solder-ring, press-fit) [S7]; pipe threads by standard (NPT, BSPT, BSPP) [S24].

**Conclusion for the first axis:** the role the product plays in the job (shell, finish, join, services, coat, bond, power, fuel). Below that, the generic product type by use or system. Size, material, grade, standard type, brand line and colour are attributes, tier modifiers or named forms, never levels.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: at most four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); a leaf may be skipped where a sub-family is small. Material, region, price, brand, user, and market (trade or DIY) are never levels.

### Candidate B: material first (timber, metal, masonry, plastic, glass)

| Level | Stated axis |
|---|---|
| Sub-family | The material the product is made of |
| Leaf | Product type in that material |
| Kind | Generic type |

Example (11 kinds): *Timber* → sawn timber, plywood, building board, logs; *Metal* → steel section, bolt, nail, rivet, hinge; *Masonry* → cement, block. ### Candidate C: standard or designation system first

| Level | Stated axis |
|---|---|
| Sub-family | The standard that names the product (IEC 60086 cell sizes, IEC plug types, ISO property classes, EN paint classes, ISO pipe threads) versus products with no designation system |
| Leaf | The class within the system |
| Kind | The designated type |

Example (10 kinds): *IEC 60086* → R6/AA, R03/AAA, 6F22/9 V, CR2032; *plug types* → Type C, F, G, I; *bolts* → class 8.8, 10.9. Where it breaks: only batteries, plugs, pipe threads, bolts and some paints have a designation scheme; timber, bricks, doors, adhesives, firewood have none; it would put a bolt in one place by strength and a screw in another by application. The designation is better kept as an attribute (see section 5).

Medium: nails, nuts and bolts, anchors, cables and flexes, paints, adhesives, doors and windows, roofing and cladding. Narrow: rivets, button cells (large in designations, small in kinds), LPG bottles, firelighters, packing tape and film.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. Sizes, lengths, colours, drive types, coatings and pack sizes are attributes; they stay below the kind.

| Sub-family | Kind stops at | Evidence | Below the kind |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fasteners (screws) | Application type (wood screw, decking screw, drywall screw, self-drilling screw, concrete screw) | Hornbach and Toolstation split by application; makers (Fischer, Tox, Kreg) appear as brands within the listing [S5, S12, S13, S14] | Diameter M3–M14, length, drive (Phillips, Pozidriv, Torx), head, material (A2) and property class are attributes; drives are named forms [S13, S17] |

No fetched page ranks hardware by a recognised consensus ladder.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Verdict: incidental overall; identity or platform only in a few narrow places.**
- *Incidental:* timber, boards, bricks, cement, tiles, plain nails, plain pipe. Brand is one attribute among many (Komeri's nails offer maker as a facet: Yahata Holdings, Trusco Nakayama and the store brand [S27]).
- *Product-line identity (brand ladder):* batteries, paint, adhesives and fixings. Retailers expose brand as a facet and list several lines per brand: Duracell, Energizer, Varta [S10]; Evo-Stik, 20 products [S9]; Fischer, Tox, Kreg at Hornbach [S12]; JG Speedfit, Aquaflow, McAlpine at Toolstation [S7].
- *Platform:* a few joint systems are proprietary (push-fit lines such as the JG Speedfit range named on the retailer page [S7]); the platform effect there was not tested here and no battery platform exists in C8, because battery sizes are cross-maker standards (IEC 60086, ANSI C18) [S16]. Cordless-tool battery platforms are C1, not C8 (rule S3).

**Maker ledger:** recommended, light. Only for consumer product lines in batteries, paint, adhesives, fasteners and plumbing fittings: maker, known-for kinds, line, tier (the line's rung), price band, country, each row attested. No ledger for timber, masonry or roofing. Brand lines are named forms under the kind; the maker row links to them.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Use **canonical form first, then the standard's own order**. Batteries: by the designation's size code (R6/AA before R03/AAA), cylindrical before button before lithium-ion; fasteners: by application type, then ascending property class and size; plugs: by the Type letter A to O, accepting that "these letters are completely arbitrary" and carry no ranking [S21]; paints: by surface/use, then gloss class from matt to gloss; fuels: by form, then bottle size. Reason: every source that offers a designation uses it as the order buyers already read [S11, S16, S21, S37]; spec order lets the attribute tier appear without a second axis.

## 9. Boundaries

Closest cross-boundary objects and precedence sentences (to be added to the key if the owner agrees):

1. **C8 / C1 (batteries, bits, ladders, tool boxes):** *A cell or battery made in a standard size (IEC and ANSI cylindrical, coin, button and rectangular cells such as AA, AAA, C, D, 9 V, CR2032, CR123A, 18650) is C8 whatever device it powers; a pack shaped for one tool or device follows that tool or device (rule S3), and a general sealed lead-acid block sold loose is C8.* Evidence: the battery shelves mix both [S10, S11].
2. **C8 / B7 (taps, valves, pipe, sanitaryware):** *Pipe, fittings, valves, stop cocks, traps, wastes, taps and gutters are C8; baths, showers, basins, toilets, bidets, hot tubs, water heaters and cold-water tanks are B7.* Evidence: Toolstation files taps with plumbing and also lists Water Heating and Cold Water Storage in its plumbing tree [S7]; Komeri puts taps (水栓, 4,470 items) with piping [S26]; UNSPSC puts "plumbing fixtures" in the structures segment [S18].
3. **C8 / B8 (locks, alarms, letterboxes):** *Hinges, handles, latches, brackets and cabinet knobs and pulls are C8; any lock, padlock, safe or lockset that includes a lock, and any smoke or carbon-monoxide alarm, is B8; EV wall-box chargers are G1.* Evidence: Bunnings lists Door Hardware and Security & Safety Products in one department [S1]; Toolstation lists carbon-monoxide detectors and EV charging under electrical [S6]; House of Antique Hardware sells locksets with hinges [S32].
4. **C8 / D1 (cables):** *Mains cable, flex, switches, sockets, plugs, fuses and extension leads are C8; cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices (USB, HDMI, audio, charging) are D1.* Evidence: Toolstation Cables & Flexes and Power Leads & Adaptors [S6]; the D1 key in step 24.
5. **C8 / C4 (paints and glues):** *Paint, stain, varnish, wood finish and adhesive made for buildings and furniture are C8; paint and glue made for picture-making, model-making or crafts are C4.* Evidence: the C8 key names paint and adhesives; step 28 names model glues and paints; Toolstation sells tile, radiator, floor and furniture paint as building products [S8].
6. **C8 / B9 (lighters) and B6 (cleaning):** *Matches, firelighters, long utility and candle lighters, lighter fluid and fuel in any bottle, bag, can or crate are C8; pocket lighters are B9; silicone or adhesive removers and other cleaning products are B6 whatever they are used on.* Evidence: Toolstation lists a silicone remover among its adhesives [S9]; S3 rule on cleaning products.
7. **C8 / C2 (garden):** *Fence panels, gates and fence paint are C8; garden stone, artificial turf, planters and pool equipment are C2.* Evidence: Bunnings and Komeri list fencing within building products [S1, S26]; key step 29.
8. **C8 / B1 and B2 (cabinetry, carpets):** *Building boards and sawn timber are C8; kitchen worktops and cabinets are B1; carpets, loose or fitted, are B2; flooring that is a surface material (laminate, tile, vinyl sheet) is C8.* Evidence: step 4 note; Komeri 建具・床材 group [S26].

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** Role in the job (12 sub-families) → product family (leaf) → generic kind → named form. Maximum depth below the category: four (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); batteries, fuels and paints may run three because the leaf is the shelf itself. Fastener tiers (property class), paint classes, battery chemistry, rechargeability, rating, material, size, colour, brand line, trade/DIY and region are attributes, modifiers or tags, never levels. Plug types, pipe threads, battery designations and screw drives are named forms or attributes of the kind. A light maker ledger covers consumer brand lines only. UNSPSC and Uniclass codes can be stored as cross-reference columns on each kind [S18, S20, S36].

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. **Gas cylinders and refill schemes.** Calor prices every bottle as "New" or "Refill" [S28]; the supplier's refill agreement, which search listings of other Calor pages describe as keeping the cylinder the supplier's property, was not on a fetched page and needs checking. Is a leased cylinder a possession, or is only the gas bought?
2. **Taps and valves in C8 or B7?** Recommended C8 (fixed plumbing) with basins and baths in B7; the sources split.
3. **Packing & protection materials and lighters/matches.** Both are in the key but no fetched page covers them; keep as sub-families C8.11 and C8.12, merge C8.12 into Fuels, or drop until a source is found?
4. **Battery kind level.** Recommended: size class is the kind; chemistry and rechargeable/primary are attributes (Bunnings splits rechargeable from non-rechargeable first [S10]). Alternative: split rechargeable cells as separate kinds.
5. **Plumbing fittings.** Kind = joint system (push-fit fitting) with shape (elbow, tee, coupler) as attribute, or kind = shape × joint? Retailers split by joint system [S7]; shapes were not researched.
6. **Tile adhesive, grout and caulk.** File with adhesives and sealants (recommended) with a generated "tiling" view, or under Interior finishes?
7. **Cabinet knobs and pulls** (C8 hardware or B1 parts) and **lockset** wording for the B8 boundary.
8. **Pump fuel and delivered fuel.** Recommended: out (service or not an owned object); only bottled, bagged or canned fuel is C8.
9. **Cross-reference codes.** Store UNSPSC and Uniclass codes on kinds?

**Gaps:** timber and board grades; structural steel, bricks and roofing at kind level; lubricants; packing materials; US retail tree (Home Depot returned nothing); collector or resale sources for hardware; Screwfix, OBI and Cainz trees (not fetched within the 40-fetch cap).
