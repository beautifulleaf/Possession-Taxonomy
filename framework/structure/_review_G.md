# Stage 2 domain review: G (G1 Vehicles, G2 Property & Structures)

*Batch S2R_G. Cold, offline review (no web, no git) of memos framework/structure/G1.md and G2.md and their sources files verify/out/S2_G1_sources.tsv and S2_G2_sources.tsv. Checked against BRIEF.md, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md and the draft shared conventions C1 to C13 in framework/structure/_conventions.md. The conventions were written after these memos, so section 3 lists the changes each memo needs to conform. C14 to C17 (added by the domain E review) are cited where they settle a point. This is a reviewer's judgement, not an owner decision. Severity: **high** = breaks a shared rule (BRIEF, Appendix A, S1 one home, C5a depth) or leaves common objects with two homes; **medium** = an inconsistency or evidence gap that changes where real objects go; **low** = wording, ordering or sourcing hygiene. Reviewer knowledge that is not backed by a fetched page is marked "(reviewer, unsourced)" and must be sourced before it enters the data.*

**Mechanical checks.**
- *Sources match.* G1 uses 34 source tags (GOO to SMG) with a tag key in its header, and its TSV has 34 rows. Two claims have no row: the Shopify crosswalk ("adds Heavier-Than-Air and Lighter-Than-Air aircraft ...") and the Hagerty figure of "more than 40,000 different collector cars" (the HAG row describes age bands only). G2 cites full URLs inline, and all 36 are in its TSV. The Von Poll row is listed although the memo says the page returned only listing metadata and was not used for any claim.
- *Depth and genre.* Both memos recommend at most four levels below the category. G1 nevertheless writes paths with five levels in its worked tree and usability table (finding G1-1). Neither memo divides anything by content genre.
- *Excerpt labelling.* G1 marks "(search excerpt only)" consistently (Goo-net facet, Google travel-trailer placement, horse-drawn carriage forms, Optimist, custom-culture names, Google parts systems). G2 names its gaps in its header (no Chinese shop pages, no German portal facets, no Indian or Brazilian portal) and marks the one search-only maker (Prestige).
- *Owner interest.* Neither memo makes a maker or a tier a level. G1 treats brand as identity and puts it in named forms and a maker ledger. G2 calls makers incidental, which is right for land and houses. G2 keeps a small ledger for factory-built objects.

## 2. Findings by memo

Each finding gives severity, memo, evidence and an exact proposed fix. "Conforms to" names the rule the fix applies.

### G1 Vehicles

**G1-1 (high) A named form under a named form gives five levels.**
- *Evidence.* Worked tree: "Convertible (cabriolet): ... roadster (no): BMW Z3, Mazda Roadster (yes)"; "SUV: ... crossover (no): Hyundai Santa Fe (yes); coupé SUV (no): BMW X6 (yes)"; "Motorhome: low-profile, overcab, A-class (no) ...". Usability rows 6 and 18 spell out the path "Cars > Convertible > roadster (no) > Mazda Roadster (yes)" and "Caravans > Motorhome > low-profile (no) > Fusion 373 (yes)". That is sub-family, leaf, kind, named form and a second named form: five levels. C5a allows four, counting the named form, and C2 has one named-form level.
- *Objects that would split.* Mazda Roadster (a named form under "roadster", or directly under Convertible); Hyundai Santa Fe (under SUV, or under "crossover"); BMW X6.
- *Fix.* Keep one named-form level. Generic body sub-styles (roadster, targa top, crossover, coupé SUV, shooting brake, fastback, liftback) become a controlled attribute `body_substyle` on the maker-bound named form, with a generated "by body sub-style" view. Mazda Roadster is then Cars > Convertible > Mazda Roadster with `body_substyle = roadster`. Keep a non-maker named form only where nothing maker-bound is filed under it (the one-design sailing classes, the carriage types). Motorhome layouts are handled in G1-2. *Conforms to:* C5a, C2(a).

**G1-2 (high) Motorhome layouts are the comparison level but are filed as named forms.**
- *Evidence.* Section 4b: "The comparison level is the layout: low-profile against overcab against A-class [PM]". Section 1.1: Practical Motorhome runs separate buying guides ("best low profile motorhome", "best A-class motorhomes"). Yet the tree files the three layouts as non-maker named forms under one kind, Motorhome.
- *Fix.* Make them kinds in the leaf Caravans, motorhomes and campers: touring caravan; folding camper; fifth-wheel; truck camper; campervan (van conversion); low-profile motorhome; overcab (coachbuilt) motorhome; A-class (integrated) motorhome. Put US Class A, B and C into the C9 table as aliases, but only where a fetched source states the equivalence. Today the memo itself says "A-class is not Class A", so until a source says otherwise the rows are homonyms. *Conforms to:* template 4b, C2(a).

**G1-3 (high) The speed-pedelec rule gives one object two homes, depending on the jurisdiction.**
- *Evidence.* P2 files a pedal-assist cycle by "the local limit for a bicycle (EU and UK 250 W and 25 km/h; US Class 1 to 3)": over the limit it is a moped in the motorcycle leaf. The EBK source says US Class 3 allows assist to 28 mph (about 45 km/h) as a bicycle, and the EU treats 45 km/h assist as an L1e-B moped. A Stromer ST5 or a Riese & Müller "HS" model (reviewer, unsourced examples) is therefore a bicycle in the US and a moped in the EU. Throttle e-bikes (US Class 2) have the same problem. This also contradicts the memo's own question 3 (legal classes are attributes) and Rule T (C10): the motor does not change the form, because the object is still a pedal cycle.
- *Fix.* Replace P2 with: *"A cycle made to be pedalled, with or without electric assist of any power, speed or throttle, is filed in the bicycle leaf by its discipline. Its legal class in each jurisdiction (EPAC under EN 15194, speed pedelec L1e-B, US Class 1, 2 or 3) goes in the jurisdiction ledger (C17). A two-wheeler with no working pedal drive is a moped, scooter or motorcycle."* *Conforms to:* S1, C10, C17.

**G1-4 (medium) Legal and registration classes are stored as attributes, not in the jurisdiction ledger.**
- *Evidence.* The memo stores as attributes `legal_class = kei`, `unece_category` (L1 to L7, M1 to M3, N1 to N3, O1 to O4), EPAC and the US e-bike classes, the FAA certification categories (normal, utility, experimental, light sport) and the "experimental or homebuilt" tag (sections 5 and 10). C17 says legal classes go in one ledger (`subject_id`, `jurisdiction`, `scheme`, `class`, `criteria`, `source_url`, `date`) and are never a level or a tag.
- *Fix.* Split the list in two. *Design standards and design classifications* the object is built to stay attributes with the standard named (C4b): ISO 4210 and ISO 8098 bands, the EN 15194 design, the CE design category A to D ("a design classification and not a navigational authorisation" [RCD]) and the ISO 3833 body terms. *Classes a jurisdiction assigns* for registration, licence or tax go to C17: kei, UNECE and EU L/M/N/O, O1 to O4, EPAC, speed pedelec and the US classes, and FAA certification categories including experimental. "Built from a kit" stays an Appendix A how-acquired attribute. *Conforms to:* C17, C4(b).

**G1-5 (medium) The leaf "Animal-drawn and hand-pushed vehicles" puts prams with horse carriages.**
- *Evidence.* Leaf G1.1.10 holds the horse-drawn carriage, the pram and the stroller. The memo rejects Candidate B because "Pram, bicycle, kayak and handcar share the muscle sub-family, which no buyer would browse". This leaf has the same fault. No fetched source sells prams beside carriages, so the stated leaf axis ("class of vehicle as registered or sold") is not met. Baby-transport terms are also unsettled: Wikipedia lists "Carrycot, travel system, twin and jogging variants" [W-BABY].
- *Objects that would split.* A travel system (a pushchair frame sold with a carrycot and a car seat: pram or stroller?); a pram-pushchair convertible; a pet stroller (B10 or G1, open in the B10 memo).
- *Fix.* Make two leaves. **Animal-drawn vehicles**: carriage, cart and wagon, sleigh (P6). **Hand-pushed carriers**: pram, stroller (pushchair), jogging stroller, double or tandem stroller, pet stroller. Add the rule *"a frame sold with both a carrycot and a seat unit is a stroller with `carrycot = yes`; a pram is a carriage whose only body is a lying carrycot"*. This gives the pet stroller a G1 home by key order (step 11 before step 13) and answers the open B10 question. Source prams and strollers from at least one retailer (see G1-14). *Conforms to:* C5c (one stated axis), S1.

**G1-6 (medium) Some Land leaves are bounded by mass and seat count, which are size and legal class.**
- *Evidence.* "Vans, pickups and light goods vehicles" against "Trucks and buses" follows N1 (up to 3.5 t) against N2 and N3, and the minibus sits in the first while bus and coach sit in the second. MPVs are in Cars. C6 makes size an attribute, and the memo itself lists "mass band" among attributes.
- *Objects that would split.* A 3.5 t Luton box van and a 7.5 t box truck (the same body type); a 12-seat Toyota HiAce Commuter or a Ford Transit minibus (MPV, minibus or bus); a VW Multivan or Mercedes V-Class (an MPV, or a window van in the van leaf); a Honda Ridgeline or Toyota Hilux (pickup, or the eBay "Truck" body inside cars).
- *Fix.* Merge the two leaves into **Vans, trucks and buses**, with kinds by body: panel van; window van (kombi); pickup truck; chassis-cab with fitted body (box, dropside and tipper as non-maker named forms); tractor unit; minibus; bus or coach; special-purpose body (fire engine, ambulance, hearse as named forms). Gross mass and seats become attributes, and the UNECE class goes to C17. Tie-break: *"A passenger body with seats where the load space would be is an MPV when it has at most three rows of seats and a minibus when it has four or more."* *Conforms to:* C6, C5c.

**G1-7 (medium) The quad is in two leaves.**
- *Evidence.* "ATV (quad)" is kind 19 in Off-road, snow and utility, while "quadricycle" is a kind not shown in Motorcycles and other powered light vehicles. A road-registered L7e quad bike and an off-road ATV are the same body; their difference is registration.
- *Fix.* Keep one kind, "quad (ATV)", in Off-road, snow and utility, with registration in C17. Light enclosed four-wheelers (quadricycles in the L6e and L7e sense, such as the Renault Twizy or Citroën Ami: reviewer, unsourced examples) become the kind "microcar (quadricycle)" in Cars, which section 3 already lists as "microcar if kept". *Conforms to:* S1, C17.

**G1-8 (medium) The sailing-craft kind axis mixes hull form with size and use.**
- *Evidence.* The axis is "hull form and size class". The kinds are sailing dinghy, cruising sailboat, sailing catamaran, trimaran and motorsailer. YachtWorld lists "Beach Catamaran" apart from "Catamaran" [YW]. J/24 and Melges 24 are filed under "Cruising sailboat (monohull keelboat)", but they are racing one-design keelboats with little or no cruising accommodation (reviewer judgement; the memo says "their builder rules were not checked").
- *Objects that would split.* A Hobie 16 (a sailing dinghy, or a sailing catamaran); a J/70 (a dinghy, or a cruising sailboat); a Farrier F-22 (a trailerable trimaran) against a Neel 47 cruising trimaran.
- *Fix.* Set the kind axis to hull configuration and accommodation: sailing dinghy (open monohull, centreboard); open keelboat or daysailer (ballast keel, no or minimal cabin: J/70, Melges 24 and J/24 as class named forms); cruising monohull (with accommodation); beach catamaran (open multihull); cruising multihull (catamaran and trimaran, with the hull count as an attribute); motorsailer. Length stays `loa` and rig stays `rig`. *Conforms to:* C5c, C6.

**G1-9 (medium) The aircraft leaves do not match across sections, and C10 is not stated.**
- *Evidence.* Section 10 gives Aircraft five leaves (airplanes; gliders; rotorcraft; lighter-than-air; powered parachute and weight-shift) for about 15 kinds. In the worked tree G1.4 has no leaves. The usability table writes "Aircraft > Airplane > Single-engine piston airplane". Three of the five leaves would hold two or three kinds. Under C5b a sub-family of about 12 kinds or fewer may skip the leaf level. The airplane kinds are split by engine type (piston, turboprop, jet), which is a technology split that needs Rule T. Rail is "about 8 kinds" in section 3 and section 10 but "about 7" in section 4.
- *Fix.* Either skip the leaf level in Aircraft and order its kinds by FAA category (C5b, C7), or keep two leaves: airplanes, and other aircraft. State that the engine-class kinds pass C10(b): the engine changes the airframe and its installation, and Controller and Trade-A-Plane list the classes as separate categories [CTRL, TAP]. Give one count for Rail. *Conforms to:* C5b, C10.

**G1-10 (medium) Racing and off-road sport machines are not placed.**
- *Evidence.* Step 11 tests "made mainly to transport people ... from place to place", and its note covers racing only for "every boat, canoe, kayak, bicycle and aircraft". The memo puts go-karts and dune buggies in Off-road (not shown) and motocross bikes in Motorcycles without a sentence, and does not mention racing cars. A kart or a formula car is not made to go from place to place.
- *Fix.* Add precedence sentence P11: *"A motor vehicle made for racing, track or off-road sport (racing car, kart, motocross, enduro or trials motorcycle, dune buggy, drag car) is G1 under the leaf of its body; racing is a use, not a form."* This needs the owner's agreement, and E2 must state the same line. *Conforms to:* S1, S5.

**G1-11 (medium) Regional names are handled inconsistently (mamachari, ute, mini-velo).**
- *Evidence.* In the tree the mamachari is a named form under City bike ("mamachari (no, JP region tag)"). Section 7 and the section 10 C2 line call it a "regional kind". "Ute" is a non-maker named form under Pickup truck ("ute (no): Holden Ute"), but in the C9 table it is a regional name for the pickup. The C9 row for ミニベロ says "kind to settle".
- *Fix.* Under C11 a generic kind exists (city bike), so the mamachari is a non-maker named form under City bike with `region = JP`. Ute becomes a C9 homonym: (a) the Australian and New Zealand word for any pickup (an alias of Pickup truck); (b) the coupé utility (Holden Ute), a non-maker named form under Pickup truck. Buychari lists mini-velo apart from folding [BUY], so the small-wheel bike is its own kind in Bicycles. *Conforms to:* C11, C9.

**G1-12 (medium) Two maker-ledger extensions sit at the wrong level.**
- *Evidence.* `base_maker_id` is put on the maker, "for a converter, the maker of the base vehicle". A converter builds on several bases, and the base is a model, not a maker (Panama P12 on a Ford Transit Custom [PM]). The class-licensed builder (470) duplicates the C1 addition `built_by_maker_id` on lines. The maker class "kit or homebuilder" mixes a kit supplier, which is a maker, with a person who builds one aircraft, which is an individual-item fact (C16).
- *Fix.* Replace `base_maker_id` with `base_named_form_id` on the conversion's named form (or variant). Record licensed builders with `built_by_maker_id` on the class named form or line. Rename the maker class to `kit supplier`. Keep `identifier_scheme` (WMI, HIN manufacturer code) only once it is sourced, as the memo says. *Conforms to:* C1 and its additions, C16.

**G1-13 (medium) The parts sub-family has no structure, though most platform roles and tier evidence sit there.**
- *Evidence.* G1.5 is left as question 9. The memo finds "no consensus tier" for whole vehicles, and that is correct. But it did not research parts, where maker-set ladders are best known (for example the bicycle groupset hierarchies of Shimano and SRAM: reviewer, unsourced). C13 names "hitch categories" and "mounts", yet the memo has no compatibility ledger. The memo's P7 sends an aftermarket portable sat-nav to D2. The domain D review (X-11(d), O-10) proposes that electronics made to be mounted on a vehicle (car stereo, dashcam, sat-nav with a vehicle mount, bike computer, OBD tracker) are G1 under S3, and asks for "the G1 memo's agreement". The domain E review puts SPD and Look pedals in G1 as C13 rows.
- *Fix.* (a) Give G1.5 a skeleton. Its leaves follow the four vehicle sub-families: parts for land vehicles, rail, watercraft and aircraft. Kinds within each leaf follow the vehicle system. Fetch the Google parts-system list before it is used, since it was seen in an excerpt only. Fitment goes in `fits_named_form_id`. (b) Open a C13 compatibility ledger with candidate rows, each to be sourced: tow-ball and hitch classes, 7-pin and 13-pin trailer sockets, ISOFIX and i-Size for child seats, EV charging connectors (Type 1, Type 2, CCS, CHAdeMO, NACS) for wall-boxes, bicycle wheel, axle, bottom-bracket and freehub standards, and pedal-cleat systems. (c) Research tier entries for groupsets and other component lines, and record them on lines (C4a), not on kinds. (d) Adopt the domain D rule on vehicle-mounted electronics and rewrite P7 to match. *Conforms to:* C5d, C13, C4.

**G1-14 (low) Several sub-families rest on one region or on secondary pages.**
- *Evidence.* Aircraft rests on US sources only, and the FAA scheme comes through a secondary guide (Pilot Institute). Prams and strollers rest on one Wikipedia page, with no retailer, yet 4b says "brand is a strong signal". Trucks and buses come from UNECE alone and have no named forms ("none sourced"). Motorcycles rest on one US marketplace and Wikipedia. Rail rests on a US commercial marketplace and a museum page the memo itself calls weak. The Recreational Craft Directive and the Google taxonomy are read through Wikipedia and WisePIM. The Shopify crosswalk claim and the Hagerty "40,000" figure have no row in the TSV.
- *Fix.* Before owner approval, fetch: one EU or Japanese motorcycle retailer; one baby-goods retailer for prams and strollers in two regions; one EU aircraft marketplace or the EASA licence categories; one truck or bus dealer. Drop the Shopify and "40,000" claims or mark them as search excerpts. *Conforms to:* S6, template section 1.

**G1-15 (low) Mobility scooters and wheelchairs are wrongly said to be unplaced.**
- *Evidence.* Question 8 says they are "not placed by the key; H1 may hold them". Step 2 (H2) names "mobility and assistive aids" and comes before step 11. The H2 memo states that "a mobility scooter or powered wheelchair made to compensate for a disability is H2".
- *Fix.* Remove them from question 8. Add P12: *"A mobility scooter or wheelchair made to compensate for a disability is H2 (step 2); an e-scooter or microcar for general use is G1; a car adapted for a wheelchair user is a car with `adapted = yes`."* *Conforms to:* the key, step 2.

**G1-16 (low) The watercraft propulsion question can be answered by Rule T, and inflatables need one rule.**
- *Evidence.* Question 2 asks whether paddle, wind and engine may divide Watercraft, given Appendix A's "power source". C10(b) now allows a technology split when it changes the form and a fetched comparison source treats the types separately. Mast, keel and rig against engine and helm meet the first test, and YachtWorld, Google and eBay meet the second. Question 5 (inflatable against rigid kayak) rests on one UK retailer [DEC], while the RIB is already a kind defined by construction.
- *Fix.* Cite C10(b) for the Watercraft leaves and reduce question 2 to the motorsailer rule. Add: *"A paddle craft keeps its hull kind whatever its drive (paddle, pedal or electric motor); the drive is an attribute."* For inflatables, apply one rule to kayaks, boats and RIBs: construction is a kind only where retailers in two regions split on it; otherwise it is an attribute (Appendix A: material, "unless it is the form"). *Conforms to:* C10, Appendix A.

### G2 Property & Structures

**G2-1 (high) The Land kinds overlap and are split by size, motive and market.**
- *Evidence.* "Smallholding (Vantage, Rightmove)" and "lifestyle block (REINZ, PGG ..., Professionals 'lifestyle farm')" are separate kinds, though section 7 says "'hobby farm', 'lifestyle farm' are aliases". Professionals defines the lifestyle farm by size and motive ("5 to 10 acres, not the main income") against the working farm ("100+ acres, main income"). Vantage defines the smallholding as "1 to 50 acres". "Paddock" is a grazing field sized "about one acre per horse". "Commercial forestry" against "woodland" is a market split. "Country estate" is a size and prestige label. Section 7 makes the scene "Hobby farming and lifestyle holdings" a kind, which C2 and C8 allow only when a scene names a distinct form.
- *Objects that would split.* A 4 ha New Zealand block with a house; a 10-acre UK smallholding; a 2-acre horse paddock; a 40 ha conifer plantation; a 1,000-acre English estate with tenanted farms.
- *Fix.* Make the Land leaf axis land cover and use: building land; cropland; grassland; permanent crops; woodland; water and wetland; mixed holdings. Kinds: building plot; arable land; grazing land (paddock and ranch as non-maker named forms, ranch with `region = US`); orchard; vineyard; woodland (with `managed_for` = timber, amenity or conservation; timberland and forestry as aliases); lake; smallholding (aliases lifestyle block NZ and AU, hobby farm US, homestead; C9 and C11); mixed farm. Country estate becomes a named form of mixed farm. Recreational land becomes a tag. Parcel area stays a facet. *Conforms to:* Appendix A (size, motive, market), C11, C8.

**G2-2 (high) Holdings with buildings have two homes.**
- *Evidence.* Farmland holds arable, grazing, orchard and vineyard. "Working and lifestyle holdings" holds working farm, smallholding, lifestyle block and country estate, and usability row 13 files a grazing farm with a house there "with `farm_type` = sheep/beef". So `farm_type` repeats the farmland kinds, and whether a dwelling is present decides the leaf. PGG Wrightson sells "Bare Land" and "Lifestyle with Dwelling" side by side.
- *Objects that would split.* A vineyard with a winery and a house (Farmland > vineyard, or Holdings > working farm with `farm_type = viticulture`); a grazing farm with or without a farmhouse.
- *Fix.* Adopt one rule, which also answers O5: *"Land is filed by its principal land use, whether or not it carries buildings; the dwelling and farm buildings on it are recorded as `improvements` (dwelling, farm buildings, none) in the facet schema (G2-11); a holding with no principal use is a mixed farm or a smallholding."* Drop the kind "working farm" and keep it as an alias. *Conforms to:* S1, C5c.

**G2-3 (high) The houseboat and tiny-house rules contradict G1 and test use instead of the object.** See cross-memo finding X-1. G2's sentences *"a boat used mainly for cruising ... is G1"* and *"a tiny house on wheels is G2 when it is kept in one place as a dwelling, G1 when it is a trailer used to travel"* test use, which S5 forbids. G2's recommended O1(a), "every houseboat G2", contradicts G1's P4, which puts only a "houseboat moored for good" in G2.

**G2-4 (medium) The house kinds mix attachment with form and style.**
- *Evidence.* The kind axis in Houses is "attachment to neighbouring dwellings", yet the leaf also holds courtyard house, cave dwelling, castle and barndominium, none of which is an attachment value. A riad in a medina is usually terraced and also a courtyard house. A French château (SeLoger "Chateaux") is a detached country house more often than a castle. The barndominium is a post-frame steel or barn building fitted out as a home (Wikipedia: "living space attached to a workshop or barn"). The memo treats modular, prefab and container construction as attributes "set on a foundation", so treating barn construction as a kind is inconsistent. O3 makes the bungalow an attribute on the same reasoning.
- *Fix.* Keep only attachment kinds: detached house, semi-detached house and terraced house, with link-detached, end-of-terrace and mid-terrace as named forms. Add a controlled attribute `house_form` (courtyard, cave, castle or château, barn-type, single-storey) with a generated "by house form" view. Regional forms (riad, trullo, finca, cortijo, longère, masia, casona) become non-maker named forms with a region tag. Each is filed under the attachment kind its fetched source shows, and under detached house where the source shows none. *Conforms to:* C5c, C10, C11.

**G2-5 (medium) The flat kinds mix layout, position and conversion, and "duplex" is a homonym.**
- *Evidence.* The kinds are flat, maisonette, penthouse and loft apartment. The memo itself says "Penthouse and loft in Flats mix position with layout". The alias table maps duplex to maisonette only. Zillow and the US census use "duplex" for a two-unit building, while idealista uses it for a flat on two levels.
- *Objects that would split.* A top-floor two-level penthouse (maisonette or penthouse); a converted warehouse loft with a mezzanine (loft or maisonette).
- *Fix.* Kinds: flat (on one level) and maisonette (on two or more levels; aliases dúplex and triplex in es, duplex in en-UK). Penthouse becomes a non-maker named form with `floor_position = top`. Loft becomes a non-maker named form with `build_history = conversion`. Studio stays a named form. Add C9 homonym rows: duplex (en-US) = a two-unit building; duplex (es, en-UK) = maisonette. *Conforms to:* C5c, C9.

**G2-6 (medium) Multi-dwelling buildings are split by unit count, and a two-unit building collides with the semi-detached house.**
- *Evidence.* "Apartment block" and "multi-family house (ACS 2, 3 or 4 units)" differ only by the ACS bins, which the memo lists among attributes (`units_in_building`). A US side-by-side duplex is ACS "1 attached" per unit but "2" as a building.
- *Fix.* Merge the two into one kind, "multi-unit residential building", with `units_in_building` as a facet, and triplex, three-decker and block of flats as non-maker named forms. Add the rule *"File what is owned: a whole building of two or more dwellings is a multi-unit residential building; one dwelling of a pair or row is a semi-detached or terraced house; one unit in a block is a flat or maisonette."* *Conforms to:* C6, S1.

**G2-7 (medium) Dwellings kept in place are split by site licence and use, which the memo itself treats as attributes.**
- *Evidence.* Section 4b sets "the regulatory class" as the kind axis (static caravan, holiday lodge, park home ...), while section 5 says the build standards are "attributes `build_standard`, not tiers". The C6 line says "holiday against residential use is an attribute". Bawtry says a holiday lodge is "legally a caravan up to 20 m by 6.8 m" and is built to EN 1647 or sometimes BS 3632. Site licences (holiday, leisure, residential) are classes a jurisdiction assigns (C17).
- *Objects that would split.* A twin-unit lodge built to BS 3632 on a residential park (holiday lodge or park home); a US manufactured home on owned land on a permanent foundation (park home, or detached house with factory construction); a park model RV (G2 kind here, but an RV type in RVIA, a G1 source).
- *Fix.* Kinds by physical form: static caravan (single section on its chassis); lodge or park home (twin-unit or wider; aliases lodge, park home, mobile home, manufactured home); park model; tiny house on wheels; houseboat; floating home. BS EN 1647, BS 3632, the HUD code and IRC Appendix Q become `standard_met` attributes. The site licence goes to C17. Add the rule *"A factory-built home set on a permanent foundation is a detached house with `construction = manufactured` or `modular`; one kept on its chassis or on a pitch is a static caravan or park home."* *Conforms to:* C17, C6, C10.

**G2-8 (medium) The leaves of Other buildings and structures mix purpose, form and setting; "log cabin" is a homonym; the annexe has no home.**
- *Evidence.* The leaf axis is "what the covered or enclosed space is for", but "Farm buildings" is named by setting, "Open garden structures" by openness, and "Garden rooms" carries a setting word. The 3A leaf list ("pool and hard landscape", "container") differs from the tree, where decking and patio sit under Open garden structures and the container under Storage and work. "Log cabin" cites Dunster (a garden building), Rightmove and LandWatch "Cabin" (dwellings), so two objects share one kind. Zillow lists "ADU" and LandWatch "Guest House", and Hornbach's guide calls a garden house a possible "Gaestehaus", yet no kind holds a separate dwelling in the garden. Historic England's "cart shed" is a vehicle shelter in a farm list.
- *Objects that would split.* A garden annexe with a shower and kitchenette (garden office, log cabin or dwelling); a log cabin holiday home on its own plot (G2.3 log cabin, or a detached house); a cart shed (vehicle shelter or farm building).
- *Fix.* State the purpose axis for every leaf: vehicle shelter (garage, carport, cart shed); storage and work (shed, workshop, container kept in place); rooms for people (summerhouse, garden office, garden bar, sauna cabin); animal and crop buildings (barn, stable block, granary, dovecote); shade and surface structures (gazebo, pergola, decking, patio); pools. Use the Japan Housing and Land Survey test, which is a fetched source: a building with "a room, a sink, a toilet and an entrance of its own" is a dwelling. An annexe or ADU that meets it is a detached house (with "annexe" and "ADU" as named forms), and one that does not is a garden room. Add a C9 homonym for log cabin: (a) the garden log cabin (G2.3); (b) a log house, which is a detached house with `construction = log`. *Conforms to:* C5c, C9, S1.

**G2-9 (medium) A commercial-buildings sub-family would split by market.**
- *Evidence.* O2 offers commercial, industrial and institutional buildings "in G2 as a fifth sub-family". Appendix A lists market (domestic, commercial) as a modifier. The heritage standard the memo cites (FISH) divides buildings by function, not market. Section 5 proposes a dated tier entry "on a building" for Grade A or 5A offices. A particular building is an individual item, and C16 stores no facts about individual items.
- *Fix.* If the owner admits them, file them by function: kinds shop, office building, warehouse, industrial unit, hotel and others, in a leaf "Non-residential buildings" under Other buildings and structures, with the axis "what the building is made for" (FISH). Do not create a market sub-family. Record the office grade only as a scheme the category uses (C16), never on a building. *Conforms to:* Appendix A, C16.

**G2-10 (medium) The choice-level test is quietly redefined.**
- *Evidence.* Section 4b replaces "where buyers compare quality or makers" with "the level at which the sources themselves keep a separate category with a separate rule set (census bin, planning use, building code, interment option)". For unique objects sold by place, this is reasonable. But it is what makes regulatory classes into kinds (G2-7), and that conflicts with C17.
- *Fix.* Put the variant to the owner as a stated rule: *"For objects that are unique and sold by place (land, buildings, burial places), a kind stops where the sources' search filters and census or survey categories stop; a legal or licence class alone never makes a kind."* *Conforms to:* template 4b, C17.

**G2-11 (medium) Attributes and price bands have nothing to attach to.**
- *Evidence.* The data holds kinds, named forms and variants only (BRIEF; C16). Almost every G2 attribute describes one property: `tenure`, `storeys`, `floor_area`, `plot_area`, `bedrooms`, `alc_grade`, `capacity`, `allocation`, `perpetual_care`. G2's named forms are non-maker styles with no list price. So O4's price bands for G2 would fall only on the few maker rows (garden buildings, lodges, houseboats, pool shells).
- *Fix.* Recast these as a **facet schema** per kind: the facets buyers filter on, with the standard named (ALC, census floor-area bands), and no values. Use price bands only on maker-ledger and line rows for factory-built objects, and drop O4 as a separate G2 question (the shared bands are X-3). *Conforms to:* C16, C4(d).

**G2-12 (low) The Burial leaves mix position, remains type and sharing.**
- *Evidence.* The leaves are in-ground, above-ground chamber, urn spaces, and shared or natural. A cremation (urn) plot is in-ground but holds an urn, a niche is above ground and holds an urn, and "shared or natural" pairs a sharing regime with a burial method. The memo says the skip rule applies, so leaves are optional, but the tree still uses them. O8 asks whether use rights are covered, yet step 4 already says "a burial plot or niche, however it is held".
- *Fix.* Skip the leaf level (C5b), and order the kinds: ground burial plot, cremation plot, lawn crypt, mausoleum crypt, private mausoleum, columbarium niche, green burial plot, communal grave space. Position and remains type become attributes with views. Reduce O8 to a confirmation. *Conforms to:* C5b, C5c.

**G2-13 (low) The pool kinds rest on two US dealer pages, and the skip rule is misused.**
- *Evidence.* The three construction kinds cite Thursday Pools, a fibreglass maker-dealer, and Pool & Spa Depot, both in the US. Construction is a matter of material and method (Appendix A material, "unless it is the form"). C10 covers technology and power, so it is not a clean fit. Section 10 applies "the skip rule (C5b) ... to Pools (3 kinds)", but C5b lets a sub-family skip its leaf level; it does not apply to a leaf. Natural swimming pools and plunge pools are missing.
- *Fix.* Either make one kind, "in-ground swimming pool", with a `construction` attribute and a view, or keep the three only with a non-US source. Add "natural swimming pool" if a fetched source shows it sold as its own category. Drop the C5b remark. *Conforms to:* Appendix A, C5b.

**G2-14 (low) Two attribute names clash or diverge.**
- *Evidence.* `origin` (new build, conversion) reuses the Appendix A word "origin" (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown). `propulsion` (self-propelled, towed, none) differs from G1's `assist`, `engine_primary` and `rig`.
- *Fix.* Rename it to `build_history`. Use the shared propulsion list (X-6). *Conforms to:* Appendix A.

**G2-15 (low) The memo gives two orders for named forms.**

**G2-16 (low) Several leaves rest on one region or on commercial guides.**
- *Evidence.* Houseboats and floating homes come from US sources only, mostly about Seattle, with no source for UK narrowboats or Dutch woonboten. **G2-17 (low) The claim that nothing is received under S3 is untested, and some kinds are missing.**
- *Evidence.* Bunnings lists "Shed Accessories and Attachments", which the memo does not place. The tree has no kind for a boathouse, a private jetty or pontoon (as distinct from a mooring right, which is OUT), a tennis court, a private hangar, or an island (LandSale lists "Island").
