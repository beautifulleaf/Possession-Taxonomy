## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 32, branch 1): *a tool, instrument or machine made to cut, shape, join, measure, test, lift, power or repair: hand and power tools, workshop machines, ladders, building and trade measuring and testing tools, portable generators, pumps, welding and soldering equipment, pocket knives and multi-tools, sack trucks, dollies and hand carts, tool boxes, tool bags and tool storage.* Branch note (K-194): *a self-propelled machine made to dig, lift, load or move material (excavator, skid steer, telehandler, forklift, dumper) is C1, unless it does a farm or forestry job (C7) or keeps gardens and grounds (C2).* Category list line: "hand and power tools, workshop machines, ladders, measuring and testing tools for building and trades, generators, tool storage."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name C1** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, with the decision that added each):
- Step 10 (E4 Weapons): machetes and axes made for cutting wood or vegetation are C1 or C2; kitchen and utility knives are B5 and C1. Fighting and hunting knives, daggers, butterfly and automatic knives are E4; EDC and other locking folders are C1 unless the blade is a weapon kind (karambit, push dagger) (K-83). Reloading presses and dies are C1; bullets, primers and powder are E4 (K-86).
- Step 11 (G1 Vehicles): hand carts, sack trucks and dollies are C1; wheelbarrows and garden carts are C2; shopping trolleys are A4. A machine that carries its operator while it does other work (tractor, ride-on mower, excavator) goes by that work. A vehicle made mainly to carry people or goods (Unimog, ATV, quad, utility vehicle) is G1 even when it powers implements (K-93).
- Step 17 (A4 Bags): a case made for the small items of one task follows the task: tool bag C1 (K-132).
- Step 23 (C6 Optical & Scientific): a measuring tool made for a task goes with that task; building and trade measuring tools are C1; rulers and postal scales C5; sewing tape measures C4; pool and soil test kits C2; kitchen scales B5. Instruments that measure dimensions, electrical quantities or material properties for building, trade, workshop or electronics work are C1; instruments that measure a sample for study or science are C6 (K-147). Building laser measurers are C1; golf and hunting laser rangefinders E2; rangefinding binoculars C6 (K-148).
- Step 28 (C4 Craft): tools for shaping wood, metal and stone, and for electronics work, are C1, whoever uses them. Glass and mosaic, candle and soap making, resin, basketry and floristry are C4 crafts (K-169). Cutting tools and adhesives are filed by the material or work they are made for; jewellery torches and soldering kits are C4, general soldering C1; household scissors C5 (K-171, C4-O2).
- Step 29 (C2 Garden): mowers, trimmers, leaf blowers, pressure washers, wheelbarrows are C2 and are tried before step 32. Hand-held chainsaws and pole saws are C2; axes, wedges and log splitters are C1; tractor-driven splitters and chippers are C7 (K-173). A drainage, transfer, sump or general-purpose pump is C1; water-moving pumps for gardens, pools and ponds C2; field irrigation pumps C7 (K-175).
- Step 30 (B1 Furniture & Storage): tool boxes and tool storage are C1. A workbench (a work surface made for tool work) is C1 (K-190); sawhorses and trestles made for tool work are C1 too (C1-O1). (B7 branch: a portable power station is B7, with the home battery, K-157.) (B6 decision B6-O1: a brush made to prepare or finish work, such as a wire or decorating brush, is C1.)
- Rule S3 (parts follow the whole), with the shared S3b sentences of v0_7: a component, attachment or consumable made to be fitted to or used up in one kind of object goes to that object's category, so bits and blades made for a tool family stay in C1. A battery pack or charger made for a maker's cordless platform is C1, whatever tools or garden machines the platform runs; a loose standard-size cell, 18650 included, is C8 (K-4); a generic charger is D1 and a charger for standard cells C8 (K-3). Welding wire and rods, solder, a grinding disc or a sanding sheet cut for one sander is C1; sandpaper and abrasive sheets and rolls for general use are C8 (K-12). Tools made to service a vehicle, bicycle or machine are C1; only parts, attachments and consumables follow the vehicle (K-13). A dust extractor made for workshop machines is C1; a wet-and-dry vacuum is B6 (K-18). Fuels are C8; cleaning products are B6 whatever they are used in.
- Step 32 branch 2 (C8): fasteners, hinges, fittings, plumbing and electrical supplies, extension leads, lubricants, fuels, packing materials are C8, not C1.

**Collectors and the resale market.**
- **Wikipedia, Antique tool** [S20]: collectors organise by maker (Stanley, Disston, Norris, Miller's Falls), by type (hammers, braces, axes, saws, patented and transitional planes), by period, by place, by occupation (cooper, machinist, watchmaker) and by "type study" of a single model (Stanley #6 jointer, Norris A5 smoother). It names the Mid-West Tool Collectors Association, the Early American Industries Association, the Tools and Trades History Society and the Hand Tool Preservation Association of Australia. Maker, period and place are collector axes, not tool-type axes.
- **Japanese hand-tool dealers** [S9, S10, S11]: Japamart groups by type (Kanna planes, Nomi chisels, Nokogiri saws, Genno hammers, Marking & Measuring, Toishi whetstones, Specialty) and offers separate browse routes by brand, by steel (White #1/#2, Blue #1/#2, Aogami Super, Swedish, tamahagane, HSS) and by forging region (Sanjo, Echigo Yoita, Banshu Miki, Ono, Kyoto). Suzuki-ya divides chisels first by use (striking versus push/paring), then lists traditional sub-types (oire-nomi bench, chu-tataki-nomi, atsu-nomi, tateguya mortise; usu-nomi, shinogi-nomi, tsuki-nomi, yama-ari-nomi dovetail) and then by named smith (Ohuchi, Takamichi, Fujikawa Yasaku).

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **UNSPSC** [S13]: segment 27 Tools and General Machinery; family 27110000 Hand tools has 19 classes by action or job: cutting/crimping/punching, forming, wrenches and drivers, measuring and layout, rough and finishing, agriculture/forestry/garden, holding and clamping, masonry and concrete, marking, fastener setting, prying and bending, sealing, power tools, tool attachments and accessories, dispensing, brushes, pulling, tool kits, precision hand tools. The sibling families are hydraulic, pneumatic and automotive specialty. UNSPSC therefore puts power tools inside "hand tools" as one class and keeps accessories and kits as classes.
- **Harmonized System, chapter 82 and heading 8467** [S14, S15]: chapter 82 covers non-powered hand tools by action (8201 agricultural hand tools; 8202 saws; 8203 files, pliers, cutters, punches; 8204 spanners and wrenches; 8205 other hand tools, vices, clamps, anvils; 8206 sets; 8207 interchangeable tools; 8208 machine knives). Powered hand tools go to heading 8467, which the EU Combined Nomenclature splits first by energy (pneumatic; self-contained electric motor; other motor) and then by action (drills; saws, with chainsaws and circular saws; grinders and sanders; planers; hedge trimmers), with parts separate. Customs thus separates hand from powered, keeps accessories apart, and divides powered tools by energy first.
- **Battery alliances** [S5, S6, S25]: the Cordless Alliance System (over 50 brands, 500+ tools, "100% compatibility in the 18V class") and Bosch's Power for All and AMPShare alliances show that a battery platform spans several makers; Bosch states that its professional 18V system and its DIY system "will not be unified" because professionals and DIYers have different requirements.

**Museums and reference works.**
- **Japan House London** [S12]: a 1943 survey of carpenters in Ōta Ward, Tokyo, found 179 tool types needed to build a full-quality building (72 for cheaper construction); a 1985 survey found only 20 types in frequent use and 59 occasional. The essay groups tools by job in construction order: axes (kiri-ono, wari-ono, masakiri), marking (sumi-kake, kebiki), saws (nokogiri), planes (kanna, awase-kanna), hammers (genno: ryōguchi, funate, kataguchi) and chisels (striking versus paring).
- **Wikipedia, Hand tool** [S21]: general categories attributed to the American Industrial Hygiene Association are wrenches, pliers, cutters, striking tools, struck or hammered tools, screwdrivers, vises, clamps, snips, saws, drills and knives; portable power tools are excluded.

**Where the sources agree.** Every retailer and every classification separates (a) hand tools, (b) powered hand tools, (c) stationary or bench machinery and (d) accessories and consumables. Below that, the stable axis is the job the tool does (saw, drill, grind, plane, fasten, clamp, measure). Measuring tools are the most ambiguous item: Bunnings, UNSPSC and Screwfix keep them under hand tools; Starrett and Mitutoyo run them as a separate product family [S28, S29]. (The earlier draft also cited Bosch [S24] for this; the S24 page is the Bosch blue versus green page and shows no measuring family, so Bosch is dropped from the claim. A Bosch measuring-family page would need a fetch †.)

**Where they split the same objects differently.** (1) Screwfix and Axminster mix trade or activity groups (plumbing, tiling, woodturning, metal working) with action groups [S3, S19]. (2) Customs splits powered tools by energy (pneumatic, electric, other) where retailers split by action and treat cordless versus corded as a filter [S14, S23]. (3) Retailers treat the battery platform as a facet or a shopping route [S23, S26, S35]; no classification treats it as a tool type. (4) Retail trees file pressure washers, compressors, chainsaws, worklights and radios under power tools [S1, S27], while the framework key sends pressure washers to C2. (5) Mitutoyo and Starrett sell precision gauging to machinists and quality labs [S28, S29], a different buyer from the tape-measure and level buyer.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence on what owners and buyers decide first.**
1. **Mechanism class: hand, powered hand-held, or machine.** It is the first cut at Bunnings [S1], Axminster (Hand Tools / Power Tools / Machinery / Accessories) [S19], Hornbach [S4], Grizzly (Woodworking / Metalworking machinery) [S16], the UN classification [S13] and the customs schedule [S14, S15]. Wikipedia's definition of a hand tool excludes portable power tools [S21].
2. **For powered tools, the battery platform comes before the tool.** Home Depot gives Battery Platform its own filter [S23]; CordlessPicker states that "the battery decides everything downstream" [S7]; Bosch lets owners "buy bare tools" because the system is compatible back to 2008 [S25]; Cainz and OBI list tools body-only (本体のみ, "ohne Akku") and Komeri and OBI sell their own battery-sharing series [S26, S27, S35]. The questions buyers ask are therefore "which platform" and "pro or DIY grade", followed by the job.
3. **Then the job (action).** Every tree below the first cut divides by the work done: drilling, sawing, grinding and sanding, routing and planing, fastening, gripping, striking [S1, S2, S13, S15, S26, S27].
4. **Then the grade.** Bosch blue versus green [S24], Komeri's "pro" and "DIY" leaf labels [S26], Axminster's Occasional/Regular/Frequent use rating [S19], and Pro Tool Reviews' Pro versus DIY/Prosumer classes [S8] all place the grade after the tool type.
5. **For hand tools in the premium and collector market, the maker and the material come before the finer tool type.** Hida Tool's main facet is brand [S10]; Japamart has parallel browse routes by brand, steel and forging region beside tool type [S9]; collectors choose maker, period or place [S20].

**Conclusion for the first axis.** Buyers' first cut is the mechanism class (how the tool is driven and mounted), but that is power source and installation, which Appendix A lists as modifiers ("power source and technology"; "installation"), and the shared contract keeps modifiers off the tree (conventions C10 and C12). The first axis on the tree is therefore **the work done**, using the verbs of the C1 membership test (cut, drill and drive, shape, strike and fasten, join, grip, measure, lift, power) plus tool storage and the S3 parts sub-family. The work done is also the axis every source uses directly below its mechanism cut (point 3). Mechanism is carried by two attributes on every kind, `drive` and `mount`, and the retail cut "Hand tools / Power tools / Workshop machinery" is a generated view built from them. Platform, grade, maker, steel and trade are second-order and belong on the tool as attributes, ledgers or generated views. **Decided (C-1, ruling):** the first level is the work done (12 sub-families); hand, power and machine are the `drive` and `mount` attributes with generated views. No C1-only exception to Appendix A is made (Q1; Q10 is answered by CV-24 and CV-28, rules T and M as conventions C10 and C12 state them).

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: the category has four levels below it at most (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; convention C5(a)). Power source, voltage, battery platform, brand, grade and trade are never levels.

### Candidate A (the retail cut; rejected by decision C-1): equipment class → work action → tool type → named form

| Level | Stated axis | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | How the equipment is driven, mounted and used (hand, held-and-powered, bench/stationary machine, process or power equipment, measuring, access and handling, storage, accessories) | 8 sub-families |
| Leaf | The work done (cut, plane and chisel, strike, fasten, grip, drill and drive, grind and sand...) or, for non-tool sub-families, the thing measured, reached or carried | about 45 leaves |
| Kind | Generic tool type a person owns one of | about 400–500 kinds (estimate) |
| Named form | Model, series or traditional pattern | open-ended |

Evidence for this sub-family axis: Bunnings, Axminster, Hornbach, Grizzly, UNSPSC and the customs schedule all make the hand / powered / machine / accessory cut first [S1, S4, S13–S17, S19].

**Why it is no longer recommended (review C, F1).** The level mixes two axes: sub-families 1 to 3 are cut by power source and mounting (Appendix A modifiers), sub-families 4 to 8 by function or part status. The result splits objects between homes:
1. *Mitre saw and tile saw*: motor-driven, bench-mounted but carried to site; Komeri lists the sliding mitre saw beside hand-held circular saws [S26], Grizzly sells jobsite table saws as machines [S16]. They fit "hand-held power" and "machine".
2. *Soldering iron, glue gun and heat gun*: Bunnings files glue and heat guns with power tools [S1]; soldering is "welding and soldering". Two homes.
3. *Air compressor*: site power or machine.
4. *Hand plane against power planer*: different sub-families, although buyers move between them (Axminster's advice list includes "Making the Leap from Hand Tools to Machines" [S17]).
5. *Combination machines* and *multi-process welders* [S16, S17, S34], and *pneumatic and hydraulic tools*, which customs and UNSPSC separate by energy [S13, S14].

Rejected by decision C-1 (ruling): no C1-only exception to Appendix A. Kept here for the record; its cut survives only as the generated mechanism views.

### Candidate B (decided, C-1): work done → tool family → kind → named form

| Level | Stated axis | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | The work done: the verbs of the C1 membership test (cut, drill and drive, shape, strike and fasten, join, grip, measure, lift, power), plus tool storage and carrying; C1.11 holds multi-use kinds (convention C14(c)); C1.12 is the closing S3 parts sub-family "Parts and accessories made for one tool" (exempt from the level-1 axis, convention C5(d), CV-17) | 12 sub-families |
| Leaf | Stated per sub-family (convention C5(c)); in the action sub-families it is the **tool family** (saws, planes, sanders), so hand, powered and machine versions of a family sit in one leaf | 36 leaves in the worked tree |
| Kind | A generic tool type whose form differs (panel saw, circular saw and cabinet table saw are three kinds in one leaf), with `drive` and `mount` as attributes | about 400–500 kinds (estimate, section 4) |
| Named form | Model, series or traditional pattern, with `maker_bound: yes | no` (convention C2) | uncapped; harvested in stage 3 (CV-7) |

**Rules that go with it.**
- *Attributes for mechanism (C10, C12; C-1, CV-24, CV-28):* every kind carries `drive` (manual | corded electric | cordless | pneumatic | hydraulic | engine) and, where it applies, `mount` (hand-held | bench | stationary | self-propelled). A manual and a powered version of the same form share one kind; a powered tool of a different form is its own kind (circular saw against panel saw). Technology defines a kind only under rule T (C10): it changes the physical form or the installation, and a fetched comparison source tests the types separately (inverter against open-frame generator [S33]; MIG, TIG and stick welders [S34]).
- *Generated views:* "Hand tools", "Power tools", "Workshop machinery" and "Cordless" from `drive` and `mount`; one "Cordless" view per platform from the platform link (a C13 `compatibility_relation` row from the named form to its `platform` row; CV-29).
- *Tie-break for multi-action tools:* file by the primary action the maker names (convention C14, CV-31). A combination machine is its own kind when one fetched seller lists it as a class, filed in the leaf of its primary action; otherwise it is filed by its primary action (C-10). The oscillating multi-tool goes to Cutting & sawing, the rotary tool and the angle grinder to Shaping & finishing, the heat gun to Joining & heat.
- *Regional types (C2(c), C11; CV-26, CV-27):* a traditional type that no generic kind covers is a kind named generically by its form, with the native word as its name in the alias table (double-edged pull saw, ryoba). By the C11 level tie-break it is a kind only when fetched sellers in two regions give it its own category; otherwise it is a non-maker named form under the nearest generic kind, and it is still in (two regions decide level, never inclusion). Where a generic kind exists, the traditional type is a named form without a maker (`maker_bound: no`) under it, tagged with the region (oire-nomi under bench (striking) chisel). A region never names a sub-family or a leaf.
- *Sub-families with about 12 kinds or fewer skip the leaf level* (C5(b)); their kinds are stored with `leaf = null`.

Evidence for the axis: UNSPSC classes by job, with power tools as one class [S13]; Komeri's function route [S26]; every retailer and classification divides by the work done directly below its mechanism cut [S1, S2, S13, S15, S26, S27]; the collector and hobby habit of comparing a hand plane with a power planer [S17].

Where it breaks, and the answer:
1. *Cordless versus corded versions of one tool* sit together by action but buyers shop them by platform [S23]. Answer: `drive` plus the C13 platform link, with a generated Cordless view per platform.
2. *Oscillating multi-tools, rotary tools and heat guns* have no single action. Answer: the primary-action tie-break above.
3. *Generators, compressors, ladders, tool boxes* have no work action. Answer: they take the other verbs of the membership test (lift, power) and its "tool storage"; none needs a catch-all.
4. *Machine-only items* (lathe, mill, press) have no hand equivalent. Harmless: the leaf simply holds no manual kind.
5. *Work surfaces and extraction* (workbench, sawhorse, dust extractor), decided C1 by K-190, C1-O1 and K-18, have no tool action of their own. Answer: workbenches, sawhorses and trestles hold the workpiece and join C1.6, renamed Gripping, clamping & work holding; a dust extractor serves machines in several sub-families and goes to the multi-use sub-family C1.11 (C14(c)).

**Worked example tree (149 kinds, with named forms where a source names them).** Sources in brackets. **†** marks a kind that still needs a fetched attestation (rule S6; attested in the stage-3 harvest); "(key)" means the name is in the CATEGORIES_v0_6 or v0_7 text only, which is not an attestation, so these carry † too. ## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers. Finer distinctions below that are named forms or attributes (convention C2(a)).

**Recommended stop for C1: the tool type defined by what it does to the work and by its form, with one exception that goes a step finer and one that stays coarser.**

1. **Hand tools: tool type, split wherever the working action or the form differs.**
   - Evidence for splitting: planes are bought by role and length (smoother, jack, jointer), not as one "plane" [S22]; chisels divide into striking and push (paring) types with different construction [S11, S12]; the pull saws are sold as separate types: the fine backed dozuki for joinery, the double-edged ryoba and the flush-cutting azebiki [S9]. Each of these is a kind, named generically by its form with the native word as alias (convention C11; CV-26): double-edged pull saw, backed pull saw, flush-cut pull saw; bench (striking) chisel, paring chisel, mortise chisel; pull plane (kanna); double-faced hammer (genno). The pull saws, the pull plane and the double-faced hammer are kinds provisional until attested by fetched sellers in two regions (C11 level tie-break, CV-27); if that fails each becomes a non-maker named form under the nearest generic kind and stays in. The traditional chisel sub-types that a generic kind already covers (oire-nomi under bench (striking) chisel; usu-nomi under paring chisel; tateguya-nomi under mortise chisel [S11]), the hammer patterns (ryōguchi, funate, kataguchi [S12]), the Stanley numbers, the Norris A5 and the Gyokucho model numbers are named forms [S10, S20, S22].
   - Evidence for stopping: size, steel, handle, finish and tooth count are filters on one type, not types: Bunnings and Screwfix leaves are the type (Hammers, Pliers, Screwdrivers) [S1, S3], Hornbach offers brand as a filter on its 9,911 hand-tool results [S4], and Screwfix filters ladders by height, material, tread count and brand [S30]. Japanese dealers list steel (White #2, Blue #1, Aogami Super, Swedish, HSS) and forging region as separate browse routes next to tool type [S9], so steel is an attribute and region a tag.
2. **Powered tools: the action mechanism, because buyers compare within it.**
   - Drill/driver, hammer drill, impact driver, rotary hammer and impact wrench are kept apart by Screwfix (Drills versus Impact Drivers & Wrenches) [S2], by Komeri (impact driver, vibration drill, hammer drill, impact wrench) [S26] and by Pro Tool Reviews, whose head-to-head is the hammer drill [S8]. Each is a kind.
   - Voltage class, brushless motor, corded versus cordless (`drive`), battery platform (C13 platform link), kit versus bare unit (attributes of a model, C-13) and DIY/prosumer/pro grade are attributes or tier entries: Screwfix sells "Bare Units" and "Kits & Twinpacks" as formats of the same tool [S2], Home Depot filters on platform and voltage [S23], Komeri repeats "18V pro / 36V pro / AC pro / DIY" across every action group [S26], Bosch sells blue and green versions of one kind of drill [S24]. Because the same grade labels repeat under every kind, they are modifiers (section 5).
   - A hand and a powered version of the same form share one kind only when the form is the same; a power planer and a smoothing plane differ in form, so they are two kinds in one leaf.
3. **Machines: one step deeper where the construction class changes the body and the use** (the exception).
   - Grizzly divides table saws into Jobsite, Hybrid, Cabinet, Sliding Table and Rip [S16]; Axminster divides lathes into Engineering (metal) and Woodturning, and planers into Planer Thicknessers and Thicknessers [S17, S19]. Each is a kind (the body differs, convention C12(a)); a combination machine such as the planer-thicknesser is its own kind because a fetched seller lists it as a class (C-10). Benchtop versus stationary appears under planers, drill presses, lathes, mortisers and scroll saws [S16]; where it repeats across types it is a `mount` and size-class attribute, not a kind.
4. **Measuring tools: instrument type** (tape, level, laser distance measurer, caliper, micrometer, indicator). Accuracy grade, resolution and range are attributes; makers such as Starrett and Mitutoyo are compared by line and tolerance [S28, S29].
5. **Ladders: ladder form** (step, platform step, combination [S30]; extension and telescopic †). The forms rest on one fetched seller, Screwfix [S30], which is enough to admit them (owner one-source rule); the Werner page [S31] was unreadable. The combination ladder is its own kind (C-10). Material (aluminium, fibreglass) and height band are attributes (Appendix A: material is a modifier unless it changes the form).
6. **Parts and accessories: accessory type** (drill bit, saw blade, grinding disc). Bit diameter, shank, tooth count and grit are attributes. Reloading dies (K-86) are parts of the reloading press. The platform battery pack is a kind in C1.9 whose platform is a C13 platform link [S23, S25].
7. **Tool storage: container form** (portable toolbox, wheeled toolbox, modular stackable tool case, tool bag); material is an attribute (C2(b)) and the stacking system (PACKOUT, TSTAK, ToughSystem and others) is a C13 platform row that the case links to [S32].

**The exception that stays coarser.** Hand-tool families that retailers treat as one leaf with many size variants (spanners and sockets, hex keys, clamps) are one kind per recognisable type, with size, drive and finish as attributes (convention C6). Types that buyers compare separately (combination spanner, ratchet, adjustable wrench) are separate kinds [S1, S15]. Measuring-tape length, clamp capacity, bit diameter and sheet size are never kinds. A set of objects of one kind (a socket set) is filed as that kind (K-1).

**Where the level differs inside C1.** Hand tools and machines are finer than powered hand-held tools because quality differences sit in the type itself (a paring chisel versus a bench chisel; a cabinet saw versus a jobsite saw). For powered hand-held tools quality sits in the platform, grade and motor spec, which are attributes and tier entries.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Choice: consensus-ranked, with a spec layer underneath.** No fetched page shows a formal standard that ranks tools from DIY to professional. The ordering in use is set by makers, retailers and review sites, and each maps to measurable specs. (Safety and performance standards for power tools, for example EN or IEC test standards, exist but no page in this batch was fetched to attest them †; where one is attested in stage 3 it is the attribute `standard_met`, never a tier or a kind, CV-36.)

**The orderings found, with their region.**
1. **Duty grade (consensus, three or four steps).**
   - Three steps, UK, one maker: Axminster rates machines Occasional use (light DIY, lower power, lighter build), Regular use (dedicated hobbyist or small professional workshop) and Frequent use (daily professional use, "not industrial grade"), by "how frequently and for how long the machine is expected to be used", with type of work, user and manufacturer also considered [S19]. Machine Atlas (UK) records the same maker's lines as Craft/Hobby, Trade, Industrial [S18].
   - Two steps, Germany/UK, one maker: Bosch splits professional (blue: daily use, multi-day jobs, extended warranty) from DIY (green: occasional use, short jobs), and attaches specs (GSR 18-2-LI Plus 63 Nm and 1,900 rpm versus PSR 18 LI-2 46 Nm and 1,340 rpm) [S24].
   - Two steps, Japan, one retailer: Komeri's leaf labels say pro or DIY (and 18V, 36V or AC) [S26].
   - Three or four steps, US, two review sites: Pro Tool Reviews uses Pro, Prosumer/Serious DIY and DIY, with brand lists per class [S8]; CordlessPicker labels each platform Professional, Prosumer, DIY & homeowner or Budget [S7].

## 6. Makers and brands

**Choice: platform.** For powered hand-held tools, tool batteries and tool storage, the maker sets compatibility: a battery fits only its own platform or an alliance of platforms, and a stacking case fits only its own system.
- *Evidence*: Home Depot gives Battery Platform its own filter [S23]; Bosch promises 18V compatibility back to 2008 (professional) and 2007 (DIY) and sells bare tools without batteries [S6, S25]; the Cordless Alliance System lists "over 50 brands" and "500+ tools" on one 18V battery, and Bosch's Power for All and AMPShare alliances do the same on separate systems [S5, S6, S25]; Komeri sells its own battery-sharing B-Share series and HiKOKI's MultiVolt batteries as products in their own right [S26]; Screwfix filters toolboxes by "Compatible Stacking System" (PACKOUT, TSTAK, ToughSystem, BUNKER Load-Up, Connecx, E-Case) [S32]; OBI sells store-brand and maker battery systems under named families (Power X-Change, 1 PowerSystem) [S35].

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Decided axis (convention C7; CV-20, CV-21): kinds canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states; named forms without a maker first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically, then maker-bound named forms by maker, first-introduction year and alphabet; variants by code or year.**
- *Secondary keys per sub-family*: the action sub-families (C1.1 to C1.5) order kinds by `drive` and `mount` from manual hand-held to stationary machine, then by size or capacity ascending (in Saws: panel saw, pull saws, circular saw, jigsaw, mitre saw, table saws, bandsaws); C1.7 by resolution, coarse to fine; C1.8 by height or load ascending; C1.6 by the work held, from hand-held to floor-standing; C1.9 by output ascending; C1.10 by container size; C1.11 kits first, then shared equipment.
- *Reason*: the sources already order by role then size. Highland orders planes by the Stanley number from 1 to 8, which tracks length and role (smoothers, jacks, jointers) [S22]; Axminster orders machines by duty class Occasional → Regular → Frequent [S19]; Grizzly orders benchtop before stationary and jobsite before cabinet [S16]; Komeri orders power-tool leaves by action and then by voltage class [S26].
- *Leaf order inside a sub-family*: by stage of the job (mark and measure → cut → shape and smooth → join and fasten → grip → finish), which is the order in which the Japan House essay presents carpenters' tools (axes, marking, saws, planes, hammers and chisels) [S12]. Alphabetical order is the fallback.
- *Named forms* (C7, CV-21): patterns with no maker (`maker_bound: no`, such as the Stanley numbers and Japanese sub-types) come first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically. Maker ladders (`line_rank`) and benchmark orders are generated views. The collector's series-number order (Stanley No. 1 to 8, then type) is a generated "type study" view [S20, S22], not the stored order.
- *Not by price or popularity*: the sources use "top sellers" and price sorts as views [S3, S30], which belong to generated pages.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure.** C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment is filed by the work done (decision C-1, ruling): category → sub-family (the verbs of the membership test) → leaf (tool family, or the axis each non-action sub-family states) → kind (generic tool type whose form differs) → named form (model or traditional pattern, with `maker_bound`), at most four levels below the category with the named form counted (C5(a)). The twelve sub-families are C1.1 Cutting & sawing; C1.2 Drilling, driving & turning; C1.3 Shaping & finishing; C1.4 Striking & fastening; C1.5 Joining & heat; C1.6 Gripping, clamping & work holding (workbenches, sawhorses and trestles, K-190, C1-O1); C1.7 Measuring, layout & testing; C1.8 Lifting, access & materials handling (earth-moving and handling machines, K-194); C1.9 Power & air supply (platform batteries and chargers, K-4, K-3; pumps, K-175); C1.10 Tool storage & carrying; C1.11 Multi-use tools & kits (C14(c): tool kit, K-1; dust extraction, K-18); and the closing parts sub-family C1.12 Parts and accessories made for one tool (C5(d), CV-17; consumables cut for one tool, K-12). C1.6, C1.10 and C1.11 skip the leaf level (C5(b)). Hand, power and machine are the `drive` and `mount` attributes with generated Hand tools, Power tools, Workshop machinery and per-platform Cordless views (C-1; C10, C12). Kinds stop at the tool type defined by what it does and by its form, one step finer for machines by construction class, with combination machines as kinds where one fetched seller lists the class (C-10). Japanese types are kinds named generically by form or non-maker named forms under generic kinds, with region tags, school rows and native names in the alias table (CV-26, CV-27). Tiers are per-source tier entries on makers, lines, named forms and variants, each source's own scheme stored and never merged (C-9, CV-10, CV-12); spec ladders are attributes; Komeri's pro and DIY labels are dated platform tags; price is the C4(d) band only. The domain-wide maker ledger, lines table, maker-relations table, standing school table, C13 compatibility ledger (battery platforms, alliances and stacking storage systems), named-form, variant and alias tables are uncapped and seeded from existing catalogues in stage 3, one fetched page per row (CV-1 to CV-8, CV-29, C-13). Scene tags are used wherever a C1 community exists, each once its definition source is fetched (CV-22). Legal classes (knife carry classes) go to the C17 ledger. Order inside a leaf follows C7 as amended by CV-21 (section 8). Boundaries are the decided sentences of section 9.
