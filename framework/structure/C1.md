## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 32, branch 1): *a tool, instrument or machine made to cut, shape, join, measure, test, lift, power or repair: hand and power tools, workshop machines, ladders, building and trade measuring and testing tools, portable generators, pumps, welding and soldering equipment, pocket knives and multi-tools, sack trucks, dollies and hand carts, tool boxes, tool bags and tool storage.* Category list line: "hand and power tools, workshop machines, ladders, measuring and testing tools for building and trades, generators, tool storage."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name C1:**
- Step 10 (E4 Weapons): machetes and axes made for cutting wood or vegetation are C1 or C2; kitchen and utility knives are B5 and C1.
- Step 11 (G1 Vehicles): hand carts, sack trucks and dollies are C1; wheelbarrows and garden carts are C2; shopping trolleys are A4. A machine that carries its operator while it does other work (tractor, ride-on mower, excavator) goes by that work.
- Step 17 (A4 Bags): tool bags are C1.
- Step 23 (C6 Optical & Scientific): a measuring tool made for a task goes with that task; building and trade measuring tools are C1; rulers and postal scales C5; sewing tape measures C4; pool and soil test kits C2; kitchen scales B5.
- Step 28 (C4 Craft): tools for shaping wood, metal and stone, and for electronics work, are C1, whoever uses them.
- Step 30 (B1 Furniture & Storage): tool boxes and tool storage are C1.
- Step 29 (C2 Garden): mowers, trimmers, leaf blowers, pressure washers, wheelbarrows are C2 and are tried before step 32.
- Rule S3 (parts follow the whole): a component, attachment or consumable made to be fitted to or used up in one kind of object goes to that object's category. Bits and blades made for a tool family therefore stay in C1. A battery pack made for a maker's cordless platform serves many kinds of tool and garden machine, so S3 does not decide it (review X3; section 9, OWNER: Q23). "Household batteries" are C8; fuels are C8; cleaning products are B6 whatever they are used in.
- Step 32 branch 2 (C8): fasteners, hinges, fittings, plumbing and electrical supplies, extension leads, lubricants, fuels, packing materials are C8, not C1.

**Enthusiast communities and reference sites.**
- **Pro Tool Reviews (US)** [S8]: ranks cordless drills in a scored head-to-head, labels classes Pro, "DIY/Prosumer" and DIY, and lists brands by user type (Prosumer/Serious DIY: Craftsman, Masterforce, Ryobi, Skil; DIY: Black and Decker, Greenworks, HART, Worx). - **Machine Atlas (UK)** [S18]: lists machines by maker and type; its Axminster entry records the maker's own ladder Craft/Hobby, Trade, Industrial.

**Collectors and the resale market.**
- **Wikipedia, Antique tool** [S20]: collectors organise by maker (Stanley, Disston, Norris, Miller's Falls), by type (hammers, braces, axes, saws, patented and transitional planes), by period, by place, by occupation (cooper, machinist, watchmaker) and by "type study" of a single model (Stanley #6 jointer, Norris A5 smoother). It names the Mid-West Tool Collectors Association, the Early American Industries Association, the Tools and Trades History Society and the Hand Tool Preservation Association of Australia. Maker, period and place are collector axes, not tool-type axes.
- **Japanese hand-tool dealers** [S9, S10, S11]: Japamart groups by type (Kanna planes, Nomi chisels, Nokogiri saws, Genno hammers, Marking & Measuring, Toishi whetstones, Specialty) and offers separate browse routes by brand, by steel (White #1/#2, Blue #1/#2, Aogami Super, Swedish, tamahagane, HSS) and by forging region (Sanjo, Echigo Yoita, Banshu Miki, Ono, Kyoto). Hida Tool's main facet is a brand list of about 60 Japanese makers and it lists named products such as the Gyokucho #661–#667 ryoba saws. Suzuki-ya divides chisels first by use (striking versus push/paring), then lists traditional sub-types (oire-nomi bench, chu-tataki-nomi, atsu-nomi, tateguya mortise; usu-nomi, shinogi-nomi, tsuki-nomi, yama-ari-nomi dovetail) and then by named smith (Ohuchi, Takamichi, Fujikawa Yasaku).

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **UNSPSC** [S13]: segment 27 Tools and General Machinery; family 27110000 Hand tools has 19 classes by action or job: cutting/crimping/punching, forming, wrenches and drivers, measuring and layout, rough and finishing, agriculture/forestry/garden, holding and clamping, masonry and concrete, marking, fastener setting, prying and bending, sealing, power tools, tool attachments and accessories, dispensing, brushes, pulling, tool kits, precision hand tools. The sibling families are hydraulic, pneumatic and automotive specialty. UNSPSC therefore puts power tools inside "hand tools" as one class and keeps accessories and kits as classes.
- **Harmonized System, chapter 82 and heading 8467** [S14, S15]: chapter 82 covers non-powered hand tools by action (8201 agricultural hand tools; 8202 saws; 8203 files, pliers, cutters, punches; 8204 spanners and wrenches; 8205 other hand tools, vices, clamps, anvils; 8206 sets; 8207 interchangeable tools; 8208 machine knives). Powered hand tools go to heading 8467, which the EU Combined Nomenclature splits first by energy (pneumatic; self-contained electric motor; other motor) and then by action (drills; saws, with chainsaws and circular saws; grinders and sanders; planers; hedge trimmers), with parts separate. Customs thus separates hand from powered, keeps accessories apart, and divides powered tools by energy first.
- **Battery alliances** [S5, S6, S25]: the Cordless Alliance System (over 50 brands, 500+ tools, "100% compatibility in the 18V class") and Bosch's Power for All and AMPShare alliances show that a battery platform spans several makers; Bosch states that its professional 18V system and its DIY system "will not be unified" because professionals and DIYers have different requirements.

**Museums and reference works.**
- **Japan House London** [S12]: a 1943 survey of carpenters in Ōta Ward, Tokyo, found 179 tool types needed to build a full-quality building (72 for cheaper construction); a 1985 survey found only 20 types in frequent use and 59 occasional. The essay groups tools by job in construction order: axes (kiri-ono, wari-ono, masakiri), marking (sumi-kake, kebiki), saws (nokogiri), planes (kanna, awase-kanna), hammers (genno: ryōguchi, funate, kataguchi) and chisels (striking versus paring).
- **Wikipedia, Hand tool** [S21]: general categories attributed to the American Industrial Hygiene Association are wrenches, pliers, cutters, striking tools, struck or hammered tools, screwdrivers, vises, clamps, snips, saws, drills and knives; portable power tools are excluded.

**Where the sources agree.** Every retailer and every classification separates (a) hand tools, (b) powered hand tools, (c) stationary or bench machinery and (d) accessories and consumables. Below that, the stable axis is the job the tool does (saw, drill, grind, plane, fasten, clamp, measure). Measuring tools are the most ambiguous item: Bunnings, UNSPSC and Screwfix keep them under hand tools; Starrett and Mitutoyo run them as a separate product family [S28, S29]. (The earlier draft also cited Bosch [S24] for this; the S24 page is the Bosch blue versus green page and shows no measuring family, so Bosch is dropped from the claim. A Bosch measuring-family page would need a fetch †.)

**Where they split the same objects differently.** (1) Screwfix and Axminster mix trade or activity groups (plumbing, tiling, woodturning, metal working) with action groups [S3, S19]. (2) Customs splits powered tools by energy (pneumatic, electric, other) where retailers split by action and treat cordless versus corded as a filter [S14, S23]. (3) Retailers treat the battery platform as a facet or a shopping route [S23, S26, S35]; no classification treats it as a tool type. (4) Retail trees file pressure washers, compressors, chainsaws, worklights and radios under power tools [S1, S27], while the framework key sends pressure washers to C2. (5) Mitutoyo and Starrett sell precision gauging to machinists and quality labs [S28, S29], a different buyer from the tape-measure and level buyer.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence on what owners and buyers decide first.**
1. **Mechanism class: hand, powered hand-held, or machine.** It is the first cut at Bunnings [S1], Axminster (Hand Tools / Power Tools / Machinery / Accessories) [S19], Hornbach [S4], Grizzly (Woodworking / Metalworking machinery) [S16], the UN classification [S13] and the customs schedule [S14, S15]. Wikipedia's definition of a hand tool excludes portable power tools [S21].
2. **For powered tools, the battery platform comes before the tool.** Home Depot gives Battery Platform its own filter [S23]; CordlessPicker states that "the battery decides everything downstream" [S7]; Bosch lets owners "buy bare tools" because the system is compatible back to 2008 [S25]; Cainz and OBI list tools body-only (本体のみ, "ohne Akku") and Komeri and OBI sell their own battery-sharing series [S26, S27, S35]. The questions buyers ask are therefore "which platform" and "pro or DIY grade", followed by the job.
3. **Then the job (action).** Every tree below the first cut divides by the work done: drilling, sawing, grinding and sanding, routing and planing, fastening, gripping, striking [S1, S2, S13, S15, S26, S27].
4. **Then the grade.** Bosch blue versus green [S24], Komeri's "pro" and "DIY" leaf labels [S26], Axminster's Occasional/Regular/Frequent use rating [S19], and Pro Tool Reviews' Pro versus DIY/Prosumer classes [S8] all place the grade after the tool type.
5. **For hand tools in the premium and collector market, the maker and the material come before the finer tool type.** Hida Tool's main facet is brand [S10]; Japamart has parallel browse routes by brand, steel and forging region beside tool type [S9]; collectors choose maker, period or place [S20].

**Conclusion for the first axis.** Buyers' first cut is the mechanism class (how the tool is driven and mounted), but that is power source and installation, which Appendix A lists as modifiers ("power source and technology"; "installation"), and the shared contract keeps modifiers off the tree (convention K1, C10). The first axis on the tree is therefore **the work done**, using the verbs of the C1 membership test (cut, drill and drive, shape, strike and fasten, join, grip, measure, lift, power) plus tool storage and the S3 parts sub-family. The work done is also the axis every source uses directly below its mechanism cut (point 3). Mechanism is carried by two attributes on every kind, `drive` and `mount`, and the retail cut "Hand tools / Power tools / Workshop machinery" is a generated view built from them. Platform, grade, maker, steel and trade are second-order and belong on the tool as attributes, ledgers or generated views. **OWNER:** keep this (review C recommendation), or approve the retail mechanism cut as an explicit C1-only exception to Appendix A? (Q1; also Q10 for the domain-wide K1 convention.)

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: the category has four levels below it at most (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; convention C5(a)). Power source, voltage, battery platform, brand, grade and trade are never levels.

### Candidate A (the retail cut; no longer recommended): equipment class → work action → tool type → named form

| Level | Stated axis | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | How the equipment is driven, mounted and used (hand, held-and-powered, bench/stationary machine, process or power equipment, measuring, access and handling, storage, accessories) | 8 sub-families |
| Leaf | The work done (cut, plane and chisel, strike, fasten, grip, drill and drive, grind and sand...) or, for non-tool sub-families, the thing measured, reached or carried | about 45 leaves |
| Kind | Generic tool type a person owns one of | about 400–500 kinds (estimate) |
| Named form | Model, series or traditional pattern | open-ended |

Evidence for this sub-family axis: Bunnings, Axminster, Hornbach, Grizzly, UNSPSC and the customs schedule all make the hand / powered / machine / accessory cut first [S1, S4, S13–S17, S19].

**Why it is no longer recommended (review C, F1).** The level mixes two axes: sub-families 1 to 3 are cut by power source and mounting (Appendix A modifiers), sub-families 4 to 8 by function or part status. The result splits objects between homes:
1. *Mitre saw and tile saw*: motor-driven, bench-mounted but carried to site; Komeri lists the sliding mitre saw beside hand-held circular saws [S26], Grizzly sells jobsite table saws as machines [S16]. They fit "hand-held power" and "machine".
2. *Soldering iron, glue gun and heat gun*: Bunnings files glue and heat guns with power tools [S1]; soldering is "welding and soldering". Two homes.
3. *Air compressor*: site power or machine.
4. *Hand plane against power planer*: different sub-families, although buyers move between them (Axminster's advice list includes "Making the Leap from Hand Tools to Machines" [S17]).
5. *Combination machines* and *multi-process welders* [S16, S17, S34], and *pneumatic and hydraulic tools*, which customs and UNSPSC separate by energy [S13, S14].

Kept here as the option for **OWNER:** Q1 (a C1-only exception to Appendix A).

### Candidate B (recommended): work done → tool family → kind → named form

| Level | Stated axis | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | The work done: the verbs of the C1 membership test (cut, drill and drive, shape, strike and fasten, join, grip, measure, lift, power), plus tool storage and carrying; C1.11 is the S3 parts sub-family (exempt, convention C5(d) and K7); C1.12 holds multi-use kinds (convention C14(c)) | 12 sub-families |
| Leaf | Stated per sub-family (convention C5(c)); in the action sub-families it is the **tool family** (saws, planes, sanders), so hand, powered and machine versions of a family sit in one leaf | 34 leaves in the worked tree |
| Kind | A generic tool type whose form differs (panel saw, circular saw and cabinet table saw are three kinds in one leaf), with `drive` and `mount` as attributes | about 400–500 kinds (estimate, section 4) |
| Named form | Model, series or traditional pattern, with `maker_bound: yes | no` (convention C2) | open-ended |

**Rules that go with it.**
- *Attributes for mechanism (K1, C10, C12):* every kind carries `drive` (manual | corded electric | cordless | pneumatic | hydraulic | engine) and, where it applies, `mount` (hand-held | bench | stationary | self-propelled). A manual and a powered version of the same form share one kind; a powered tool of a different form is its own kind (circular saw against panel saw). Technology defines a kind only under rule T (C10): it changes the physical form or the installation, and a fetched comparison source tests the types separately (inverter against open-frame generator [S33]; MIG, TIG and stick welders [S34]).
- *Generated views:* "Hand tools", "Power tools", "Workshop machinery" and "Cordless" from `drive` and `mount`; one "Cordless" view per platform from the `system` attribute.
- *Tie-break for multi-action tools:* file by the primary action the maker names (convention C14). The oscillating multi-tool goes to Cutting & sawing, the rotary tool and the angle grinder to Shaping & finishing, the heat gun to Joining & heat.
- *Regional types (C2(c), C11, K8):* a traditional type that no generic kind covers is a kind named generically by its form, with the native word as its name in the alias table (double-edged pull saw, ryoba). Where a generic kind exists, the traditional type is a named form without a maker (`maker_bound: no`) under it, tagged with the region (oire-nomi under bench (striking) chisel). A region never names a sub-family or a leaf.
- *Sub-families with about 12 kinds or fewer skip the leaf level* (C5(b)); their kinds are stored with `leaf = null`.

Evidence for the axis: UNSPSC classes by job, with power tools as one class [S13]; Komeri's function route [S26]; every retailer and classification divides by the work done directly below its mechanism cut [S1, S2, S13, S15, S26, S27]; the collector and hobby habit of comparing a hand plane with a power planer [S17].

Where it breaks, and the answer:
1. *Cordless versus corded versions of one tool* sit together by action but buyers shop them by platform [S23]. Answer: `drive` plus the `system` attribute, with a generated Cordless view per platform.
2. *Oscillating multi-tools, rotary tools and heat guns* have no single action. Answer: the primary-action tie-break above.
3. *Generators, compressors, ladders, tool boxes* have no work action. Answer: they take the other verbs of the membership test (lift, power) and its "tool storage"; none needs a catch-all.
4. *Machine-only items* (lathe, mill, press) have no hand equivalent. Harmless: the leaf simply holds no manual kind.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers. Finer distinctions below that are named forms or attributes (convention C2(a)).

**Recommended stop for C1: the tool type defined by what it does to the work and by its form, with one exception that goes a step finer and one that stays coarser.**

1. **Hand tools: tool type, split wherever the working action or the form differs.**
   - Evidence for splitting: planes are bought by role and length (smoother, jack, jointer), not as one "plane" [S22]; chisels divide into striking and push (paring) types with different construction [S11, S12]; the pull saws are sold as separate types: the fine backed dozuki for joinery, the double-edged ryoba and the flush-cutting azebiki [S9]. Each of these is a kind, named generically by its form with the native word as alias (convention C11, K8): double-edged pull saw, backed pull saw, flush-cut pull saw; bench (striking) chisel, paring chisel, mortise chisel; pull plane (kanna); double-faced hammer (genno). The traditional chisel sub-types that a generic kind already covers (oire-nomi under bench (striking) chisel; usu-nomi under paring chisel; tateguya-nomi under mortise chisel [S11]), the hammer patterns (ryōguchi, funate, kataguchi [S12]), the Stanley numbers, the Norris A5 and the Gyokucho model numbers are named forms [S10, S20, S22].
   - Evidence for stopping: size, steel, handle, finish and tooth count are filters on one type, not types: Bunnings and Screwfix leaves are the type (Hammers, Pliers, Screwdrivers) [S1, S3], Hornbach offers brand as a filter on its 9,911 hand-tool results [S4], and Screwfix filters ladders by height, material, tread count and brand [S30]. Japanese dealers list steel (White #2, Blue #1, Aogami Super, Swedish, HSS) and forging region as separate browse routes next to tool type [S9], so steel is an attribute and region a tag.
2. **Powered tools: the action mechanism, because buyers compare within it.**
   - Drill/driver, hammer drill, impact driver, rotary hammer and impact wrench are kept apart by Screwfix (Drills versus Impact Drivers & Wrenches) [S2], by Komeri (impact driver, vibration drill, hammer drill, impact wrench) [S26] and by Pro Tool Reviews, whose head-to-head is the hammer drill [S8]. Each is a kind.
   - Voltage class, brushless motor, corded versus cordless (`drive`), battery platform (`system`), kit versus bare unit and DIY/prosumer/pro grade are attributes or tier entries: Screwfix sells "Bare Units" and "Kits & Twinpacks" as formats of the same tool [S2], Home Depot filters on platform and voltage [S23], Komeri repeats "18V pro / 36V pro / AC pro / DIY" across every action group [S26], Bosch sells blue and green versions of one kind of drill [S24]. Because the same grade labels repeat under every kind, they are modifiers (section 5).
   - A hand and a powered version of the same form share one kind only when the form is the same; a power planer and a smoothing plane differ in form, so they are two kinds in one leaf.
3. **Machines: one step deeper where the construction class changes the body and the use** (the exception).
   - Grizzly divides table saws into Jobsite, Hybrid, Cabinet, Sliding Table and Rip [S16]; Axminster divides lathes into Engineering (metal) and Woodturning, and planers into Planer Thicknessers and Thicknessers [S17, S19]. Each is a kind (the body differs, convention C12(a)). Benchtop versus stationary appears under planers, drill presses, lathes, mortisers and scroll saws [S16]; where it repeats across types it is a `mount` and size-class attribute, not a kind.
4. **Measuring tools: instrument type** (tape, level, laser distance measurer, caliper, micrometer, indicator). Accuracy grade, resolution and range are attributes; makers such as Starrett and Mitutoyo are compared by line and tolerance [S28, S29].
5. **Ladders: ladder form** (step, platform step, combination [S30]; extension and telescopic †). The forms rest on one source, Screwfix [S30]; the Werner page [S31] was unreadable. Material (aluminium, fibreglass) and height band are attributes (Appendix A: material is a modifier unless it changes the form).
6. **Parts and accessories: accessory type** (drill bit, saw blade, grinding disc). Bit diameter, shank, tooth count and grit are attributes. The platform battery pack is a kind in C1.9 whose platform is a `system` attribute [S23, S25].
7. **Tool storage: container form** (portable toolbox, wheeled toolbox, modular stackable tool case, tool bag); material is an attribute (K9) and the stacking system (PACKOUT, TSTAK, ToughSystem and others) is a `system` attribute [S32].

**The exception that stays coarser.** Hand-tool families that retailers treat as one leaf with many size variants (spanners and sockets, hex keys, clamps) are one kind per recognisable type, with size, drive and finish as attributes (convention C6). Types that buyers compare separately (combination spanner, ratchet, adjustable wrench) are separate kinds [S1, S15]. Measuring-tape length, clamp capacity, bit diameter and sheet size are never kinds. A set of like items (a socket set) is filed as the item (K6).

**Where the level differs inside C1.** Hand tools and machines are finer than powered hand-held tools because quality differences sit in the type itself (a paring chisel versus a bench chisel; a cabinet saw versus a jobsite saw). For powered hand-held tools quality sits in the platform, grade and motor spec, which are attributes and tier entries.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Choice: consensus-ranked, with a spec layer underneath.** No fetched page shows a formal standard that ranks tools from DIY to professional. The ordering in use is set by makers, retailers and review sites, and each maps to measurable specs. (Safety and performance standards for power tools, for example EN or IEC test standards, exist but no page in this batch was fetched to attest them †; they are left for stage 3 sourcing.)

**The orderings found, with their region.**
1. **Duty grade (consensus, three or four steps).**
   - Three steps, UK, one maker: Axminster rates machines Occasional use (light DIY, lower power, lighter build), Regular use (dedicated hobbyist or small professional workshop) and Frequent use (daily professional use, "not industrial grade"), by "how frequently and for how long the machine is expected to be used", with type of work, user and manufacturer also considered [S19]. Machine Atlas (UK) records the same maker's lines as Craft/Hobby, Trade, Industrial [S18].
   - Two steps, Germany/UK, one maker: Bosch splits professional (blue: daily use, multi-day jobs, extended warranty) from DIY (green: occasional use, short jobs), and attaches specs (GSR 18-2-LI Plus 63 Nm and 1,900 rpm versus PSR 18 LI-2 46 Nm and 1,340 rpm) [S24].
   - Two steps, Japan, one retailer: Komeri's leaf labels say pro or DIY (and 18V, 36V or AC) [S26].
   - Three or four steps, US, two review sites: Pro Tool Reviews uses Pro, Prosumer/Serious DIY and DIY, with brand lists per class [S8]; CordlessPicker labels each platform Professional, Prosumer, DIY & homeowner or Budget [S7].
2. **Spec ladders (numeric; attributes, not tiers, convention C4(b)).** Voltage class (7.2 V, 10.8 V, 14.4 V, 18 V, 36 V and over in Japanese listings [S26, S27]; 12 V versus 18 V systems at Bosch [S25]); torque in N·m, speed, hammer rate and weight [S8, S24]; motor power thresholds on machines (Axminster flags 1.1 kW and 2.2 kW as the points where extension leads and installer advice change) [S19]; amperage on welders [S34]; maximum static load and height on ladders [S30]; resolution and range on gauges [S28, S29].
3. **Material rank for hand tools (consensus).** Japanese dealers rank blade steel in a stated order: White #2 and #1, Blue #2 and #1, Aogami Super, Swedish steel, tamahagane, HSS [S9]; forging region and named smith add prestige [S9, S11]. Steel is an attribute of the named form; the dealer order is the attribute's sort order. Collectors rank by maker and rarity [S20]; condition belongs to an individual item and is never stored (convention C16).
4. **Score-based ranking.** Pro Tool Reviews scores drills out of 100 on standardized tests [S8]; a dated review score is a tier entry with `basis: consensus` on the model it scores.

**How it appears in the structure (conventions C4 and K5).** Never a level and never on a kind:
- *Tier entries* (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `basis`, `source_url`, `date`) sit on a maker, a line or a named form. The maker's own grade is a tier entry on the line row with `basis: maker label` (Bosch blue and green lines [S24]; Axminster Craft/Hobby, Trade, Industrial [S18]; Axminster Occasional, Regular, Frequent use [S19]; Komeri pro and DIY [S26]). A named form does not inherit its maker's or line's tier unless an entry says so. A subject may hold several entries; the computed `disputed` flag marks disagreement.
- *Scheme:* one C1 scheme for duty grade. **OWNER:** three values (Occasional, Regular, Frequent use: one UK maker [S19]) or four (DIY, Prosumer, Professional, Industrial: two US review sites [S7, S8])? (Q13b.) Until the owner chooses, each entry keeps its source's own label and the mapping is not made.
- *Platform tier:* CordlessPicker's Professional, Prosumer, DIY or Budget label [S7] is a tier entry on the line row that the platform's system-ledger row points to (`basis: consensus`), not a column of the system ledger.
- *Spec attributes* with units and, where one exists, the standard named: voltage class, torque, duty cycle, motor power, steel grade, load rating, accuracy.
- *Price band:* the domain-wide USD bands of convention C4(d), derived from a dated, sourced list price; never a tier basis.
- Some kinds exist only at professional grade (for example earth-moving machines); for those no tier entry is needed.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Choice: platform.** For powered hand-held tools, tool batteries and tool storage, the maker sets compatibility: a battery fits only its own platform or an alliance of platforms, and a stacking case fits only its own system.
- *Evidence*: Home Depot gives Battery Platform its own filter [S23]; Bosch promises 18V compatibility back to 2008 (professional) and 2007 (DIY) and sells bare tools without batteries [S6, S25]; the Cordless Alliance System lists "over 50 brands" and "500+ tools" on one 18V battery, and Bosch's Power for All and AMPShare alliances do the same on separate systems [S5, S6, S25]; Komeri sells its own battery-sharing B-Share series and HiKOKI's MultiVolt batteries as products in their own right [S26]; Screwfix filters toolboxes by "Compatible Stacking System" (PACKOUT, TSTAK, ToughSystem, BUNKER Load-Up, Connecx, E-Case) [S32]; OBI sells store-brand and maker battery systems under named families (Power X-Change, 1 PowerSystem) [S35].
- *Identity applies in the premium hand-tool market*: Hida Tool's main facet is the maker (about 60 Japanese makers) [S10]; Japamart has separate browse routes by brand, steel and forging region [S9]; Suzuki-ya sorts chisels by named smith [S11]; collectors choose by maker (Stanley, Disston, Norris, Miller's Falls) [S20]; Highland points buyers to Lie-Nielsen as the premium alternative to refurbished Stanley [S22]; Starrett and Mitutoyo market their names as standards of precision [S28, S29].
- *Makers are incidental for commodity hand tools and consumables* (hammers, pliers, drill bits): the retailer trees treat them as filters [S1, S3, S4].

**Recommendation: the shared ledgers of the conventions, kept as tables, never as levels.**
- *Maker ledger* (convention C1 core schema, with review C's K2 additions): `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name` (for Japanese makers and smiths); `aliases`; `maker_class` (house or manufacture; independent; retail own-label, for B-Share, LUX-TOOLS and Erbauer-type store brands; supplier; and, from K2, smith or forge for the Japanese smiths [S11]); `country`; `place` (forging region such as Sanjo or Miki [S9] goes here, as a place, not a tier); `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id` (only where a fetched page attests the link: Komeri lists Ryobi under Kyocera [S26] while US sources list Ryobi ONE+ as a DIY platform [S7, S23], and HiKOKI in Japan [S26] and Metabo HPT in the US [S7] both name a MultiVolt line; these corporate links are not attested in a fetched page †, so no row may say they are the same maker; OWNER: Q43b); `known_for_kinds`; `scene_tags` (C8; none is sourced yet, so all are held back); `price_band` (C4(d)); `sources`. No platform columns: platforms go to the system ledger. Known-for named forms are derived from the named-form ledger, not stored as a column.
- *Lines table* (C1, K4): `line_id`, `maker_id`, `built_by_maker_id`, name, kinds spanned, years, sources. Rows such as the Bosch Professional (blue) and DIY (green) ranges [S24], the Axminster Craft/Hobby, Trade and Industrial lines and its Engineer series and Professional ranges [S17, S18], Milwaukee M12 and M18 and DeWalt 20V MAX and 60V MAX FLEXVOLT as tool ranges [S23], Bosch 12V and 18V [S25]. Grades are tier entries on these rows (section 5).
- *System (compatibility) ledger* (C13, K3), replacing the earlier "platform ledger" and "storage-system ledger": `system_id`, `name`, `type` (battery platform | modular storage), `openness` (proprietary | alliance | open standard), `makers`, `alliance` (CAS, Power for All, AMPShare [S5, S6, S25]), `spec` (nominal and marketed voltage, for example "20V MAX" against "18V" labels [S7]), `compatible_from` (2007 and 2008 for the two Bosch systems [S6, S25]), `categories` (C1, C2 where the platform runs garden machines), `attestation_urls`. Tools, battery packs, chargers and cases carry a `system` attribute pointing here. One row serves C1 and C2 for platforms that run both (review X3; C2 F9).
- *Named-form ledger* (C2): `maker_bound: yes` for models (Flex FX1371T, Gyokucho #661, Bosch ProCORE18V); `no` for patterns (Stanley No. 4 as a pattern, oire-nomi, ryōguchi). *Variant ledger* (C3): sparse; filled only if the owner wants collector depth (for example the production "types" of a Stanley pattern in a type study [S20], which would be variants, not named forms †).
- *Optional school table* (conventions, Additions): for the Japanese smith traditions (Sanjo, Miki, Echigo Yoita [S9]) if the owner wants them as more than `place`.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommended axis (convention C7): kinds canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states; named forms by maker, then first-introduction year, then alphabetically; variants by code or year.**
- *Secondary keys per sub-family*: the action sub-families (C1.1 to C1.5) order kinds by `drive` and `mount` from manual hand-held to stationary machine, then by size or capacity ascending (in Saws: panel saw, pull saws, circular saw, jigsaw, mitre saw, table saws, bandsaws); C1.7 by resolution, coarse to fine; C1.8 by height or load ascending; C1.9 by output ascending; C1.10 by container size.
- *Reason*: the sources already order by role then size. Highland orders planes by the Stanley number from 1 to 8, which tracks length and role (smoothers, jacks, jointers) [S22]; Axminster orders machines by duty class Occasional → Regular → Frequent [S19]; Grizzly orders benchtop before stationary and jobsite before cabinet [S16]; Komeri orders power-tool leaves by action and then by voltage class [S26].
- *Leaf order inside a sub-family*: by stage of the job (mark and measure → cut → shape and smooth → join and fasten → grip → finish), which is the order in which the Japan House essay presents carpenters' tools (axes, marking, saws, planes, hammers and chisels) [S12]. Alphabetical order is the fallback.
- *Named forms*: by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically (C7). Patterns with no maker (`maker_bound: no`, such as the Stanley numbers and Japanese sub-types) sort with no maker first, then by year. The collector's series-number order (Stanley No. 1 to 8, then type) is a generated "type study" view [S20, S22], not the stored order.
- *Not by price or popularity*: the sources use "top sellers" and price sorts as views [S3, S30], which belong to generated pages.
