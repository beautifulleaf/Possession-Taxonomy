## 0. Category

**Code and name.** F1 Media & Publications, in domain F Culture, Records & Meaning.

**Membership test (placement key step 21, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md).** A published work fixed on a physical carrier, made to be read, viewed or listened to: books (including religious texts, colouring, puzzle and activity books, braille and large-print books), comics, magazines, maps, atlases and globes, sheet music, records, CDs, tapes, films and discs, and other recording carriers of any format.

**Category line.** F1 Media & Publications: published works on physical carriers: books, comics, magazines, maps and globes, sheet music, recordings, films, divided by carrier and format, never by genre.

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch F1.**
- Step 21 note: video games on any carrier are D5; software on a carrier is I1. Blank recording media, and carriers the owner has recorded with published music or film such as mixtapes, are F1 too. Content genre never decides or divides a category: literary, music, film and game genres belong to separate taxonomies. Home recordings on a physical carrier are F5 (K-141).
- Step 19 (F5) note: published works are F1. A photographic print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2; posters are F2. Kept printed ephemera (menus, invitations, trade cards, bookplates) are F5 (K-140). Blank notebooks, diaries, greeting cards and calendars are C5; blank scrapbook albums are C4.
- Step 20 (F2) note: figures of characters are E1; garden statues B4. Prints and posters are F2. A map, including a single sheet from an atlas or a pictorial wall map, is F1; a print made as art that depicts a place is F2 (K-142).
- D3 holds record players, TVs, projectors and hi-fi; E3 holds DJ turntables and equipment to record music; D2 holds e-readers; I2 holds e-books, audiobooks, music and film files and fonts (data held as files). D4 holds film and darkroom equipment.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): cases, bags, covers and stands are parts only when made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time and travel or stand with it (so a stand sold for one globe follows the globe, and a comic bag, record sleeve or CD jewel case follows its carrier into F1.5); furniture, albums, binders, stock books, boxes, trays and shelving that hold several objects are never parts and are B1 when empty (K-9).
- S3a (sets and kits, K-1): a set of objects of one kind is filed as that kind; a mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed; a kit becomes a kind only when it is sold, standardised and compared as one object (none is proposed for F1).
- S5 (test the object, not the motive): a signed book is a book; a collected record is a record.
- Framework wording adopted in v0.7 (K-141, K-139): the step 21 note reads 'Home recordings on a physical carrier are F5' and step 19 drops 'of the owner's own life'; no F1 object moves. Step 14 note (K-112): a printed religious text is F1; a Torah scroll, mezuzah or prayer card is F4.
- Appendix A (modifiers, never levels): region and culture, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user (baby, child, large print, braille), setting, market, power source and technology (illuminated, electronic), occasion, motive, origin, material, condition (note: being written, drawn or recorded on is not condition), edition (limited, signed).

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK, France, Germany, Japan)

- **Barnes & Noble, US (S05).** Its help page defines book formats: hardcover (trade cloth), paperback (flexible paper cover, better paper and larger than mass market), mass-market paperback (about four by seven inches, pulp or newsprint paper), library binding (reinforced hardcover), spiral binding, and audiobook on CD (abridged or unabridged). Formats are defined by binding, trim and paper, not by subject.
- **Fnac, France (S31, S32).** The home page shelves by medium: Livres, BD, Ebooks, Papeterie; Musique, CD, Vinyles; Jeux vidéo; Films, Séries TV, DVD, Blu-ray (with 4K Ultra HD Blu-ray and Steelbook pages; used CD and vinyl, used DVD and Blu-ray). Inside Livres the sub-navigation is genre (Romans, Polar et Thriller, BD et Humour, Manga, Fantasy et Science-fiction) with format-led exceptions (Livre audio, Beaux livres, Méthodes de musique, Livre de coloriage, Ebooks). A category page carries a Format facet with Poche, Ebook, Relié, Broché (S32). Format is a facet, genre is the tree.
- **Thalia, Germany.** The fetched Thalia assortment page showed only four department icons (Filme, Musik, Software, Games) and no usable tree; German retail evidence therefore comes from S07 and S21 (and the ONIX German terms in S03).
- **Musikalienhandel, Germany (S07).** Noten > Partituren > Studienpartitur (4,694 hits); each listing carries Verlag, Besetzung (instrumentation) and Ausgabe (STUDIENPARTITUR, Stp). The edition type is the form; instrumentation is a listing attribute.
- **Sheet Music Plus, US (S06).** Its category page has filters Item Types (Digital, Physical), Ensembles, Formats (Score and Parts, Set of Parts, Score, Part, Score and Part, Collection/Songbook, Octavo, CD, and two MP3 audio formats), Levels, Forms, Instructionals, Usages, Editions (Urtext, Interactive Download, Teacher, Complete). 373,842 items. Format is one facet among several; the first facet buyers see is the ensemble (an instrumentation, i.e. content).
- **Stanfords, UK (S13).** The map shop's menu: Maps (Custom Maps, Road Maps, Street Maps, Wall Maps), Atlases (Reference Atlases, Road Atlases, Street Atlases), Globes. Product titles carry the binding as a name part: "Spiral Bound", "Flexi-bound", "Laminated 3-Sheet Wall Map", "A3 Spiral Bound", "Folded Map".
- **Replogle (US) and Globes-and-maps.com (Germany) (S20, S21, S22).** Replogle's desktop-globe category has filters for diameter (4.3 in to 27 in), map style (celestial, moon, ocean currents, political, topographical, vegetational), material, and features (illuminated, raised relief) (S22). The German shop says "basically two main types", terrestrial and celestial, then splits table-top from foot-stand, then three design styles (classic, modern, rustic), then specials (antique, electronic, mini under 15 cm, illuminated two-image) (S21).
- **Rakuten Ichiba used-book shop, Japan (S30).** The shop's category list is subject-led (business, children's books, science, humanities, fiction, hobby, qualifications, light novels, comics, travel, languages, lifestyle, photo books, boys' love, foreign books) with format-led classes mixed in: 文庫・新書 (with sub-classes 新書, 文庫, コミック), 楽譜 (sheet music), カレンダー・ポスター・パンフレット, 雑誌. This is the pattern the framework forbids as a spine: genre first, format as a stray class.
- **Manga retailer, Japan (S09).** Manga Zenkan lists comic formats by size (判型): 文庫判 105 x 148 mm, 新書判 112 x 174, B6 128 x 182, 四六判 128 x 188, A5 148 x 210 (called "wide" by some publishers), 菊判 about 150 x 220, and larger sizes; it ties each to a reader type (shinsho for boys' and girls' comics, B6 for young adult comics) and notes that hit titles are also issued as bunko and as A5 "complete editions".

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities and wikis

- **Comics (S12, CBR).** A format guide with a ladder: single issues ("floppies", about 6.6 by 10.25 inches, 24 to 32 pages), trade paperbacks (4 to 8 issues, 15 to 20 dollars), compendiums (varied, "no official standard"), hardcovers (Marvel Masterworks and DC Archive Editions since the late 1980s), oversized hardcovers, deluxe hardcovers (DC Deluxe Hardcovers about 12 issues, about 35 dollars), omnibuses, premium large-trim lines (DC Absolute Editions, Marvel Gallery Editions, Dynamite Definitive Editions, trims about 9.6 by 13.3 or 9.6 by 15 inches), and budget small-format lines (DC Compact Comics at 10 dollars, older Marvel Essentials and DC Showcase). The page says definitions differ by publisher (Dark Horse and Image label softcover compendiums "omnibuses"; IDW labels hardcovers of any size "compendiums").
- **Manga (S11, Wikipedia).** Tankōbon is the standard format (B6 12.8 x 18.2 cm and A5 14.8 x 21.0 cm most common), with named special formats: bunkoban (A6, thicker, fewer volumes), kanzenban (A5, reproduces chapter covers and colour pages), shinsōban/tokusōban, sōshūhen (Shueisha, from 2008, B5 176 x 250 mm), wide-ban (A5) and gōka aizōban. US "Tokyopop trim" is about 13 x 19 cm.
- **Magazines (S27).** Digest size is about 14 x 21 cm (Reader's Digest, TV Guide), between journal size and a paperback, close to a DVD case; A5 is used by many UK fanzines; the manga tankōbon is described as similar to digest size.
- **Tape (S25).** Intertapes lists tape families with years: Compact Cassette 1963 (Type I Normal, II Chrome, III Ferrochrome, IV Metal), Elcaset 1976, Minicassette 1967, Microcassette 1969, M-Cassette 1975, Picocassette 1985, DAT 1987, Akai MK 1984, ADAT 1992, DTRS 1992, DCC 1993, NT 1992.
- **Compact cassette types (S23).** Wikipedia records four IEC types set in 1979 (I ferric, II chrome, III ferrichrome, IV metal) and says the numerals follow the historical sequence and do not imply relative quality.
- **CD formats (S24, a record shop's knowledge base).** "The compact disc is not a single format": standard CD (16-bit, 44.1 kHz), HDCD (about 20-bit equivalent), SACD (hybrid with a CD layer), DVD-Audio (up to 24-bit/192 kHz), SHM-CD (a Japanese premium pressing), and the 3-inch Mini-CD single (needs a tray loader and adapter). It notes Japanese SACDs and mini-CD singles are collectible.
- **Globes (S20).** A manufacturer's buying guide: terrestrial vs celestial, floor vs desktop, illuminated vs non-illuminated, political vs physical, antique vs blue-ocean design, wooden, paper, day-night, bar, design, electronic and spinning globes.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- **Discogs (S01, S26).** The collector database defines Format as the carrier (Vinyl, Cassette, CD, CDr, File, Acetate, Flexi-disc, Lathe Cut, Shellac, Cylinder, Edison Disc, Pathé Disc, Mighty Tiny, Sopic, Laserdisc, VHS, Betamax, Video 2000, Video8, U-matic, MiniDV, DVD, HD DVD, Blu-ray, Ultra HD Blu-ray, UMD, VCD, SVCD, CDV, SelectaVision, VHD, Film Reel, Minidisc, Floppy Disk and more), with a Description field carrying size (16 in down to 1 in), speed (8⅓, 16⅔, 33⅓, 45, 78, 80, 90, 120 rpm; tape speeds in ips; reel widths), and release-type tags (LP, Album, Mini-Album, EP, Maxi-Single, Single). Its guidelines say "LP" by itself means a 12-inch 33 rpm microgroove record, that Album, EP and Single tags are independent of speed and media, and that Limited Edition, Numbered, Reissue, Repress, White Label, Promo, Test Pressing and Record Store Day are tags, not formats ("a CD can be a reissue of an LP") (S01, S26). The same listing records capacity facts (DVD 4.7 GB, HD DVD 15 GB per layer, Blu-ray 25 GB per layer).
- **ABAA and AbeBooks (S17, S18).** The rare-book trade's book formats are folio, quarto, octavo, duodecimo, sextodecimo (by folds of the printing sheet and, today, by height: folio over 13 in, quarto 10 to 13, octavo 8 to 10, duodecimo 7 to 8, sextodecimo 6 to 7), extended on AbeBooks to a table from miniature and 64mo to elephant and double elephant folio. ABAA quotes John Carter that modern sellers often drop the terms; some now give the height in inches or centimetres.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **ONIX list 150 Product form (EDItEUR, S03, S04).** The first letter is the medium: A audio (AB cassette, AC CD-Audio including SACD, AD DAT, AE audio disc, AF audio tape, AG MiniDisc, AH CD-Extra, AI DVD-Audio, AJ downloadable file, AK pre-recorded digital audio player, AL pre-recorded SD card); B book (BB hardback, BC paperback, BD loose-leaf, BE spiral bound, BF pamphlet "includes low-extent wire-stitched books ... eg many comic book floppies", BG leather/fine binding, BH board book, BI rag book, BJ bath book, BK novelty, BN part-work, BO fold-out, BP foam book); C cartographic (CA sheet map among others); V video (VF videodisc such as Laserdisc, VI DVD, VJ VHS, VK Betamax, VL VCD, VM SVCD, VN HD DVD, VO Blu-ray, VP UMD, VQ CBHD); S and W multiple-item products. Paperback size classes are a second list (ONIX list 175, form detail, S04): B101 mass market rack, B102 US trade, B103 digest, B104 UK A-format (about 178 x 111 mm), B105 UK B-format (about 198 x 129 mm), B106 UK trade, B107 tall rack, B108 to B110 Japanese tankōbon A5, B5, B6, B111 Japanese bunko A6, B112 shinsho B40, B113 and B114 Swedish pocket and storpocket; audio detail A101 CD standard, A102 SACD, A103 MP3 (S04).
- **RDA carrier types (Library of Congress, S15).** Carriers are grouped by media: audio (audio cartridge, belt, cylinder, disc, sound track reel, roll, wire reel, audiocassette, audiotape reel), computer, microform, microscopic, projected (film cartridge, cassette, reel, roll, filmstrip, slide, overhead transparency), stereographic, unmediated (card, flipchart, roll, sheet, volume, object), video (videocassette, videodisc, video cartridge, videotape reel).
- **Harmonized System chapter 49 (US HTS, S16).** 4901 printed books, brochures and leaflets; 4902 newspapers, journals and periodicals (split by "at least four times a week" versus others); 4903 children's picture, drawing or colouring books; 4904 music, printed or in manuscript (US statistical split: sheet music not otherwise bound versus other); 4905 maps and hydrographic charts including atlases, wall maps, topographical plans and globes (globes 4905.10; in book form 4905.91; other 4905.99). Legal notes: relief maps and globes go to heading 9023; periodicals "bound otherwise than in paper" and sets of more than one issue under a single cover go to 4901. The US statistical lines also split books into hardbound, rack-size paperbound, textbooks, religious, technical and art books by value. Heading 8523 (recorded media) was not fetched.
- **Japanese publishing trade body (S10).** The Publishing Research Institute of the All Japan Magazine and Book Publishers and Editors Association lists book sizes (判型): 文庫判 A6 105 x 148 mm (bunko, pocket magazines), 新書判 B40 106 x 173 (shinsho, comics), B6 128 x 182 (single volumes, young adult comics), 四六判 127 x 188, A5 148 x 210 (specialist books, literary magazines), 菊判 152 x 218 (academic books), B5 182 x 257 (weekly and general magazines), AB 210 x 257, A4 210 x 297 (photo books, art collections).

### 1.5 Museums, libraries and reference works

- **University library, US (S08).** Distinguishes printed-music formats: full score, miniature score (also "pocket score" or "study score", shelved separately), vocal or piano-vocal score, score and parts or parts alone, piano reduction, arrangement; "printed music" for contemporary editions versus "sheet music" for historic popular editions.
- **Berlin State Library via Europeana (S19).** Newspaper formats: newsbook, broadsheet (a sheet printed on one side; usually six columns), tabloid (generally half the size of a broadsheet; up to five columns), Berliner (slightly larger than tabloid; three columns).
- **Film preservation (S28, S29).** The University of Illinois PSAP guide and the US National Archives define film formats by gauge (35 mm from 1893, 9.5 mm 1922 to the 1950s, 16 mm from 1923, 8 mm from 1932, Super 8 from 1965); gauges run from 7.5 to 105 mm with 8, 16 and 35 the most common; Super 8 is 8 mm wide with smaller perforations. Film base (nitrate, acetate, polyester) is identified separately.
- **Record page on Wikipedia (S02).** A table of standard formats by diameter and speed with names: 16 in transcription disc; 12 in LP (33⅓), maxi single and 12-inch single (45); 10 in LP (33⅓), EP (45), single (78); 7 in EP (33⅓ or 45) and single (45). Preferred weights: about 180 g for a 12 in LP, 50 g for a 7 in 45. Vinyl replaced 78s by 1958.

### 1.6 Where sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

**They agree** that the first cut is the physical medium: ONIX letters (S03), RDA media groups (S15), Fnac departments (S31), Discogs Format (S01), the film archivists' gauge (S29), and the Stanfords split between maps, atlases and globes (S13). They agree that sizes sit below the binding: hardback and paperback first, then trim classes (S04, S05).

**They split** in these places.
1. **Comics.** ONIX puts floppies under BF pamphlet and collected editions under hardback or paperback (S03); the community treats comics formats as their own ladder (S12). Japanese retail puts comics in a subject tree beside fiction (S30), while the trade body lists them by size (S10).
2. **Globes.** Two cuts in the same sources: terrestrial or celestial (what is depicted) and desktop or floor (what the object is) (S20, S21, S22).
3. **Records.** Wikipedia fuses diameter, speed and release type into names (LP, EP, single) (S02); Discogs separates the physical (diameter, speed) from the release-type tag and warns against fusing them (S26).
4. **Audiobooks.** B&N lists the audiobook as a book format (S05); ONIX files it under audio (S03); Fnac shelves "Livre audio" inside Livres (S31).
5. **Journals and magazines.** HS treats "journals and periodicals" as one class (S16); the only fetched sources that divide periodicals by form divide newspapers by page size (S19) and magazines by trim (S27).
6. **Bound periodicals.** HS sends a bound set of issues to books (S16 note 3), which matches how a library would shelve a bound volume.
7. **Globes and trade law.** HS 4905 holds printed globes with maps, but relief globes and maps fall under 9023 (S16 note 1).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Carrier family (what it is played or read on) is the first axis.** Evidence:
- Retail navigation opens by medium: Fnac's departments are Livres, Musique CD Vinyles, Films DVD Blu-ray (S31); Stanfords opens Maps, Atlases, Globes (S13).
- ONIX codes the medium before anything else (A, B, C, V) (S03). RDA groups carriers by media type (S15).
- Collectors ask which carrier they hold before anything else: Discogs "Format describes the sound carrier used for the release" (S01, S26); archivists ask "What is the film gauge?" first (S29); a film guide notes that a projector must match the gauge (S28).
- Buyer questions in the guides: "which one to choose?" for globes (S20), "understanding what you are buying" for CDs (S24), "learning to tell different formats apart" for printed music (S08), "a guide to reading different comic formats" (S12), "guide to book formats" (S18).

**Second axis, within printed matter, is the binding class** (hardback, paperback; F-7) and the way the work is issued (single issue, collected volume, serial) (S03, S04, S05, S12); trim size (`trim_class`) and paper class sit below it as attributes (ONIX list 175, S04, S05). **Within recordings it is the groove standard or tape system** (S01, S23); diameter, speed and release type (LP, EP, single) are separate attributes, which Discogs states outright (S01, S26), although Wikipedia's table fuses them into names (S02). **Within cartographic items it is physical form** (folded, wall, bound atlas; desktop, floor) (S13, S14, S20).

**Axes buyers also use first but that the framework forbids as levels.** Subject or genre (Rakuten, Fnac Livres tree: S30, S31); instrumentation (the first Sheet Music Plus filter after item type: S06); map style (political, physical, celestial: S22). These go to attributes and views.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): carrier family, then form of issue, then format class, then edition design or model

- **Sub-family** (axis: the physical carrier family the work is fixed on): F1.1 Printed volumes and serials; F1.2 Cartographic items; F1.3 Sound carriers; F1.4 Moving-image carriers; F1.5 Parts and accessories made for one carrier (the closing parts sub-family, exempt from this axis, C5(d)).
- **Leaf** (axis, stated for each sub-family under C5(c): the form in which the carrier is issued or read): F1.1.1 Books; F1.1.2 Comic and manga editions; F1.1.3 Periodicals and newspapers; F1.1.4 Printed music; F1.2.1 Maps; F1.2.2 Atlases; F1.2.3 Globes; F1.3.1 Grooved discs and cylinders; F1.3.2 Magnetic tape (audio); F1.3.3 Optical audio discs; F1.3.4 Solid-state sound carriers (F-8); F1.4.1 Videotape; F1.4.2 Video discs; F1.4.3 Motion-picture film; and in F1.5 five leaves whose axis is the object served: book, comic, record, disc, globe. F1.2 holds about ten kinds, so C5(b) would allow it to store `leaf = null`; the three leaves are kept because maps, atlases and globes are different things to shelve ('about 12 kinds' is a guide, not a cap, A-21).
- **Kind** (axis, one per leaf): books, binding class; comic and manga editions, edition form; periodicals, periodical form; printed music, completeness of the notation; maps, physical form; atlases, binding; globes, mount; grooved discs, groove standard; audio tape, tape system; optical audio, disc system; videotape, tape system; video discs, disc system; film, gauge.
- **Named form** (axis: edition design or maker model, with a `maker_bound` flag, C2(a); uncapped, CV-7): bunkoban, bande dessinée album, DC Absolute Editions, Replogle "Bartlett 12 inch", Philip's road atlas.

**What is an attribute, not a level (so that no object has two homes).** Trim class (ONIX list 175 codes B101 to B114, S04; bunko B111 and shinsho B112 are values, F-7), paper class (trade or mass-market, S05), antiquarian height class (folio to sextodecimo, S17, S18), page format of periodicals (journal, digest, A5; for newspapers broadsheet, Berliner, tabloid, F-7), binding material (cloth, library, leather), record diameter, speed and release type (LP, EP, single, maxi single), cassette type I to IV, fidelity specs (bit depth, sample rate, capacity, encoding), pressing material, disc capacity, globe diameter, depicted body (terrestrial, celestial, lunar), map style, illumination, relief, map and atlas scope (reference, road, street, walking, cycling, thematic), content type (spoken word, music), instrumentation, level, urtext, Steelbook, Record Store Day, numbered and limited editions, set size (`unit`), the globe's `bar_cabinet` (F-8), and condition. Illumination and the HDCD encoding are technology attributes under C10.

Example kinds (14): hardback book, paperback book, single-issue comic, collected softcover volume, collected hardcover volume, newspaper, magazine, miniature score, folded map, desktop globe, microgroove record, compact cassette, SACD, Blu-ray. The full worked tree is below.

**Where it breaks.**
1. **Audiobook CD.** A CD of a spoken book is a CD by carrier (ONIX AC, S03) but a "book" to B&N (S05) and Fnac (S31). Placed with the CD (a kind by carrier); content type (spoken word versus music) is an attribute (F-8). An audiobook held only as a file is I2 (I-10).
2. **Book with a CD in the back cover, or a Blu-ray plus DVD combo, or a VinylDisc.** ONIX has multiple-item codes for these (S03, S04). The rule (S3a, K-1) is one home by the item of greatest value, normally the primary carrier, with the contents listed (section 9, item 9).
3. **Comic floppy.** ONIX calls it a pamphlet (BF, S03); retailers and the community treat it as a comic (S12); the framework lists comics as a media type. Placed as the single-issue kind in the comics leaf, with a cross-reference to periodicals, because it is a staple-bound serial issue. Precedence: a comic or manga volume is filed in F1.1.2 whatever its binding or trim; F1.1.1 holds books that are not comics.
4. **Bound volume of a magazine.** HS sends it to books (S16 note 3). It is a book-form object holding periodical issues; placed in periodicals as "bound periodical volume", which departs from HS on purpose so that the volume stays with its issues (decided, F-8).
5. **Bar globe.** A globe that opens to hold drinks (S20) is a globe by form and a drinks cabinet by use; it is a globe with the attribute `bar_cabinet` (decided, F-8); rule in section 9.

### Candidate B: work type first (book, periodical, map, music, recording, film)

- **Sub-family** (axis: what kind of publication it is): books; periodicals; maps; printed music; recordings; films.
- **Leaf and kind**: by binding or carrier.
- Example kinds (12): hardback, trade paperback, newspaper, magazine, folded map, atlas, vocal score, LP, CD, cassette, DVD, Blu-ray.

**Where it breaks.** This is the library and tariff view (HS 4901 to 4905, S16; RDA media, S15), but "recordings" and "films" share carriers: a DVD, a CD-ROM and an audio CD are the same family; an 8 mm film strip is a different carrier from a VHS tape of the same film. It also splits the record player's world (LP, cassette, CD) across "music" and "audiobook" and invites sorting by content type, which the framework forbids. Usable as a generated view ("everything printed on paper").

### Candidate C: playback or reading device first (needs nothing; needs a turntable; needs a cassette deck; needs a disc player; needs a projector)

- **Sub-family** (axis: what you need to use it): nothing (print); turntable; tape deck; optical disc player; film projector; video recorder.
- Example kinds (12): hardback, folded map, microgroove record, 78 rpm record, compact cassette, CD, DVD, Blu-ray, VHS, 16 mm print, Super 8 print, LaserDisc.

**Where it breaks.** The device cut is how archivists and collectors warn buyers (S28 says a projector must match the film gauge; S24 says mini-CDs need a tray loader). But a Blu-ray plays on a Blu-ray player or a games console, a VCD on several players, and "nothing" holds half the category. Rejected for navigation; usable as a generated compatibility view, and the compatibility itself is recorded in the C13 ledger (section 6).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule.** A kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. Below it the distinction is a named form or an attribute. A size class is a kind only when it changes what the object can be used with and sellers sell the sizes apart (C6 size against format, CV-19); otherwise it is an attribute with its standard named.

- **Books: stop at binding class (hardback, paperback, plus the other binding forms and the material-led children's forms).** B&N explains the formats by binding, paper and trim, with the quality difference stated (trade paper better than mass market) (S05); Fnac's Format facet stops at Poche, Relié, Broché, Ebook (S32); ONIX stops at BB and BC and puts the size classes in a second list (S03, S04). Decided (F-7): one paperback kind; the trade or mass-market paper difference is the attribute `paper_class`, and trim class (B101 to B114, bunko and shinsho included), antiquarian height class and binding material are attributes. Publisher, series and genre are not levels.
- **Comics: go one level deeper, by edition form.** CBR treats single issue, trade, compendium, hardcover, oversized hardcover, deluxe hardcover, omnibus and premium large-trim editions as separate buying decisions with different prices, page counts and trims (S12). Kinds are the generic edition forms (single-issue, collected softcover volume, collected hardcover volume, omnibus or compendium, pocket-size reissue, premium large-format edition); the lines (Masterworks, Absolute) and the Japanese formats (tankōbon, bunkoban, kanzenban) are named forms (C11). The bande dessinée album is a named form of the collected hardcover volume, pending a French or Belgian source †.
- **Periodicals: stop at newspaper, magazine, bound volume, zine.** The fetched sources divide only by page format (S19, S27) and the HS class (S16); page format (broadsheet, Berliner, tabloid, digest, journal size, A5) is an attribute (F-7), and digest-size magazine is no longer a kind. Single issues and back issues are individual items, not kinds. There was no fetched magazine retailer tree; this leaf is the thinnest (section 10).
- **Printed music: stop at score type** (full, miniature, vocal, parts, octavo, songbook) (S06, S08). Instrumentation, level, urtext or facsimile are attributes (S06 Editions facet; S08).
- **Maps and atlases: maps stop at physical form (folded, wall, booklet); atlases at binding (bound, spiral-bound)** (S13, S14). Scope (reference, road, street, walking, cycling, thematic) is an attribute; series and region are attributes or named forms.
- **Globes: stop at mount (desktop, floor, hanging).** Replogle sorts by desktop, floor and collection, with diameter, style and features as filters (S22); the German guide does the same (S21). Depicted body, map style, illumination, relief and size (mini globes) are attributes.
- **Records: stop at groove standard** (microgroove, coarse-groove, flexi-disc, instantaneous disc, cylinder, and the 16-inch transcription disc if the size rule allows). Discogs fixes Format as the carrier and carries size and speed in the description, and says Album, EP and Single tags are independent of speed and media (S01, S26); Wikipedia's table, which fuses diameter, speed and release type into names, is not followed (S02). Diameter, speed, release type, weight, pressing, colour, reissue, limited and numbered are attributes and tags; diameter kinds (7-inch, 10-inch, 12-inch) are not adopted (F-6). The 16-inch transcription disc is a kind under CV-19, provisional until attested.
- **Compact disc family: stop at CD, SACD, DVD-Audio.** A record shop treats them as different formats with different players and sound (S24); HDCD is an `encoding` attribute and SHM-CD a `pressing_material` attribute (C10). The 3-inch Mini-CD is a kind under the size-against-format rule (CV-19, F-6), provisional until attested.
- **Tape: stop at the tape system (compact cassette, microcassette family, and the left-out open reel, 8-track, DAT, DCC).** The cassette type I to IV is an attribute of blank and some pre-recorded tape (S23).
- **Video and film: stop at the carrier system.** Each video system has its own ONIX code (S03) and its own Discogs Format (S01) because the players are incompatible; film stops at gauge (S29, S28), where the projector must match, with sound, base and colour as attributes.

The choice level differs by leaf: coarse in books and tape, fine in comics and video.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Finding: no single tier order covers F1, and no tier sits on a kind (C4(a)).** The category mixes four situations.

1. **Spec-ranked pockets in carriers (C4(b): attributes with the standard named).** Optical audio runs from standard CD (16-bit, 44.1 kHz) through HDCD (about 20-bit equivalent) and SACD (DSD64 plus a CD layer) to DVD-Audio (up to 24-bit/192 kHz) (S24). Video discs run DVD 4.7 GB, HD DVD 15 GB per layer, Blu-ray 25 GB per layer (S01) and Ultra HD Blu-ray listed as a separate Format (S01; no fetched page states its resolution or capacity). Cassette tape has four IEC types, but the numerals do not imply quality (S23). Film gauge is a width spec (S29). These are the attributes `bit_depth`, `sample_rate`, `capacity` and `encoding`, `tape_type` and `gauge`. S24 is a record shop's knowledge base, not the CD or SACD specification; the standards themselves have not been fetched †.
2. **Consensus-ranked premium lines (C4(a): tier entries on the line or named form).** Hardcover and large-trim comics are described as "the pinnacle of what can be collected" and "exceeded in pure quality by other formats" (S12, omnibus and Absolute/Gallery lines). A tier entry (C4(a): `subject_id`, `scheme_id`, `value`, `rank`, `direction`, `source_url`, `date`) for such a line enters the data on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10, F-9); CBR (S12) is that source, so entries for the Absolute, Omnibus and Masterworks lines enter at the stage-3 load, in scale class (iii) editorial or community class, on the maker-bound named form (never on a kind). A second region adds weight and feeds `disputed`; none has been fetched †.
3. **Measured attributes of the object.** Record weight: "around 180 grams usually considered ideal" for a 12-inch LP (S02), an attribute `weight_g`. Japanese pressings are called collectible (S24); `pressing_material` (SHM-CD) is an attribute.
4. **Binding and paper in print.** Mass-market paper is lower grade than trade paperback (S05); library binding is reinforced (S05); leather binding has its own ONIX code (S03 BG). These are values of the attributes `binding` and `paper_class`, not tiers.

**Price bands.** CBR's price ladder (10 dollars compact, 15 to 20 trade, 25 to 30 hardcover, about 35 deluxe, 75 to 150 omnibus) is context only (S12). A price band is derived under C4(d) from a dated, sourced list price in USD; no such price has been fetched for any F1 named form †.

**Condition and grading scales.** Goldmine-style grades for records and book condition scales are condition grades (Appendix A). They go in the shared grading-scale registry (C16, CV-34: `scale_id`, name, owner, `scale_type` = condition grade, `steps`, `kinds_served`, `sources`) linked to kinds; no grade value is ever stored. The scales have not been fetched and the registry rows are written at stage 3 †. The AbeBooks, CBR and Discogs pages cited here do not rank condition.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommendation: identity for publishers, imprints, labels, cartographic houses and globe makers; platform for proprietary carriers, held in the C13 compatibility ledger, not in kinds or in the maker ledger.** Neither is incidental.

- **Identity.** Edition designs are named by their publishers (Marvel Masterworks, DC Archive Editions, DC Absolute Editions, Marvel Gallery Editions, Dynamite Definitive Editions: S12), and the same format word means different things by publisher (Dark Horse and Image omnibus versus IDW compendium: S12). Shueisha introduced sōshūhen (S11). Map and atlas shops sell by publisher and series (Bollmann Bildkarten, Philip's, AA, A-Z: S13). Globes carry maker collections (Replogle's National Geographic Collection: S22).
- **Platform (C13).** Carriers are made by firms and their players are incompatible: Sony MiniDisc, UMD and Blu-ray, Toshiba HD DVD, RCA's CED SelectaVision, JVC's VHD, Telefunken's TeD (S01, S03, S04); Kodak introduced 8 mm in 1932, 16 mm in 1923 and Super 8 in the mid-1960s, Pathé 9.5 mm in 1922 (S28). Each carrier system is one compatibility-ledger row (C13): `platform_id`, `name`, `owner` (maker or standards body), `platform_type` (film format for the 8, 9.5, 16 and 35 mm gauges; the C13 list has no value yet for disc and tape carrier systems, section 12), `kinds_served` (the F1 kind plus the D3 players and D4 projectors that play it), `standard_ref`, `sources`. Starting rows: MiniDisc (Sony), HD DVD (Toshiba), CED SelectaVision (RCA), VHD (JVC), Super 8, 16 mm and 8 mm (Kodak, S28), 9.5 mm (Pathé, S28); with owners not yet attested †: Compact Disc (Philips and Sony), SACD, Blu-ray Disc (Blu-ray Disc Association), VHS (JVC). Domain I and D5 share the same ledger and `platform_id`s (disc games and the consoles that read Blu-ray). Platforms are never levels; the kind stays generic and points to its row.
- **The maker ledger is the one domain-wide ledger (CV-2, C1)**, with the C1 columns `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `licensor_id`, `authority_id` (VIAF or ISNI where one exists), `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`. `maker_class` is multi-valued and takes the C1 culture-and-records values `publisher`, `imprint`, `record label`, `cartographic publisher` and `globe maker` (and `artist` or `print studio` where a maker is also one). `known_for_kinds` is not stored: it is derived from the named-form ledger. `scene_tags` stay empty until a definition source is fetched (C8). The ledger has no cap: one fetched page admits a maker (CV-5), it is seeded from existing catalogues first (Discogs for labels, S01; library and trade catalogues for publishers; a source census comes before harvesting), and publishers, imprints, labels, cartographic houses and globe makers are recorded with their lines and formats (F1-O1). The maker-relations table records licensed editions, rebrands and imprint ownership (`parent_id`); the standing school table is used if a tradition of makers is attested, and none has been found for F1. The issuer table is not used: F1 has no issuing authority. Starter rows come from the fetched pages: Marvel, DC, Dynamite, Dark Horse, Image, IDW (S12); Shueisha (S11); Replogle (S22); Bollmann, Philip's, AA, A-Z (S13). **Individual releases are out of scope**, so a label's catalogue and a publisher's list stay out.
- **Lines against named forms (CV-7, C2(d)).** A maker's line that spans several kinds is a C1 line row (`line_id`, `maker_id`, `name`, `kinds`, `line_rank`, `built_by_maker_id`, `licensor_id`, `years`, `sources`; tier entries are C4); a line or design inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (C2(a)). A line is admitted on one fetched page, with no cap. Marvel Masterworks, DC Archive Editions, DC Absolute Editions and the other S12 lines sit inside one kind each and are maker-bound named forms. Penguin Classics (pocket paperback, trade paperback, hardback) and the Criterion Collection (DVD, Blu-ray) are line rows † because they span kinds; no fetched page attests them. Marvel Masterworks becomes a line row if a source shows it also in softcover †.
- **Named forms carry `maker_bound`.** Yes: the S12 lines and budget lines, sōshūhen (Shueisha), the Replogle globe models, the Philip's, A-Z and AA atlases. No: bunkoban, kanzenban, gōka aizōban, tankōbon, wide-ban, comic trade paperback, bande dessinée album †. (Bunko and shinsho are `trim_class` values, and broadsheet, Berliner and tabloid are `page_format` values: F-7.)
- **Variants (C3, CV-8, CV-9) are used for maker models, never for a title's releases.** Where shops and collectors compare versions of a maker-bound named form (a Replogle globe model in blue or bronze, illuminated or not, or a diameter), the versions are variant rows (types colourway, material, size, configuration, edition, bundle; S22), uncapped. A release, pressing or edition of one title is an individual work (S2, C16), so no variant row is written for it: Steelbook, Record Store Day, white label, test pressing, numbered, limited, signed and reissue stay attributes and tags (S01, S26).
- **Evidence of edge.** The S12 page itself shows that a maker's naming is not stable across makers, which is why the alias and homonym table (section 7) carries "omnibus (Dark Horse, Image)" against "compendium (IDW)".

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then by a stated secondary key that differs by leaf (C7).**
- **Books:** hardback, paperback, then the other binding forms in ONIX order, then the material-led children's forms. Reason: this is the order retailers and B&N present and the order in ONIX (BB, BC, BD ...) (S03, S05).
- **Comics:** single issue, collected softcover volume, collected hardcover volume, omnibus or compendium, pocket-size reissue, premium large-format edition. Secondary key: step-up in cost, size and page count (S12).
- **Periodicals:** newspaper, magazine, bound volume, zine; secondary key: page size, large to small.
- **Printed music:** by completeness of the notation: full score, miniature score, vocal score, score and parts, parts, octavo, songbook (S08).
- **Maps, atlases and globes:** by physical size or portability: folded, booklet, wall; bound then spiral-bound; desktop, floor, hanging.
- **Grooved discs:** microgroove record first, then the other groove standards in the order of the Discogs Format list (S01). Attribute values (not kinds) are ordered diameter descending, then speed (S02's table is ordered this way).
- **Tape and video carriers:** chronology of introduction, because dating is how collectors place a format (S25 gives the year beside each family; S28 gives dates for each film gauge).
- **Film:** by gauge ascending, 8 mm to 35 mm (S29).
- **Parts:** by the order of the objects served (book, comic, record, disc, globe).

**Named forms (C7, CV-21):** non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested. Variants go by `variant_code` or year. Maker ladders (`line_rank`), newest-first and similar orders are generated views, not stored order. Individual releases are not listed.

## 9. Boundaries

1. **D5 Video Games and I1 Software.** Nearest objects: a UMD disc that holds a game or a film (S01 says UMD can hold games, movies, music); a Blu-ray game disc; a CD-ROM or an enhanced CD. **A disc or cartridge made to run a video game is D5 whatever its shape, and a disc of software is I1; a disc of film or music is F1, and an enhanced CD with data tracks stays F1 when it is published as a music release.**
2. **I2 Digital Media & Items and D2 e-readers.** Nearest objects: an e-book, a downloaded audiobook, a ripped file, a "downloadable audio file" ONIX code (S03 AJ), a pre-recorded digital audio player such as Playaway (S03 AK). **A work held only as a file or entitlement is I2, a work on a physical carrier is F1.** A pre-recorded digital audio player is F1 (the work is published fixed on a device that plays only that work, F1.3.4, decided F-8); a digital audio player that plays any loaded content is D3 (key step 24, K-150).
3. **F5 Documents & Personal Records.** Nearest objects: home movies on regular 8 mm or Super 8 reels (S28 says these gauges are common in home-movie collections), home audio and video recordings, mixtapes, kept programmes, picture postcards. **A film reel, tape or disc shot or recorded by the owner or the family is F5 (step 21 note as reworded, K-141); a commercially released print or reel is F1; a recording the owner made from published music or film (a mixtape) is F1 by the step 21 note; programmes and postcards kept as keepsakes are F5.**
4. **F2 Art and C5 Office & Stationery.** Nearest objects: art books, posters, calendars with maps or pictures, blank manuscript paper, wall maps used as décor (S13 sells pictorial wall maps), engraved antique map sheets. **A published book of art is F1, a poster is F2, a calendar or blank staff paper is C5. A map, including a single sheet from an atlas or a pictorial wall map, is F1, whether it hangs on a wall or lies in a glove box; a print made as art that depicts a place is F2** (K-142, now in the key at step 20).
5. **D3 Audio & Video and E3 Musical Instruments.** Nearest objects: record players and tape decks (D3), DJ turntables and timecode control vinyl (E3; S02 lists timecode vinyl among less common recording formats). **A player or deck is D3, DJ equipment including timecode vinyl is E3, and a published record, tape or disc of music is F1.**
6. **B4 Home Décor and B1 Furniture.** Nearest objects: a snow globe, a bar globe (S20), a globe stand. **A snow globe is B4 because it depicts no map; a globe that depicts the Earth or sky is F1 even when it opens as a drinks cabinet (the attribute `bar_cabinet`, F-8), and a stand sold for one globe is its part by S3 (F1.5.5).**
7. **C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments.** Nearest objects: celestial globes, star charts, planispheres (S20, S21). **A published celestial globe or star chart is F1; a telescope or working astronomical instrument is C6.**
8. **Blank media.** Nearest objects: blank cassettes by IEC type (S23) and CDr (S01). **Blank recording media are F1 by the step 21 note; film stock bought unexposed for a camera is D4 film.**
9. **Box sets, combo packs and sets.** Nearest objects: Blu-ray plus DVD, a book with a CD, a VinylDisc, a three-LP box set. **A set of like carriers is filed as that carrier with a `unit` attribute (a three-LP box set is a microgroove record, unit 3). A mixed multi-carrier pack is filed once by its item of greatest value, normally its primary carrier, and its contents are listed (ONIX S-codes and Discogs "Box Set" treat it as packaging; S03, S04, S26).** This is the S3a sets rule (K-1) applied to media; no set is a kind for F1.
10. **B1 Furniture & Storage, F1.5 parts and F5.** Nearest objects: a comic bag, a record sleeve, a CD jewel case, a slipcase, a CD wallet binder, a record crate, a bookcase, an empty photo album. **A holder, case, sleeve or stand made for one carrier follows it into F1.5 (S3); an album, binder, box, crate or shelf made to hold several carriers is B1 when empty, whatever it is made to hold; a filled photo album or scrapbook is F5; a blank scrapbook album is C4** (K-9).
11. **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects and E1 Toys & Games.** Nearest objects: tarot and oracle decks, a tarock or French tarot pack, a printed Torah or Bible, a Torah scroll. **A tarot, oracle or Lenormand deck is F4 by step 14, not a book; a pack made for card play (tarock, French tarot) is E1 (domain E review); a printed religious text is F1, a scroll made for use in a rite is F4.**
12. **Pattern and tutor books.** Method and tutor books (S06 "Instructionals") are books, not printed music (F1-O2). A paper sewing or knitting pattern is F1 and a PDF pattern I2 (C4-O1); a pattern book is a book by its binding, and a single-pattern sheet has no kind yet (section 3, books note).
