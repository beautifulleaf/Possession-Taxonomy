# I1 Software — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md, framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md and framework/structure/_conventions.md (C1 to C13); revised by conformance pass S2C_I1 against the domain I review (framework/structure/_review_I.md) and the shared conventions C1 to C17 (the domain-I additions that were pending are now adopted in CONVENTIONS_v1). Stage-2 decision pass S2U_I1 then applied the delegated decisions of D1, D3 and D4 and checked the text against framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md (section 12); no owner mark is left. The revision log is section 11. Research budget used: 11 searches, 37 fetched pages (the list is in verify/out/S2_I1_sources.tsv, 31 rows). Every claim about how a source divides the category cites a fetched page by key (S01 to S31, table in section 1). Where a claim rests on the framework or on my own inference, it says so.*

**One-line result.** Divide I1 by the job the software does for its owner (eleven function sub-families, plus one closing parts-and-accessories sub-family for add-ons and unlocks), then by the kind of task inside that job (leaf), then by the generic program type that buyers compare (kind), then by the product or product line (named form, almost always maker-bound). Platform, licence, delivery, plan, edition, version, AI-enablement and region are never levels: platform goes to a compatibility ledger and a generated "by platform" view, licence to an SPDX-coded attribute, plan and edition to a variant table, and AI to an attribute except for the assistants the placement key names. Makers are central (identity plus platform); tiers are consensus-ranked or lab-measured for security software and AI models and are a preference elsewhere.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** I1 Software (domain I, Software, Digital & Subscriptions).

**Membership test (placement key, step 5, first branch).** "software: a program or app, or a hosted tool whose value is what the program does for the user (password managers, security and VPN software, operating systems, creative, productivity and finance programs, AI assistants, apps), however it is licensed or delivered (download, subscription, licence key, boxed disc, web), including a separately paid licence or feature unlock for software in a device? I1 Software." Branch note: "a hosted service whose value is the storage, catalogue, connection, place or play it gives access to, such as cloud storage, email, a streaming catalogue or online play, is I3, even when an app is used to reach it."

**Category line (Domain I).** "programs, apps and hosted tools by what they do (password managers, security, creative, productivity, finance, system, AI assistants), however licensed or delivered."

**Rules and notes that already touch I1.**
- Step 5 lead note: video games and their expansions are D5 on any carrier or download; game subscriptions and passes are I3; software built into a device is part of the device unless it is paid for separately as a licence, unlock or subscription, which is I1; the account or platform an item sits in is filed with the item's service, the item held in it is in.
- Step 5 branch 2 (I2): anything that exists only as data (fonts, e-books, in-game items, domain names, NFTs). Branch 3 (I3): subscriptions, memberships and online utility services. Its note files bundles by the component they are named and sold for: Microsoft 365 (office software) is I1; iCloud+, Google One and Dropbox are I3.
- Step 1 note: "software is in however it is licensed"; online utility services (email, messaging, cloud storage, maps, password vaults) are in whether free or paid; credits redeemable for a choice of items, in-game and platform currencies, and wallet value are OUT.
- Step 21 note (F1): software on a carrier is I1; blank recording media are F1.
- Step 24 (D1) says "software is I1"; step 2 (H2) comes before step 5 in the key.
- S3 (parts follow the whole) and step 8 are tried after step 5, so a program is never a "part" of the device it runs on unless it is built in.
- S2 and the brief: a kind is a generic type; a specific product is a named form; an individual item is never a kind. The brief says types of software are in "by what the software does (for example a password manager), however it is licensed or delivered", and that genres never divide media, games or software catalogues.
- v0.7 additions that touch I1 (decisions K-37, K-68, K-71 in D1): software made to diagnose, treat or monitor a condition, such as a regulated medical app, is H2 (step 2); seat structure is a plan attribute; the step 5 note now carries boundary sentences (a) to (c) and (e) to (k): recorded class libraries I3 and interactive learning software I1; local backup software I1 and cloud backup plans I3; map services I3 and GIS software I1; game launchers and their free accounts not filed, their makers in the ledger; asset-library plans I3 and each licensed asset I2; paid unlocks of built-in device functions I1; files a program loads without code I2, plug-ins I1, game mods D5; reference works as e-book files I2 and as their own program I1; credit-renewing plans filed as the plan; NFT-carried memberships I2 unless also sold as a plan, messaging and conferencing I3, email client I1.
- Conventions C14 to C17 that touch I1: C14 (multi-use kinds, used for the creative suite), C16 (no individual-item facts: the condition of one item, such as a sealed box, is not stored) and C17 (legal regimes such as EU resale of licences go to the jurisdiction ledger, never to the tree).
- Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in I1 (never levels): brand and maker, platform-like technology and connectivity (cloud, web, desktop, mobile), price band and tier, edition, market (domestic or commercial), user (child, family, student seat), region and culture, motive (collecting vintage software), condition (sealed), and "power source and technology" (AI-enabled).

### Retailers, marketplaces and software libraries (US, JP, CN, DE, global)

- **Apple App Store (S01)** has about 25 named categories, defined by what the app does ("Productivity: apps that make a specific process or task more organized or efficient"; "Utilities: apps that enable the user to solve a problem or complete a specific task"; "Graphics & Design"; "Photo & Video"; "Developer Tools"; "Finance"; "Business"; "Music"; "Reference"). Three things matter. (1) The same kind has two homes: "password management" is listed as an example in both Productivity and Utilities, and "cloud storage" and "email clients" are listed under Productivity. (2) Audience categories exist beside function categories: Kids is an age-band category (5 and under, 6–8, 9–11). (3) Games has its own subcategories and an app may take two. The secondary-source summary S30 gives Apple 27 categories with one optional second category.
- **Google Play (S04, S30)** requires exactly one category from 32 app categories (Art & Design, Communication, Finance, Productivity, Tools, Photography, Video Players & Editors, Music & Audio, Personalization, Maps & Navigation …) plus up to five tags that describe what the app does, with a warning against tags for "related" genres. The category is function; tags refine it.
- **Amazon.com (S17)** files software by function in sub-departments (Tax Preparation, Internet Security Suites, Antivirus, Business & Office Management, Document Management, Home Publishing, Photography) and then offers separate refinements for Operating System and Brand (Intuit, Microsoft, Avanquest). Listings carry the licence terms in the title ("Norton 360 Deluxe … 3 Devices with Auto-Renewal", "Office Home 2024 … One-Time Purchase for 1 PC/MAC … Instant Download"). Amazon.com splits Antivirus from Internet Security Suites; Amazon.co.jp (S18) lists "online code version" items, subscription terms ("5 devices, 3 years", "12 months") and non-software items such as Game Pass and prepaid cards in the same store node.
- **Japan, Vector (S07, S09)** is the oldest large freeware and shareware library. Its Windows area has 13 headings with counts: document creation 1,789; internet and communication 2,635; utilities 5,695; image and sound 3,801; business 5,585; personal 1,280; home and hobby 828; learning and education 1,962; games 6,623; amusement 1,385; programming 1,099; hardware 483 (33,165 items in the listed headings). Each heading carries 10 to 25 sub-types, for example under utilities: archive, uninstall, backup, file management, program launcher, security. The paid shop (S09) uses the same headings and adds OS and fonts; Windows, Macintosh and mobile/UNIX are the platform split above them. Licence (freeware, shareware) is a label on each item, not a branch.
- **China, Huajun Software Park (S08)** opens with platform tabs (PC software, PC games, Android, iPhone, system software) and then function sorts (system, network, media tools, graphics, desktop, office, security, video playback). One sort is "AI office" (AI办公), under which a Chinese AI assistant client is listed. Function is second to platform here.
- **Germany, CHIP Downloads (S10)** has 15 categories that mix the two axes: platform categories (iPhone & iPad Apps, Android Apps, Linux-Apps, MacOS-Apps, Windows-Apps) beside function categories (Browser/Kommunikation/Online-Tools, Office/Produktivität/Finanzen, Security & Datenschutz, System & Tuning, Multimedia & Kreativtools, Betriebssysteme, Handbücher & Vorlagen, Spiele & Unterhaltung).
- **B2B directories.** G2 (S02) lists 2,251 category pages under about 40 parent groups (Collaboration & Productivity 59 children, Artificial Intelligence 40, Design 18, Security 16, Development, IT Management, Marketing, HR, Vertical Industry 82 …). Capterra (S03) shows eleven named headline groups by business department (the sources file says "10 headline groups" and then names eleven; recount needed †) (Human Resources, Product Management & Productivity, Marketing & Sales, Operations, Service, IT & Security, Finance & Accounting, Legal & Compliance, Customer Service, Hospitality, Healthcare), each with 6 to 10 function categories. Both place consumer-grade kinds awkwardly: G2 puts Password Managers under Identity Management and Antivirus under Endpoint Protection; its Design parent includes Graphic Design, Photography, 3D Design, Audio Editing and Font Management.
- **Maker storefronts (S26, S27, S28).** Microsoft sells the same Word/Excel/PowerPoint suite as Microsoft 365 Personal ($99.99 a year), Family ($129.99, up to 6 people, 2 TB), Premium ($199.99) and as Office Home 2024 ($179.99, one-time, one PC or Mac, "no upgrade options"). Windows 11 is sold as Home and Pro editions. Licence model, seats, term and storage are plan attributes of one function-named product.

### Collectors and the resale market

- **Vintage software (S24)** is sold as lots, described by carrier (5.25-inch floppy 28 listings, 3.5-inch floppy 4, CD-ROM), by "Big Box" and "new factory sealed", and by named programs (Quicken 99, The Print Shop Premier 5.0, Windows 95/3.1 CD-ROM, Encarta 99). Filters are Format, For Operating Systems, Brand and Type (for example Desktop Publishing). Carrier and condition are modifiers; the program is the object.
- **Resale of licences (S23).** The Court of Justice of the European Union held in 2012 (case C-128/11) that the distribution right in a perpetual licence for a downloaded program is exhausted on first sale, so a "used" licence can be resold if the first acquirer makes his own copy unusable. A licence entitlement is therefore an ownable, transferable object in the EU whether it arrived by download or on a disc.

### Standards, trade classifications and test labs

- **UNSPSC (S14, S12)** has a Software family (4323) with 18 classes by function or field: business-function-specific, finance and ERP, game or entertainment, content authoring and editing, content management, data management, development, educational or reference, industry-specific (19 commodities including CAD, medical, point of sale, legal, facial recognition), network applications, network management, networking, operating environment, security and protection, utility and device driver, information exchange, electrical equipment, system management. There is no licence or delivery axis.
- **SPDX (S29)** is the standards body list of licence identifiers (version 3.29, 2026-09-16) with FSF Free/Libre and OSI Approved flags and a licence-expression syntax (WITH, +). It covers licences for software, data, hardware and documentation. Licence is a coded attribute, not a sub-family.
- **Test labs (S20, S21).** AV-TEST scores each home antivirus product 0 to 6 on Protection, Performance and Usability (18 maximum): 10 or more earns the seal, 17.5 or more "TOP PRODUCT". AV-Comparatives awards Standard, Advanced and Advanced+ per test (5, 10, 15 points), Top-Rated at 90 points or more and Approved at 35 or more. Each tests its vendors' consumer suites as one class (AV-TEST names Kaspersky Premium, ESET Security Ultimate, Bitdefender Total Security, Avast Free Antivirus and AVG Antivirus Free; AV-Comparatives names the vendors Avast, AVG, Bitdefender, ESET, G Data, Kaspersky, Norton, McAfee, Microsoft, Malwarebytes and others).
- **Arena (S22)** ranks AI models by human votes in separate modality leaderboards (Agent, Text, WebDev, Text-to-Image, Image Edit, Text-to-Video, Vision, Document, Search).

### Museums and reference works

- **Computer History Museum (S25)** filters its collection by item type (documents, images, software, objects): software is an item type beside hardware, so museums divide by form and carrier, not function.
- **Wikipedia (S11, S13, S31).** "Software categories" gives three broad classes (application, system, programming tools), a copyright-status scheme (free, open source, copylefted, proprietary, freeware, shareware), a seven-part Microsoft TechNet scheme and a market scheme (horizontal against vertical). "Software by type" has 42 subcategories, one function axis (Ad blocking, AI software, Business software, Productivity software, Security software, System software, Utility software by type 31 children, Mobile applications, Web software), beside licence and platform siblings (Free software by type, Proprietary software by type, Portable software). "Utility software by type" (S31) lists 31 functions (backup, data compression, file managers, application launchers, screenshot software, uninstallers, virtualization, computer security 29 children).

### Where sources agree and where they split

- **Agree.** Every source that divides the category at all divides it first by function (what the program is for): S01, S02, S03, S04, S06, S07, S08, S10, S12, S13, S14, S17, S19. Every source with a licence term treats it as a label or facet (S06, S07, S17, S26, S29, S11).
- **Platform.** Three sources make platform the first split (S08 tabs, S10 categories, S09 and S07 area pages); four make it a facet or a second step (S06, S17, S04 single category plus tags, S28 edition within one product). Chinese and German download libraries lean platform-first; the community directory and the biggest retailer lean function-first.
- **AI.** Three sources make AI a top-level group (S02 Artificial Intelligence software, S06 AI Tools & Services, S19 ten AI headings); the app stores, UNSPSC, Vector and Wikipedia's utility list do not (S01, S04, S14, S07, S31) and Huajun adds one AI office sort (S08). AI is therefore a technology that the function-first sources absorb into the function it serves, and a separate heading only in sources that exist to list AI.
- **Same kind, different homes.** Password managers: Apple Productivity and Utilities (S01), G2 Identity Management (S02), Ninite Other (S05). Antivirus: Amazon splits it from Internet Security Suites (S17); G2 nests it under Endpoint Protection (S02); the labs treat suites and antivirus as one list (S20, S21).
- **Games.** Vector, CHIP and AlternativeTo carry games as a heading (S07, S10, S06); the framework gives games to D5, so I1 uses none of these headings.
- **Audience.** Apple's Kids and Google's Parenting are audience categories (S01, S30); the framework treats user age as a modifier, so they are tags, not sub-families.
- **Depth.** G2 splits one idea into several categories (Account-Based Marketing has six children); Futurepedia overlaps its own headings (design generators 158, image generators 136, text to image 42, image editing 124). The consumer sources stop one level lower (App Store 25, Play 32, Vector sub-types 10 to 25 per heading).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence points to a three-step choice, and only the first is a candidate for the tree.

1. **Function comes first.** All the directories above list the job before anything else (S01, S02, S04, S06, S07, S17). The labs and reviewers compare products inside one function (S15, S16, S20, S21, S22); AV-Comparatives tells buyers "There is no such thing as the perfect security program, or the best one for all needs and every user", and to evaluate a vendor's trial version (S21).
2. **Platform and delivery come second, as a compatibility check.** AlternativeTo and Amazon expose Operating System and Platform as facets after function (S06, S17); Google gives one category and five tags (S04); AV-TEST runs separate Windows, MacOS and Android lab series for the same kind (S20). A buyer first asks "does it run on my system", but the answer filters products and does not change what the object is.
3. **Licence, plan and price come last.** One-time against subscription, number of devices or people, and free against paid appear as plan attributes of one product (S26, S27, S15, S18) and as the Licence facet (S06). Microsoft states the trade directly: Office 2024 is one-time, one computer, "no upgrade options"; Microsoft 365 is a subscription with cloud storage and updates (S26).

Conclusion for the tree: place by function (what is it for), record platform as a compatibility ledger entry and a generated view, record licence with SPDX codes where open and with a controlled class where proprietary, and record plan and edition as variants. This is consistent with the framework's own line, "however it is licensed or delivered" (step 5), and with Appendix A.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates use the framework's fixed level names (C5a): category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, with at most four levels below the category. They differ in the axis of the sub-family level.

### Candidate A (recommended): sub-family by the job the software does for its owner

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family: the job (system and device care, security and identity, office and personal work, creative production, media playback, money and business, learning and reference, development, engineering and science, assistants, internet access tools). Leaf: the kind of task or material inside the job; the axis is stated per sub-family (C5c) and a sub-family of about 12 kinds or fewer skips the leaf (C5b). Kind: the generic program type that buyers compare. Named form: the product or product line, nearly always maker-bound (`maker_bound: yes`, C2).
- **One closing parts sub-family (C5d, CV-17).** "Parts and accessories made for one program or device" (add-ons and unlocks; S3 parts follow the whole; leaf axis: the host or device; the only sub-family exempt from the level-1 axis). Its leaves are browser; code editor or IDE; office or productivity suite; audio or graphics host; software for one device (paid feature unlocks of built-in device software, K-71(g), and device companion apps, R-14). The last leaf has no fetched source yet and stays provisional until one fetched page shows a kind sold (section 10); a leaf with one kind is allowed (R-11). This replaces the earlier draft's two exempt sub-families (review I1-1).
- **Evidence.** This is the shape of S01, S04, S06, S07, S13, S14, S17 and S31, and it matches the framework's own category line. It keeps one home for each program: a password manager is a password manager on every platform, in every plan and under every licence.
- **Leaf axes per sub-family.** System and device care: layer of the machine (operating environments, maintenance and recovery, files and remote access). Creative production: medium (images and design; video and animation; audio and music), plus one leaf "suites across media" for an all-apps bundle of one maker (C14(b): the one family that holds all its uses). Development: part of the work (writing code; data and servers). Money and business: business function (I-4). Parts and accessories made for one program or device: host or device. Every other sub-family, including Security, privacy and identity, Office and personal work and Learning and reference, has about 12 kinds or fewer in the sourced core and stores `leaf = null` (C5b); if stage-3 harvest takes one past about 12 kinds it gets leaves on a stated axis (C5b, CV-16). Money and business is opened at Capterra's headline depth (I-4: about 40 to 80 kinds), so it keeps leaves; their axis is business function, never market, because a split into personal and business would be a market split and market is a modifier (Appendix A; see Candidate C). Each kind carries a `market` tag (consumer, business) and a generated "business software" view (review I1-2).
- **Where it breaks (edge objects).**
  1. A hosted design tool (Adobe Express Premium, S27, "design flyers, TikToks, resumes") fits neither "raster image editor" nor "vector graphics editor". It now has its own kind, "graphic design app" (G2 lists Graphic Design Software, S02).
  2. A general AI assistant that also edits images, writes code and searches the web (S16 compares Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude on all of these) has no single function; the placement key names "AI assistants" as software, so it is its own kind in the Assistants sub-family.
  3. A game launcher or store client such as Steam or the Epic Games Launcher (listed in S05 under "Other") is a program whose value is the game catalogue. K-71(e) and the step 5 note do not file it: the games are D5, in-game and platform items I2, a paid store subscription I3, and the launcher's maker stays in the maker ledger (boundary sentence 3).
  4. An email client (Thunderbird, S05) is a program whose value is what it does (I1) while the mailbox it reads is I3; a messaging or conferencing client (Zoom, Discord, Slack in S05) is "a connection … even when an app is used to reach it" (I3).
  5. An all-apps bundle (Adobe Creative Cloud Pro, S27) is named for no single component, so the bundle rule alone gives it no home. Rule added: *"An all-apps bundle of one maker's creative programs (Creative Cloud Pro, Affinity) is the kind 'creative suite'; a single-app plan is filed under that app's kind."* ("Affinity" is the review's example and is not yet sourced †.)

### Candidate A2: as A, with all AI tools in one sub-family

- Same levels; the sub-family "AI tools" collects assistants, image, video, audio and code generators and meeting assistants, as G2 (Artificial Intelligence: 40 children, Generative AI: 15), AlternativeTo (AI Tools & Services) and Futurepedia (ten AI headings) do (S02, S06, S19).
- **Where it breaks.** It makes technology a sub-family axis, against Appendix A and C10: a Photoshop generative feature, an AI image generator and an image editor would sit in different sub-families although buyers compare them as image tools (Adobe sells Firefly credits inside the same plan listing as Photoshop, S27). It also breaks the "one stated axis per level": the other sub-families are jobs, this one is a technology. Edge objects: Cursor (S05, "Programmer's Editor") is an editor and an AI assistant; an AI note-taker (G2 AI Note-Taking, S02) is a note-taking app; a text-to-speech tool (Futurepedia, 55) is audio.
- **When to prefer it.** If the owner wants the AI market tracked as its own scene (the research pointer for I1 names AI tools), this is the simplest shape, at the cost of the problems above. A generated "AI-enabled" view over A gives most of the benefit without it. Decided: I-1 adopts Candidate A (AI an attribute with a generated AI view); A2 is not adopted.

### Candidate B: platform first (Windows, macOS, Linux, iOS, Android, web, self-hosted), then function

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family: operating environment. Leaf: function. Kind: program type. Named form: product.
- **Evidence.** CHIP Downloads, Huajun, the Vector area pages and Ninite are built this way (S10, S08, S07, S05).
- **Where it breaks.** (1) A product on several platforms would need several homes or a dummy "cross-platform" bucket; the framework forbids multiple homes (S1). S06's platform counts (Windows 48.8K, Mac 32.3K, Linux 24.4K, Android 34.4K, iPhone 30.7K, Online 85.6K) add up to more than its licence totals (Free 119.7K plus Paid 46.4K), so many products carry several platform tags (my inference from those numbers). (2) Web-only and self-hosted tools (S06 counts Online 85.6K, SaaS 23K, Self-Hosted 9.1K) are not an operating system. (3) Platform changes by year (Windows Phone 1.5K, Blackberry 472, Symbian 162 in S06). (4) The platform of a boxed vintage program (MS-DOS on 5.25-inch floppies, S24) is a carrier plus operating system, not a sub-family. Platform belongs in the compatibility ledger (C13).

### Candidate C: market first (consumer, business by department, vertical industry), then function

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family: who uses it and where (personal, department such as HR or marketing, industry such as healthcare). Leaf: function. Kind and named form as in A.
- **Evidence.** Capterra, G2 and the market-based scheme in Wikipedia (S03, S02, S11) and UNSPSC's industry-specific class (S12).

Business categories are still useful as kinds inside Candidate A's Money and business sub-family at Capterra's headline depth (section 4b); I-4 decided that depth, and the leaf axis there is business function, never market.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds.** About 200 for the personal and prosumer scope (plausible range 160 to 260). The estimate comes from the consumer-depth sources: Vector's Windows area has 12 function headings with 10 to 25 sub-types each (about 190 sub-types, S07); Wikipedia's "Utility software by type" has 31 functions and "Software by type" has 42 (S31, S13); UNSPSC has 18 classes (S14); AlternativeTo has 27 categories (S06); Capterra's eleven headline groups have about 90 function categories (S03). Business software is opened at Capterra's headline depth and adds 40 to 80 kinds (I-4), so the whole is about 200 to 340 kinds; G2's depth (2,251 category pages, S02) would add roughly two thousand and is not used as kinds by the choice level (section 4b), though a product listed under a deeper G2 category still enters as a named form of the nearest kind.
- **Named forms.** Every product shown made or sold by one fetched page (a storefront, directory, library or catalogue page) enters as a named form, with no cap (CV-5, CV-7; the one-source rule). The first harvest, the sourced core (3 to 10 widely compared products per kind), is about 1,000 to 2,000 products and is a starting point, not a limit. The long tail is in the hundreds of thousands and is harvested in stage 3 from the catalogues that already list it: AlternativeTo counts 119.7K free, 46.4K paid and 31.2K open-source entries (S06), Vector lists 33,165 items in its Windows headings alone (S07). Product lines add little (Microsoft 365 and Office 2024 are two named forms in one kind, S26; Creative Cloud Pro and Standard are two named forms of one kind, creative suite, with plans as variants, S27). Releases (Windows 95, 98, XP, 11) are named forms (I-21), so the count follows the release history; editions and plans are variants.
- **Where depth concentrates.** Security (AV-TEST scores 15 or more consumer suites in each two-monthly round and AV-Comparatives reports yearly, S20, S21), creative production (Adobe alone sells about 20 per-app plans, S27), development, and business software (opened at Capterra depth, I-4). AI is the fastest-growing area (Futurepedia counts 329 agents and 273 research tools, S19) but its kinds are few; its product count is large.
- **Broad, medium, narrow leaves.** Broad: images and design; writing code; maintenance and recovery; the business functions of Money and business (I-4). Medium: video and animation; audio and music; files and remote access; data and servers. Narrow (sub-family of 12 kinds or fewer, leaf null): security, office and personal work, media playback, learning and reference, engineering and science, assistants, internet access tools; the closing parts sub-family has one leaf per host or device.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied.** A kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers: the point at which a lab, a "best X" list or a head-to-head page puts several makers' products in one class.

Evidence by sub-family:
- **Security.** AV-TEST and AV-Comparatives test one list of consumer suites together (S20, S21); PCMag compares password managers as one class (S15). So "antivirus and security suite" is one kind, and Amazon's split of "Antivirus" from "Internet Security Suites" (S17) becomes an edition tag (the labs' product names range from free antivirus to premium suites in one list: Avast Free Antivirus, AVG Antivirus Free and Kaspersky Premium in S20). "Password manager" is one kind; family, premium and free plans are variants.
- **Creative.** Adobe sells Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere, Lightroom and Audition as separate plans (S27) and Ninite lists Paint.NET (raster) and Inkscape (vector) separately (S05), so kinds follow technique within medium: raster image editor, vector graphics editor, photo manager, video editor, audio editor. Features inside one program (generative fill, masks) are attributes. Ninite is a bulk installer, not a comparison page, so this split rests on a weak comparison source until a comparison page from a non-US region is fetched † (review I1-6).
- **Office.** Microsoft sells the suite, not the single app (S26), so "office suite" is the kind; a single program such as Word is a component of a named form and becomes a separate kind only if it is sold or reviewed alone (for example a standalone PDF editor, S09, S27).
- **AI.** Arena publishes separate boards for Agent, Text, WebDev, Search and Document (S22), and Futurepedia and G2 list assistants, agents, research tools and coding assistants separately (S19, S02). So the kind is the task the leaderboard ranks (conversational assistant, answer and research engine, agent platform, coding assistant). Input mode (text-to-image against image editing, which S22 and S19 both split) is recorded as a `task_mode` attribute of "AI image generator"; I-5 keeps it one kind.
- **Business.** Stop at Capterra's headline categories (CRM, accounting, payroll, point of sale, inventory), not G2's sub-sub-categories (S03 against S02). This is the decided depth (I-4): leaf by business function, a `market` tag, and every attested product a named form, uncapped.
- **Learning and reference.** Stop at the method (language learning, reference, genealogy-style hobby tools), not the subject; Vector divides education by subject (foreign languages, mathematics, history, S07), which is content, and the owner's rule is that genre never divides software catalogues.
- **Comparison evidence is mostly US.** The only comparison pages fetched for kinds are US reviews (S15, S16) and two test labs (DE, AT); the Japanese, Chinese and German sources are download libraries (S07 to S10). A non-US "best X" page for raster against vector editors, office suites and media players is still needed †.
- **Not kinds.** Plan or edition (Home, Pro, Family, Premium: S26, S28), term and seat count, licence class, platform, delivery, AI-enablement, audience tab (Students, Teachers, Schools: S27), release year, carrier and condition. Kinds differ in how buyers rank them: a lab ranks security suites by version; no one ranks note-taking apps.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type: consensus-ranked for two areas, preference everywhere else.**
- **Security software** has two independent measured ladders, both dated and per product version: AV-TEST (0 to 6 on Protection, Performance and Usability, 10+ points Certified, 17.5+ TOP PRODUCT; August 2026 round, 15 products) (S20) and AV-Comparatives (Standard, Advanced, Advanced+ per test, Top-Rated at 90+ points and Approved at 35+; 2025 report) (S21). Their outputs differ in the same year (AV-TEST's August 2026 round gave TOP PRODUCT to every product visible in the fetched excerpt of its 15; AV-Comparatives' Top-Rated list for 2025 has seven products and excludes Microsoft and McAfee), which is the case C4(a) handles with several tier entries and a computed `disputed` flag.
- **Seals against scores.** The pass marks (AV-TEST Certified, AV-Comparatives Approved) are certifications against a published test, like COSC in C4(b): they are dated `certification` attributes. The scored outcomes (AV-TEST points and TOP PRODUCT; AV-Comparatives Advanced+ and Top-Rated) are tier entries under two schemes, one per lab (review I1-4).
- **Where the entry sits.** Lab results are tier entries on the **named form** (C4(a)), with the tested version (for example Kaspersky Premium 21.24 and 21.25 in S20) and the test date recorded in the entry; when a lab scores a specific version, the entry may sit on that `version` variant row instead (C4(b)(ii), CV-11). Each lab is one registered scheme (scale class ii, with `test_version` and `stale_after`), and a scheme enters on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10).
- **AI models and agents** have a human-vote ladder by modality (S22), updated continuously. It ranks models and agent products, not the consumer subscription a person owns. Arena entries are stored only as dated snapshots and only where the ranked model and the product coincide (I-5, CV-11); one fetched Arena page is enough to enter the scheme (C4(e), CV-10).
- **Maker edition ladders.** Windows 11 Home below Pro (S28), Microsoft 365 Personal below Family below Premium (S26) and Creative Cloud Standard below Pro (S27) are `edition` variants whose order the maker sets. Each carries a tier entry with `scheme = maker ladder: <named_form_id>` and a `rank`, attested by the maker's page alone, exempt from the second-source weighting and never compared across named forms (C4(b)(i), CV-11). The lines table keeps the maker's order of its lines in `line_rank`.
- **All other kinds** are preference: PCMag's "best budget pick" style lists (S15, S16) are editorial, not a standard. Enthusiast catalogues (S05) and AlternativeTo (S06) rank by community votes with no named scheme. Tiers never sit on a kind (C4a). A platform's own labels (G2 Grid, App Store editors' choice) would be `platform_tag`s (C4c); none was fetched.
- **Price band (C4(d), CV-14).** Software adds a **free** class, a **recurring price** and **per-seat pricing** to the shared USD scheme, and the shared rule handles all three. The band is stored on each variant or plan row, not on a kind or named form, since one product has several prices. It is computed from the annualised list price of the plan as sold, all seats included with `seat_count` recorded, at a dated USD rate; a promotional first-year price is recorded but not used; one-time, perpetual and lifetime terms count at full price with the term recorded; free plans take the band "0 (free)" (AlternativeTo counts 119.7K free entries, S06); a named form's band is that of its cheapest paid plan, with `has_free_plan`. `price_model` (free, freemium, one-time, subscription, bundle) is a separate attribute. By this rule Microsoft 365 Personal ($99.99 a year, S26) is 50 to 150; Microsoft 365 Family ($129.99 a year, `seat_count` 6, S26) is 50 to 150; Premium ($199.99) is 150 to 500; Office Home 2024 ($179.99 one-time, full price, S26) is 150 to 500; Photoshop at $22.99 a month on an annual plan (about $276 a year, S27) is 150 to 500; and Creative Cloud Pro at $69.99 a month (about $840 a year, S27) is 500 to 1,500.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended type: identity, with platform as a separate compatibility ledger.**
- **Identity.** Buyers and labs treat the maker as part of the object. Amazon exposes Brand as its own facet (Intuit, Microsoft, Avanquest, S17). AV-TEST lists results "by manufacturer" (S20) and AV-Comparatives by vendor (S21). Maker storefronts sell named lines (Microsoft 365, Creative Cloud Pro, Photoshop, S26, S27). AlternativeTo's whole model is "alternatives to a named product" (S06). Ninite lists maker products as its items (S05). The named form of a kind is the product, so `maker_bound = yes` for almost every named form (C2); a non-maker-bound named form would be a generic variant that many makers ship (for example a "portable edition" of a program), and none was found as a distinct form in the fetched sources.
- **Platform.** The maker also sets compatibility: operating system (Amazon's Operating System facet, S17; AlternativeTo's Windows, Mac, Linux, Android and iPhone facets, S06), host program for add-ons (Google Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Visual Studio Code, JetBrains IDE, S06; Google Workspace and Salesforce marketplaces, S02) and package source (Flathub, Homebrew, Chocolatey, Snapcraft, S06). Record these in the C13 compatibility ledger (`platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `sources`), which is shared across domain I and D5. Platforms are never levels. AlternativeTo's "Online", "SaaS" and "Self-Hosted" values are delivery, not platforms: they are a `delivery` attribute (section 7), never a ledger row (review I1-8).
- **Maker ledger.** Yes: the one domain-wide C1 ledger (CV-1, CV-2), with `platform_ids` not a maker column (the C13 `owner` field links maker and platform) and `known_for_kinds` derived from the named-form ledger. One maker row per company across I1, I2, I3 and D5. One fetched page admits a maker and the ledger has no cap (CV-5); it is seeded from existing catalogues first (AlternativeTo, Vector, Ninite, the lab vendor lists) and grows as far as the evidence goes. `maker_class` is the shared multi-valued C1 list; for I1 the values used are software publisher, community or foundation project (LibreOffice, OpenOffice, Blender and KeePass appear in S05 and have no company maker), platform holder (Apple, Google, Microsoft) and storefront or marketplace (with its `resale` flag). `parent_id` carries the group when one vendor sells several brand names (the fetched lab pages list AVG and Avast as separate rows, S20, S21; the group link would have to be sourced separately †), and the maker-relations table records rebrands and licences. A **licence** column does not belong to the maker; it belongs to the named form (SPDX identifier for open-source licences, S29; a controlled class for proprietary licences: perpetual, subscription, freemium, free for personal use), and a variant may override it.
- **Lines table.** A line that spans several kinds goes in the C1 lines table: Windows, Microsoft Office, Microsoft 365, Norton 360, Creative Cloud; a line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (C2(d)). A line is not a kind and not a tier; its order is `line_rank`.
- **Names and aliases (C9).** Examples from the fetched pages: "Microsoft 365 (formerly Office 365)" (S26 page title); "Antivirus", "Internet Security" and "Total Security" are product names for one kind (S17, S20, S21); "suite" means office suite, security suite or (with a maker's name) creative suite; "launcher" means application launcher (S31) or game launcher (S05); "client" means email client, remote-desktop client or game client. Japanese terms such as 家計簿 (household account book) and 付箋 (sticky notes) map to personal-finance manager and note-taking app (S07).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Kinds:** canonical form first, meaning the most general and most widely compared kind in the leaf, then narrower or more specialised kinds. Example in Images and design: raster image editor, vector graphics editor, graphic design app, photo manager, 3D modelling, then screenshot tool, font manager and home publishing. No numeric spec orders kinds against each other; lab tables change monthly (S20), so ranking is never a sort key.
- **Named forms:** non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms by maker in ledger `name` order, then first-release year as attested, then alphabetically (C7 as amended by CV-21). Almost every I1 named form is maker-bound.
- **Variants:** by `variant_code` or year (C7): version number or release date first, then plan. Windows 95, 98, XP and 11 are named forms (I-21); an edition, plan or minor version of one of them is a variant.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure.** Candidate A (I-1): category I1 > sub-family (the job the software does for its owner: eleven function sub-families SF1 to SF11) > leaf (the task, material or business function; null when the sub-family has about 12 kinds or fewer) > kind (the generic program type buyers compare) > named form (a product, or a release sold under its own name, almost always maker-bound; I-21), at most four levels below the category. AI is an attribute with a generated AI view; the key-named assistants keep their own sub-family SF10, and AI coding assistant and AI image generator are kinds, the image kind one kind with `task_mode` (I-5).
