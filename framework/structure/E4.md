# E4 Weapons & Armour — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md. Owner pointers for E4 (batch S2_E4): firearms by action and use (legal classes are modifiers), air guns, bows, edged weapons and swords by tradition-defined form (katana, sabre: argue kinds vs region tags), armour, ammunition; clinical, descriptive register. This memo classifies objects only; it contains no instructions for making, modifying or using any weapon. Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_E4_sources.tsv; the keys S01 to S37 below point to them (full URLs in the key table of section 1). A dagger mark (†) means the detail was read from the search excerpt of a page that was also fetched, because the fetch excerpt itself returned less.*

**One-line result.** Divide E4 into ten sub-families by how the object acts (firearms; air and gas guns; bows and crossbows; swords; knives and daggers; polearms, axes and impact weapons; self-defence devices; armour and shields; ammunition and projectiles; parts, fittings and accessories), then by form class (leaf: pistol, rifle, shotgun, air rifle, longsword, dagger), then by action or tradition-named type (kind: bolt-action rifle, PCP air rifle, katana), then by model or typology number (named form). Legal class, licence, discipline, era, region, user and material stay off the tree as tags and jurisdiction ledgers. Maximum depth below the category: four levels.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** E4 Weapons & Armour (domain E, Play, Sport & Music).

**Membership test (placement key, step 10).** "A weapon: made for fighting, self-defence or hunting, by striking, cutting, shooting, firing, spraying or electric shock (firearms, air guns, bows and crossbows, swords, fighting and hunting knives (including butterfly knives), martial-arts weapons, pepper spray, stun guns and tasers), including firearms, air guns, bows and crossbows made for target shooting; armour made to protect against such weapons; ammunition? E4 Weapons & Armour." Category line: "weapons for fighting, self-defence or hunting, target firearms and bows, armour against weapons, ammunition."

**Notes bound to step 10 (S1b).** Blunted or padded implements made for a combat sport (fencing foils, épées and sabres, shinai, bokken, training weapons) and the protective gear for them are E2. Toy and foam replicas are E1. Airsoft and paintball markers are E2. Decorative replicas that cannot injure are B4. Tools made to kill pests are B8; slaughter and stunning tools for livestock are C7; spearguns and fishing tackle are E2; machetes and axes made for cutting wood or vegetation are C1 or C2; kitchen and utility knives are B5 and C1.

**Other rules that touch E4.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): rifle scopes are E4 (step 23 note); a magazine, sling, choke tube, bowstring, broadhead or scabbard made for one kind of weapon follows it. A case made to carry one firearm or bow follows it. Furniture, racks, shelves and cabinets that hold several objects are never parts (B1); safes are B8. Cleaning products are B6 whatever they clean.
- Step 17 (A1 Clothing) lists protective clothing and helmets made against a hazard (hard hat, motorcycle helmet, welding helmet, ear defenders); step 10 comes first, so armour made against weapons is E4 and hazard gear stays A1.
- The tools step (C1): pocket knives and multi-tools are C1; machetes and axes made for wood or vegetation are C1 or C2.
- Step 23 (C6): binoculars, rangefinders and spotting optics are C6; "rifle scopes are E4 under S3".
- S5 (test the object, not the motive): an antique sword is a sword, a museum helmet is a helmet; collecting, heirloom and display are motives, not levels.
- BRIEF.md: brand depth and quality tiers must be capturable; condition, user and region are modifiers.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in E4** (never levels): region and culture (Japanese, Chinese, European, Indian), era (antique, medieval, Edo), price band and quality tier (NIJ level, certificate grade), brand and maker, scene (hunting, target, historical re-enactment, martial arts), user (youth, adapted), setting, market (civilian, law-enforcement, military), power source and technology (manual, gas, electric), material (steel, laminate), how acquired (reproduction, kit), condition (deactivated, inert), edition. Two E4-specific attributes behave like modifiers and are treated as such below: **legal class** (EU category A to D, US NFA class, UK Section 1) and **calibre or gauge** for weapons (but not for ammunition, where the cartridge is the kind).

### Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Archery Gear Junkie and Archery Care [S12, S35].** Four bow families: recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow. Traditional and barebow are shooting styles, not bow types; the World Archery barebow class is a recurve stripped of accessories.
- **Oakeshott typology [S14, S31].** Thirteen main types (X to XXII) with lettered subtypes for medieval European swords, defined by blade profile, fuller, cross-section and grip; Oakeshott preferred form to date "because dates are unreliable".
- **Chinese weapons guides [S19, S29, S34].** The apparatus list groups by length and handling: 长兵器 long, 短兵器 short, 软兵器 flexible, 双兵器 paired, 奇门兵器 exotic and 暗器 hidden projectiles. The "eighteen weapons" (十八般兵器) is a conventional list that differs by source. A kung fu guide repeats short, long, flexible and double, and notes blunt or wax-wood practice variants.
- **Wikipedia lists [S13, S20].** The classification of swords uses two cuts: by hilt (one-handed, two-handed, hand-and-a-half) and by blade (double-edged straight; edgeless and thrusting; single-edged and curved). The list of martial arts weapons uses weapon type (bladed, polearms, blunt, flexible, projectile) or, alternatively, the martial-arts school.

### Collectors and the resale market

- **NihontoWatch [S10].** Sword types as listed for sale: Katana, Wakizashi, Tanto, Tachi, Naginata, Yari, Daisho; then browse paths by NBTHK certification grade (Hozon, Tokubetsu Hozon, Juyo, Tokubetsu Juyo), by school (Bizen, Mino, Yamato, Yamashiro, Soshu) and by era (Koto, Shinto, Shinshinto). Type, grade, school and era are separate axes on the same listing.
- **katana24 and Wikipedia on Japanese swords [S11, S30].** Type is fixed by blade length in shaku: tachi and uchigatana (katana) over 2 shaku (about 60 cm), wakizashi 1 to 2, tantō under 1; ōdachi/nodachi over about 90 cm, kodachi under 2 shaku, with the carry style (edge down or edge up) separating tachi from katana. Wikipedia notes the thresholds are "arbitrary but necessary" at the edges. Certificate rank sets price bands.
- **Metropolitan Museum [S18].** One department of about 14,000 objects divided by culture (European, Near Eastern, Far Eastern) and, within, by object class (armour, helmets, shields, swords and smallswords, firearms).

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS / US HTS chapter 93 [S16].** 9301 military weapons; 9302 revolvers and pistols; 9303 other firearms (shotguns, rifles, pistols and revolvers that fire only blank cartridges †); 9304 other arms (spring, air or gas guns and pistols, truncheons); 9305 parts and accessories of 9301 to 9304; 9306 bombs and munitions, cartridges and other ammunition and projectiles including shot and wads (paintballs too †); 9307 swords, cutlasses, bayonets, lances, parts and scabbards. The trade code agrees with the framework's grouping of firearms, air guns, blades and ammunition, but makes parts one heading and puts swords with bayonets and lances.
- **EU Firearms Directive 91/477/EEC [S15].** Four legal categories by danger: A prohibited, B authorisation, C declaration, D other, defined by short or long, repeating, semi-automatic or single-shot, rimfire or centre-fire and overall length; ammunition follows the firearm. This is a legal class, not a physical kind, and it differs by country.
- **NIJ body armor standards [S17, S32].** Ballistic levels were renamed from II, IIIA, III, IV to HG1, HG2, RF1, RF3, with a new RF2 (HG for handgun, RF for rifle); a separate stab-resistance standard (0115.00) has two knife protection classes. A retailer guide shows the old numbering as soft armour (II, IIIA), hard plates (III, IV) and in-conjunction-with plates worn over soft armour [S36].

### Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree.** The physical form of a firearm (handgun, rifle, shotgun) is the first cut at every retailer and in the trade code. Air guns divide by powerplant. Bows divide into recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow. Japanese swords divide by length and carry into tachi, katana, wakizashi, tantō. Body armour is divided by a threat level set by a standard.
- **Split 1: legal class against physical form.** Frankonia puts bows, crossbows, air guns, airsoft and blank-firing guns together as "Freie Waffen" (free to adults), and the EU directive and the UK 12 ft-lb limit divide the same air rifle by energy [S04, S15, S33]. US retailers do not use legal class as a shelf.
- **Split 2: use against form.** Pyramyd's "Product Use" and Lancaster's "Archery Style" put hunting, competition and plinking beside the form tree [S06, S09]; Frankonia opens hunting and sport weapons on separate pages [S21].
- **Split 3: culture and era against blade form.** Kult of Athena and the Met divide by era and culture [S18, S25, S26]; the Oakeshott typology and Wikipedia divide by blade and hilt form [S13, S31]; NihontoWatch uses form (type) and offers era and school as parallel browse paths [S10].
- **Split 4: where knives live.** Frankonia files knives with equipment, Blade HQ sells them as a stand-alone shop, Kult of Athena files them under Weapons, and HS 9307 files only swords and bayonets as arms [S07, S16, S22, S26].
- **Split 5: where accessories and parts live.** Retailers keep them as separate shelves beside the weapon class (S02, S03, S04); the trade code makes them one heading (9305).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Form class first, at nearly every shop.** The first split on US firearm shelves is handgun, rifle, shotgun, with ammunition as a peer [S01, S02, S23]; Frankonia opens on Kurzwaffen and Langwaffen, then Büchsen and Flinten [S04]; Euroshot opens Büchsen then action (Repetier, Selbstlade, Kipplauf) [S05]. For air guns the first split is rifle against pistol, then **powerplant** (PCP, spring piston, CO2), which is the mechanism buyers compare most [S06, S27].

**Action or mechanism second.** Sportsman's has an "Action" facet on handguns [S23]; Euroshot and Frankonia split rifles by bolt, lever, self-loading, break-barrel and shotguns by over-under (Bockdoppel) against self-loading [S04, S05]; Pyramyd splits by powerplant [S06]; archery guides split by recurve, compound, longbow [S12, S35].

**Use is a parallel filter, not the tree.** Pyramyd's Product Use facet (competition, hunting, plinking) and Lancaster's Archery Style (Olympic recurve, 3D, bowhunting) sit beside the form tree [S06, S09]; Bass Pro and Frankonia keep Hunting and Shooting as store sections but the weapon pages repeat. The same bolt-action rifle serves hunting, target and military-style uses.

**Legal eligibility comes before the shopping starts** in Germany and the UK (Frankonia's split of Freie Waffen against licensed weapons; the 12 ft-lb limit [S21, S33]) but is not a navigation level in the US. It is a jurisdiction property, so it cannot be a tree level in a global taxonomy.

**For cold steel, the buyer chooses by tradition-named form** (katana, jian, longsword, halberd) and then by authenticity class (battle ready, decorative, blunted) at Kult of Athena [S25, S26] and by certificate grade at NihontoWatch [S10]. Era and culture are the browse path at the Met and at Kult of Athena's Cultures shelf [S18, S25].

**For armour the buyer chooses by protection level first** (soft against hard, then NIJ level), and by body region second [S17, S36]; for historical armour by region protected (helmet, breastplate, arm armour) [S08].

**Maker comes after type** everywhere except for knives and nihonto, where the maker or smith is part of the search (Blade HQ brand shelf, NihontoWatch schools) [S10, S37].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates keep legal class, discipline, era, region, user and material off the tree. They differ in the **sub-family axis** (what the first cut is).

### Candidate A (recommended): mechanism of action → form class → action or tradition-named type → model

| Level | Axis (one per level) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | how the object acts on a target or protects against it (propellant, compressed gas, stored muscle energy, blade, shaft or impact, non-lethal agent, protection, projectile, fitting) | Firearms; Air and gas guns; Bows and crossbows; Swords; Knives and daggers; Polearms, axes and impact weapons; Self-defence devices; Armour and shields; Ammunition and projectiles; Parts, fittings and accessories |
| Leaf | form class of the whole object | Pistols, Revolvers, Rifles, Shotguns; Air rifles; Daggers |
| Kind | action, powerplant or tradition-named type that buyers compare | bolt-action rifle; PCP air rifle; katana |
| Named form | model, platform, lettered typology or iconic design | Oakeshott type XV; AR-15 platform; Hoyt Rx-10 |

Why it fits: it follows the form-then-action tree of Frankonia, Euroshot, Sportsman's, Pyramyd and the HS code [S04, S05, S06, S16, S23], keeps blades, impact weapons and armour in their own groups as the Met and Kult of Athena do [S18, S26], and lets every Appendix A modifier become a tag.

*Note:* E4.10 is shown with one kind only to keep the example inside 60 kinds; its other kinds (magazine, sling, choke tube, bowstring, broadhead, scabbard) are counted in section 4.

**Where Candidate A breaks.**
1. *Tantō.* A sword by Japanese classification (under one shaku) but a knife by use [S30]. Placed under Daggers (E4.5) with the region tag "Japan, nihonto"; the nihonto view gathers it with katana and wakizashi.
2. *Muzzle-loaders.* Sportsman's and Frankonia give them a shelf of their own [S02, S04], but the muzzle-loading action is a mechanism, so it is a kind (muzzle-loading rifle) under each form leaf. A "muzzle-loading" tag generates the shelf view.
3. *Combination gun and drilling.* Rifle barrel plus shotgun barrel: it belongs to neither Rifles nor Shotguns; it needs its own leaf (Frankonia keeps "Kombinierte Waffen" apart [S04]).
4. *Bayonet, spear and bow-knife hybrids* (a bayonet is a knife by form but HS 9307 pairs it with swords and lances [S16]); and an arrow, which Lancaster and Kult of Athena file with archery but which is ammunition [S09, S25].
5. *Baton.* Bass Pro files it with self-defence [S01]; by form it is an impact weapon. Recommended: E4.6 by form, flagged for owner.

### Candidate B: use first (hunting, target and sport, self-defence, military and historical, collecting)

| Level | Axis |

Used by Frankonia's separate Jagd and Schießsport weapons pages, Pyramyd's Product Use facet and Lancaster's Archery Style [S06, S09, S21].

(2) A stab-resistant vest has no use sub-family but sits between self-defence and military. (3) A katana is historical, collectible, martial-arts and self-defence at once. (4) Use is motive and market in Appendix A and may not divide the top level or inner levels. Rejected, but kept as tags: *use* = hunting, target, competition, self-defence, collecting.

### Candidate C: tradition or culture first for blades and armour (Japanese, Chinese, European, Indian and Middle Eastern, American), mechanism for firearms

| Level | Axis |
|---|---|
| Sub-family | culture of origin (cold steel) or mechanism (firearms) |
| Leaf | weapon or armour class |
| Kind | tradition-named type |
| Named form | school, typology number or model |

Used by Kult of Athena's Cultures shelf, the Met's European, Near Eastern, Far Eastern galleries and the Chinese apparatus lists [S18, S19, S25].
Example kinds: Japanese: katana, tachi, wakizashi, tantō, naginata, yari, ō-yoroi; Chinese: jian, dao, qiang, gun staff, nine-section whip; European: longsword, smallsword, halberd, plate harness, shield; plus the firearms kinds of Candidate A.

**Where it breaks.** (1) Region and culture are Appendix A modifiers; making them a level splits "sabre" (Hungarian, French, British, American) across four places. (2) A cutlass or scimitar crosses European, Arab and Ottoman usage. (3) A Western-made katana reproduction has no clear culture. (4) Chinese weapons are classed by length and handling (long, short, flexible), not by region, so the culture level hides a real form axis [S19, S34]. Rejected as a level, **kept as a region/tradition tag and a generated "nihonto" or "Chinese weapons" view**.

### Katana and sabre: kinds against region tags

Argued position, recommended:
- The tradition-named term is a **kind** when it names a generic type that many makers produce and a person can own one of: katana, tachi, wakizashi, tantō, jian, dao, longsword, smallsword, halberd, naginata. NihontoWatch, Wikipedia and katana24 each treat katana, tachi, wakizashi and tantō as parallel types set by length and carry [S10, S11, S30]; that is a form axis, so they are kinds, not region tags.
- The **region or tradition is a tag** on those kinds (Japan, China, Europe), together with era (Koto, Shinto, Shinshinto, Edo), school (Bizen, Mino, Soshu) and certificate grade (Hozon to Tokubetsu Juyo) [S10]. A generated view "nihonto" collects every kind with tag Japan and form of a sword.
- **Sabre** is a cross-cultural kind (single-edged curved cavalry sword); national patterns are named forms or tags, not kinds. The evidence in this batch for sabre and rapier as separate pages is thin: the fetched sword lists name cutlass, falchion, scimitar and smallsword [S13, S18], so the kind list for single-edged and thrusting swords needs a second attestation round.
- Lettered typologies (Oakeshott X to XXII; Japanese blade-construction names such as shinogi-zukuri) are **named forms** under longsword and katana: the typology classifies form, not a distinct kind a person owns [S30, S31].

## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.** Evidence per sub-family:

- **Firearms: stop at action type within form** (bolt-action rifle, lever-action rifle, over-under shotgun). Euroshot and Frankonia split Büchsen by action and Flinten by over-under against self-loading [S04, S05]; Sportsman's offers an Action facet on handguns [S23]. Calibre, capacity and barrel length are facets on the named form [S23]. Buyers compare makers and models inside the kind (Euroshot brand counts, Sportsman's brand counts of 100 to 190 per major maker) [S05, S23]. Below this level (model, e.g. a particular bolt-action line) is a named form.
- **Air guns: stop at powerplant within rifle or pistol.** Pyramyd builds its tree on powerplant and keeps caliber, action and product use as facets [S06, S27]; Air Arms sells spring and PCP rifles as separate ranges [S33].
- **Bows: stop at the family** (recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow). Lancaster and both guides stop there; Olympic, 3D and bowhunting styles are disciplines [S09, S12, S35]. Draw weight and draw length are attributes.
- **Swords: stop at the tradition-named type** (katana, tachi, wakizashi, longsword). NihontoWatch lists type as its first browse path and dealers price by type [S10, S11]; finer distinctions (Oakeshott subtype, blade construction, school) are named forms or tags [S30, S31].
- **Knives: stop at fixed against folding against automatic, plus the named style** (karambit, push dagger). Blade HQ uses type then style, with steel and size as facets [S07, S37].
- **Armour: stop at product class** (soft vest, hard plate, stab vest, helmet, harness); the protection level is a modifier [S17, S32, S36].
- **Ammunition: go deeper, to the cartridge, gauge or pellet calibre**, because retailers branch by calibre and buyers must match it to the weapon [S24, S03 †, S05 †]. Bullet weight, bullet type and brand are named forms or facets (title strings such as "380 Auto 95 gr FMJ" [S24]).
- **Parts: stop at part type**, with the weapon platform as a compatibility attribute, since Brownells and Pyramyd shelve parts by firearm model or rail type [S03, S28].

*Limit of the evidence.* This batch did not fetch review-site "best X" lists (cap on searches), so the comparison argument rests on retailer facets, guides and dealer pricing, not on review groupings.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Overall: mixed. Spec-ranked for armour and for a few legal-energy thresholds; consensus-ranked for antique swords; preference elsewhere.**

- **Armour: spec-ranked.** NIJ Standard 0123.00 ballistic levels HG1, HG2, RF1, RF2, RF3 (formerly II, IIIA, III, IV), a stab standard 0115.00 with two knife classes [S17, S32, S36]. Appears as a modifier `protection_level` on the kind; it is not a level.
- **Antique swords: consensus-ranked.** NBTHK certification: Hozon, Tokubetsu Hozon, Juyo, Tokubetsu Juyo, where Juyo is described as roughly the top 5 per cent of submissions [S10 †]; price bands follow the rank [S11]. A modifier `certificate_grade`.
- **Authenticity class: a modifier that crosses categories.** Kult of Athena's Battle Ready, Sport Combat, Decorative, LARP [S26] decide whether an object is E4, E2, B4 or E1; for objects that remain E4 it is `function_class` (battle-ready, working replica, antique).
- **Firearms, air guns, bows, knives: preference, with spec attributes.** Price bands and maker reputation set the order; spec facets (calibre, velocity, regulated, draw weight, steel type) are attributes, not tiers [S06, S07, S23, S27]. UK and EU legal energy or category thresholds [S15, S33] are legal class, not quality.
- **Ammunition: spec-ranked by specification only** (calibre, bullet weight), no tier.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity and platform.** Identity: knife makers and nihonto smiths and schools are part of what is sought (Blade HQ's brand shelf with Spyderco, Kershaw, Boker, Cold Steel, Benchmade [S37]; NihontoWatch's school pages [S10]); Sportsman's lists dozens of handgun makers with counts (Smith & Wesson 193, Sig Sauer 178, Taurus 175, Springfield Armory 144, Ruger 109) [S23], and Frankonia keeps an A to Z brand list for weapons, ammunition and equipment [S04]. **Platform:** parts are chosen by firearm model or rail system (Brownells magazines by P365 and P320; Pyramyd rail mounts by rifle; Frankonia Wechselsysteme for pistols) [S03, S04, S28], and bow makers advertise their own cam and limb systems [S09].

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger,** with columns maker, known-for kinds, scene tags (hunting, target, historical), tier (preference), price band, country, era, platform or chambering, and for nihonto a parallel *school and smith* table. Each row attested. Brand is not a level. Platform families (AR-15 family, striker-fired pistol families, rail standards) belong in a compatibility table linked to named forms and parts.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then complexity of action, then chronology of the named form.** Within a leaf such as Rifles, order kinds from the oldest and simplest action (single-shot) through repeating (bolt, lever, pump) to self-loading; within a kind, order named forms by introduction date or typology number. Reasons: the Oakeshott typology, the lettered Japanese periods and the sequence of mechanisms are already chronological or progressive [S10, S31]; retailers' alphabetical or price order does not give a stable position. For ammunition, order kinds by bore diameter within the cartridge class.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure.** Candidate A: sub-family (mechanism of action; ten sub-families E4.1 to E4.10) > leaf (form class) > kind (action, powerplant or tradition-named type) > named form (model, platform, typology number). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels.** Appendix A modifiers become tags or ledgers: legal class and jurisdiction (a ledger by country: EU A to D, UK Section 1, US NFA), discipline and use, region and tradition, era, school, certificate grade, protection level, function class (battle-ready, working replica, antique, decorative-excluded), user (youth), material, calibre, condition (deactivated, inert).

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. Confirm the ten sub-families by mechanism of action, or merge E4.4 and E4.5 into one "Blades" sub-family.
2. Knife boundary with C1: fixed fighting and hunting knives, daggers and folding fighting knives (butterfly, automatic) are E4; confirm that general-use fixed-blade, EDC and tactical folding knives stay C1 or B5.
3. Katana and sabre: accept tradition-named types as kinds with region and school as tags, and typologies as named forms (section 3).
4. Arrows and bolts: in E4.9 Ammunition (recommended; S3 consumables) or in E4.3 with bows.
5. Calibre: confirm that calibre is a kind for ammunition but a facet for weapons.
6. Muzzle-loaders and antiques: kinds under each form leaf with a tag (recommended) or a separate leaf.
7. Sword fittings (tsuba, menuki, scabbards) and collectible loose armour pieces: E4 under S3, or F2 when sold as art.
8. Ordnance: bombs, grenades, torpedoes, mines, missiles and artillery (HS 9301, 9306) [S16] are not touched by the key; confirm out of scope except as deactivated or inert display items.
9. Slingshots, blowguns, boomerangs, shuriken and similar projectile devices: E4, E2 or E1?
10. Reloading presses, dies and components (primers, bullets, powder): E4 under S3 or C1 as workshop machines.
11. Maker ledger scope: firearms, bows, knives, nihonto smiths and schools (recommended) against armour and ammunition makers as well.
12. Legal class: keep it out of the tree and record it in a per-country ledger, or omit it from the dataset.
13. Reproduction armour and swords for re-enactment: E4 when battle ready, E2 when blunted, B4 when decorative (Kult of Athena Class spec as the working rule).
14. Modern helmets made against bullets or fragments: no source on a fetched page confirmed a retailer or standards grouping (the key is silent); recommended as E4.8 Armour, needs an attestation round.
15. Sabre, rapier and the other European sword kinds need a second attestation round; this batch fetched general lists and the Oakeshott typology but no retailer or museum page naming them as kinds.
