# E4 Weapons & Armour — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md. Owner pointers for E4 (batch S2_E4): firearms by action and use (legal classes are modifiers), air guns, bows, edged weapons and swords by tradition-defined form (katana, sabre: argue kinds vs region tags), armour, ammunition; clinical, descriptive register. This memo classifies objects only; it contains no instructions for making, modifying or using any weapon. Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_E4_sources.tsv; the keys S01 to S37 below point to them (full URLs in the key table of section 1). A dagger mark (†) means the detail was read from the search excerpt of a page that was also fetched, because the fetch excerpt itself returned less.*

**One-line result.** Divide E4 into ten sub-families by how the object acts (firearms; air and gas guns; bows and crossbows; swords; knives and daggers; polearms, axes and impact weapons; self-defence devices; armour and shields; ammunition and projectiles; parts, fittings and accessories), then by form class (leaf: pistol, rifle, shotgun, air rifle, longsword, dagger), then by action or tradition-named type (kind: bolt-action rifle, PCP air rifle, katana), then by model or typology number (named form). Legal class, licence, discipline, era, region, user and material stay off the tree as tags and jurisdiction ledgers. Maximum depth below the category: four levels.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** E4 Weapons & Armour (domain E, Play, Sport & Music).

**Membership test (placement key, step 10).** "A weapon: made for fighting, self-defence or hunting, by striking, cutting, shooting, firing, spraying or electric shock (firearms, air guns, bows and crossbows, swords, fighting and hunting knives (including butterfly knives), martial-arts weapons, pepper spray, stun guns and tasers), including firearms, air guns, bows and crossbows made for target shooting; armour made to protect against such weapons; ammunition? E4 Weapons & Armour." Category line: "weapons for fighting, self-defence or hunting, target firearms and bows, armour against weapons, ammunition."

**Notes bound to step 10 (S1b).** Blunted or padded implements made for a combat sport (fencing foils, épées and sabres, shinai, bokken, training weapons) and the protective gear for them are E2. Toy and foam replicas are E1. Airsoft and paintball markers are E2. Decorative replicas that cannot injure are B4. Tools made to kill pests are B8; slaughter and stunning tools for livestock are C7; spearguns and fishing tackle are E2; machetes and axes made for cutting wood or vegetation are C1 or C2; kitchen and utility knives are B5 and C1.

**Other rules that touch E4.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): rifle scopes are E4 (step 23 note); a magazine, sling, choke tube, bowstring, broadhead or scabbard made for one kind of weapon follows it. A case made to carry one firearm or bow follows it. Furniture, racks, shelves and cabinets that hold several objects are never parts (B1); safes are B8. Cleaning products are B6 whatever they clean.
- Step 17 (A1 Clothing) lists protective clothing and helmets made against a hazard (hard hat, motorcycle helmet, welding helmet, ear defenders); step 10 comes first, so armour made against weapons is E4 and hazard gear stays A1.
- The tools step (C1): pocket knives and multi-tools are C1; machetes and axes made for wood or vegetation are C1 or C2.
- Step 23 (C6): binoculars, rangefinders and spotting optics are C6; "rifle scopes are E4 under S3".
- S5 (test the object, not the motive): an antique sword is a sword, a museum helmet is a helmet; collecting, heirloom and display are motives, not levels.
- BRIEF.md: brand depth and quality tiers must be capturable; condition, user and region are modifiers.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in E4** (never levels): region and culture (Japanese, Chinese, European, Indian), era (antique, medieval, Edo), price band and quality tier (NIJ level, certificate grade), brand and maker, scene (hunting, target, historical re-enactment, martial arts), user (youth, adapted), setting, market (civilian, law-enforcement, military), power source and technology (manual, gas, electric), material (steel, laminate), how acquired (reproduction, kit), condition (deactivated, inert), edition. Two E4-specific attributes behave like modifiers and are treated as such below: **legal class** (EU category A to D, US NFA class, UK Section 1) and **calibre or gauge** for weapons (but not for ammunition, where the cartridge is the kind).

### Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Archery Gear Junkie and Archery Care [S12, S35].** Four bow families: recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow. Traditional and barebow are shooting styles, not bow types; the World Archery barebow class is a recurve stripped of accessories.
- **Oakeshott typology [S14, S31].** Thirteen main types (X to XXII) with lettered subtypes for medieval European swords, defined by blade profile, fuller, cross-section and grip; Oakeshott preferred form to date "because dates are unreliable".
- **Chinese weapons guides [S19, S29, S34].** The apparatus list groups by length and handling: 长兵器 long, 短兵器 short, 软兵器 flexible, 双兵器 paired, 奇门兵器 exotic and 暗器 hidden projectiles. The "eighteen weapons" (十八般兵器) is a conventional list that differs by source. A kung fu guide repeats short, long, flexible and double, and notes blunt or wax-wood practice variants.
- **Wikipedia lists [S13, S20].** The classification of swords uses two cuts: by hilt (one-handed, two-handed, hand-and-a-half) and by blade (double-edged straight; edgeless and thrusting; single-edged and curved). The list of martial arts weapons uses weapon type (bladed, polearms, blunt, flexible, projectile) or, alternatively, the martial-arts school.

### Collectors and the resale market

- **NihontoWatch [S10].** Sword types as listed for sale: Katana, Wakizashi, Tanto, Tachi, Naginata, Yari, Daisho; then browse paths by NBTHK certification grade (Hozon, Tokubetsu Hozon, Juyo, Tokubetsu Juyo), by school (Bizen, Mino, Yamato, Yamashiro, Soshu) and by era (Koto, Shinto, Shinshinto). Type, grade, school and era are separate axes on the same listing.
- **katana24 and Wikipedia on Japanese swords [S11, S30].** Type is fixed by blade length in shaku: tachi and uchigatana (katana) over 2 shaku (about 60 cm), wakizashi 1 to 2, tantō under 1; ōdachi/nodachi over about 90 cm, kodachi under 2 shaku, with the carry style (edge down or edge up) separating tachi from katana. Wikipedia notes the thresholds are "arbitrary but necessary" at the edges. Certificate rank sets price bands.
- **Metropolitan Museum [S18].** One department of about 14,000 objects divided by culture (European, Near Eastern, Far Eastern) and, within, by object class (armour, helmets, shields, swords and smallswords, firearms, spurs, ornamental fittings).

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS / US HTS chapter 93 [S16].** 9301 military weapons; 9302 revolvers and pistols; 9303 other firearms (sporting shotguns and rifles, muzzle-loaders, signal pistols, blank-firing pistols); 9304 other arms (spring, air or gas guns and pistols, truncheons); 9305 parts and accessories of 9301 to 9304; 9306 bombs and munitions, cartridges, shot and wads, air-gun pellets; 9307 swords, cutlasses, bayonets, lances, parts and scabbards. The trade code agrees with the framework's grouping of firearms, air guns, blades and ammunition, but makes parts one heading and puts swords with bayonets and lances.
- **EU Firearms Directive 91/477/EEC [S15].** Four legal categories by danger: A prohibited, B authorisation, C declaration, D other, defined by short or long, repeating, semi-automatic or single-shot, rimfire or centre-fire and overall length; ammunition follows the firearm. This is a legal class, not a physical kind, and it differs by country.
- **NIJ body armor standards [S17, S32].** Ballistic levels were renamed from II, IIIA, III, IV to HG1, HG2, RF1, RF3, with a new RF2 (HG for handgun, RF for rifle); a separate stab-resistance standard (0115.00) has two knife protection classes. A retailer guide shows the old numbering as soft armour (II, IIIA), hard plates (III, IV) and in-conjunction-with plates worn over soft armour [S36].

### Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree.** The physical form of a firearm (handgun, rifle, shotgun) is the first cut at every retailer and in the trade code. Air guns divide by powerplant. Bows divide into recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow. Japanese swords divide by length and carry into tachi, katana, wakizashi, tantō. Body armour is divided by a threat level set by a standard.
- **Split 1: legal class against physical form.** Frankonia puts bows, crossbows, air guns, airsoft and blank-firing guns together as "Freie Waffen" (free to adults), and the EU directive and the UK 12 ft-lb limit divide the same air rifle by energy [S04, S15, S33]. US retailers do not use legal class as a shelf.
- **Split 2: use against form.** Pyramyd's "Product Use" and Lancaster's "Archery Style" put hunting, competition and plinking beside the form tree [S06, S09]; Frankonia opens hunting and sport weapons on separate pages [S21].
- **Split 3: culture and era against blade form.** Kult of Athena and the Met divide by era and culture [S18, S25, S26]; the Oakeshott typology and Wikipedia divide by blade and hilt form [S13, S31]; NihontoWatch uses form (type) and offers era and school as parallel browse paths [S10].
- **Split 4: where knives live.** Frankonia files knives with equipment, Blade HQ sells them as a stand-alone shop, Kult of Athena files them under Weapons, and HS 9307 files only swords and bayonets as arms [S07, S16, S22, S26].
- **Split 5: where accessories and parts live.** Retailers keep them as separate shelves beside the weapon class (S02, S03, S04); the trade code makes them one heading (9305).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Form class first, at nearly every shop.** The first split on US firearm shelves is handgun, rifle, shotgun, with ammunition as a peer [S01, S02, S23]; Frankonia opens on Kurzwaffen and Langwaffen, then Büchsen and Flinten [S04]; Euroshot opens Büchsen then action (Repetier, Selbstlade, Kipplauf) [S05]. For air guns the first split is rifle against pistol, then **powerplant** (PCP, spring piston, CO2), which is the mechanism buyers compare most [S06, S27].

**Action or mechanism second.** Sportsman's has an "Action" facet on handguns [S23]; Euroshot and Frankonia split rifles by bolt, lever, self-loading, break-barrel and shotguns by over-under (Bockdoppel) against self-loading [S04, S05]; Pyramyd splits by powerplant [S06]; archery guides split by recurve, compound, longbow [S12, S35].

**Use is a parallel filter, not the tree.** Pyramyd's Product Use facet (competition, hunting, plinking) and Lancaster's Archery Style (Olympic recurve, 3D, bowhunting) sit beside the form tree [S06, S09]; Bass Pro and Frankonia keep Hunting and Shooting as store sections but the weapon pages repeat. The same bolt-action rifle serves hunting, target and military-style uses.

**Legal eligibility comes before the shopping starts** in Germany and the UK (Frankonia's split of Freie Waffen against licensed weapons; the 12 ft-lb limit [S21, S33]) but is not a navigation level in the US. It is a jurisdiction property, so it cannot be a tree level in a global taxonomy.

**For cold steel, the buyer chooses by tradition-named form** (katana, jian, longsword, halberd) and then by authenticity class (battle ready, decorative, blunted) at Kult of Athena [S25, S26] and by certificate grade at NihontoWatch [S10]. Era and culture are the browse path at the Met and at Kult of Athena's Cultures shelf [S18, S25].

**For armour the buyer chooses by protection level first** (soft against hard, then NIJ level), and by body region second [S17, S36]; for historical armour by region protected (helmet, breastplate, arm armour) [S08].

**Maker comes after type** everywhere except for knives and nihonto, where the maker or smith is part of the search (Blade HQ brand shelf, NihontoWatch schools) [S10, S37].
