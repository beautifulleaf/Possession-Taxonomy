# E4 Weapons & Armour — structure memo (decisions applied)

*Stage 2 memo with the stage-2 decisions applied (batch S2U_E4). The decisions are delegated by the owner (BRIEF.md) and bind until the owner overturns them; they are in framework/structure/decisions/ (D1 to D4). Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md (now v0.7), BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md, and revised in batch S2C_E4 to follow the domain E review (framework/structure/_review_E.md) and the shared conventions C1 to C17 (framework/structure/_conventions.md); then brought into line with the decisions, framework/structure/CONVENTIONS_v1.md and framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md in batch S2U_E4. Owner pointers for E4 (batch S2_E4): firearms by action and use (legal classes are modifiers), air guns, bows, edged weapons and swords by tradition-defined form (katana, sabre: argue kinds vs region tags), armour, ammunition; clinical, descriptive register. This memo classifies objects only; it contains no instructions for making, modifying or using any weapon. Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_E4_sources.tsv; the keys S01 to S37 below point to them (full URLs in the key table of section 1). **A dagger mark (†) means the detail is not yet backed by a fetched page.** Two cases carry it: the detail was read only from the search excerpt of a page that was also fetched (original research pass), or the name or claim was added by the review fix and no page fetched in this batch attests it (conformance pass). Every † is listed in section 10 under "Sourcing still needed". Every owner mark of the earlier draft has been resolved; section 12 lists each decision applied.*

**One-line result.** Divide E4 into ten sub-families by how the object acts (firearms; air and gas guns; bows and crossbows; swords; knives and daggers; polearms, axes and impact weapons; self-defence devices; armour and shields; ammunition and projectiles; parts, fittings and accessories). Below the sub-family the leaf axis is stated per sub-family (form class for firearms, air guns and bows; blade profile for swords; handling for polearms and impact weapons; part of the body protected for armour; weapon class served for parts). The kind is then the action, powerplant, blade style or tradition-named type that buyers compare (bolt-action rifle, PCP air rifle, katana, karambit, centrefire rifle cartridge), and the named form is the model, the typology number, the standard cartridge or the iconic design. Sub-families with about 12 kinds or fewer (knives, self-defence devices, ammunition) store `leaf = null` (C5b). Legal class, licence, discipline, era, region, school, user, material, calibre and protection level stay off the tree as tags, attributes and ledgers (C4(c), C13, C17). Maximum depth below the category: four levels. The decisions applied in batch S2U_E4 are in section 12.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** E4 Weapons & Armour (domain E, Play, Sport & Music).

**Membership test (placement key, step 10).** "A weapon: made for fighting, self-defence or hunting, by striking, cutting, shooting, firing, spraying or electric shock (firearms, air guns, bows and crossbows, swords, fighting and hunting knives (including butterfly knives), martial-arts weapons, pepper spray, stun guns and tasers), including firearms, air guns, bows and crossbows made for target shooting; armour made to protect against such weapons; ammunition? E4 Weapons & Armour." Category line: "weapons for fighting, self-defence or hunting, target firearms and bows, armour against weapons, ammunition."

**Notes bound to step 10 (S1b), as the key v0.7 states them, with the decisions K-83 to K-88, K-163 and R-16(a).** Blunted or padded implements made for a combat sport (fencing foils, épées and sabres, shinai, bokken, training weapons) and the protective gear for them are E2. Toy and foam replicas are E1. Airsoft and paintball markers are E2. Decorative replicas that cannot injure are B4. Tools made to kill pests are B8; slaughter and stunning tools for livestock are C7; spearguns and other fishing tackle are E2; axes, splitting wedges and log splitters are C1 (K-173); machetes, billhooks and pruning saws are C2 (R-16a); kitchen and utility knives are B5 and C1. Fighting and hunting knives, daggers, butterfly and automatic knives are E4; EDC and other locking folders are C1 unless the blade is a weapon kind (karambit, push dagger) (K-83). Batons and tonfa are E4. Holsters are A4; plate carriers are E4 armour; slingshots, blowguns and shuriken are E4 unless made as toys (E1); boomerangs are E2; distress flare pistols and flares are E2 (K-84). Bombs, grenades, mines and artillery are OUT, except deactivated display pieces (B4) (K-85). Reloading presses and dies are C1; bullets, primers and powder are E4 ammunition; loose tsuba, menuki and armour pieces are E4 under S3; a reproduction is E4 when battle-ready, E2 when blunted for sport, B4 when decorative and E1 when made of foam (K-86). Blunt or unsharpened wushu implements made for practice or competition are E2; decorative ones are B4; sharpened ones are E4 (K-87). A personal attack alarm is not a weapon; it is B8 (K-88). Hunting accessories (decoys, blinds, game calls) and clay throwers are E2; archery targets, arm guards and finger tabs are E2; arrows, bolts, bipods and rests follow the weapon to E4 (K-163).

**Other rules that touch E4.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): rifle scopes are E4 (step 23 note); a magazine, sling, choke tube, bowstring, broadhead or scabbard made for one kind of weapon follows it. A light made to mount on a weapon is E4 (K-162). A case made to carry one firearm or bow follows it. Furniture, racks, shelves and cabinets that hold several objects are never parts (B1); safes are B8. **Anything worn on the body is never a part (step 17)**, so a holster is not an E4 part (A4, K-84); a plate carrier is armour in its own right (K-84), not a part (section 9). Cleaning products are B6 whatever they clean.
- Step 17 (A1 Clothing) lists protective clothing and helmets made against a hazard (hard hat, motorcycle helmet, welding helmet, ear defenders, safety glasses); step 10 comes first, so armour made against weapons is E4 and hazard gear stays A1.
- The tools step (C1): pocket knives and multi-tools are C1; axes, splitting wedges and log splitters are C1 (K-173); machetes, billhooks and pruning saws are C2 (R-16a).
- Step 23 (C6): binoculars and spotting optics are C6; golf and hunting laser rangefinders are E2 (K-148); "rifle scopes are E4 under S3".
- S5 (test the object, not the motive): an antique sword is a sword, a museum helmet is a helmet; collecting, heirloom and display are motives, not levels.
- BRIEF.md: brand depth and quality tiers must be capturable; condition, user and region are modifiers; **individual unique items are out**, so nothing that is a fact about one particular blade, firearm or armour piece is stored (C16).
- Conventions applied in this memo: C1 (maker ledger core, lines, `built_by_maker_id`, standing `school` table), C2 (`maker_bound` flag on named forms), C3 (variants), C4 (no tier on a kind; specification ladders are attributes), C5 (levels, leaf axis per sub-family, parts sub-family), C6 (size and calibre are attributes), C7 (order), C9 (aliases and homonyms), C10 (technology as attribute unless it changes the body), C11 (regional names), C13 (compatibility ledger), C15 (toy or real), C16 (no individual-item facts), C17 (jurisdiction ledger), all as written in CONVENTIONS_v1.md.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in E4** (never levels): region and culture (Japanese, Chinese, European, Indian), era (antique, medieval, Edo), price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene (hunting, target, historical re-enactment, martial arts), user (child, teen, adult, adapted; CV-19, R-20), setting, market (civilian, law-enforcement, military), power source and technology (manual, gas, electric), material (steel, laminate), how acquired (reproduction, kit), condition (deactivated, inert), edition. Three E4-specific properties behave like modifiers and are treated as such below: **legal class** (EU category A to D, US NFA class, UK Section 1; C17 ledger), **calibre or gauge** (an attribute of the weapon; for ammunition the standard cartridge is a named form and a C13 row, see section 4b), and **protection level** (NIJ HG1 to RF3 and the stab standard; a C4(c) class (i) attribute with the standard named).

### Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Archery Gear Junkie and Archery Care [S12, S35].** Four bow families: recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow. Traditional and barebow are shooting styles, not bow types; the World Archery barebow class is a recurve stripped of accessories.

### Collectors and the resale market

- **Metropolitan Museum [S18].** One department of about 14,000 objects divided by culture (European, Near Eastern, Far Eastern) and, within, by object class (armour, helmets, shields, swords and smallswords, firearms).

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS / US HTS chapter 93 [S16].** 9301 military weapons; 9302 revolvers and pistols; 9303 other firearms (shotguns, rifles, pistols and revolvers that fire only blank cartridges †); 9304 other arms (spring, air or gas guns and pistols, truncheons); 9305 parts and accessories of 9301 to 9304; 9306 bombs and munitions, cartridges and other ammunition and projectiles including shot and wads (paintballs too †); 9307 swords, cutlasses, bayonets, lances, parts and scabbards. The trade code agrees with the framework's grouping of firearms, air guns, blades and ammunition, but makes parts one heading and puts swords with bayonets and lances.
- **EU Firearms Directive 91/477/EEC [S15].** Four legal categories by danger: A prohibited, B authorisation, C declaration, D other, defined by short or long, repeating, semi-automatic or single-shot, rimfire or centre-fire and overall length; ammunition follows the firearm. This is a legal class, not a physical kind, and it differs by country.
- **NIJ body armor standards [S17, S32].** Ballistic levels were renamed from II, IIIA, III, IV to HG1, HG2, RF1, RF3, with a new RF2 (HG for handgun, RF for rifle); a separate stab-resistance standard (0115.00) has two knife protection classes. A retailer guide shows the old numbering as soft armour (II, IIIA), hard plates (III, IV) and in-conjunction-with plates worn over soft armour [S36].

### Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree.** The physical form of a firearm (handgun, rifle, shotgun) is the first cut at every retailer and in the trade code. Air guns divide by powerplant. Bows divide into recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow. Japanese swords divide by length and carry into tachi, katana, wakizashi, tantō. Body armour is divided by a threat level set by a standard.
- **Split 1: legal class against physical form.** Frankonia puts bows, crossbows, air guns, airsoft and blank-firing guns together as "Freie Waffen" (free to adults), and the EU directive and the UK 12 ft-lb limit divide the same air rifle by energy [S04, S15, S33]. US retailers do not use legal class as a shelf.
- **Split 2: use against form.** Pyramyd's "Product Use" and Lancaster's "Archery Style" put hunting, competition and plinking beside the form tree [S06, S09]; Frankonia opens hunting and sport weapons on separate pages [S21].
- **Split 3: culture and era against blade form.** Kult of Athena and the Met divide by era and culture [S18, S25, S26]; the Oakeshott typology and Wikipedia divide by blade and hilt form [S13, S31]; NihontoWatch uses form (type) and offers era and school as parallel browse paths [S10].
- **Split 4: where knives live.** Frankonia files knives with equipment, Blade HQ sells them as a stand-alone shop, Kult of Athena files them under Weapons, and HS 9307 files only swords and bayonets as arms [S07, S16, S22, S26].
- **Split 5: where accessories and parts live.** Retailers keep them as separate shelves beside the weapon class (S02, S03, S04); the trade code makes them one heading (9305).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Form class first, at nearly every shop.** The first split on US firearm shelves is handgun, rifle, shotgun, with ammunition as a peer [S01, S02, S23]; Frankonia opens on Kurzwaffen and Langwaffen, then Büchsen and Flinten [S04]; Euroshot opens Büchsen then action (Repetier, Selbstlade, Kipplauf) [S05]. For air guns the first split is rifle against pistol, then **powerplant** (PCP, spring piston, CO2), which is the mechanism buyers compare most [S06, S27].

**Action or mechanism second.** Sportsman's has an "Action" facet on handguns [S23]; Euroshot and Frankonia split rifles by bolt, lever, self-loading, break-barrel and shotguns by over-under (Bockdoppel) against self-loading [S04, S05]; Pyramyd splits by powerplant [S06]; archery guides split by recurve, compound, longbow [S12, S35].

**Use is a parallel filter, not the tree.** Pyramyd's Product Use facet (competition, hunting, plinking) and Lancaster's Archery Style (Olympic recurve, 3D, bowhunting) sit beside the form tree [S06, S09]; Bass Pro and Frankonia keep Hunting and Shooting as store sections but the weapon pages repeat. The same bolt-action rifle serves hunting, target and military-style uses.

**Legal eligibility comes before the shopping starts** in Germany and the UK (Frankonia's split of Freie Waffen against licensed weapons; the 12 ft-lb limit [S21, S33]) but is not a navigation level in the US. It is a jurisdiction property, so it cannot be a tree level in a global taxonomy.

**For cold steel, the buyer chooses by tradition-named form** (katana, jian, longsword, halberd) and then by authenticity class (battle ready, decorative, blunted) at Kult of Athena [S25, S26] and by certificate grade at NihontoWatch [S10]. Era and culture are the browse path at the Met and at Kult of Athena's Cultures shelf [S18, S25].

**For armour the buyer chooses by protection level first** (soft against hard, then NIJ level), and by body region second [S17, S36]; for historical armour by region protected (helmet, breastplate, arm armour) [S08].

**Maker comes after type** everywhere except for knives and nihonto, where the maker or smith is part of the search (Blade HQ brand shelf, NihontoWatch schools) [S10, S37].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates keep legal class, discipline, era, region, school, user and material off the tree. They differ in the **sub-family axis** (what the first cut is).

### Candidate A (recommended): mechanism of action → form class → action or tradition-named type → model

| Level | Axis (one per level) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | how the object acts on a target or protects against it (propellant, compressed gas, stored muscle energy, blade, shaft or impact, non-lethal agent, protection, projectile). E4.10 (parts) is the C5(d) sub-family and is exempt from this axis | Firearms; Air and gas guns; Bows and crossbows; Swords; Knives and daggers; Polearms, axes and impact weapons; Self-defence devices; Armour and shields; Ammunition and projectiles; Parts, fittings and accessories |
| Leaf | stated per sub-family (C5c), see the list below | Pistols, Revolvers, Rifles, Shotguns; Air rifles; Straight double-edged swords |
| Kind | action, powerplant, blade style or tradition-named type that buyers compare | bolt-action rifle; PCP air rifle; katana; karambit |

**Leaf axis of each sub-family (C5c).**
- E4.1 Firearms: form class of the whole firearm (pistol, revolver, rifle, shotgun, combination gun, blank-firing pistol).
- E4.2 Air and gas guns: form class (rifle, pistol).
- E4.3 Bows and crossbows: bow (hand-drawn), crossbow (stock-mounted) or other hand-held launcher (slingshot, blowgun; K-84).
- E4.4 Swords: **blade profile** (straight double-edged; single-edged; thrusting). Hilt length (one-handed, hand-and-a-half, two-handed) is an attribute (review E4-1).
- E4.5 Knives and daggers: no leaf (about 12 kinds or fewer, C5b; stored with `leaf = null`). Kinds go by blade style; `deployment` (fixed, folding, automatic) is an attribute.
- E4.6 Polearms, axes and impact weapons: form class by handling (polearm; axe; staff; impact; flexible), following the long, short and flexible classes of the Chinese apparatus lists [S19].
- E4.7 Self-defence devices: no leaf (fewer than 12 kinds, C5b).
- E4.8 Armour and shields: part of the body protected, or hand-held (head; torso; limbs and hands; full body; shields). Era is not a leaf (Appendix A).
- E4.9 Ammunition and projectiles: no leaf (12 kinds, C5b). Kinds are cartridge classes, muzzle-loading components and reloading components (K-86).
- E4.10 Parts, fittings and accessories: weapon class served (C5d), with one multi-use leaf (C14b: the one family that holds all the uses).

Why it fits: it follows the form-then-action tree of Frankonia, Euroshot, Sportsman's, Pyramyd and the HS code [S04, S05, S06, S16, S23], keeps blades, impact weapons and armour in their own groups as the Met and Kult of Athena do [S18, S26], and lets every Appendix A modifier become a tag or attribute.

**Where Candidate A breaks.**
1. *Tantō.* A sword by Japanese classification (under one shaku) but a knife by use [S30]. **Resolved by tradition:** it is a kind in E4.4, Single-edged, with katana, tachi and wakizashi, and the sentence "a tantō is a sword kind by tradition" keeps its sub-family from depending on length (review E4-3). The region tag Japan and the nihonto view gather it with the other Japanese blades.
2. *Muzzle-loaders.* Sportsman's and Frankonia give them a shelf of their own [S02, S04]. Under the ignition rule, `ignition` (flintlock, percussion, cartridge) is an attribute, and "muzzle-loading" is a kind only for long guns (kind 8), where it changes the loading form. A "muzzle-loading" tag generates the shelf view. Decided E-21.
3. *Combination gun and drilling.* Rifle barrel plus shotgun barrel: it belongs to neither Rifles nor Shotguns; it needs its own leaf (Frankonia keeps "Kombinierte Waffen" apart [S04]).
4. *Bayonet, spear and bow-knife hybrids.* A bayonet is a knife by form but HS 9307 pairs it with swords and lances [S16]. An arrow, which Lancaster and Kult of Athena file with archery, is ammunition (kind 81); decided E4-O1.
5. *Baton.* Bass Pro files it with self-defence [S01]; by form it is an impact weapon. Decided E4-O3: E4.6 by form. The tonfa overlaps with the baton: both are kinds under Impact weapons.
6. *Blank-firing pistols.* The leaf follows the Frankonia shelf and HS 9303 [S04, S16]; whether "fires only blanks" is a form class or a legal class is not settled by a fetched source †.
7. *Karambit and other knives sold both fixed and folding.* One kind; `deployment` is an attribute (review E4-4), so the object has one home.

### Katana and sabre: kinds against region tags (C11, C2c, X6)

Decided position (E-4, CV-26, CV-27), as C11 states it ("a regional name is a kind only when it names a form that no generic kind in the tree already covers; where a generic kind exists, the regional name is a named form under it, tagged with the region"; two regions decide level only, never inclusion):
- A tradition-named term is a **kind** only when (a) a fetched source sells or compares it as its own category (C2c), (b) its form (construction, length class with its own mounting, component set) differs from every generic kind in the leaf, and (c) sellers in two regions give it that category (C11 level tie-break, CV-27); otherwise it is a named form and is still in. Katana, tachi, wakizashi and tantō pass (a) and (b), and the level is provisional until (c) is attested (R-19): NihontoWatch, Wikipedia and katana24 each treat them as parallel types set by length and carry, and a long and short pair is mounted and sold as a daishō [S10, S11, S30]. Their label is "name (generic description)", for example "katana (Japanese long sword)".
- **Jian, dao, liuyedao, dadao, shamshir, kilij and talwar are named forms** with a region tag, under the generic kind they belong to: the jian under arming sword †, the dao and the others under sabre †. This applies to the Chinese blades the same logic the memo already applied to the sabre (review E4-2). A generated view "Chinese weapons" collects every kind and named form with the tag China, whatever its sub-family.
- Decided E-4: the asymmetry is accepted (Japanese long and short sword types are kinds, Chinese and Middle Eastern types are named forms). "Japanese sword (nihontō)" as one kind with length class and mounting as attributes was not chosen.
- **Region and era** (Koto, Shinto, Shinshinto, Edo) are tags; **schools** (Bizen, Mino, Soshu) are rows of the standing school table, linked from smiths (E-22, CV-3) [S10]. A generated view "nihonto" collects every kind with tag Japan and form of a sword. The NBTHK certificate grade is a certificate issued to one individual blade [S10]; BRIEF puts individual items out and C16 forbids storing it (section 5).
- **Sabre** is a cross-cultural kind (single-edged curved cavalry sword); national patterns are named forms or tags, not kinds. The evidence in this batch for sabre and rapier as separate pages is thin: the fetched sword lists name cutlass, falchion, scimitar and smallsword [S13, S18], so the kind list for single-edged and thrusting swords needs attestation at the stage-3 harvest (†). The fencing sabre is a different object in E2 (C9 homonym row, section 6).
- Lettered typologies (Oakeshott X to XXII; Japanese blade-construction names such as shinogi-zukuri) are **non-maker-bound named forms** under longsword and katana: the typology classifies form, not a distinct kind a person owns [S30, S31].

## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers** (C2a). Evidence per sub-family:

- **Firearms: stop at action type within form** (bolt-action rifle, lever-action rifle, over-under shotgun). Euroshot and Frankonia split Büchsen by action and Flinten by over-under against self-loading [S04, S05]; Sportsman's offers an Action facet on handguns [S23]. Calibre, capacity and barrel length are facets on the named form [S23]. Buyers compare makers and models inside the kind (Euroshot brand counts, Sportsman's brand counts of 100 to 190 per major maker) [S05, S23]. Below this level (model, e.g. a particular bolt-action line) is a named form. Ignition system (flintlock, percussion, cartridge) is an attribute; "muzzle-loading" is a kind only for long guns (kinds 8 and 87) because it changes the loading form (E-21).
- **Air guns: stop at powerplant within rifle or pistol, where the powerplant changes the body (C10).** PCP (a reservoir and filling equipment) and CO2 (a capsule housing) are kinds; spring piston and gas piston share one body and are one kind, "piston air rifle", with `piston_type` an attribute. Pyramyd builds its tree on powerplant and keeps caliber, action and product use as facets [S06, S27]; Air Arms sells spring and PCP rifles as separate ranges [S33]. C10(b) also asks for a fetched comparison source that tests or ranks the types separately; none is fetched yet †.
- **Bows: stop at the family** (recurve, compound, longbow, crossbow). Lancaster and both guides stop there; Olympic, 3D and bowhunting styles are disciplines [S09, S12, S35]. Draw weight and draw length are attributes.
- **Swords: stop at the generic form or the tradition-named type** (longsword, greatsword, sabre, katana, tachi, wakizashi, tantō). NihontoWatch lists type as its first browse path and dealers price by type [S10, S11]; finer distinctions (Oakeshott subtype, blade construction, school) are named forms or tags [S30, S31]. Hilt length (one-handed, hand-and-a-half, two-handed) is an attribute, not a leaf. Katana, wakizashi and tantō are kinds although they differ by blade length, because the length class names a different carried object and a different mounting set (the daishō pair) and is the first browse path of the trade (the C2a comparison test, not size alone) [S10, S11]. The kind level of these four is provisional until attested (R-19, CV-27).
- **Knives: stop at the blade style** (dagger, push dagger, karambit, butterfly knife, automatic knife). Blade HQ uses type then style, with steel and size as facets [S07, S37]. Fixed, folding and automatic (`deployment`) is an attribute; the butterfly knife and the automatic knife stay kinds because the opening mechanism is their form and the reason the key and the law name them [S22].
- **Armour: stop at product class** (soft vest, hard plate, stab vest, helmet, harness); the protection level (NIJ HG1 to RF3, 0115.00) is an attribute with the standard named [S17, S32, S36].
- **Ammunition: stop at cartridge class** (centrefire handgun cartridge, centrefire rifle cartridge, rimfire cartridge, shotshell, muzzle-loading projectile, black powder and substitute propellant, air-gun pellet, arrow, crossbow bolt), with the reloading components of K-86 (loose bullet, primer, smokeless propellant powder) as kinds of their own. Retailers branch by calibre and buyers must match it to the weapon [S24, S03 †, S05 †], and that matching is done through the named form: each standard cartridge or gauge (9×19 mm Parabellum, .308 Winchester, 12 gauge 70 mm) is a non-maker-bound named form and a C13 row (`owner` = CIP or SAAMI †), so calibre is the same field on the weapon and on the ammunition. Bullet weight, bullet type and brand make a maker's load, a C3 variant (title strings such as "380 Auto 95 gr FMJ" [S24]; E-6). A maker's ammunition brand line that spans several cartridge kinds is a C1 lines row (C2d). Calibre is never a kind (C6).
- **Parts: stop at part type**, with the weapon platform as a C13 row and a compatibility attribute, since Brownells and Pyramyd shelve parts by firearm model or rail type [S03, S28].

*Limit of the evidence.* This batch did not fetch review-site "best X" lists (cap on searches), so the comparison argument rests on retailer facets, guides and dealer pricing, not on review groupings. Eighteen of the 37 fetched sources are US-only. Under the one-source rule (BRIEF) a second source or a second region is not needed for inclusion; two regions decide level only for regional-word kinds (CV-27, R-19), and a second source adds weight to a tier scheme. Comparison sources that test or rank the types separately are fetched at the stage-3 harvest (section 10).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Overall: mixed. Spec-ranked for armour and for a few legal-energy thresholds; certificate-ranked for antique swords (not stored); preference elsewhere. No tier sits on a kind (C4a); no tier entry is in the data yet.**

- **Armour: a specification attribute (C4(c) class (i)).** NIJ Standard 0123.00 ballistic levels HG1, HG2, RF1, RF2, RF3 (formerly II, IIIA, III, IV), and stab standard 0115.00 with two knife classes [S17, S32, S36]. Recorded as the attribute `protection_level` with the standard and its edition named, on the **named form** (the product), not on the kind and not as a tier. The old and the new level names are both held, each tied to its standard edition.
- **Antique swords: certificate grade is not stored (C16, BRIEF).** NBTHK certification (Hozon, Tokubetsu Hozon, Juyo, Tokubetsu Juyo; Juyo is described as roughly the top 5 per cent of submissions [S10 †]; price bands follow the rank [S11]) is issued to one individual blade. The grade is therefore not a modifier of any kind or named form. C16 allows the scale to be recorded as a row of the grading-scale registry (`scale_id`, `name`, `owner` NBTHK, `steps`, `kinds_served`), never as values for items. Era stays a tag and schools are school-table rows (E-22).
- **Authenticity class: a modifier that crosses categories.** Kult of Athena's Battle Ready, Sport Combat, Decorative, LARP [S26] decide whether an object is E4, E2, B4 or E1. The Kult of Athena four-class scheme is a grading-scale registry row of `scale_type` "classification", a class a kind or named form belongs to and never a grade of one object (R-2, C16). For objects that remain E4, `function_class` takes two values only: **battle-ready** and **working replica** (K-86). "Antique" is an era tag; decorative, blunted and foam objects leave E4 by the key.
- **Firearms, air guns, bows, knives: preference, with spec attributes.** Price bands and maker reputation set the order; spec facets (calibre, velocity, regulated, draw weight, steel type) are attributes, not tiers [S06, S07, S23, S27]. UK and EU legal energy or category thresholds [S15, S33] are legal class and go to the C17 ledger, not quality. A design standard an object is built to is an attribute `standard_met`.
- **Price band.** One domain-wide USD scheme (C4d), derived from a dated, sourced list price on maker and line rows and on named forms of factory-built objects.
- **Maker ladders.** None is attested in this batch. A maker's own range level enters as a C4 tier entry on the line (`scheme` = the maker's name); a reviewer's grade enters on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10, CV-12). Nothing is entered until a source is fetched at the harvest.
- **Ammunition: spec-ranked by specification only** (calibre, bullet weight), no tier.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity and platform.** Identity: knife makers and nihonto smiths are part of what is sought (Blade HQ's brand shelf with Spyderco, Kershaw, Boker, Cold Steel, Benchmade [S37]; NihontoWatch's school pages [S10]); Sportsman's lists dozens of handgun makers with counts (Smith & Wesson 193, Sig Sauer 178, Taurus 175, Springfield Armory 144, Ruger 109) [S23], and Frankonia keeps an A to Z brand list for weapons, ammunition and equipment [S04]. **Platform:** parts are chosen by firearm model or rail system (Brownells magazines by P365 and P320; Pyramyd rail mounts by rifle; Frankonia Wechselsysteme for pistols) [S03, S04, S28], and bow makers advertise their own cam and limb systems [S09].

**Decided (CV-1, CV-2, CV-5, E-22): a maker ledger on the C1 core schema** (`maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `licensor_id`, `authority_id`, `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`; `known_for_kinds` is derived from the named-form ledger, not stored). Applied to E4:
- `maker_class`: firearm, air-gun, bow and knife makers are "house or manufacture" (or independent, or microbrand); nihonto smiths are "smith or forge" and custom knifemakers "artisan or atelier" (the class is multi-valued); armourers' workshops are "artisan or atelier" and ammunition manufacturers "house or manufacture"; barrel, optic and magazine makers are "supplier"; a person or studio credited with a design that others make is a "designer" row, linked by "designed by" (R-24); retailer own-labels are "retail own-label".
- **Lines and collections** (a maker's line spanning several kinds: Hoyt Rx, Spyderco ranges) are rows in the C1 lines table, with `built_by_maker_id` where the designer and the builder differ. A line or design inside one kind is a maker-bound named form. A licensed replica (for example an air pistol built under a firearm maker's licence) carries `licensor_id` on its named form (C2) †.
- **Tier** is not a ledger column. Any tier is a C4 tier entry on a line or named form, with one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10); none is entered yet.
- **Platform or chambering is not a ledger column.** Platform families (AR-15 pattern, striker-fired pistol magazine patterns, MIL-STD-1913 / Picatinny and M-LOK rails, bow cam and limb systems) and standard cartridges (CIP, SAAMI) go in the **C13 compatibility ledger** (`platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `sources`), linked to named forms and parts. Each row needs a fetched source †.
- **Schools and smiths** (E-22, CV-3). Smiths are C1 rows (class "smith or forge", or "artisan or atelier"). Schools (Bizen, Mino, Yamato, Yamashiro, Soshu [S10]) are not makers and are not tags: they are rows of the standing C1 school table (`school_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `tradition`, `region`, `years`, `sources`), and smiths link to them by `school_id` (multi-valued).
- **Scope** (E-22). Firearm, air-gun, bow and knife makers, nihonto smiths, armourers and ammunition makers, each admitted on one fetched page that shows the maker making or selling; no cap (CV-5).
- **Legal class** is not a ledger column. It goes in the **C17 jurisdiction ledger** (`subject_id` = kind or named form, `jurisdiction`, `scheme`, `class`, `criteria`, `source_url`, `date`). Example rows from fetched pages: EU Directive 91/477/EEC categories A to D [S15]; UK Section 1 at 12 ft-lb for air rifles [S33]; German free weapons and the ban on fist knives, butterfly knives and switchblades [S04, S22]. A US NFA class would need a fetched source †. Decided (CV-35): legal class stays in this ledger, never a level or a tag on the tree.
- Each row attested; brand is never a level.

**Alias and homonym rows (C9).** Aliases: Schreckschusswaffe = blank-firing pistol (de); Büchse = rifle, Flinte = shotgun, Bockdoppelflinte = over-under shotgun, Selbstladeflinte = semi-automatic shotgun, Blockbüchse = single-shot rifle (de, S04); 软兵器 = flexible weapons (zh, S19); 日本刀 nihontō = Japanese sword, uchigatana = katana (ja, S11, S30); TASER = projectile electroshock weapon (relation: trade mark, genericised name; S03). ## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds: canonical form first, then complexity of action** (C7). Within a leaf such as Rifles, order kinds from the oldest and simplest action (single-shot) through repeating (bolt, lever, pump) to self-loading. For ammunition, order the kinds by the weapon class served (handgun, rifle, rimfire, shotshell, muzzle-loading, air gun, arrow, bolt).

**Named forms (C7).** Maker-bound named forms go by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested. Non-maker-bound named forms come first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; where a published typology exists (Oakeshott types X to XXII; Japanese blade-construction names), the typology number is an attribute of the named form and the typology order is a generated view; stored order follows C7 (E4-O4, CV-21). Standard cartridges are non-maker-bound named forms and go by first-attested year; bore diameter is a sort option of generated views. Variants go by `variant_code` or year (C3).

Reasons: the Oakeshott typology, the lettered Japanese periods and the sequence of mechanisms are already chronological or progressive [S10, S31]; retailers' alphabetical or price order does not give a stable position.
