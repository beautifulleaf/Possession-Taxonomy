# H2 Health, Medical & Mobility — structure memo (research draft)

Research, not decision. Written from fetched pages only (34 sources, listed in `verify/out/S2_H2_sources.tsv`; keys S1 to S34 below are the row numbers of that file). Calls used: 16 search calls (48 queries inside them) and 40 fetches, which is the fetch cap. Evidence is strong for monitors, walking and wheeled mobility aids, hearing aids, contact lenses, first-aid kits, drug-sale classes and device-risk classes. It is thinner for stairlifts and home hoists (named by the placement key, no retailer page fetched), for the contents of the medicines tree (the U.S. News and Chemist Warehouse trees were fetched with middle sections elided), for ISO 9999 (the standard's own page would not load, see the blocked-hosts file), and for continence products, braces below the neck and prosthetics (group names only). There is no collector, resale or museum evidence: the Science Museum Group collection page loaded without its category list. Regions fetched: US, UK, Germany, Austria, Japan, China, Australia, Canada, plus WHO and EU standards.

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** H2 Health, Medical & Mobility, in domain H Body.

**Membership test (placement key step 2, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** made to **diagnose, treat, monitor or compensate for an illness, injury or disability of a person, or for pregnancy, fertility or breastfeeding**, or for first aid: medical devices and monitors, medicines and medical supplies, vitamins and dietary supplements, sports-nutrition powders, mobility and assistive aids (including ones installed in a home, such as a stairlift or grab bar), hearing aids, spectacles and contact lenses that correct vision, white canes, contraceptives, pregnancy tests and fertility monitors, breast pumps, incontinence products, medical braces and supports, devices implanted to treat a condition, medical alert bracelets and tags, first-aid kits. Step 2 is the first category step after the status filter on "not ownable" (step 1), so it is tried before groceries, vehicles, clothing, jewellery, personal care and furniture.

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch H2:**
- Step 2 notes: protective gear goes by what it is (step 17); devices that count steps or heart rate for fitness are D2; an ordinary object made in an adapted form for a disabled user (large-print book, talking watch, big-button phone, adapted cutlery, wheelchair-accessible car) is that object and "adapted" is a modifier; veterinary goods are B10; massagers for comfort are H1; restorative tonics and essences sold for health (essence of chicken) are H2; infant formula is J3 except formulas for a medical condition, which are H2; sports-nutrition and meal-replacement products are H2 in every form (powders, bars, gels, ready-to-drink shakes, tablets); CBD products are B9.
- Step 1 notes: medicines, vitamins, supplements and sports nutrition in any form are H2 and not groceries; tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products are B9 (step 27), and "medicinal nicotine, and cannabis prescribed or licensed as a medicine, are H2" (step 27 note).
- Step 3 (groceries): vitamins, supplements and sports nutrition are H2, not J.
- S3: "cleaning products are B6 ... whatever they are used in"; parts, attachments and consumables made for one kind of object follow that object (so a blood-pressure cuff, a glucose test strip and a hearing-aid battery are H2).
- Step 18 (H1): bathroom and body scales, massagers for comfort, hot-water bottles, heat packs and hand warmers, wigs and hairpieces are H1; massagers made to treat a condition, such as TENS units, are H2.
- Step 17 (A3, A4): medical alert bracelets and tags are H2; non-corrective eyewear is A4.
- Step 9 (H3): contraceptives are H2; sexual-stimulation and restraint objects are H3.
- Step 11 (G1): prams and strollers, e-scooters and other powered personal transport are G1; step 2 comes first, so a mobility scooter or powered wheelchair made to compensate for a disability is H2.
- Step 30 (B8): baby monitors are B8 (not an H2 monitor).
- Step 23 (C6): magnifiers and loupes for observation or study are C6; a magnifier made to compensate for low vision is H2 by step 2 (see section 9).

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK, Australia, Germany, Japan, Canada)
- **CVS Home Health Care, US (S1).** One department, 14 categories: hearing aids and accessories, home tests, monitors, braces and supports, wheelchairs and accessories, scooters and accessories, diabetes care, bathroom safety, walkers and rollators, incontinence, daily living aids, fitness and rehab, canes and crutches, foot care. The department holds no medicines or supplements. The tree mixes objects (walkers), conditions (diabetes care), places (bathroom safety) and tasks (daily living).
- **Mobility Shop, UK (S2).** Top level: Electric Wheelchairs, Wheelchairs, Walking, Living, Bedroom, Bathroom, Kitchen, Incontinence, Disability Aids, Accessories, PowerPacks. Wheelchairs are then cut by weight and use (lightweight, travel, crash tested), by user (children's) and by manufacturer. That is three different axes in one level, which the framework would send to attributes, tags and the maker ledger.
- **Complete Care Shop, UK (S11).** Braces and supports are filtered by body region: head and neck, back, arm and elbow, wrist and hand.
- **Chemist Warehouse, Australia (S14, S15).** The pharmacy puts Vitamins and Supplements, Medicines (cut by symptom or user: sore throats, allergy nasal sprays, symptom relief, children's medicine, ears and eyes), Pregnancy and Baby, and Medical Supplies side by side at the top. Medical Supplies holds Medical Devices, Mobility Aids and Face Masks; Mobility Aids holds Walkers, Wheelchairs, Crutches, and Chairs and Stools. Breastfeeding, pregnancy care, postpartum support and pregnancy and fertility tests sit under Pregnancy and Baby, "For Parents". Here the regime (supplement, medicine, device) is the first cut, and life stage cuts the reproductive goods away from the rest.
- **Sanitäts-Online, Germany (S16).** Five areas: Mobilität (Gehstöcke, Rollatoren, Rollstühle, Elektromobile), Alltagshilfen und Möbel (bath aids, commode chairs, care beds with mattresses), Gesundheit (supports, mattresses and pillows for pressure-sore prevention), Pflege (consumables for home care), Technik (blood-pressure monitors, emergency-call systems, inhalation and oxygen devices). The cut is the user's situation.
- **Omron Healthcare Store, Japan (S12).** One maker's tree, by device: weight and body-composition scales, ECG monitors, electric toothbrushes, pulse oximeters, massagers, activity trackers, hearing aids, nebulisers, thermometers (predictive and actual-measurement electronic, women's), treatment devices. - **Contact-lens shops, US and UK (S19, S20).** VisionPros offers one "type" list that mixes axes: daily, weekly, monthly, toric, multifocal and bifocal, coloured; it also cuts by brand and by manufacturer (Alcon, Bausch and Lomb, CooperVision, Johnson and Johnson). ### 1.5 Standards bodies, regulators and trade classifications
- **FDA (S3).** About 1,700 generic device types in 16 specialty panels; three risk classes (I, II, III) assigned by intended use and risk.
- **EU MDR (S31).** Annex VIII, four classes I, IIa, IIb, III by intended purpose, duration of use and invasiveness; Class I has sub-marks Is (sterile), Im (measuring), Ir (reusable surgical).
- **China NMPA (S13).** 22 categories since the 2018 catalogue (for example diagnostic and monitoring, physiotherapy, nursing and protective, bed and transportation, ophthalmic, rehabilitation, traditional Chinese medical, software), three risk classes.
- **WHO ATC (S4).** Drugs, not products: 14 anatomical-pharmacological main groups, five levels down to the chemical substance, one code per main therapeutic use; "other" groups at the third level; combinations by main use.
- **Medicine sale classes.** UK: prescription-only (POM), pharmacy (P), general sales list (GSL) (S18). Japan: prescription, behind-counter "guidance-required", and OTC classes 1, 2 (with designated 2) and 3 by ingredient risk; the Ministry example for class 3 includes vitamin C and petroleum jelly, and kampo is class 2 (S17).
- **German statutory insurance, Hilfsmittelverzeichnis (S5).** 38 product groups arranged "in line with the therapy goal" plus four care groups. The groups are named by object or function: 01 suction devices, 02 adaptation aids, 04 bath and shower aids, 05 bandages, 07 aids for the blind, 08 insoles, 09 electro-stimulation, 10 walking aids, 11 anti-decubitus, 13 hearing aids, 14 inhalation and respiratory therapy, 15 incontinence, 16 communication aids, 17 compression therapy, 18 patient and disabled vehicles, 19 nursing articles, 20 positioning aids, 21 measuring devices for body states, 22 mobility aids, 23 orthoses, 24 leg prostheses, 25 vision aids, 26 seating aids, 27 speech aids, 28 standing aids, 29 stoma articles, 30 glucose management, 31 shoes, 32 therapeutic exercise devices, 33 toilet aids, 34 hair replacement, 35 epitheses, 36 eye prostheses, 37 breast prostheses, 38 arm prostheses. Walking aids (group 10) are divided into hand sticks, forearm crutches, axilla crutches and wheeled walkers (delta walkers; four-wheeled rollators; rollators with higher load rating; rollators with forearm supports), then walkers with wheelchair function.
- **Japanese long-term-care insurance (S6).** 13 rental items (wheelchair, wheelchair accessories, care bed, bed accessories, pressure-relief mattress, position changer, hand rails, slopes, walkers, walking sticks, wandering detector, lift, automatic excretion processor) and 9 purchase-only items (toilet seats, parts of the excretion device, bath aids, simple bath, lift slings, a bladder-timing prediction device, and the purchase option of slope, walker and stick). Rollator-type "walking vehicles" are inside the rental item "walker" but outside the purchase item "walker".
- **First-aid kit standards (S9, S28, S29).** ANSI/ISEA Z308.1: Class A and Class B by contents, Type I to IV by container (mounted indoor to portable and rugged). BS 8599-1: small, medium and large kits by number of people and risk. Germany: DIN 13157 (small workplaces), DIN 13169 (double consumables, larger workplaces), DIN 13164 (car), DIN 14142 (fire service). Austria: ÖNORM Z 1020 Type 1 and Type 2.
- **UK mobility scooter classes (S27).** Class 2 (up to 4 mph, pavements) and class 3 (8 mph, roads, DVLA registration); vehicles over 8 mph, wider than 0.85 m or heavier than 150 kg are "not in a class".

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects
**Agree.** Every source gives walking aids, wheelchairs, hearing aids, incontinence products, blood-pressure monitors and braces a group of their own. The German and Japanese insurance lists, the UK and German shops and CVS all separate walking aids from wheeled seating. Every regulator uses risk class as a separate dimension from the product type.

**Split.**
1. **Regime against function.** Chemist Warehouse and the FDA/NMPA split by regime; the German list, CVS, Omron and the Sanitätshaus split by function or situation. Hearing aids show why regime cannot be the structure: the same hearing aid is a prescription product or an OTC product by region and date (S10).
2. **Same product, different legal class.** Vitamin C is a class-3 drug in Japan (S17) and a supplement in the US and UK (S7, S30). The ATC system leaves herbal and traditional products mostly out (stated on S4).
3. **Where daily-living aids sit.** Bathroom safety is its own CVS category (S1) but a branch of "living" in the Mobility Shop (S2); the Japanese list splits the same grab rail and bath seat between rental and purchase (S6).
4. **Where braces and supports sit.** One CVS category (S1), body regions at Complete Care Shop (S11), three groups in the German list (bandages, insoles, orthoses) plus compression therapy and prostheses (S5).
5. **Where reproductive goods sit.** Family planning (Walmart, S33), Pregnancy and Baby (Chemist Warehouse, S14), Baby and Maternity (Best Buy, S32), home tests (CVS, S1). No source treats them as part of a health-device tree, but the framework key does.
6. **Sports nutrition.** A separate department at Holland and Barrett that still cross-lists vitamins (S25); the framework key puts it with supplements.
7. **Mixed axes inside one level.** The Mobility Shop wheelchair list (S2) and the VisionPros lens "type" list (S19) mix weight, user, use, optics and replacement schedule. Alensa (S20) shows the same facts as separate facets.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose by the **function or problem first**, then by **form**, then by **maker and price**, and only last by regime, though regime can decide where they may buy.

- **Function or situation first.** The German list is built "in line with the therapy goal" (S5); the Sanitätshaus is built on the user's situation (mobility, daily life, health, care, technology) (S16); CVS and the Mobility Shop name the problem (S1, S2). WHO's priority list and ISO 9999 are also function-based, but their pages did not load, so this is not used as evidence.
- **Form second.** Inside a function buyers choose a type: arm against wrist (S12, S22), BTE against RIC against canal (S10), rollator against walking frame against cane (S21, S5), daily against monthly lens (S19, S20), manual against electric against wearable pump (S32).
- **Regime third, but it can come first at the till.** HearingTracker opens with prescription against OTC (S10); glucose monitors are sorted by prescription need (S23); Chemist Warehouse opens with medicines against supplements against devices (S14). The reason is purchase channel, not what the object is.
- **Medicines and supplements are chosen by need.** Medicines by symptom or condition (S14, S8); supplements by need, format or ingredient (S26); the NIH fact sheets are by ingredient with a second list "for specific purposes" (S7).
- **Filter order on major stores.** Alensa's lens facets run replacement schedule, correction type, brand, pack size, characteristics, material, manufacturer (S20). CHOICE's run type, then feature display, then "validated" (S22). In both, the physical type comes before maker and price, and the maker is last.
- **Buyer questions in guides.** "Which style fits my hearing loss" (S10); "where will I use it and how strong and stable am I" for walking aids (S21); "arm or wrist, and is it validated" (S22); "can I buy it without a prescription" (S10, S23).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's fixed level names (category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; at most four levels below the category, C5). They differ in the axis of the first level.

### 3.1 Candidate A (recommended): by the health function the object serves

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = what the owner uses the object to do for health (measure or test; deliver therapy; move or be moved; manage daily living and care; see, hear or communicate; treat with a medicine; supplement nutrition; fuel training or meals; conceive, prevent, carry or feed; dress a wound or give first aid; support or replace a body part; plus parts). Leaf = a per-sub-family axis stated in 3.4 (C5c). Kind = a generic type at the level where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). Named form = a model, line or generic variant below the kind, with `maker_bound` yes or no (C2).
- **Why it fits the sources.** The German list is built on therapy goal (S5), the Sanitätshaus on the user's situation (S16), CVS, the Mobility Shop and Omron on object and problem (S1, S2, S12). It follows the framework key, whose verbs are diagnose, treat, monitor, compensate (step 2).
- **Example sub-families and leaves, 12 kinds.** Monitoring and testing > Blood pressure > upper-arm blood-pressure monitor, wrist blood-pressure monitor, wearable blood-pressure monitor; Blood glucose > blood glucose meter, continuous glucose monitor. Mobility and transfer > Walking aids > walking stick, forearm crutch, rollator; Wheeled seating > manual wheelchair, mobility scooter. Sensory and communication > Hearing > BTE hearing aid, RIC hearing aid.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) A basal-body thermometer is a thermometer in form (Omron files it with thermometers, S12) but serves fertility tracking: one home needs a tie-break (section 9, precedence P1). (2) A TENS unit treats pain, as do pain patches and gels in the U.S. News pain-relief group (S8): device and medicine sit in different sub-families, which a "shop by condition" view must stitch. (3) Daily-living aids have no clean leaf axis: the Mobility Shop and CVS cut them by room, task and object at once (S1, S2). (4) A first-aid kit is an assembly of medicines, dressings and devices (S9); it is one object and is filed once.

### 3.2 Candidate B: by regime (medicine, supplement, device, assistive product, medical supply)

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = regulatory regime; leaf = use or body function; kind; named form.
- **Support.** Chemist Warehouse has Medicines, Vitamins and Supplements and Medical Supplies as sibling top-level groups (S14). FDA, EU and NMPA classify devices and not medicines (S3, S31, S13); ATC classifies medicines and not devices (S4).
- **Example sub-families and leaves, 12 kinds.** Medicines > Cough, cold and flu > cough suppressant, expectorant, oral decongestant; Supplements > Vitamins > multivitamin, vitamin D supplement; Devices > Monitors > upper-arm blood-pressure monitor, wrist blood-pressure monitor; Assistive products > Walking aids > walking stick, rollator; Medical supplies > Dressings > adhesive bandage.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) Hearing aids are prescription or OTC depending on the year and country (S10), so the same kind would move between sub-families. (2) Vitamin C is a Japanese class-3 drug and a supplement elsewhere (S17, S30). (3) A medical alert bracelet and a wheelchair cushion have no regime at all (S34, S2). (4) CBD, medicinal nicotine and herbal remedies change regime by country, and Appendix A forbids region from splitting an inner level.

### 3.3 Candidate C: by body system or condition

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = organ system or specialty (the ATC first level, the 16 FDA panels or the 22 NMPA categories); leaf = object class; kind; named form.
- **Support.** ATC main groups are anatomical (S4); FDA panels are medical specialties (S3); NMPA has ophthalmic, rehabilitation, nursing and protective, bed and transportation categories (S13); CVS has "diabetes care" and U.S. News groups by condition (S1, S8).
- **Example sub-families and leaves, 10 kinds.** Cardiovascular > blood-pressure monitors, compression stockings; Diabetes > glucose meters, continuous monitors; Musculoskeletal > walking sticks, wrist supports; Respiratory > nebulisers; Ear > hearing aids.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) A wheelchair or a rollator serves no single system; NMPA puts them in "bed and transportation" and "rehabilitation" (S13), which is a function and not a system. (2) A multivitamin or vitamin D supplement serves bones, immunity and energy at once; the NIH lists supplements by ingredient and by purpose in two separate lists (S7). (3) A BP monitor and an aspirin tablet would share a leaf but have nothing in common as objects. (4) U.S. News mixes condition groups with a user group ("Children's Health Products") (S8), so the condition list is not one axis.
- **Use.** Keep it as a generated view ("by body system", tagged with ATC level 1 or FDA panel), not as the tree.

## 4. Depth and size

**Rough count of generic kinds: about 450 to 550** (my estimate from group counts, not a line-by-line count). Basis: the German list has 38 therapy-goal groups (S5); the Japanese insurance list has 22 items (S6); U.S. News ranks 128 OTC and personal-care categories, of which I guess about half are medicines, supplements or devices (S8; not counted); Holland and Barrett lists 8 vitamin groups plus minerals, botanicals and sports lines (S25, S30); FDA has about 1,700 generic device types but most are professional (S3). By sub-family: monitoring and testing 20; therapy devices 20; mobility and transfer 30; daily-living and care 45; sensory and communication 25; medicines 90 (OTC) and up to 200 more if prescription classes are admitted (open question Q1); supplements 120; sports and meal-replacement nutrition 15; reproductive and maternal 15; first aid and wound care 15; supports, braces and prostheses 30; parts and accessories 40.

**Rough count of named forms: 400 non-maker-bound, 2,000 or more maker-bound if every model were taken.** Non-maker-bound forms are chiefly active ingredients (about 300 for OTC medicines), hearing-aid sub-styles, rollator load classes and standard-defined kit variants. Maker-bound forms are models (Omron HCR-7106, a Phonak or Dexcom line) and brand products. The owner's emphasis on brand depth suggests taking models only where a maker ledger and a review source exist (hearing aids, glucose monitors, blood-pressure monitors, rollators).

**Where depth concentrates.** Supplements (hundreds of single-ingredient kinds, many from the NIH lists of vitamins, minerals, botanicals and probiotics, S7); medicines (ingredient against brand against dose form); assistive and daily-living aids (a long tail of one-task objects); hearing aids (style against model against technology level, S10).

**Broad, medium and narrow leaves.**
- Broad: Vitamins and minerals; Botanicals and other supplements; Pain and inflammation; Cough, cold and flu; Allergy and sinus.
- Medium: Walking aids; Wheeled seating; Hearing; Vision; Blood pressure; Blood glucose; Bathing; Protein; Performance.
- Narrow: Body temperature; Oxygen saturation; Breastfeeding; Home tests; First-aid kits; Alerting and identification.

**Size, user and strength are attributes, never levels (C6).** Evidence that retailers mix them in: "children's wheelchairs" and "bariatric wheelchairs" (S2), brace sizes (S11), contact-lens correction values (S20), vitamin strength in IU or mg (S30), pump single/double (S32). Under C6 these are an attribute with a named standard, or a user tag with a generated "children" or "bariatric" view.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: spec-ranked and regulatory attributes, with sparse consensus entries on makers; no tier on any kind (C4a).** Mixed, in four layers.

1. **Measured or standard-defined specifications are attributes (C4b).** Device risk class: FDA I, II, III (S3); EU I, IIa, IIb, III with Is, Im, Ir (S31); China I, II, III (S13). Drug sale class: UK POM, P, GSL (S18); Japanese prescription, guidance-required, 1, 2 (designated 2), 3 (S17). First-aid kit class and container type: ANSI/ISEA Z308.1 Class A, Class B and Type I to IV (S9); BS 8599-1 small, medium, large (S29); DIN 13157/13169/13164/14142 (S28). UK mobility-scooter class 2 or 3 (S27). Blood-pressure monitor "validated" status (S22). Third-party seals for supplements: USP, NSF, Informed Choice (S8). These sit as attributes with the standard named; none is a tier.
2. **A jurisdiction-dependent class needs its own record.** The same object has different classes by region and by claim: the FDA and TGA both say identical devices can have different classes by intended use (S3, and the TGA page in the search results, not fetched). So the data needs a `regulatory_status` table: `subject_id`, `jurisdiction`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`. It looks like a tier entry (C4a) but records a legal fact, not a quality rank. Open question Q5.
3. **Consensus-ranked entries on makers or lines (C4a).** U.S. News publishes brand rankings by a pharmacist survey (S8); HearingTracker reports lab-based scores and prices (S10); CHOICE's ratings are members-only (S22). Each is a dated platform source (C4c) to attach to a line, never to the kind. Maker-defined technology levels for hearing aids (essential, standard, advanced, premium) appear in a UCSF guide found by search and not fetched; if used, each is a per-maker tier entry.
4. **Price bands (C4d).** Hearing aids span the bands: OTC averages $502 per pair and ranges $20 to $2,000; prescription averages $3,432 and ranges $1,000 to $8,500 (S10). Blood-pressure monitors from the Omron store are about ¥9,300 to ¥24,000 (about $60 to $160 at ¥150 to the dollar) (S12). No price page was fetched for vitamins and OTC medicines; they are expected to sit mostly in the lowest band.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Choice: incidental for most kinds, with platform pockets and identity pockets. A light maker ledger is recommended.**
- **Identity pocket: hearing aids.** HearingTracker's guide centres on a "brands" infographic of the hearing-aid industry and compares makers' models (S10). Buyers know the maker; the same maker may sell the prescription and OTC line.
- **Platform pockets.** Parts are tied to the device: Omron lists parts for most device kinds (S12), including hearing-aid batteries, ear tips and battery holders; blood-pressure monitor parts; a "Connect" app family spanning scales, thermometers, activity trackers and ECG (S12). Compatibility is a real record: `platform_id`, owner, kinds served (C13). I did not fetch a page on glucose test-strip or CGM sensor compatibility, wheelchair-fitting standards or hearing-aid battery size codes, so those pockets are named but unverified.
- **Brand against generic in medicines.** U.S. News ranks brands in each category (S8) and the supplement guidance asks for third-party seals (S8). Buyers compare brand against store brand within one kind, which is exactly the maker-bound named form (C2).
- **Maker ledger.** Core C1 columns suffice. Extensions to consider: `regime_by_jurisdiction` is not a maker column (it sits on the regulatory table); `platforms_owned` for the compatibility ledger; `maker_class` values include medical-device manufacturer, pharmaceutical company, retail own-label (store brand), supplement brand. Known-for kinds should be derived from the named-form ledger (C1). **Variant ledger (C3):** not needed except where the owner wants depth for hearing-aid or glucose-monitor generations and colourways; fill it sparsely.
- **Scene tags.** None for H2 from the domain A vocabulary (C8); see section 7.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Canonical form first, then the secondary key each leaf states (C7). Reason: the sources already list in these orders.
- **Walking aids: by support given, least to most.** Stick, forearm crutch, axilla crutch, walking frame, rollator, then walkers with wheelchair function. This is the order of the German list (S5), the only fetched source that lists them in a graded order; the retail trees (S1, S15, S21) do not use a single order.
- **Blood-pressure monitors: upper-arm, then wrist, then wearable.** CHOICE lists arm before wrist (S22); Omron lists upper-arm cuff, fully automatic, wrist, wearable (S12).
- **Hearing aids: by position, BTE, RIC, ITE, ITC, CIC, IIC**, which is the order of two independent guides (S10 and a second guide in search results, not fetched).
- **Contact lenses: by replacement interval, daily, weekly, monthly** (S19, S20).
- **Medicines and supplements: canonical use first, then alphabetical by kind** (no ranking order exists; U.S. News ranks brands, not kinds, S8).
- **Named forms: by maker, then first-introduction year, then alphabetical (C7).** Active-ingredient forms (non-maker-bound) go alphabetical. Standard-defined variants go in the standard's own order (ANSI Class A before B; BS 8599-1 small, medium, large; DIN 13157 before 13169, S9, S28, S29).

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (P1 to P12). Each states which category wins and why, using the framework's step numbers.

**With H1 Personal Care & Beauty (step 18 comes after step 2, so H2 wins when both fit)**
- P1. A thermometer made to measure body temperature is H2; a basal-body or women's thermometer made to track fertility is H2 under reproductive health (step 2 names fertility monitors); a kitchen or room thermometer is B5 or B7.
- P2. A bathroom or body-composition scale is H1 (step 18 names it); a device that measures a clinical quantity (blood pressure, glucose, oxygen saturation, ECG) is H2. The Omron store sells both (S12), and they go to two categories.
- P3. A massager for comfort is H1; a TENS unit or other device made to treat a condition is H2 (step 18 note, S8, S12). A wig for hair loss is H1 (step 18 names wigs), not H2.
- P4. A contact lens that corrects vision is H2; a coloured or costume lens with no prescription is H1 as a cosmetic attached to the body (open question Q8). Contact-lens care solution follows the lens under S3, unless the owner prefers B6 for cleaning products (Q7).

**With H3 Adult Objects and B9 Smoking & Vaping**
- P5. A condom or other contraceptive is H2 (step 2); an object made for sexual stimulation or restraint is H3 (step 9). Personal lubricant is not named by either step and needs an owner call (Q6).
- P6. A nicotine patch, gum or lozenge sold as a medicine for stopping smoking is H2; tobacco, vapes and nicotine pouches are B9; CBD products are B9 even when sold as supplements (step 27 notes).

**With A (worn and carried), B (home), E (sport)**
- P7. A medical alert bracelet or tag is H2 (step 2 and the step 17 note); an ordinary bracelet is A3. ICE ID sells both as "ID jewellery" (S34).
- P8. Protective gear goes by what it is: a respirator, hi-vis vest or safety glasses is A1; a medical brace or support made to treat or support an injury is H2; a guard made for a sport (knee pad, shin guard) is E2 or A1 (step 2 note, step 17 note).
- P9. Spectacles that correct vision, including ready-made reading glasses, are H2; sunglasses and other non-corrective eyewear are A4 (step 17).
- P10. Adapted cutlery and other ordinary objects made in an adapted form are that object (step 2 note); an assistive object with no ordinary counterpart (a reacher, a sock aid, a shower chair, a commode chair, a care bed) is H2. ## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (candidate A).** Category H2 > 12 sub-families by health function (monitoring and testing; therapy devices; mobility and transfer; daily-living and care; sensory and communication; medicines; supplements; sports and meal-replacement nutrition; reproductive and maternal; first aid and wound care; supports, braces and prostheses; parts and accessories) > leaf by a per-sub-family axis (section 3.4) > kind at the level where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b) > named form (maker-bound or not). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Small sub-families may skip the leaf (C5b).

**Modifiers kept off the tree.** Regulatory device class (FDA, EU, NMPA), drug sale class (POM/P/GSL, Japanese classes, US prescription/OTC), supply class (OTC against prescription hearing aids and glucose monitors), UK scooter class, first-aid kit class and container type and BS or DIN size, user (child, bariatric, women's), size, strength, format, body system or condition, reimbursement-list membership, tradition (kampo, TCM). Each is an attribute, a tag or a generated view, and the regulatory ones go in a `regulatory_status` table.

**Owner decisions needed.**
- **Q1.** Are prescription-only medicines and controlled drugs in scope? The key says "medicines", and ATC classes thousands of substances (S4). Option: only consumer-sold OTC classes plus a short list of prescription classes people keep at home (insulin, inhalers, antibiotics, contraceptive pills).
- **Q2.** Medicines depth: stop at the OTC use class (recommended) or at pharmacological class? Is the active ingredient a named form (recommended) or a kind?
- **Q3.** Medical-condition infant formulas (step 2 note): own kind "medical nutrition" in H2.7 or H2.8?
- **Q4.** Care beds, pressure-relief mattresses, adjustable chairs and lift recliners: H2 (recommended for beds and mattresses made for care) or B1?
- **Q5.** Add a `regulatory_status` table (jurisdiction, scheme, value, source) so the same object can carry its FDA class, EU class and sale class? Recommended yes; it is not a tier.
- **Q6.** Personal lubricants and intimate-care products: H1, H2 or H3? Neither step 2 nor step 9 names them.
- **Q7.** Contact-lens solutions and other device-care liquids: H2 under S3 (recommended) or B6 under "cleaning products are B6"?
- **Q8.** Coloured non-corrective contact lenses: H1 as a cosmetic (recommended) or H2?
- **Q9.** Therapeutic and diabetic footwear: A2 (recommended, step 17 "footwear of any kind") or H2?
- **Q10.** Personal alarms, fall detectors and wandering detectors (Japan rents a wandering detector as a care item, S6): H2 or B8?
- **Q11.** Supplement ingredient kinds are very many; set a cap or admit kinds on the NIH fact-sheet list (S7) plus those that sell in volume.
- **Q12.** Should the data model keep hearing-aid technology levels as tier entries, and is a second source (not fetched in this draft) required first (C4e)?
- **Q13.** Medical face masks and surgical masks (Chemist Warehouse files them under Medical Supplies, S14): A1 as PPE (recommended by the step 17 note) or H2?

**Gaps in this draft.** No museum, collector or resale source (section 1.4). ISO 9999, WHO priority assistive products and the TGA classification page were found but not fetched. Stairlifts, hoists, ostomy and incontinence kinds, braces below the elbow and the knee, prosthetics and speech aids are attested only by group names. The medicines and supplements trees were fetched with middle sections elided. Compatibility records (glucose strips, CGM sensors, hearing-aid battery sizes, wheelchair standards) are named but unverified. No source from India, Latin America or Africa.
