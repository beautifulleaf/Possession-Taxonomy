## 0. Category

**Code and name:** H2 Health, Medical & Mobility, in domain H Body.

**Membership test (placement key step 2, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md):** made to **diagnose, treat, monitor or compensate for an illness, injury or disability of a person, or for pregnancy, fertility or breastfeeding**, or for first aid: medical devices and monitors, medicines and medical supplies, vitamins and dietary supplements, sports-nutrition powders, mobility and assistive aids (including ones installed in a home, such as a stairlift or grab bar), hearing aids, spectacles that correct vision, contact lenses of every kind (K-35), white canes, contraceptives, pregnancy tests and fertility monitors, breast pumps, incontinence products, medical braces and supports, devices implanted to treat a condition, medical alert bracelets and tags, first-aid kits. Step 2 is the first category step after the status filter on "not ownable" (step 1), so it is tried before groceries, vehicles, clothing, jewellery, personal care and furniture.

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch H2:**

- Step 1 notes: medicines, vitamins, supplements and sports nutrition in any form are H2 and not groceries; tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products are B9 (step 27), and the step 27 note now reads: "medicinal nicotine and cannabis medicines are H2 only under step 2's H1/H2 sentence ... prescription or licence status alone goes to C17 and does not move the object" (K-31).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects
**Agree.** Every source gives walking aids, wheelchairs, hearing aids, incontinence products, blood-pressure monitors and braces a group of their own. The German and Japanese insurance lists, the UK and German shops and CVS all separate walking aids from wheeled seating. Every regulator uses risk class as a separate dimension from the product type.

**Split.**
1. **Regime against function.** Chemist Warehouse and the FDA/NMPA split by regime; the German list, CVS, Omron and the Sanitätshaus split by function or situation. Hearing aids show why regime cannot be the structure: the same hearing aid is a prescription product or an OTC product by region and date (S10).
2. **Same product, different legal class.** Vitamin C is a class-3 drug in Japan (S17) and a supplement in the US and UK (S7, S30). The ATC system leaves herbal and traditional products mostly out (stated on S4).
3. **Where daily-living aids sit.** Bathroom safety is its own CVS category (S1) but a branch of "living" in the Mobility Shop (S2); the Japanese list splits the same grab rail and bath seat between rental and purchase (S6).
4. **Where braces and supports sit.** One CVS category (S1), body regions at Complete Care Shop (S11), three groups in the German list (bandages, insoles, orthoses) plus compression therapy and prostheses (S5).
5. **Where reproductive goods sit.** Family planning (Walmart, S33), Pregnancy and Baby (Chemist Warehouse, S14), Baby and Maternity (Best Buy, S32), home tests (CVS, S1). No source treats them as part of a health-device tree, but the framework key does.
6. **Sports nutrition.** A separate department at Holland and Barrett that still cross-lists vitamins (S25); the framework key puts it with supplements.
7. **Mixed axes inside one level.** The Mobility Shop wheelchair list (S2) and the VisionPros lens "type" list (S19) mix weight, user, use, optics and replacement schedule. Alensa (S20) shows the same facts as separate facets.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose by the **function or problem first**, then by **form**, then by **maker and price**, and only last by regime, though regime can decide where they may buy.

- **Function or situation first.** The German list is built "in line with the therapy goal" (S5); the Sanitätshaus is built on the user's situation (mobility, daily life, health, care, technology) (S16); CVS and the Mobility Shop name the problem (S1, S2). WHO's priority list and ISO 9999 are also function-based, but their pages did not load, so this is not used as evidence.
- **Substances apart.** Pharmacies and health stores sell medicines, supplements and sports nutrition in separate departments from devices and aids (S14, S25, S26). This is why level 1 uses two ordered tests (section 3.1).
- **Form second.** Inside a function buyers choose a type: arm against wrist (S12, S22), BTE against RIC against canal (S10), rollator against walking frame against cane (S21, S5), manual against electric pump (S32). A retail facet such as the contact-lens replacement schedule (S19, S20) is an attribute, not a type (section 4b).
- **Regime third, but it can come first at the till.** HearingTracker opens with prescription against OTC (S10); glucose monitors are sorted by prescription need (S23); Chemist Warehouse opens with medicines against supplements against devices (S14). The reason is purchase channel, not what the object is.
- **Medicines and supplements are chosen by need.** Medicines by symptom or condition (S14, S8); supplements by need, format or ingredient (S26); the NIH fact sheets are by ingredient with a second list "for specific purposes" (S7).
- **Filter order on major stores.** Alensa's lens facets run replacement schedule, correction type, brand, pack size, characteristics, material, manufacturer (S20). CHOICE's run type, then feature display, then "validated" (S22). In both, the physical type comes before maker and price, and the maker is last.
- **Buyer questions in guides.** "Which style fits my hearing loss" (S10); "where will I use it and how strong and stable am I" for walking aids (S21); "arm or wrist, and is it validated" (S22); "can I buy it without a prescription" (S10, S23).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's fixed level names (category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; at most four levels below the category, C5). They differ in the axis of the first level.

### 3.1 Candidate A (decided, H-9): substances by what they are, then devices and aids by health function

- **Levels and axes.** Level 1 (sub-family) is set by **two ordered tests** (C5c): **(1)** a medicine, a supplement, or a sports, meal or medical nutrition product is filed by what it is (H2.6 Medicines, H2.7 Supplements, H2.8 Sports, meal and medical nutrition), because pharmacies and health stores sell these in separate departments (S14, S25, S26); **(2)** every other device, aid or supply is filed by the health function it serves (measure or test; deliver therapy; move or be moved; manage daily living and care; see, hear or communicate; prevent pregnancy or infection, or feed; give first aid; support or replace a body part), plus the C5(d) parts sub-family. Decided by H-9: level 1 is these two ordered tests, and every test and monitor, pregnancy and fertility included, sits in H2.1. Leaf = a per-sub-family axis stated in 3.4 (C5c). Kind = a generic type at the level where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). Named form = a model, line or generic design below the kind, with `maker_bound` yes or no (C2); specifications, sub-styles, measuring modes and active ingredients are attributes, not named forms (C2b; in H2.6 only brand products are named forms, H-8).
- **Why it fits the sources.** The German list is built on therapy goal (S5), the Sanitätshaus on the user's situation (S16), CVS, the Mobility Shop and Omron on object and problem (S1, S2, S12). It follows the framework key, whose verbs are diagnose, treat, monitor, compensate (step 2). Test (1) follows the pharmacy and health-store department split (S14, S25, S26).
- **Example sub-families and leaves, 12 kinds.** Monitoring and testing > Blood pressure > upper-arm blood-pressure monitor, wrist blood-pressure monitor, wearable blood-pressure monitor; Blood glucose > blood glucose meter, continuous glucose monitor. Mobility and transfer > Walking aids > walking stick, forearm crutch, rollator; Wheeled seating > manual wheelchair, mobility scooter. Sensory and communication > Hearing > BTE hearing aid, RIC hearing aid.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) A basal-body thermometer is a thermometer in form (Omron files it with thermometers, S12) but serves fertility tracking; it is resolved by putting every test and monitor in H2.1, with a leaf "Fertility and pregnancy" (P1). (2) A TENS unit treats pain, as do pain patches and gels in the U.S. News pain-relief group (S8): device and medicine sit in different sub-families under the two ordered tests, and a "shop by condition" view stitches them. (3) Daily-living aids have no clean leaf axis: the Mobility Shop and CVS cut them by room, task and object at once (S1, S2); H2.4 uses the daily task or care need. (4) A first-aid kit is an assembly of medicines, dressings and devices (S9); it is one object, compared and standardised as one, and is filed once as a kind under the key's sets rule S3a (K-1: "A kit sold, standardised and compared as one object (first-aid kit, ...) is a kind").

### 3.2 Candidate B: by regime (medicine, supplement, device, assistive product, medical supply)

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = regulatory regime; leaf = use or body function; kind; named form.
- **Support.** Chemist Warehouse has Medicines, Vitamins and Supplements and Medical Supplies as sibling top-level groups (S14). FDA, EU and NMPA classify devices and not medicines (S3, S31, S13); ATC classifies medicines and not devices (S4).
- **Example sub-families and leaves, 12 kinds.** Medicines > Cough, cold and flu > cough suppressant, expectorant, oral decongestant; Supplements > Vitamins > multivitamin, vitamin D supplement; Supplements > Minerals and other > magnesium supplement, probiotic; Devices > Monitors > upper-arm blood-pressure monitor, wrist blood-pressure monitor; Assistive products > Walking aids > walking stick, rollator; Medical supplies > Dressings > adhesive bandage.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) Hearing aids have split into prescription and OTC classes inside one market since 2022 (S10), so the same kind would sit in two sub-families and its class would change over time. (2) Vitamin C is a Japanese class-3 drug and a supplement elsewhere (S17, S30). (3) A medical alert bracelet and a wheelchair cushion have no regime at all (S34, S2). (4) CBD, medicinal nicotine and herbal remedies change regime by country, and Appendix A forbids region from splitting an inner level.
- **What candidate A keeps from it.** Only the substance departments (test 1), which are defined by what the product is (medicine, supplement, nutrition), not by its legal class in one country; legal class goes to the C17 ledger (section 5).

### 3.3 Candidate C: by body system or condition

- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = organ system or specialty (the ATC first level, the 16 FDA panels or the 22 NMPA categories); leaf = object class; kind; named form.
- **Support.** ATC main groups are anatomical (S4); FDA panels are medical specialties (S3); NMPA has ophthalmic, rehabilitation, nursing and protective, bed and transportation categories (S13); CVS has "diabetes care" and U.S. News groups by condition (S1, S8).
- **Example sub-families and leaves, 12 kinds.** Cardiovascular > upper-arm blood-pressure monitor, wrist blood-pressure monitor, compression stocking; Diabetes > blood glucose meter, continuous glucose monitor; Musculoskeletal > walking stick, forearm crutch, orthotic insole; Respiratory > nebuliser, pulse oximeter; Ear > BTE hearing aid; Eye > soft contact lens.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) A wheelchair or a rollator serves no single system; NMPA's own list includes "bed and transportation" and "rehabilitation" categories (S13), which are functions and not systems. (2) A multivitamin or vitamin D supplement serves bones, immunity and energy at once; the NIH lists supplements by ingredient and by purpose in two separate lists (S7). (3) A BP monitor and an aspirin tablet would share a leaf but have nothing in common as objects. (4) U.S. News mixes condition groups with a user group ("Children's Health Products") (S8), so the condition list is not one axis.
- **Use.** Keep it as a generated view ("by body system", tagged with ATC level 1 or FDA panel), not as the tree. The one exception is the leaf level of H2.6 Medicines (section 3.4).

## 4. Depth and size

**Rough count of generic kinds: about 450 to 550** (my estimate from group counts, not a line-by-line count). Basis: the German list has 38 therapy-goal groups (S5); the Japanese insurance list has 22 items (S6); U.S. News ranks 128 OTC and personal-care categories, of which I guess about half are medicines, supplements or devices (S8; not counted); Holland and Barrett lists 8 vitamin groups plus minerals, botanicals and sports lines (S25, S30); FDA has about 1,700 generic device types but most are professional (S3). By sub-family: monitoring and testing 25; therapy devices 20; mobility and transfer 30; daily-living and care 45; sensory and communication 30; medicines 90 OTC use classes and perhaps 200 more now that prescription medicines are in scope, filed by labelled use class (H-2); supplements 120; sports, meal and medical nutrition 15; contraception, barriers and breastfeeding 10; first aid and wound care 15; supports, braces and prostheses 30; parts and accessories 45.

**Rough count of named forms: a few dozen non-maker-bound, and many thousands maker-bound, with no cap (CV-7).** Non-maker-bound forms are now chiefly generic supplement formulas (joint formula; hair, skin and nails formula) and restorative essences; the active ingredients and labelled fixed combinations of medicines (about 300 under the S2C model) are values of the `active_ingredients` attribute, not named forms (H-8, C2b), and a traditional formula such as a kampo formula is recorded the same way, with a `region` or `culture` tag on the brand product that carries it. Hearing-aid shell styles, rollator load classes, thermometer measuring modes and standard-defined kit classes are attributes, not named forms (C2b). Maker-bound forms are brand medicine products (H-8), device models (Omron HCR-7106, a hearing-aid or CGM model line), contact-lens families and spectacle-frame models. Under the depth-and-breadth rule (BRIEF; CV-5, CV-7) every maker-bound form that one fetched page shows made or sold is admitted, harvested in stage 3 from existing catalogues first (pharmacy and medicine registers, hearing-aid and contact-lens databases, maker catalogues) and grown without a cap; there is no condition that a review source exist.

**Where depth concentrates.** Supplements (hundreds of single-ingredient kinds, many from the NIH lists of vitamins, minerals, botanicals and probiotics, S7); medicines (use class, then brand products as named forms, with ingredient and dose form as attributes); assistive and daily-living aids (a long tail of one-task objects); hearing aids (style against model against technology level, S10).

- Broad: Vitamins; Other single ingredients; Pain and inflammation; Cough, cold and flu; Allergy and sinus.
- Medium: Walking aids; Wheeled seating; Hearing; Vision; Blood pressure; Blood glucose; Fertility and pregnancy; Bathing; Protein; Performance; Support.
- Narrow: Body temperature; Oxygen saturation; Heart rhythm; Infection tests; Breastfeeding; First-aid kits; Alerting and identification.

A size class names a kind only when it changes what the object works with and sellers sell the sizes apart (CV-19, C6); no H2 size class does so on the present evidence.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Decided: measured and standard-defined attributes (`standard_met`), legal classes in the C17 ledger, and tier entries on makers, lines and named forms that each enter on one fetched source; no tier on any kind (C4a, CV-10, CV-35, CV-36).** Four layers.

1. **Measured or voluntary-standard specifications are the attribute `standard_met`, with the standard named (C4c class (i), C17, CV-36).** First-aid kit class and container type: ANSI/ISEA Z308.1 Class A, Class B and Type I to IV (S9); BS 8599-1 small, medium, large (S29); DIN 13157/13169/13164/14142 and ÖNORM Z 1020 (S28). Blood-pressure monitor validation (S22), with the validation list or protocol named (†: the list's name is attested at harvest). Third-party seals for supplements: USP, NSF, Informed Choice (S8). None is a tier, and none defines a kind.
2. **Legal classes go to the C17 jurisdiction ledger** (`subject_id`, `jurisdiction`, `scheme`, `class`, `criteria`, `source_url`, `date`; never a level or a tag on the tree). Device risk class: FDA I, II, III (S3); EU I, IIa, IIb, III with Is, Im, Ir (S31); China I, II, III (S13). Drug sale class: UK POM, P, GSL (S18); Japanese prescription, guidance-required, 1, 2 (designated 2), 3 (S17); US prescription or OTC for hearing aids and glucose monitors (S10, S23). The generated view "no prescription needed" is built from the ledger. This replaces the first draft's own `regulatory_status` table (CV-35).
3. **Tier entries on makers, lines and named forms (C4a to C4c, C4e).** U.S. News publishes brand rankings by a pharmacist survey (S8): an editorial or community class (C4c class (iii)), a tier entry on the brand product or line. HearingTracker reports lab-based scores (S10): a third-party measured score (class (ii)), a tier entry with `test_version`. CHOICE's ratings are members-only (S22) and are not read. Each entry is dated and sits on a maker, line, named form or variant, never on the kind. A scheme, a reviewer's grade included, enters on one fetched source; a second source or region only adds weight and feeds `disputed` (CV-10, CV-12; the old domain-H "second region before entry" clause is dropped). A maker's own technology levels for hearing aids (essential, standard, advanced, premium) are tier entries with `scheme` = the maker's name, with `line_rank` in the lines table (C4c class (iv), CV-12); the only page seen was a guide found by search and not fetched, so the scheme enters when one fetched page attests it at harvest (†; provisional until attested).
4. **Price bands (C4d, USD).** Hearing aids span the bands from under 50 to 5,000–15,000: OTC averages $502 per pair and ranges $20 to $2,000; prescription averages $3,432 and ranges $1,000 to $8,500 (S10). Blood-pressure monitors from the Omron store are about ¥9,300 to ¥24,000 (about $60 to $160 at ¥150 to the dollar, bands 50–150 and 150–500) (S12). No price page was fetched for vitamins and OTC medicines; they are expected to sit mostly in the lowest band. Bands are derived from a dated, sourced list price.

**Remaining risks.**
- *Medicines depth.* With prescription medicines in (H-2), H2.6 needs labelled use classes for prescription products. The fetched sources show OTC aisles only (S8, S14). Prescription use classes will be built from labels and pharmacy trees at harvest, and some may turn out to be ATC-style therapeutic classes rather than labelled uses. The rule "filed by the use printed first on the pack" carries more weight for multi-indication prescription drugs.
- *Brand products as named forms (H-8).* Brand medicine products run to many thousands across jurisdictions, and the same brand carries different ingredients in different countries. One brand name can therefore be several named forms. Stage 3 needs a rule for matching brand names across countries; jurisdiction-specific formulation is not a C17 legal class.
- *Software (K-37 against CV-30).* The line between a regulated medical app (H2) and a companion app for one maker's devices (I1, C13 row) rests on what the app is made to do. Omron Connect-type apps that store blood-pressure readings sit near the line. P25 files by the app's own function; borderline apps need their maker page read at harvest.
- *Magnifiers (K-38 against K-135).* AFB's flip-down and head-worn magnifying glasses are sold as low-vision aids (S24), but the A4 note puts head-worn magnifiers in C6. Under K-38 ("made and sold as a low-vision aid is H2") a low-vision head-worn magnifier would be H2, so the two notes should be read together. This tension is passed to the session for the conflicts log; this memo does not resolve it.
- *Objects with H2 homes but no H2 kind yet.* Examination tables and dentist chairs (K-190) and therapeutic weighted blankets and pillows (K-40) are H2 by the key, but the tree has no kind for them yet (section 12, not applied).
- *Reimbursement lists* (S5, S6) are kept as generated views with flags on kinds. If such membership counts as a legal class, the flags move to C17 rows instead; the tree is unchanged either way.
- *Thin evidence.* No optician, collector or resale source; group names only for stairlifts, hoists, ostomy and continence kinds, braces and prostheses; no source from India, Latin America or Africa (gaps below).
