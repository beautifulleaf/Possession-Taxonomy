# J6 Coffee, Tea & Cocoa — structure memo (research draft)

Research, not decision. Written from fetched pages only (36 sources, listed in `verify/out/S2_J6_sources.tsv`; keys S1 to S36 below are the row numbers of that file, header excluded). Calls used: 14 search calls (2 to 4 queries each) and 37 fetched URLs (one of them, a EUR-Lex OJ PDF, returned 404 and is not a source); caps were 18 and 40. Evidence is strong for coffee retail and grading, tea types and tea retail (Germany, Japan, US, China), cocoa legal definitions and the ICCO fine/bulk split, and yerba mate. It is thinner for herbal infusions (single-herb names rest on one retailer text, S25), for kava (one buyer guide, S31), for cocoa retail outside baking (S20, S21, S28, S34) and for coffee substitutes (no direct retail source). No museum source was fetched. Where an item was located in a search excerpt of a page that was also fetched, the memo says so.

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J6 Coffee, Tea & Cocoa, in domain J Groceries.

**Membership test (placement key step 3, third branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** "coffee, tea and cocoa as beans, leaves, powders or capsules, and other leaves and flowers brewed as infusions". The J6 line in the category list adds: "coffee, tea, cocoa and infusions as bought, graded by origin, leaf or bean grade and score."

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch J6:**
- Step 1 (not an ownable object, prepared food or drink): "ready-to-drink coffee and tea, kombucha" and "chocolate bars and other confectionery" are OUT and left to Culinary. Drying, fermenting, ageing or plain cooking of one ingredient does not make a food prepared.
- Step 3 note: "a product is filed by what it is, not by what it contains": sauces, pastes, stocks and cooking fats are J5. The J5 branch names "baking ingredients (raising agents and dried yeast, baking chocolate, unbaked doughs and pastry)" and "spices, dried herbs and salt". So baking chocolate and dried cooking herbs are J5, not J6.
- Step 3 note: a set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value.
- Step 5(e): "a recurring delivery of goods chosen or curated by the provider (subscription boxes, wine and coffee clubs, meal-kit plans)" is I3, not J6.
- Step 27 note (B9): "Coffee, tea and kava are J6; alcoholic drinks are J7." Kratom, khat, areca and betel are B9.
- Step 2 note (H2): vitamins, supplements, sports nutrition and restorative tonics sold for health are H2.
- Step 3 note, last clause: "food made for animals ... is B10"; not relevant here.
- Rule S3 (parts follow the whole) against the step 3 wording "capsules": a capsule is a consumable made for one kind of machine, which rule S3 would send to B5, but step 3 names capsules in J6 and rule S1b says named notes bind. Section 9 states the resolution.
- B5 branch: "tea and coffee equipment" (kettles, grinders, machines, teapots) is B5.
- Sister taxonomy: Culinary FRAMEWORK_v1 holds the drinks side. DR-A Tea & Infusions (A1 Teas by how the leaf is processed, A2 Prepared Teas, A3 Infusions & Tisanes by plant part) and DR-B Coffee & Cocoa (B1 Coffee by brew, B2 Cocoa & Chocolate) are the links to plan (section 10).

**Edge wording issue found:** the test says "other leaves and flowers brewed as infusions". Ginger and kava roots, rose hips and fruit, roasted barley and seeds are brewed as infusions and sold beside herbs, but they are not leaves or flowers. Section 10 proposes one wording change.

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK/Italy, Germany, Japan, Canada, Belgium)
- **Coffee, US (S1 Walmart).** The coffee hub is organised by how the buyer brews: coffee pods, iced and cold brew, ground coffee, whole-bean coffee, instant coffee, espresso pods. Tea is the neighbouring hub, split into iced, green, herbal, wellness, black (tiles seen in the search excerpt of the fetched page). The whole-bean page carries facets Container, Form, Fruit Type, Product Category.
- **Coffee, UK/Italy (S2 illy).** The type menu mixes format and system: X-Caps capsules, Iperespresso capsules, Nespresso-compatible capsules, E.S.E. pods, coffee beans, ground coffee, Arabica Selection, filter, instant, cold brew, decaffeinated. Filters are intensity (soft 3-4, medium 5-6, strong 7-9), roast or origin (Arabica Selection, classico, intenso, decaffeinated) and pack size. The bean lines are brand lines (Classico, Intenso, Arabica Selection Nicaragua and Ethiopia).
- **Coffee, Germany (S29 Tchibo).** The Kaffees menu mixes use and format: Kaffee für Vollautomaten, Espresso, Caffè Crema, Filterkaffee, Omni Roast, Entkoffeinierter Kaffee, Specialty Coffee von lokalen Röstereien, Kaffeekapseln, Kaffeepads, Instantkaffee, Probiersets. Whole bean and ground are variants of one product ("Ganze Bohne" / "Gemahlen" under Weitere Varianten), each with Intensität x/6 and a taste tag (mild, nussig, fruchtig, blumig, schokoladig). Tee, machines and accessories are sibling branches.
- **Coffee, Japan (S26 Kaldi).** Coffee is a top-level group beside Tea, Wine, Sweets and Baking. Inside it: coffee beans, drip coffee (drip bags), iced coffee, Cup of Excellence, equipment, gifts, flavoured, instant, liquid, regular (ground), milk/sugar/syrup, plus certification lines (Bird Friendly, Doi Tung, Woman's Hand, Fair Trade). Beans filter by coffee type (straight by South America, Central America, Asia-Oceania, Africa-Middle East, or blend), roast (light, medium, medium-dark, dark, green/raw), taste and body. Product names are origin names (マンデリン, キリマンジャロ), blend styles (モカブレンド, エスプレッソブレンド, アメリカンブレンド, イタリアンロースト) and house blends (マイルドカルディ, リッチブレンド).
- **Green coffee, US (S3 Sweet Maria's).** Origin menu (Asia, Arabia, decaf, own blends, roasted), then flavour-profile facets (Light and Bright, Classic Balance, Chocolate Bittersweets, Good for Espresso, Good for Light/Medium/Dark Roast) and a Process Method facet (washed, machine washed, natural, honey, pulp natural, wet-hulled, anaerobic, Swiss Water and other decaf processes).
- **Tea, Germany (S7 Teespeicher, S8 Tee Gschwendner, S12 EdelTee).** All three open with the processing type: Schwarztee, Grüntee, Weißer and Gelber Tee, Oolong, Pu Erh, then Früchtetee, Rooibos, Kräutertee, with Matcha as its own branch (S8 top nav: Tee, Matcha, Zubehör; S12 quick links: Grüner Tee, Schwarzer Tee, Matcha, Kräutertee, Rooibos, Kaffee). Under black tea S7 splits by region (Assam, Darjeeling with First Flush / Second Flush, Nepal and Sikkim, Sri Lanka, China) and offers filters Anbauregion, Anbauland, Jahrgangstee, Zubereitungsart (gaiwan, gongfu), Eigenschaften (rarity, koffeinfrei, for iced tea) and Bio. S8 filters are Anbaugebiet, Anbaumethode (Bio, Naturland, TransFair), Attribute (First Flush, Second Flush, caffeine) and Geschmacksrichtung; its product names carry leaf grades (FTGFOP, SFTGFOP1, TGBOP, OP) and styles (Earl Grey, English Breakfast, Ostfriesen blend, Lapsang Souchong).
- **Tea, Japan (S9 Lupicia, S10 Ippodo, S27 Kaldi).** Lupicia puts flavoured black tea (フレーバード) under black tea under tea and filters by package form (leaf in bag, leaf in tin, tea bag). Ippodo's product handles fall into matcha, sencha, bancha (hojicha, genmaicha), tea bags, gifts and utensils; items carry a Light or Balanced character label. Kaldi's tea group is black tea (82), Japanese-Chinese-health tea (41), herbal tea (38), instant (9), liquid (13).
- **Tea, Canada/US (S24 Tao Tea Leaf, S11 The Tea Spot, S25 Adagio, S32 NM Tea).** Tao Tea Leaf: White, Green, Yellow, Black, Oolong, Rare Oolong, Pu-er, Ancient Tree Pu-er, Aged Tea, then Yerba Mate, Rooibos, Chai, Blooming, Iced Tea, Scented and Blend, Herbal, Caffeine-Free. Tea Spot's herbal page filters by Tea Type, Caffeine Level and Flavor (citrus, floral, fruity, ginger, minty, spiced, unflavored, vanilla). Adagio treats herbal tea as "not Camellia sinensis" and keeps Rooibos, Decaf and Wellness as neighbouring collections; mate sits inside herbal as the caffeinated exception. NM Tea splits pu-erh into loose and cakes, with Black (shou), Green (sheng), White and Dark Tea sub-branches.
- **Cocoa and chocolate (S20 Callebaut, S21 King Arthur, S28 Kaldi, S34 Etsy).** Callebaut's professional catalogue is by function: Chocolate (couvertures), Cocoa ingredients (cocoa powder, cocoa butter, cocoa mass), Coatings and fillings, Nut ingredients, Inclusions (roasted cocoa nibs), Decorations, Toppings and sauces, Instants and mixes, Drinks. Kaldi files 製菓用チョコレート・ココア (baking chocolate and cocoa) under baking, not under coffee and tea. Etsy lists raw cacao beans, ceremonial cacao paste blocks and cacao pods side by side, labelled by variety (criollo, forastero), origin and "raw".
- **Mate and kava (S22 Yerbatica, S31 Kava.com).** Mate is typed by leaf and stem composition (con palo, despalada), grind and dust, ageing, drying (barbacuá smoked, hot air), flavoured, compuesta (herb blends), organic, cocido and tea bags. Kava root powder is one product category beside tinctures, blends, sampler packs and accessories.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, reviewers and trade guides
- **Coffee (S4 Coffee Review, S35 Wikipedia).** Roast level is one numeric scale (Agtron M-Basic, light above 80 down to extreme-dark below 25) with SCA terms, and the reviewers deliberately avoid French, Viennese, Italian roast names because use varies. Single-origin means one known geographic origin, with estate coffee and micro-lot as narrower types; no body enforces the labels.
- **Tea (S36, S23, S14).** Tea is classified by degree of oxidation or fermentation: green (steamed in Japan, pan-fired in China), yellow, white, oolong, black, post-fermented. The Chinese "six tea categories" (green, yellow, dark, qing/oolong, white, red/black) use fermentation degree, process and liquor colour; "dark tea" and "black tea" are easy to confuse because Chinese "red tea" is English black tea (S36).
- **Mate (S22).** The community database reads a mate bag by composition (con palo, despalada), dust, ageing and smoke before brand.
- **Pu-erh collectors (S33).** Buyers' first decision is raw sheng against ripe shou; ancient-tree, vintage, blended and single-origin cakes follow.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market
- **Cup of Excellence (S30).** Lots score 86 or more to advance and 87 or more to enter the online auction; 90 or more earn a Presidential Award; winners are traceable to farm and micro-lot. Kaldi sells a Cup of Excellence line (S26).
- **Pu-erh (S33, S32).** Aged raw cakes are a collecting class: factories (Dayi, Xiaguan, Haiwan), vintage year, storage conditions and provenance drive value; counterfeit cakes are a stated problem. This is a collectible channel for a grocery kind (framework rule S5: the object decides, not the motive).
- **Etsy (S34).** Cacao beans and ceremonial paste are sold in a general marketplace, mixed with germination pods and soap.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications
- **ISO 20715:2023 (S13).** Six basic tea types by processing and aeration (black ISO 3720, green ISO 11287, white ISO/TR 12591, oolong ISO 20716, dark, yellow). Scented, compressed and instant tea are reprocessed products. Matcha is defined as steamed, unrolled, shade-grown leaf ground fine.
- **SCA (S5), ICO (S6), COE (S30).** SCA-110 is green coffee grading and specifications; SCA-131 is roast level test method; the 100-point cupping protocol and the 80-point specialty threshold are quoted in S6 with zero category-1 defects and at most five category-2 per 350 g. The ICO collation shows that definitions differ by country and trade body (Brazilian, Colombian, Rwandan tiers; ITC standard, premium and specialty).
- **Tea leaf grading (S14).** Orthodox black tea is graded by size and tip content: whole leaf OP, FOP, GFOP, TGFOP, FTGFOP, SFTGFOP; broken BOP; fannings BOPF; dust D1 and PD. CTC tea has its own grades. The page notes no equivalence for most Chinese appellation grades.
- **HS / CN (S16).** Chapter 09 puts coffee (0901: not roasted / roasted, decaffeinated or not, husks and skins, substitutes containing coffee), tea (0902: green not fermented; black and partly fermented, each by pack size over or under 3 kg) and mate (0903) in separate headings. It has no heading for white, oolong or pu-erh. Cocoa and herbal infusions are outside the chapter (cocoa is chapter 18 in the standard HS; not fetched).
- **EU Directive 2000/36/EC (S17, S19).** Sales names: cocoa butter; cocoa powder; fat-reduced cocoa powder; powdered chocolate; drinking chocolate (sweetened cocoa); chocolate (with couverture, gianduja, vermicelli, flakes); milk chocolate; family milk chocolate; white chocolate; filled chocolate; chocolate a la taza; pralines. Labels must state total dry cocoa solids. The German explainer (S19) gives the cocoa-powder cut (at least 20 % cocoa butter; below 20 % is fat-reduced, roughly 10-12 % in trade talk) and couverture (at least 35 % total cocoa solids and 31 % cocoa butter).
- **ICCO (S18).** Cocoa beans are fine or flavour against bulk or ordinary; generally Criollo and Trinitario against Forastero, with exceptions; "there is no single universally-accepted criterion"; the difference is flavour, not other quality factors.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works
Wikipedia pages (S14, S35, S36) and the Chinese Wikipedia page (S23) are the reference works. No museum or national-collection source was fetched; the trade standards stand in for it.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split
- **Agree:** tea is divided first by processing type, with the six ISO or Chinese types; coffee is divided by roast, origin or blend and a brew or machine fit; herbal infusions are everything that is not Camellia sinensis; rooibos and mate are sold as their own branches.
- **Split on coffee form:** Walmart, Kaldi and illy make whole bean, ground, capsule and instant separate departments or types; Tchibo makes whole bean and ground variants of one product. Capsules and pods are split by system everywhere (K-Cup, Nespresso-compatible, Iperespresso, Cafissimo, E.S.E., Senseo-type pads).
- **Split on matcha:** Teegschwendner, EdelTee, Teespeicher give matcha a top-level branch; ISO and Ippodo treat it as a green tea derivative.
- **Split on scented tea:** Lupicia and Tao Tea Leaf give flavoured or scented tea its own category; Teespeicher keeps aromatised white tea under white tea.
- **Split on tea bags:** Ippodo and Lupicia treat bags as a collection or package form; ISO treats form (compressed, instant) as reprocessing.
- **Split on mate and herbal:** Adagio files mate under herbal; Tao Tea Leaf and HS give it its own branch or heading.
- **Split on cocoa:** consumer retail files cocoa with baking (S21, S28); industry and law file cocoa products (S17, S20) together with chocolate; Culinary files the drink.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence that the first axis is the **plant**, then a plant-specific second axis:
- Every retail site opens with coffee, tea or chocolate and cocoa as separate departments (S1, S26 to S29). Kaldi and Tchibo keep coffee and tea in different top branches; Callebaut separates chocolate from cocoa ingredients (S20).
- For **coffee**, buyers choose next by **machine fit and format** (capsule system, pods, whole bean, ground, instant: S1, S2, S29, S26), then by roast and origin or blend (S26, S3, S4). The question coffee guides address is "what is my brewer" (S1 tile heading "How you brewin?" seen in the search excerpt).
- For **tea**, buyers choose by **processing type** first (all of S7, S8, S12, S24), then by region, flush and leaf grade (S7, S8, S14).
- For **infusions**, buyers choose by **plant identity and flavour** (S11 flavor facets; S25 names; S7 Früchtetee, Rooibos, Kräutertee).
- For **cocoa**, buyers choose by **ingredient function** (nibs, powder, paste, drinking chocolate: S20, S34) and, for powder, by alkalisation and fat (S21, S19).
- Among community questions: raw against ripe pu-erh (S33), con palo against despalada mate (S22), noble kava and lateral root (S31), specialty against commercial coffee (S6).
- Origin is a filter in every shop (S7, S8, S26, S3) but never the first split, which fits Appendix A: region is a modifier.

All candidates keep the framework limits: no region, era, price, brand, motive or setting level; one stated axis per level within each sub-family (C5(c)); at most four levels below the category.

- **Where it breaks:**
  1. *Earl Grey, jasmine tea and masala chai* have a base tea (black, green) and a flavouring; the axis says processing, so they need a separate "reprocessed" leaf. That follows ISO (S13) and Lupicia, Tao Tea Leaf, but Teespeicher files aromatised white tea under white tea (S7).
  2. *Genmaicha* (green tea with roasted rice) is a blend of tea and grain. It is a named form under bancha here (Ippodo files it there); Culinary files it with scented and blended teas (DR-A1-7).
  3. *Cacao paste/ceremonial cacao and 3-in-1 coffee mixes* sit on the line between J6, J5 baking chocolate and prepared drinks (section 9).
  4. *Barley tea and chicory* are infusions or coffee substitutes depending on the shop; the test wording ("leaves and flowers") does not cover them.

### Candidate B: form as sold first, then plant
- Level 1 axis: physical form (whole bean or leaf; ground or powder; capsule, pod or bag; instant; paste). Sub-families such as "Whole leaf and bean", "Ground and powdered", "Single-serve and instant".
- Evidence for: Walmart and Kaldi coffee departments (S1, S26), Lupicia package filters (S9), Tchibo format (S29).
- Example kinds: whole-bean coffee, ground coffee, coffee capsule, instant coffee, loose-leaf tea, tea bag, matcha powder, cocoa powder, cocoa nibs, kava powder, mate leaf.
- Where it breaks: (1) *matcha* is a powder but is judged against sencha and gyokuro, not against ground coffee; (2) *a pu-erh cake* is neither whole leaf nor powder; (3) *whole bean and ground* are variants of one product at Tchibo (S29); (4) *cocoa nibs* are neither bean nor powder. Form also cuts across every plant, so the tree repeats the plant names inside each form.

### Candidate C: effect or use first (caffeinated, caffeine-free, relaxing, wellness)
- Level 1 axis: caffeine and claimed effect. Evidence for: Walmart "wellness tea", Adagio Wellness and Decaf collections (S1, S25), Tao Tea Leaf "Caffeine-Free" branch (S24).
- Example kinds: caffeinated leaf tea, decaf tea, caffeine-free herbal, mate, coffee, kava.
- Where it breaks: effect is a motive or modifier (Appendix A); (1) *mate* is herbal and caffeinated; (2) *decaf* is a treatment applied to coffee and tea; (3) *kava* is sedating and sits with neither; (4) *rooibos* is caffeine-free but not a herb in the shops' sense. Reject; keep caffeine as an attribute.

*Origin-first (coffee) and region-first (tea) were considered and rejected as levels: S3, S7, S8 and S26 all offer origin as a facet, and Appendix A bars region from inner levels. Origin appears as a tag and as a facet.*

## 4. Depth and size

**Kinds.** The tree above has 42 kinds: coffee 8 (two queried), tea 13, cocoa 6 (two queried), infusions 15. Adding sourced herbs one by one could bring infusions to about 30 and the category to about 60 kinds. The category is shallow and wide in the leaf, narrow in depth.

**Named forms.** Rough counts, from what the sources show:
- Coffee: origin and appellation names about 40 to 80, blend styles about 15, maker-bound lines (illy, Tchibo, Kaldi house blends, other roasters) several hundred in the maker ledger.
- Tea: about 120 to 200 non-maker-bound names (Culinary DR-A1 already lists dozens; Tao Tea Leaf names 12 "famous" Chinese teas, S24; Tee Gschwendner names about a dozen black teas, S8), plus maker-bound blends and house lines.
- Cocoa: about 15 (natural, Dutch, black, burgundy; single-origin beans).
- Mate: about 10 (con palo, despalada, chimarrão, barbacuá, aged, compuesta).
Total around 300 to 500 named forms before the maker-bound lines.

**Where depth concentrates:** tea (the seven-value leaf level with named teas) and coffee named forms. Cocoa and infusions are shallow.

**Leaves by breadth:** broad: Green tea (five kinds), Leaves and herbs, Flowers. Medium: Dark tea, Reprocessed tea, Roots and spice. Narrow: White, Yellow, Oolong, Black (one kind each, but with many named forms), Seeds and grains, Mixed. Coffee and cocoa have no leaf level.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers.

**Coffee.** Kind = roasted coffee, green coffee, instant, capsule, pod, drip bag. Evidence: one department with facets for coffee type, roast, taste and body (S26); Tchibo lists whole bean and ground as variants of one product (S29); Sweet Maria's keeps roast level, process and flavour as facets within green coffee (S3); illy filters intensity and roast within beans (S2). Buyers compare quality between lots through scores (S6, S30) and compare makers within roasted coffee. So origin, blend against single-origin, roast, process, decaf method and grind are attributes, not kinds. *Capsules and pods are kinds* because the system fixes what fits (S1, S2, S29 separate K-Cup, Nespresso-compatible, Iperespresso, Cafissimo, E.S.E., pad systems); this meets C10(b), since one cannot replace the other without a different machine and fetched sources list them separately. Drip bags are a kind only because Kaldi gives them their own large category; the alternative is a pack-form attribute (owner question).

**Tea.** Kind = the type that retailers sell as a category: the ISO six (S13) where a shop has a branch for them, and for Japanese green tea sencha, gyokuro, matcha and bancha because Ippodo and Kaldi sort them as such (S10, S27). Region, flush, grade and certification are facets (S7, S8). Individual teas (Darjeeling, Tie Guan Yin, Long Jing) are named forms because many sellers sell them and the comparison happens within the type. Black tea stays one kind: leaf size, orthodox against CTC and tea-bag form are attributes (S14, S36).

**Cocoa.** Kind = the product the buyer uses (nibs, powder, paste, drinking chocolate): Callebaut and King Arthur separate them (S20, S21), and the EU names them (S17). Natural against Dutch-process against black is an attribute with named forms; buyers compare makers at the powder level (S21).

**Infusions.** The choice level is **weakest** here. Tea Spot filters by flavour across a single herbal collection (S11); Adagio lists herbs individually with scores (S25); four shops give rooibos and mate their own branch (S7, S12, S24, S25). Recommended: herb identity is the kind, because a box of chamomile is a different thing from a box of peppermint and Adagio and Tao Tea Leaf list them separately. The alternative is one kind per plant-part group with herbs as named forms; this saves about 12 kinds but makes "chamomile" a design, not a type. Owner question.

The choice level differs between sub-families (coffee: product-and-system level; tea: type level; infusions: herb level).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: incidental**, with two exceptions that the memo asks the owner to confirm.
- **Platform** for capsules and pods: the maker or system sets compatibility (Nespresso, K-Cup, Iperespresso, E.S.E., Cafissimo, Qbo, pads). These go into the compatibility ledger of C13 (`platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`), not the tree.
- **Identity** in two niches: pu-erh factories (Dayi, Xiaguan, Haiwan: S33) and the Japanese tea houses (Ippodo, Lupicia: S9, S10) where the house is part of what is bought. Even there the maker is a ledger row and a tag on named forms, not a level.

**Maker ledger: recommended, lean.** Core columns from C1 only. Coffee roasters and brands (illy, Tchibo, Kaldi), tea houses (Ippodo, Lupicia, Tee Gschwendner, Teespeicher, Teekampagne), cocoa and chocolate makers (Callebaut, Guittard, Valrhona: S20, S21), mate brands. Known-for kinds derive from the named-form ledger. `maker_class` values needed beyond C1's list: roaster, tea house, estate or garden (Darjeeling gardens are in the search excerpts only and are not attested here).

**Named forms that are maker-bound** (maker_bound yes): illy Classico and Intenso (S2), Tchibo Feine Milde and Caffè Crema lines (S29), Kaldi Mild Kaldi and Rich Blend (S26), King Arthur Double Dark Cocoa Blend (S21). **Not maker-bound** (maker_bound no): origin and appellation names, blend styles, tea names, mate styles.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Coffee (no leaf):** canonical form first (roasted coffee), then green coffee, instant, capsule, pod, drip bag, substitute, concentrate. Reason: the order of physical closeness to the roasted bean, with machine-bound items last.
- **Tea leaves:** order the leaves by degree of oxidation as ISO and Wikipedia define it (S13, S36): white, green, yellow, oolong, black, dark, then reprocessed. Within Green tea: Sencha, Gyokuro, Matcha, Bancha, Pan-fired (Japanese steamed types first because they are the retail split; Culinary DR-A1 orders steamed then pan-fired).
- **Cocoa (no leaf):** the bean-to-product chain: beans, nibs, shells, paste, powder, drinking chocolate.
- **Infusions:** plant-part leaves in the order leaves, flowers, fruits, roots, seeds, mixed; within a leaf, kinds alphabetically by English name, except that the commonest first (rooibos, mate, chamomile, peppermint) is a judgement for the owner.
- **Named forms (C7):** by maker in ledger order, then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetical. Non-maker-bound names alphabetical.

## 9. Boundaries

**Closest cross-boundary objects and precedence sentences.**

1. **J6 against J5 (baking chocolate, cocoa fat, dried herbs, spices).** *Cocoa beans, nibs, cocoa powder, cocoa paste and drinking-chocolate powder bought to brew, drink or bake with are J6; baking chocolate, couverture, chocolate chips and cocoa butter are J5, and plain unsweetened cocoa powder stays J6 even when bought for baking.* *A leaf, flower or root sold as an infusion in bags or as loose tea is J6; the same plant sold in a jar as a dried cooking herb or spice (mint, ginger, turmeric) is J5.* Evidence: the J5 branch names baking chocolate and dried herbs; Callebaut and Kaldi separate "cocoa ingredients" from chocolate (S20, S28); Adagio and Tea Spot sell ginger and turmeric as tea (S11, S25).
2. **J6 against Culinary (prepared drinks) and mixed products.** *Ground, instant, capsule and pod coffee, tea in any leaf form, and cocoa powders are J6; a drink already made (bottled or canned cold brew, bubble tea, kombucha, milk tea, hot chocolate as served) is OUT.* *A 3-in-1 or drinking-chocolate mix is J6 only when cocoa or coffee is the main ingredient by weight; a mix that is mainly sugar, creamer or malt is prepared.* The second sentence needs owner confirmation (S26 sells a 3-in-1 milk coffee; S17 defines drinking chocolate as at least 25 % cocoa powder; S1, S29).
3. **J6 against B5 (equipment) and rule S3.** *Machines, grinders, kettles, teapots, infusers and filter papers are B5; capsules, pods, tea bags and coffee drip bags are J6 because step 3 names capsules, even though a capsule is a consumable made for one kind of machine.* rule S3 would send them to B5, so the framework needs one sentence to say that step 3 wins; the compatibility ledger records which machine each fits (C13).
4. **J6 against B9 (smoking and vaping).** *Kava is J6; kratom, khat, areca and betel, cannabis and CBD tea or drinks, and herbal smoking blends are B9.* This follows the step 27 note.
5. **J6 against H2 (health).** *An herbal infusion sold as a tea (loose herb or bags, named for the plant) is J6 even when it is marketed as a wellness blend; a product sold as a supplement or medicine, with a dose and a health claim, or as a tincture, capsule or extract, is H2.* Evidence: "wellness teas" are sold as tea at Walmart and Adagio (S1, S25).
6. **J6 against I3 (subscriptions).** *A recurring coffee or tea delivery is I3; one bag, tin or box bought once is J6.*
7. **J6 against F (collecting).** *Aged pu-erh, rare coffee lots and vintage tea kept as a collection are still J6 tea or coffee (rule S5: test the object, not the motive); the cake is not a collectible.*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A):** category J6; sub-family by plant (Coffee, Tea, Cocoa, Herbal & other infusions); leaf by the plant's own axis (tea type by ISO processing; plant part for infusions; none for coffee and cocoa); kind = the product or type buyers compare; named form = origin names, blend styles, tea names and maker lines. **Maximum depth:** four levels below the category in tea and infusions (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); three in coffee and cocoa (sub-family, kind, named form). Region, origin, harvest, grade, certification, roast, process, grind, pack form and caffeine are attributes or tags.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. **Baking chocolate and couverture.** The research pointer for J6 lists "cocoa and baking chocolate", but the framework puts baking chocolate in J5. Confirm J5 for chocolate and J6 for cocoa products, or move couverture into J6.
2. **Cocoa paste and cocoa butter.** Cocoa paste or mass (and ceremonial cacao) as J6 or J5 baking chocolate; cocoa butter as a J5 cooking fat or as a J6 cocoa product.
3. **Mixes.** 3-in-1 coffee, drinking-chocolate and malted drink mixes: J6 or prepared (OUT).
4. **Capsule and pod rule.** Approve the sentence that step 3 wins over rule S3 for capsules and pods.
5. **Form against kind in coffee.** Keep whole bean and ground as one kind with a grind attribute (recommended, from Tchibo S29), or split them (Walmart, Kaldi, Kaldi's 5-item "regular coffee").
6. **Drip bags and tea bags.** Drip bags as a kind (recommended on Kaldi's 41-item category) or an attribute; tea bags as an attribute (recommended).
7. **Choice level for infusions.** Herb identity as kind (about 15 now, up to 30) or plant-part group as kind with herbs as named forms.
8. **Coffee substitutes and grain infusions.** Chicory and barley: J6 coffee substitutes and seed infusions (recommended) or J5.
9. **Test wording.** Change "other leaves and flowers brewed as infusions" to "other plant parts brewed as infusions (leaves, flowers, fruit, roots, bark, roasted grains)", and add kava (already in the step 27 note) to the J6 line.
10. **Matcha.** Keep matcha under Green tea (recommended: ISO and Ippodo) or promote it to a sub-family leaf (Teegschwendner, EdelTee, Teespeicher).
11. **Unit price.** A derived price per kilogram with its own bands for groceries, in place of the per-item USD bands in C4(d).
12. **Raw/ripe pu-erh.** Attribute on one kind (recommended) or two kinds.

Specifications such as SCA-131 roast level and ISO tea types are attributes. Traditional types (sencha, gyokuro, chimarrão) are kinds or non-maker named forms because fetched shops sell them as categories (S10, S22).
- C3: variant ledger used sparsely: pu-erh vintage and recipe codes, harvest year and flush, limited editions.
- C4: no tier on a kind; `bean_class`, leaf grade and `specialty_grade` are attributes with the standard named; matcha grade is a `platform_tag`; price per kg proposed.
- C5: fixed level names; coffee and cocoa skip the leaf (leaf null); per-sub-family leaf axes are stated in section 3.
- C6: pack size is an attribute (grams); user does not apply.
- C7: see section 8.
- C8: the scene table in section 7.
- C9: alias and homonym table proposed: tisane = herbal tea; mate = yerba mate = erva-mate; chimarrão (Brazil) = a form of mate; Chinese hong cha (red tea) = black tea, but rooibos "red tea" is a different plant; Chinese hei cha = dark tea, not black tea (S36); 紅茶 = black tea; レギュラーコーヒー = roast-and-ground coffee; ドリップコーヒー = drip-bag coffee; Kaffeepad = coffee pod; cacao and cocoa = same plant, different processing in some shops (S19, S34).
