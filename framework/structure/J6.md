## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J6 Coffee, Tea & Cocoa, in domain J Groceries.

**Membership test (placement key step 3, third branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md, as reworded by K-50 and K-51):** "coffee, tea and cocoa as beans, leaves, powders or capsules, cocoa butter and cocoa paste, and other plant parts brewed as infusions (leaves, flowers, fruit, roots, bark, roasted grains), kava included". The J6 line in the category list adds: "coffee, tea, cocoa, cocoa butter and paste, and infusions of any plant part (kava included) as bought, graded by origin, leaf or bean grade and score."

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch J6:**
- Step 1 (not an ownable object, prepared food or drink): "ready-to-drink coffee and tea, kombucha" and "chocolate bars and other confectionery" are OUT and left to Culinary. Drying, fermenting, ageing or plain cooking of one ingredient does not make a food prepared.
- Step 3 note: "a product is filed by what it is, not by what it contains": sauces, pastes, stocks and cooking fats are J5. The J5 branch names "baking ingredients (raising agents and dried yeast, baking chocolate, unbaked doughs and pastry)" and "spices, dried herbs and salt". So baking chocolate, couverture and dried cooking herbs are J5, not J6. K-50 adds the exception that goes the other way: cocoa butter and cocoa paste are J6 cocoa products.
- Step 3 note: a set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value. K-49: "A dry mix for a drink is J6 when coffee, tea or cocoa is its main ingredient by weight; a mix led by sugar, creamer or malt is a prepared drink (OUT)." K-46: a mix of kinds from one category is filed under the kind that makes up half or more of its weight, otherwise under a final kind "mixed <class>".
- Step 5(e): "a recurring delivery of goods chosen or curated by the provider (subscription boxes, wine and coffee clubs, meal-kit plans)" is I3, not J6.
- Step 27 note (B9): "Coffee, tea and kava are J6; alcoholic drinks are J7." Kratom, khat, areca and betel are B9. K-168: every plant or fungus product taken for a psychoactive effect is B9 whatever its legal status, but kava is J6.
- Step 2 note (H2): vitamins, supplements, sports nutrition and restorative tonics sold for health are H2.
- Step 3 note, last clause: "food made for animals ... is B10"; not relevant here.
- Rule S3 (parts follow the whole) and the word "capsules" in step 3: a capsule is a consumable made for one kind of machine. This is **not a conflict**: the key is run in order and stops at the first yes, and step 3 (groceries, which names capsules) comes before step 8 (S3 parts), so a capsule is already J6 and no new sentence is needed (review J6-4). Machines, grinders, kettles and teapots stay B5 (step 30).
- B5 branch: "tea and coffee equipment" (kettles, grinders, machines, teapots) is B5.
- Sister taxonomy: Culinary FRAMEWORK_v1 holds the drinks side. DR-A Tea & Infusions (A1 Teas by how the leaf is processed, A2 Prepared Teas, A3 Infusions & Tisanes by plant part) and DR-B Coffee & Cocoa (B1 Coffee by brew, B2 Cocoa & Chocolate). The link plan is written in section 10 ("Culinary link plan").

**Edge wording issue (resolved by K-51).** The earlier test said "other leaves and flowers brewed as infusions", which did not cover ginger and kava roots, rose hips and fruit, or roasted barley and seeds. K-51 rewrote the branch to "other plant parts brewed as infusions (leaves, flowers, fruit, roots, bark, roasted grains)" and named kava, so these kinds are J6.4 on the key's own words.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, reviewers and trade guides
- **Coffee (S4 Coffee Review, S35 Wikipedia).** Roast level is one numeric scale (Agtron M-Basic, light above 80 down to extreme-dark below 25) with SCA terms, and the reviewers deliberately avoid French, Viennese, Italian roast names because use varies. Single-origin means one known geographic origin, with estate coffee and micro-lot as narrower types; no body enforces the labels.
- **Tea (S36, S23, S14).** Tea is classified by degree of oxidation or fermentation: green (steamed in Japan, pan-fired in China), yellow, white, oolong, black, post-fermented. The Chinese "six tea categories" (green, yellow, dark, qing/oolong, white, red/black) use fermentation degree, process and liquor colour; "dark tea" and "black tea" are easy to confuse because Chinese "red tea" is English black tea (S36).
- **Mate (S22).** The community database reads a mate bag by composition (con palo, despalada), dust, ageing and smoke before brand.
- **Pu-erh collectors (S33).** Buyers' first decision is raw sheng against ripe shou; ancient-tree, vintage, blended and single-origin cakes follow.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market
- **Cup of Excellence (S30).** † Lots score 86 or more to advance and 87 or more to enter the online auction; 90 or more earn a Presidential Award; winners are traceable to farm and micro-lot. The score thresholds come from a **search excerpt** of the S30 page; the fetch gave only the structure of the rules (jury rounds), so the thresholds need a fetched attestation. Kaldi sells a Cup of Excellence line (S26).
- **Pu-erh (S33, S32).** Aged raw cakes are a collecting class: factories (Dayi, Xiaguan, Haiwan), vintage year, storage conditions and provenance drive value; counterfeit cakes are a stated problem. This is a collectible channel for a grocery kind (framework rule S5: the object decides, not the motive). What drives the value of one particular cake (its storage and provenance) is an individual-item fact and is not stored (C16); only the kind, the factory recipe and the vintage year are.
- **Etsy (S34).** Cacao beans and ceremonial paste are sold in a general marketplace, mixed with germination pods and soap.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications
- **ISO 20715:2023 (S13).** Six basic tea types by processing and aeration (black ISO 3720, green ISO 11287, white ISO/TR 12591, oolong ISO 20716, dark, yellow). Scented, compressed and instant tea are reprocessed products. Matcha is defined as steamed, unrolled, shade-grown leaf ground fine.
- **SCA (S5), ICO (S6), COE (S30).** SCA-110 is green coffee grading and specifications; SCA-131 is roast level test method; the 100-point cupping protocol and the 80-point specialty threshold are quoted in S6 with zero category-1 defects and at most five category-2 per 350 g. The ICO collation shows that definitions differ by country and trade body (Brazilian, Colombian, Rwandan tiers; ITC standard, premium and specialty).
- **Tea leaf grading (S14).** Orthodox black tea is graded by size and tip content: whole leaf OP, FOP, GFOP, TGFOP, FTGFOP, SFTGFOP; broken BOP; fannings BOPF; dust D1 and PD. CTC tea has its own grades. The page notes no equivalence for most Chinese appellation grades.
- **HS / CN (S16).** Chapter 09 puts coffee (0901: not roasted / roasted, decaffeinated or not, husks and skins, substitutes containing coffee), tea (0902: green not fermented; black and partly fermented, each by pack size over or under 3 kg) and mate (0903) in separate headings. It has no heading for white, oolong or pu-erh. Cocoa and herbal infusions are outside the chapter (cocoa is chapter 18 in the standard HS; not fetched).
- **EU Directive 2000/36/EC (S17, S19).** Sales names: cocoa butter; cocoa powder; fat-reduced cocoa powder; powdered chocolate; drinking chocolate (sweetened cocoa); chocolate (with couverture, gianduja, vermicelli, flakes); milk chocolate; family milk chocolate; white chocolate; filled chocolate; chocolate a la taza; pralines. Labels must state total dry cocoa solids. The German explainer (S19) gives the cocoa-powder cut (at least 20 % cocoa butter; below 20 % is fat-reduced, roughly 10-12 % in trade talk) and couverture (at least 35 % total cocoa solids and 31 % cocoa butter).
- **ICCO (S18).** Cocoa beans are fine or flavour against bulk or ordinary; generally Criollo and Trinitario against Forastero, with exceptions; "there is no single universally-accepted criterion"; the difference is flavour, not other quality factors.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works
Wikipedia pages (S14, S35, S36) and the Chinese Wikipedia page (S23) are the reference works. No museum or national-collection source was fetched; the trade standards stand in for it.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split
- **Agree:** tea is divided first by processing type, with the six ISO or Chinese types; coffee is divided by roast, origin or blend and a brew or machine fit; herbal infusions are everything that is not Camellia sinensis; rooibos and mate are sold as their own branches.
- **Split on coffee form:** Walmart, Kaldi and illy make whole bean, ground, capsule and instant separate departments or types; Tchibo makes whole bean and ground variants of one product. Capsules and pods are split by system everywhere (K-Cup, Nespresso-compatible, Iperespresso, Cafissimo, E.S.E., Senseo-type pads).
- **Split on matcha:** Teegschwendner, EdelTee, Teespeicher give matcha a top-level branch; ISO and Ippodo treat it as a green tea derivative.
- **Split on scented tea:** Lupicia and Tao Tea Leaf give flavoured or scented tea its own category; Teespeicher keeps aromatised white tea under white tea.
- **Split on tea bags:** Ippodo and Lupicia treat bags as a collection or package form; ISO treats form (compressed, instant) as reprocessing.
- **Split on mate and herbal:** Adagio files mate under herbal; Tao Tea Leaf and HS give it its own branch or heading.
- **Split on cocoa:** consumer retail files cocoa with baking (S21, S28); industry and law file cocoa products (S17, S20) together with chocolate; Culinary files the drink.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence that the first axis is the **plant**, then a plant-specific second axis:
- Every retail site opens with coffee, tea or chocolate and cocoa as separate departments (S1, S26 to S29). Kaldi and Tchibo keep coffee and tea in different top branches; Callebaut separates chocolate from cocoa ingredients (S20).
- For **coffee**, buyers choose next by **machine fit and format** (capsule system, pods, whole bean, ground, instant: S1, S2, S29, S26), then by roast and origin or blend (S26, S3, S4). The question coffee guides address is "what is my brewer" (S1 tile heading "How you brewin?" seen in the search excerpt).
- For **tea**, buyers choose by **processing type** first (all of S7, S8, S12, S24), then by region, flush and leaf grade (S7, S8, S14).
- For **infusions**, buyers choose by **plant identity and flavour** (S11 flavor facets; S25 names; S7 Früchtetee, Rooibos, Kräutertee).
- For **cocoa**, buyers choose by **ingredient function** (nibs, powder, paste, drinking chocolate: S20, S34) and, for powder, by alkalisation and fat (S21, S19).
- Among community questions: raw against ripe pu-erh (S33), con palo against despalada mate (S22), noble kava and lateral root (S31), specialty against commercial coffee (S6).
- Origin is a filter in every shop (S7, S8, S26, S3) but never the first split, which fits Appendix A: region is a modifier.

- **Where it breaks:**
  1. *Earl Grey, jasmine tea and masala chai* have a base tea (black, green) and a flavouring; the axis says processing, so they need a separate "reprocessed" leaf. That follows ISO (S13) and Lupicia, Tao Tea Leaf, but Teespeicher files aromatised white tea under white tea (S7).
  2. *Genmaicha* (green tea with roasted rice) is a blend of tea and grain. It is a named form of Scented, flavoured and blended tea, as in Culinary (DR-A1-7); Ippodo files it under bancha (S10), and that arrangement is the alternative if the owner prefers the shop's view.
  3. *Cocoa paste/ceremonial cacao and 3-in-1 coffee mixes* sat on the line between J6, J5 baking chocolate and prepared drinks. Decided: paste and cocoa butter are J6 (K-50); a dry mix is J6 only when coffee, tea or cocoa is its largest ingredient by weight (K-49); section 9.
  4. *Barley tea and chicory* are infusions or coffee substitutes depending on the shop; the test wording ("leaves and flowers") does not cover them. K-51 files **both** in J6.4 (roots and roasted grains brewed as infusions), and J6.1's axis is the plant Coffea, which a chicory root is not. Culinary groups chicory with coffee (DR-B1-7); the link plan carries that.
  5. *Green-tea kinds.* Japanese shops sell sencha, gyokuro, matcha and bancha as categories (S10, S27), but those names sit on different axes (leaf maturity, shading, form), and "pan-fired" is a process. The tree puts the kinds on the process axis and the shop names below them as named forms (review J6-6); J-15 confirms it. The earlier arrangement (kinds Sencha, Gyokuro, Matcha, Bancha and Pan-fired) mixed axes and is not used.

### Candidate B: form as sold first, then plant
- Level 1 axis: physical form (whole bean or leaf; ground or powder; capsule, pod or bag; instant; paste). Sub-families such as "Whole leaf and bean", "Ground and powdered", "Single-serve and instant".
- Evidence for: Walmart and Kaldi coffee departments (S1, S26), Lupicia package filters (S9), Tchibo format (S29).
- Example kinds: whole-bean coffee, ground coffee, coffee capsule, instant coffee, loose-leaf tea, tea bag, matcha powder, cocoa powder, cocoa nibs, kava powder, mate leaf.
- Where it breaks: (1) *matcha* is a powder but is judged against sencha and gyokuro, not against ground coffee; (2) *a pu-erh cake* is neither whole leaf nor powder; (3) *whole bean and ground* are variants of one product at Tchibo (S29); (4) *cocoa nibs* are neither bean nor powder. Form also cuts across every plant, so the tree repeats the plant names inside each form. Pack form is kept as the attribute `pack_form` in Candidate A.

### Candidate C: effect or use first (caffeinated, caffeine-free, relaxing, wellness)
- Level 1 axis: caffeine and claimed effect. Evidence for: Walmart "wellness tea", Adagio Wellness and Decaf collections (S1, S25), Tao Tea Leaf "Caffeine-Free" branch (S24).
- Example kinds: caffeinated leaf tea, decaf tea, caffeine-free herbal, mate, coffee, kava.
- Where it breaks: effect is a motive or modifier (Appendix A); (1) *mate* is herbal and caffeinated; (2) *decaf* is a treatment applied to coffee and tea; (3) *kava* is sedating and sits with neither; (4) *rooibos* is caffeine-free but not a herb in the shops' sense. Reject; keep caffeine as an attribute.

*Origin-first (coffee) and region-first (tea) were considered and rejected as levels: S3, S7, S8 and S26 all offer origin as a facet, and Appendix A bars region from inner levels. Origin appears as a tag and as a facet.*

## 4. Depth and size

**Kinds.** The tree above has 41 kinds: coffee 5, tea 13, cocoa 7 (cocoa butter added by K-50), infusions 16 (ten carry †). Adding sourced herbs one by one could bring infusions to about 30 and the category to about 55 kinds; this is an estimate, not a cap (a herb enters on one fetched page showing it sold). The category is shallow and wide in the leaf, narrow in depth. (The earlier draft had 42 kinds: drip bags become an attribute, capsules and pods become one kind, and the coffee substitute moves to J6.4.)

**Named forms.** Rough counts, from what the sources show. They are estimates, not caps (CV-7): named forms, lines and makers are uncapped and grow as far as attested evidence goes, and large sets (coffee origin and appellation names, tea names, maker lines) may live in registers linked to their kind (CV-45).
- Coffee: origin and appellation names about 40 to 80, blend styles about 15, maker-bound lines (illy, Tchibo, Kaldi house blends, other roasters) several hundred in the maker ledger.
- Tea: about 120 to 200 non-maker-bound names (Culinary DR-A1 already lists dozens; Tao Tea Leaf names 12 "famous" Chinese teas, S24; Tee Gschwendner names about a dozen black teas, S8), plus maker-bound blends and house lines.
- Cocoa: about 15 (natural, Dutch, black, burgundy; single-origin beans).
- Mate: about 10 (con palo, despalada, chimarrão, barbacuá, aged, compuesta).
Total around 300 to 500 named forms before the maker-bound lines, as a starting estimate.

**Where depth concentrates:** tea (the seven-value leaf level with named teas) and coffee named forms. Cocoa and infusions are shallow.

**Leaves by breadth:** broad: Green tea (five kinds), Leaves and herbs, Flowers. Medium: Dark tea, Reprocessed tea, Roots, bark and spice (four kinds). Narrow: White, Yellow, Oolong, Black (one kind each, but with many named forms), Fruits, Seeds and grains, Mixed. Coffee and cocoa have no leaf level.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers.

**Coffee.** Kind = roasted coffee, green coffee, instant, machine capsule or pod, liquid concentrate. Evidence: one department with facets for coffee type, roast, taste and body (S26); Tchibo lists whole bean and ground as variants of one product (S29); Sweet Maria's keeps roast level, process and flavour as facets within green coffee (S3); illy filters intensity and roast within beans (S2). Buyers compare quality between lots through scores (S6, S30) and compare makers within roasted coffee. So origin, blend against single-origin, roast, process, decaf method, grind and **pack form** are attributes, not kinds. *Capsules and pods are one kind* because the system fixes what fits (S1, S2, S29 separate K-Cup, Nespresso-compatible, Iperespresso, Cafissimo, E.S.E., pad systems) and one cannot replace another without a different machine. The basis is **C13 and JC4(c)**: a single-serve pack made for a machine system is a kind, with the system recorded in the compatibility ledger. It is not Rule T (C10(b)), which is about technology that changes an object's physical form. **Drip bags are not a kind**: they need no machine, so they are the attribute `pack_form` (review J6-2). Kaldi gives them a large category of their own (S26); J-14 keeps them an attribute, because level follows what the buyer needs to own, not a shop's department.

**Tea.** Kind = the type that retailers and ISO sort as a category: the ISO six (S13) where a shop has a branch for them, with one kind per defining process inside Green tea (steamed, shade-grown steamed, pan-fired, roasted, powdered). Sencha, Bancha and Gyokuro are named forms below those kinds even though Ippodo and Kaldi sort them as shop categories (S10, S27): the kind axis inside a leaf must be one axis (review J6-6; J-15). Region, flush, grade, certification and **pack form (loose, tea bag, pyramid bag)** are facets or attributes (S7, S8). Individual teas (Darjeeling, Tie Guan Yin, Long Jing) are named forms because buyers ask for them by name and the comparison happens within the type (C2(a): the number of makers is not the test). Black tea stays one kind: leaf size, orthodox against CTC and tea-bag form are attributes (S14, S36). A tea capsule or pod for a machine system would follow the coffee rule (one kind, system in C13) once one fetched source shows it (†); J-14 then moves the capsule kind to a J6 multi-use sub-family.

**Cocoa.** Kind = the product the buyer uses (beans, nibs, shells, paste, butter, powder, drinking chocolate): Callebaut and King Arthur separate them (S20, S21), and the EU names them (S17). Cocoa butter and cocoa paste are J6 by K-50; couverture and baking chocolate are J5. Natural against Dutch-process against black is an attribute with named forms; buyers compare makers at the powder level (S21).

**Infusions.** The choice level is **weakest** here. Tea Spot filters by flavour across a single herbal collection (S11); Adagio lists herbs individually with scores (S25); four shops give rooibos and mate their own branch (S7, S12, S24, S25). Decided (J-15): herb identity is the kind, because a box of chamomile is a different thing from a box of peppermint and Adagio and Tao Tea Leaf list them separately. The alternative (one kind per plant-part group with herbs as named forms) is not taken: it would make "chamomile" a design, not a type.

The choice level differs between sub-families (coffee: product-and-system level; tea: type level; infusions: herb level).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: incidental**, with two exceptions.
- **Platform** for capsules and pods: the maker or system sets compatibility (Nespresso, K-Cup, Iperespresso, E.S.E., Cafissimo, Qbo, pads). These go into the compatibility ledger of C13 (`platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`), not the tree. It is the only J platform case (review section 4).
- **Identity** in two niches: pu-erh factories (Dayi, Xiaguan, Haiwan: S33) and the Japanese tea houses (Ippodo, Lupicia: S9, S10) where the house is part of what is bought. Even there the maker is a ledger row and a tag on named forms, not a level.

**Maker ledger: uncapped (CV-5).** One fetched page admits a maker, and the ledger is seeded from existing catalogues and grows without a cap. Core columns from C1 only, and `maker_class` takes only C1's values (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; retail own-label; and so on). Coffee roasters and brands (illy, Tchibo, Kaldi), tea houses (Ippodo, Lupicia, Tee Gschwendner, Teespeicher, Teekampagne), cocoa and chocolate makers (Callebaut, Guittard, Valrhona: S20, S21) and mate brands are maker rows. **Roaster, tea house, and tea estate or garden are not `maker_class` values** (review section 4, X6). The multi-valued J column `supply_role` (JC3, CV-41) carries them (values used here: roaster; tea house or estate; processor or packer; importer or trader; grower or marketing body; cooperative; retail own-label), and certifications sit on the named form or product as attributes. Darjeeling gardens appear only in search excerpts and are not attested here †. Known-for kinds derive from the named-form ledger. Roasters, tea houses, estates and gardens, pu-erh factories, cocoa and chocolate makers and mate brands are all ledger rows with no cap; none is a kind. A school or tradition of makers, if a fetched page attests one, goes in the school table (CV-3), not on the tree.

**Designations.** A protected name that binds one kind (Darjeeling for black tea, if a fetched source attests the designation †) is a non-maker named form of that kind carrying a `designation_id`; one that spans several kinds is a tag; a consortium is the `authority` of a designation row, never a maker (JC1, CV-40; the wine and spirit exception does not reach J6). Mandheling and Kilimanjaro are origin names, not designations, unless a source shows otherwise.

**Named forms that are maker-bound** (maker_bound yes): illy Classico and Intenso (S2), Tchibo Feine Milde and Caffè Crema lines (S29), Kaldi Mild Kaldi and Rich Blend (S26), King Arthur Double Dark Cocoa Blend (S21). Each stands as a maker-bound named form when the line sits inside one kind; a brand line that spans more than one kind (for example the same blend sold as roasted coffee and as capsules) is a lines row, not a named form (C2(e), R-35; CV-46 applies to this everyday consumable, while teas keep named forms). † whether illy Classico and Intenso span kinds is read from the maker's pages at harvest. **Not maker-bound** (maker_bound no): origin and appellation names, blend styles, tea names, mate styles.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Coffee (no leaf):** canonical form first (roasted coffee), then green coffee, instant, machine capsule or pod, liquid concentrate. Reason: the order of physical closeness to the roasted bean, with machine-bound items last.
- **Tea leaves:** order the leaves by degree of oxidation as ISO and Wikipedia define it (S13, S36): white, green, yellow, oolong, black, dark, then reprocessed. Within Green tea, kinds go by process, steamed Japanese tea first because it is the retail mainstream and Culinary orders it first (DR-A1-1, DR-A1-2, DR-A1-8): steamed, shade-grown steamed, pan-fired, roasted, powdered. Within Dark tea: pu-erh, then other dark tea. Within Reprocessed tea: scented, flavoured and blended, then spiced blends.
- **Cocoa (no leaf):** the bean-to-product chain: beans, nibs, shells, paste, butter, powder, drinking chocolate.
- **Infusions:** leaves in the order leaves, flowers, fruits, roots and spice, seeds and grains, mixed; within a leaf, kinds **alphabetically by English name** (reproducible; a "commonest first" order has no source and is dropped).
- **Named forms (C7, CV-21):** non-maker-bound forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound forms, by maker (ledger name order), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically. Ladder and benchmark orders are generated views, not stored order.

## 9. Boundaries

**Closest cross-boundary objects and precedence sentences.**

1. **J6 against J5 (baking chocolate, cocoa fat, dried herbs, spices).** *Cocoa beans, nibs, shells, cocoa powder, cocoa butter, cocoa paste (liquor, mass, ceremonial cacao) and drinking-chocolate powder bought to brew, drink or bake with are J6; couverture, baking chocolate and chocolate chips are J5, and plain unsweetened cocoa powder stays J6 even when bought for baking* (K-50: "Cocoa butter and cocoa paste are J6 cocoa products; couverture and baking chocolate stay J5"). The J5 memo lists baking chocolate and couverture and not cocoa butter among its cooking fats. *A leaf, flower or root sold as an infusion in bags or as loose tea is J6; the same plant sold in a jar as a dried cooking herb or spice (mint, ginger, turmeric) is J5.* *A roasted grain or root sold to brew (barley tea, chicory) is J6* (K-51). Evidence: the J5 branch names baking chocolate and dried herbs; Callebaut and Kaldi separate "cocoa ingredients" from chocolate (S20, S28); the EU Directive names cocoa butter a cocoa product (S17); Adagio and Tea Spot sell ginger and turmeric as tea (S11, S25).
2. **J6 against Culinary (prepared drinks) and mixed products.** *Ground, instant, capsule and pod coffee, tea in any leaf form, and cocoa powders are J6; a drink already made (bottled or canned cold brew, bubble tea, kombucha, milk tea, hot chocolate as served) is OUT.* *A dry mix for a drink is J6 when coffee, tea or cocoa is its main ingredient by weight (largest single ingredient); a mix led by sugar, creamer or malt is a prepared drink (OUT)* (K-49). *A liquid coffee or tea concentrate sold to dilute, with coffee or tea its main ingredient, is J6; a sweetened or milk ready-to-drink is prepared (OUT)* (J6-O1, extends K-49). A coffee-led mix files as Instant coffee with the attribute `mix`; a cocoa-led mix as Drinking chocolate powder. J3's sentence on powdered milk-tea and hot-chocolate mixes must follow the same K-49 test (cross-memo note, not edited here). Evidence: S26 sells a 3-in-1 milk coffee; S17 defines drinking chocolate as at least 25 % cocoa powder (so a sugar-led one falls OUT by K-49); S1, S29. Prepared kinds that Culinary lacks (for example a 3-in-1 stick judged prepared) are recorded in the review log, not filed here (CATEGORIES, "How to read this file").
3. **J6 against B5 (equipment) and rule S3.** *Machines, grinders, kettles, teapots, infusers and filter papers are B5; capsules, pods, tea bags and coffee drip bags are J6.* There is no conflict with rule S3: the key stops at the first yes, and step 3 comes before step 8, so a capsule is J6 already. The compatibility ledger records which machine each fits (C13). (Equipment and storage containers go to B5 and B1, as in every J memo; "Parts and accessories made for one object" under C5(d) does not apply to groceries.)
4. **J6 against B9 (smoking and vaping).** *Kava is J6; kratom, khat, areca and betel, cannabis and CBD tea or drinks, and herbal smoking blends are B9.* This follows the step 27 note and K-168 (kava is J6 whatever its legal status elsewhere; the legal status goes to C17).
5. **J6 against H2 (health).** *An herbal infusion sold as a tea (loose herb or bags, named for the plant) is J6 even when it is marketed as a wellness blend; a product sold as a supplement or medicine, with a dose and a health claim, or as a tincture, capsule or extract, is H2.* Evidence: "wellness teas" are sold as tea at Walmart and Adagio (S1, S25).
6. **J6 against I3 (subscriptions).** *A recurring coffee or tea delivery is I3; one bag, tin or box bought once is J6.*
7. **J6 against F (collecting).** *Aged pu-erh, rare coffee lots and vintage tea kept as a collection are still J6 tea or coffee (rule S5: test the object, not the motive); the cake is not a collectible.*
8. **Tea capsules.** *A tea capsule or pod made for a machine system follows the coffee rule: one machine-system kind, system in C13.* † No fetched source attests tea capsules, so no kind is entered yet. J-14: attested tea or cocoa capsules join the machine-system kind and move it to a J6 multi-use sub-family (C14(c), CV-31), whose leaf axis is stated at the move; a generated view lists the kind under each use. Provisional until attested: tea capsules sold for a machine system.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure (Candidate A).** Category J6; four sub-families by plant: J6.1 Coffee, J6.2 Tea, J6.3 Cocoa, J6.4 Herbal & other infusions. The leaf level follows each plant's own axis: tea type by ISO 20715 processing (white, green, yellow, oolong, black, dark, reprocessed); plant part infused for infusions (leaves and herbs, flowers, fruits, roots bark and spice, seeds and roasted grains, mixed); no leaf for coffee and cocoa (`leaf = null`). The kind is the product or type buyers compare: in coffee roasted coffee (whole bean and ground as one kind with a grind attribute), green coffee, instant coffee, the machine-system capsule or pod and liquid coffee concentrate; in tea one kind per ISO type with, inside Green tea, one kind per defining process (steamed, shade-grown steamed, pan-fired, roasted, powdered), pu-erh and other dark tea, scented and blended tea and spiced blends; in cocoa beans, nibs, shells, paste, butter, powder and drinking-chocolate powder; in infusions the herb or plant, with a final "mixed" kind. The named form is an origin or appellation name, blend style, tea name or a maker-bound line inside one kind (lines that span kinds are lines rows). Total 41 kinds. **Maximum depth:** four levels below the category in tea and infusions (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); three in coffee and cocoa (sub-family, kind, named form). Region, origin, harvest, grade, certification, roast, process, grind, pack form (drip bag, tea bag, pyramid bag), caffeine and the dry-mix attribute are attributes or tags. Makers, roasters, tea houses and estates, pu-erh factories and chocolate makers are ledger rows with `supply_role`, uncapped; a unit price per kg or litre (JC2) replaces the item price band. Questions Q1 to Q15 of the earlier draft are all decided (section 12).
