## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B1 Furniture & Storage.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, branch 5): *furniture and storage: made to sit, lie, work at, display, or store and organise things on or in, indoor or outdoor, or to hold a small child in place: seating, tables, beds and mattresses, bean bags, cots, high chairs and playpens, low tray-tables such as the soban, cabinets and fitted cabinetry, shelving, wardrobes, racks (wine, shoe, coat, magazine), hooks, hangers, boxes, baskets, shoe bags, chests and trunks, plant stands, display stands, counters and shop fittings, movable partitions, folding and camping furniture, pop-up gazebos, lap desks, rolling carts.* Category list line: "pieces and containers to sit, lie, work at, display, store and organise things, indoor or outdoor, fitted or not, including mattresses."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name B1:**
- Step 30 note on the branch: tool boxes and tool storage are C1.
- B1 or B8 note (R-8, key sentence for the next framework revision): a freestanding play yard or playpen is B1; a gate or guard fitted to the home is B8.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): furniture, racks, shelves, hooks, hangers, boxes and other storage that hold several objects or stand in a room as furniture are never parts, "whatever they are made to hold" (B1); safes are B8. Cases and stands made to hold one object and travel or stand with it are parts of that object.
- Step 3 (groceries) note: a set or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value; "the basket or box kept afterwards is B1".
- Step 4 (G2) note: kitchen and wardrobe cabinetry is B1; pop-up gazebos are B1 (a built gazebo is G2).
- Step 30 note on B7: tables with a built-in heater (kotatsu, korsi) are B1.
- Step 27 (B9) note: humidors are B1.

- Step 26 (E2) note: portable furniture is filed by what it does (B1), "camping" is a setting; sleeping bags and sleeping mats are E2.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **What the object is for (function/posture).** The first choice in the biggest stores, the standards and the reference works is sit, lie, work at or store: IKEA's four furniture families, Google's Chairs/Beds/Tables/Cabinets & Storage, the AAT's seating/sleeping/support/storage, Wikipedia's Seating/Sleeping/tables/storage [S1, S16, S17, S27]. This is also the wording of the framework key ("to sit, lie, work at, display, or store").
2. **Room, as a second route.** Room appears at IKEA, Wayfair, Chairish, the Container Store, Otto, Segmüller and Pepperfry as an alternative way in [S1, S4, S5, S8–S10, S14]. Under Appendix A, room is a setting, so it is a view or tag. It cannot be a level because the same kind lives in several rooms (a bench, a chest, a desk).
3. **Size and fit.** For beds the size name is decided early and is national (Twin/Full/Queen/King in the US, Single to Super King in Australia, numeric 90/140/160/180 × 200 in Northern Europe, "piazza" in Italy) [S3, S11, S25]; ISO 24975 exists to make frames, bases and mattresses fit [S18]. Size is an attribute with its standard named (C6), not a level; the national bed-size names are held in one size table shared with B2 (review C-10).
4. **Construction and material, second.** For mattresses the construction type (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) is the first thing guides explain [S21, S3, S8]; IKEA's own page says shoppers "prefer to start" with firmness, then sleeping position or material [S3]. For sofas John Lewis filters by cover and style [S7]. Trade codes split by material (wooden, metal, plastic) [S19]; Wikipedia keeps material in a separate list [S27]. Material is therefore an attribute, except for mattresses, where construction is the form.
5. **Style, period, maker.** These lead on resale sites only after the category (Seating, Tables, Case Pieces) is chosen: Mid-Century Modern, Art Deco, Creators, Period are facets [S12–S15].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the same four named levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form) and differ in the axis at the top.

### Candidate A (decided, B-1): function first

Levels and one axis each (conventions C5):
- **Sub-family** — primary use named in the framework key: sit / lie (and be held in place) / work at / store in furniture / hold and organise loose items / show, divide or shelter. This is the level-1 axis.
- **Leaf** — axis stated per sub-family (C5(c)): B1.1 the form of seat (settee, armchair, side chair, stool or bench, task seat, floor or soft seat); B1.2 what is lain on or in (bed, mattress, bed base); B1.3 how the surface is used (dining, occasional, desk, folding or floor, mobile); B1.4 the case form (drawers, doors, shelves, chest); B1.5 how it holds (closed container, open rack or hook); B1.6 has no leaf (six kinds; `leaf = null`, C5(b)); B1.7 (parts) has no leaf (four provisional kinds; axis = the object served, C5(d)). Leaf codes are numbers only, `B1.<sub-family>.<leaf>` (review C-9).
- **Kind** — a generic type by structure or mechanism (sofa, sectional sofa, sofa bed; platform bed, bunk bed; hybrid mattress). Power source, technology and connectivity are attributes (C10); a mechanism that changes the form (recliner, sit-stand desk, sofa bed) may define a kind.
- **Named form** — a specific design below a kind, recorded with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2). **Maker-bound:** a designer or maker model or a product line (Eames Lounge Chair, CH24 Wishbone Chair, POÄNG). **Not maker-bound:** a generic cut or traditional type that many makers make (Chesterfield, Windsor chair, highboy, zaisu, kaidan tansu). Every named form sits under exactly one kind. Sizes, finishes, colourways, fabric covers and editions are attributes or variants (C2(b), C3), never named forms.

Sub-families: B1.1 Seating; B1.2 Sleeping & lying (beds, mattresses, bed bases; the key also puts the playpen, which holds a small child in place, with the cot); B1.3 Tables & work surfaces; B1.4 Storage furniture (case furniture, shelving, chests); B1.5 Containers & organisers (boxes, baskets, bags, racks, hooks, hangers); B1.6 **Display, division & shelter** (stands, fittings, partitions, pop-up shelters), renamed from "Display & division" (B-1) because the single-kind sub-family could not hold the objects the key names (review B1-1); and the closing B1.7 **Parts and accessories made for one furniture object** (C5(d), CV-17; leaf axis = the object served: bed, table, shelving). B-1 decides the function-first axis and the six function sub-families; B1.7 is added by CV-17, which exempts only J, G2, C3, C8 and I3.

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds.** About 200 to 260 across the six sub-families: roughly 60 to 70 in seating, 25 to 30 in sleeping, 35 to 40 in tables, 50 to 60 in storage furniture, 25 to 35 in containers and organisers, and 10 to 15 in display, division and shelter. This is an estimate from the category lists of IKEA US (about 60 furniture and storage entries [S1]), Google (about 120 lines under Furniture, many of them accessories [S16]), Chairish (about 70 sub-types) [S14] and Wikipedia's lists [S27, S28]. The worked tree in section 3 holds 90 kinds, the ones attested here or named by the key; it is a sample, not the full list. The estimate is a guide, not a cap. Parts and accessories made for one furniture object (bed slats, bed guard rails, table legs, replacement shelves) are kinds in the closing sub-family B1.7 (CV-17); sofa and chair covers are B2 (B2-O1).

**Named forms.** Several hundred designs are the realistic first pass; thousands if every manufacturer product line is counted. Three reservoirs: designer classics (Wikipedia's list of chairs alone names a dozen or more designer chairs with designer and year [S28]; the makers' own pages add the CH22–CH26 series, CH07, CH445, CH468, CH101 [S30]); traditional named types (Windsor with its brace-back, Philadelphia, wheel-back, sack-back variants [S34]; Chesterfield; highboy and lowboy; tansu types and regional styles [S35, S36]); and retailer product lines (IKEA POÄNG, KALLAX, HEMNES, LISABO, VALEVÅG [S2, S3]); and a small fourth, album lines (seven seeded in B1.5.3 from [F5:S1] to [F5:S5]). 1stDibs indexes "Designs" and "Design Series" as separate lists [S13]. Each named form carries `maker_bound`, and every product line is a row of the lines table, not a level. Large sets of named forms (chairs are the likely case) live in registers keyed by kind, not as tree rows, and are uncapped (CV-45, C2(g)); a register row has the named-form columns, and no design is held back as "not iconic": each enters on one attested row (C2(f)).

**Where depth concentrates.** Seating (chairs have the most named designs and the oldest design-collector market [S12, S28, S30]); mattresses (comparison by construction, firmness and size [S3, S21, S22]); case furniture (traditional forms recorded to sub-sub-type level on resale sites: chests of drawers split into chiffoniers, commodes, highboys, lowboys [S14]).

**Breadth of leaves (rough).** Broad: dining & side chairs, occasional tables, cupboards & cabinets, shelving. Medium: sofas & settees, armchairs & lounge chairs, beds, mattresses, desks & work tables, boxes & baskets. Narrow: floor & soft seating, bed bases, folding & floor tables, carts & islands, racks & hooks. The sub-family B1.6 has about six kinds and no leaf.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind is the level at which buyers compare quality or makers across items (C2(a)). Below that, distinctions are named forms or attributes. The number of makers is not the test; comparison is.

**Decided stop for B1: the use-type kind, with one exception.**
- **Use-type kinds** (sofa, dining chair, dining table, desk, chest of drawers, wardrobe, bookcase, storage box). Evidence: IKEA, Wayfair and Container Store navigate and filter at this grain, and attributes (seat count, cover, size, finish, number of drawers) sit in filters beside it [S1, S2, S5, S10]; John Lewis lets shoppers filter sofas by seat count, cover and style rather than creating more categories [S7]. Quality comparison in sofas and chairs runs on construction attributes (fabric class by Martindale rub count [S20]) that apply across kinds.
- **Mattresses go one step deeper, by construction**, because the type is where buyers compare. The Sleep Foundation's type page links separate "best" guides for innerspring, memory foam and latex mattresses [S21]; IKEA runs separate landing pages for spring, foam and hybrid [S3]; Segmüller separates Taschenfederkern, Kaltschaum, Gelschaum and Viscoschaum [S8]. The decided kinds are the Sleep Foundation's five (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) [S21]. The finer German splits (pocket spring, cold foam, gel foam, viscoelastic) would be attributes under those kinds. B-12 decides: five kinds, the German splits stay attributes, and the latex mattress is accepted as a kind. The floor mattress (shiki-futon) is a kind in this leaf by form (it lies on the floor and folds away), because the key and B2's boundary send it to B1; the quilt laid over it is B2. Firmness and size are attributes, never kinds [S22, S23, S25].
- **Beds** stop at structure type (platform, panel, bunk, four-poster, daybed, cot) [S26, S27]; headboard height, size and finish are attributes. Named models (Wishbone, POÄNG, HEMNES) are forms. A camp bed and a baby cot are different kinds with different words in the UK and the US; the labels follow the alias table.
- **Storage furniture** stops at the case type (chest of drawers, sideboard, wardrobe, bookcase, kitchen cabinet); the antique market compares highboy against lowboy against chiffonier [S14], so those traditional names are recorded as non-maker named forms of the kind chest of drawers, and Chairish's sub-sub-types are the evidence to attest them (B-13). Fitted or freestanding is an installation attribute, because the same body is offered both ways (C12); built-in wardrobes and fitted kitchen cabinets are the same kinds with installation "fitted" (B-13: fitted cabinetry is kinds with an `installation` attribute).
- **Containers** stop at container form (box, basket, bag, rack, hook, hanger). Volume (the Container Store sells boxes by quart size [S10]) is an attribute. Albums stop at what the album holds (photo album, film album, stamp album, stock book, coin album, card binder); how the pages hold the objects is the attribute `page_mechanism`, although Hakuba, Gaylord and the German retailers lead with it [F5:S1][F5:S2][F5:S5], because the F5 memo decided it so for the filled kind (F5 section 12, O1) and the two kinds of one product keep one attribute set.
- **Designer classics** are compared against their reproductions, so maker and licence status are attached at the maker and line level (Vitra: "sole authorised manufacturer" [S31]; Vitsœ: "exclusive worldwide licensee" [S32]), not stored for any one object (C16).

**Shared rules applied.**
- *Rule T (C10):* power source, technology and connectivity are attributes. The sit-stand desk is defined by its height mechanism, not by its drive; a motorised or manual recliner is one kind. No B1 kind is defined by technology.
- *Rule R (C11):* the regional word names a kind only where no generic kind covers the form. Kotatsu has none in the tree (a heated low table; the key names it) and is a kind. Zaisu sits under floor chair, soban under tray table, and the tansu types under chest of drawers, kitchen cabinet and blanket chest, each tagged with region.
- *Rule M (C12):* the wall shelf is a kind apart from the bookcase because its body is made only for the wall; fitted and freestanding cabinets share a body, so installation is an attribute.

**Where the choice level differs inside B1:** mattresses (construction level) versus everything else (use-type level); kotatsu is the only regional kind, and collectors who compare tansu type against type are served by named forms of their use kinds with a region tag and a generated "by tradition" view (C2(c)) [S35, S36].

## 5. Quality and tier

**Decided: no tier scheme is seeded for B1 yet; tier entries are allowed on one fetched source (CV-10, CV-12, C4(a), C4(e)).** No page fetched in this batch ranks sofas, tables or cabinets on a recognised scale, so none is entered now. Any editorial or community class, reviewer grade (a mattress reviewer's rating, for example) or maker's own ladder that a fetched page shows enters at the stage 3 harvest as a tier entry on a maker, line, named form or, under C4(b), a variant; never on a kind. A second source or region only adds weight (`disputed`, scheme weight in views). What buyers rank by now is carried as attributes with the standard named:
- **Fabric durability: attribute, spec class (C4(c) class i).** Martindale rub-count classes (ISO 12947): decorative under 10,000; light domestic 10,000–15,000; general domestic 15,000–25,000; heavy domestic or light contract 25,000–40,000; contract 40,000 and above; severe contract 100,000 and above. The US Wyzenbeek test (ASTM D4157) gives double rubs with no reliable conversion [S20]. It applies to upholstered kinds and is stored as the attribute "fabric class" with the standard named, as `standard_met` where a standard is met (C17). † The bands come from one retail guide page (S20), which is enough to enter them under the one-source rule; the ISO 12947 and ASTM D4157 standards pages are fetched at the harvest to name the standards (they add weight, not entry).
- **Mattress firmness: attribute, not a ladder.** A 10-point scale is common (1 extra soft to 9–10 extra firm) but "there is no universally accepted measurement" [S22]; the German H1–H5 grades "are not standardised" and are matched to body weight [S23]; Wikipedia notes a CEN "Hs" scale used in a 2003 trial [S24]. Because the scales are not standards measured on the object, the value is stored with the scale's name and is never compared across scales.
- **Contract versus domestic grade.** The rub-count classes above and the "market (domestic, commercial, professional)" modifier of Appendix A cover it [S20].
- **Licence status of the maker or line (not of an object).** Decided (B-14): authorised, licensed, original maker or reproduction maker is recorded on maker and line rows [S31, S32] (with `licensor_id` and a maker-relations row "licensed by", C1); it is not a tier, and no value is ever stored for one object (C16). "Vintage original" is a fact about one object and is not stored.
- **Price band.** One domain-wide scheme in USD (C4(d)), derived from a dated, sourced list price. † No dated list price was fetched in this batch, so no band is assigned; bands are computed per named form at the harvest.
- **Construction quality** (solid wood versus engineered wood, hardwood frame, spring system) appears in retailer text [S9] and stays an attribute.
- **Archival conformity of albums (pass or fail attribute).** Gaylord's album lines carry the spec line "barrier board; buffered; metal edges; passed P.A.T." [F5:S5]. It is a yes or no attribute on photo album (empty) and the other B1.5.3 kinds, with the standard named as `standard_met` (C17); the F5 memo names ISO 18902:2013 for albums and the Photographic Activity Test, and the standards pages are fetched at harvest †. Not a tier.
- The typed tier entry, the scheme registry with its six scale classes and the one price-band vocabulary are shared by all categories (CV-10 to CV-14, C4).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Decided: mixed in kind, uncapped in extent.** Incidental for mass-market furniture; **identity** for designer classics, vintage and antique furniture; **platform** for modular systems. Makers, workshops, ateliers and houses are rows of the one domain-wide maker ledger (C1), never kinds.
- **Identity.** On resale sites the maker or designer is a primary filter: 1stDibs "Creators" (for example Cassina) and Chairish "Shop by Brand" [S12, S13, S14]; Pamono's furniture navigation is by Seating, Tables, Storage [S15]. Carl Hansen & Søn and Vitra sell designs by named designers and say who is authorised [S30, S31].
- **Platform.** USM states that "each new development can be seamlessly integrated into existing furniture" [S33]; Vitsœ calls 606 a "kit of parts" and is the exclusive licensee of Rams's designs [S32]. Mattress and bed-frame fit is a size standard rather than a brand (ISO 24975) [S18]. These go in the compatibility ledger (C13): `platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `sources`. First rows: Vitsœ 606 (owner Vitsœ; modular shelving system) [S32]; USM Haller (owner USM; modular shelving system) [S33]; bed and mattress dimensions (owner ISO; standard ISO 24975; bed bases and mattresses) [S18]. Platforms are never levels.
- **Incidental.** IKEA, Nitori and Pepperfry sell own-label lines where the brand is a named form, not a reason to own [S1, S2, S6, S9]; they are ledger rows all the same (class "retail own-label"), and their lines are lines rows.

**Maker ledger: yes, no cap (CV-5; owner depth-and-breadth rule; one fetched page admits a maker).** The ledger uses the shared C1 schema: `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class` (multi-valued); `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `licensor_id`; `authority_id`; `scene_tags`; `price_band`; `sources`; `school_id` (multi-valued, via the school table). `known_for_kinds` is not stored; it is derived from the named-form ledger.
- *Scope (decided, item 8 closed by CV-5):* every maker of furniture and storage that a fetched page shows: designer-classic houses and modular-system makers, for example Vitra (house or manufacture, Switzerland) [S31], Carl Hansen & Søn (house or manufacture, Denmark) [S30], Vitsœ [S32] and USM [S33]; mass-market and mattress houses and retail own-labels, for example IKEA [S1, S2, S3] and Nitori [S6]; album and collection-holder makers seeded from the F5 pages, each "house or manufacture": Hakuba and Chululu [F5:S1]; Walther, Goldbuch, Henzo, Deknudt and ZEP [F5:S2][F5:S3]; Gaylord Archival and Pioneer [F5:S5] (Hakuba, Walther, Goldbuch, Henzo, Deknudt and Gaylord Archival own the seven album lines; the others have no line yet); and ateliers, cabinetmakers and workshops that resale catalogues name. Each row needs its own fetched source; the `maker_class` of each is proposed from the fetched pages and confirmed when the rows are built.
- *Harvest order (source census, not a limit):* existing catalogues first, namely the 1stDibs Creators list [S12, S13], Chairish's brand list [S14], Pamono's dealers page [S15], Wikipedia's lists of furniture types and chairs [S27, S28] and the museum and trade records behind them [S17], then retailer brand directories, then open search for gaps.
- *Lines table (C1):* `line_id`, `maker_id`, name, the kinds it spans, `line_rank`, `built_by_maker_id` where the designer's licensee builds it, `licensor_id`, years, `sources`. Proposed first rows: HEMNES, KALLAX and POÄNG (IKEA) [S2]; the CH22–CH26 series (Carl Hansen & Søn, Wegner) [S30]; Lounge Chair (Eames; built by Vitra for Europe and the Middle East) [S31]; 606 Universal Shelving System (Rams; built by Vitsœ) [S32]; USM Haller [S33]. A line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (C2(d)); none of the seven album lines spans two kinds, so they are named forms and not lines rows.
- *Maker-relations table (C1):* first rows "licensed by" for the Eames Lounge Chair (Vitra, Europe and the Middle East) [S31] and for Rams's 606 (Vitsœ, exclusive worldwide licensee) [S32]; OEM and rebrand rows are added at the harvest.
- *B1 extension columns (C1, optional):* `designer` (on lines and named forms) and `licence_status` (authorised, licensed, original maker, reproduction maker; maker or line level only; B-14).
- *School table (standing, CV-3):* rows are added at the harvest wherever a fetched page shows a tradition of makers. Candidates, each †: the regional tansu traditions of Sendai, Sado, Kyoto and Yamagata [S36] and Windsor chair making [S34]; makers link by `school_id`.
- *Tier entries (C4):* none seeded (section 5).
- *Variants (C3):* filled wherever a community compares variants (CV-8), without a cap: colourways, materials, prints, editions, limited editions, collaborations, generations and re-issues of designer classics and retailer lines, and reference or model-year variants where a maker publishes them. Finishes, colourways and covers are variants, never named forms. Attested at the harvest.
- *Scene tags (C8):* the style names that resale sites use (Mid-Century Modern, Art Deco, Bauhaus, Biedermeier, Brutalist) are `scene_tags` with `core` or `adjacent` strength, tagged at maker, kind and named-form level without inheritance; scene tags are used wherever a community exists. † Each tag needs a definition source and is held back until it has one; the 1stDibs facets name the styles but do not define them [S12, S13].
- *Price band:* † see section 5.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Decided (C7, CV-20, CV-21): canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states; named forms non-maker first, then maker-bound.**
- Kinds: the plain generic type first (sofa), then structural variants in increasing complexity (sectional, sofa bed, futon sofa). This matches how IKEA, John Lewis and the AAT list them [S1, S7, S17]. The secondary key by sub-family: seating, structural complexity; mattresses, construction in the Sleep Foundation's order (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) [S21], then the floor mattress; tables, formality of use (dining, occasional, desk, folding); storage furniture, number of closed compartments; containers, open to closed; parts, the object served (bed, table, shelving).
- Named forms: non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically where no year is attested (Windsor chair, Chesterfield, highboy, zaisu and the like); then maker-bound named forms, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested (CH24 1949/1950, Lounge Chair 1956, Panton 1967, 606 1960, Haller 1965 [S29–S33]), then alphabetically.
- Album lines (B1.5.3) have no attested year: they are listed by maker within a `page_mechanism` value, then alphabetically, as in the F5 memo.
- Variants (C3) go by `variant_code` or year.
- Maker ladders (`line_rank`), newest-first and benchmark orders are generated views, not stored order.
- Reason: a canonical-first order puts the thing most people own first; the order above is the shared one (C7).

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

### Final structure

B1 is divided in four levels below the category (C5): the function-first axis of Candidate A (B-1), with six function sub-families (Seating; Sleeping & lying; Tables & work surfaces; Storage furniture; Containers & organisers; Display, division & shelter, renamed from "Display & division") and the closing Parts sub-family (B1.7, CV-17: parts made for one furniture object, leaf axis = the object served, no leaf, four provisional kinds). Each function sub-family states the axis of its leaf level (form of seat; what is lain on or in; how the surface is used; case form; how it holds); B1.6 and B1.7 have no leaf (`leaf = null`, C5(b)). The kind is the use type (sofa, dining chair, dining table, desk, chest of drawers, wardrobe, bookcase, storage box), with one step deeper for mattresses, which are five construction kinds plus the floor mattress (B-12; the latex mattress is accepted, the German pocket-spring, cold-foam, gel and viscoelastic splits are attributes). Convertibles are filed by the state they are sold in: sofa bed and futon sofa in Seating, daybed in Sleeping, storage bench and ottoman as attributes, no multi-use sub-family (B-2). Nightstand is storage furniture, and fitted cabinetry and built-in wardrobes are kinds with an `installation` attribute (B-13). The named form is a designer or maker model, a product line or a traditional type with `maker_bound`; Chesterfield, Windsor chair, highboy, lowboy and kaidan tansu are non-maker named forms under generic kinds (B-13), uncapped and held in registers where a kind has many (CV-45). Room, style, period, region, material, setting, user age, market, installation, power source, size, firmness and finish are tags, attributes, filters or generated views. Makers go in the one domain-wide ledger with no cap (retail own-labels, mass-market and mattress houses, designer-classic houses, modular-system makers, ateliers), with lines, maker-relations, the standing school table and the compatibility ledger for modular systems and bed and mattress dimensions; licence status is recorded on makers and lines only (B-14). Variants are filled wherever a community compares them; tier entries enter on one fetched source (none is seeded yet). The closing leaf of B1.5 (B1.5.3) holds the empty albums and collection holders that K-9 sends here, with `page_mechanism` an attribute and the seven album lines moved from F5 as maker-bound named forms.
