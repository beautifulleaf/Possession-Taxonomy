## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B1 Furniture & Storage.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, branch 5): *furniture and storage: made to sit, lie, work at, display, or store and organise things on or in, indoor or outdoor, or to hold a small child in place: seating, tables, beds and mattresses, bean bags, cots, high chairs and playpens, low tray-tables such as the soban, cabinets and fitted cabinetry, shelving, wardrobes, racks (wine, shoe, coat, magazine), hooks, hangers, boxes, baskets, shoe bags, chests and trunks, plant stands, display stands, counters and shop fittings, movable partitions, folding and camping furniture, pop-up gazebos, lap desks, rolling carts.* Category list line: "pieces and containers to sit, lie, work at, display, store and organise things, indoor or outdoor, fitted or not, including mattresses."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name B1:**
- Step 30 note on the branch: tool boxes and tool storage are C1.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): furniture, racks, shelves, hooks, hangers, boxes and other storage that hold several objects or stand in a room as furniture are never parts, "whatever they are made to hold" (B1); safes are B8. Cases and stands made to hold one object and travel or stand with it are parts of that object.
- Step 3 (groceries) note: a set or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value; "the basket or box kept afterwards is B1".
- Step 4 (G2) note: kitchen and wardrobe cabinetry is B1; pop-up gazebos are B1 (a built gazebo is G2).
- Step 30 note on B7: tables with a built-in heater (kotatsu, korsi) are B1.
- Step 27 (B9) note: humidors are B1.
- Step 14 (F4) note: a festival basket is a basket (B1); decorations are B4.
- Step 26 (E2) note: portable furniture is filed by what it does (B1), "camping" is a setting; sleeping bags and sleeping mats are E2.
- Step 30 order: B5, B6, B7, B8, B3 are tried before B1; B2 (bedding, mattress toppers, cushions, throws) and B4 (decor) after it. Garden furniture is B1 (note on C2); planters and plant pots are C2, plant stands are B1.
- Step 2 (H2): medical devices and hospital-type furniture go to H2 before B1 is reached; an ordinary object made in an adapted form is filed as that object.
- Step 13 (B10): animal housing and pet beds are B10.
- Step 17: anything worn on the body is not B1. Step 20: tapestries and textile hangings are F2.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **What the object is for (function/posture).** The first choice in the biggest stores, the standards and the reference works is sit, lie, work at or store: IKEA's four furniture families, Google's Chairs/Beds/Tables/Cabinets & Storage, the AAT's seating/sleeping/support/storage, Wikipedia's Seating/Sleeping/tables/storage [S1, S16, S17, S27]. This is also the wording of the framework key ("to sit, lie, work at, display, or store").
2. **Room, as a second route.** Room appears at IKEA, Wayfair, Chairish, the Container Store, Otto, Segmüller and Pepperfry as an alternative way in [S1, S4, S5, S8–S10, S14]. Under Appendix A, room is a setting, so it is a view or tag. It cannot be a level because the same kind lives in several rooms (a bench, a chest, a desk).
3. **Size and fit.** For beds the size name is decided early and is national (Twin/Full/Queen/King in the US, Single to Super King in Australia, numeric 90/140/160/180 × 200 in Northern Europe, "piazza" in Italy) [S3, S11, S25]; ISO 24975 exists to make frames, bases and mattresses fit [S18]. Size is an attribute with its standard named (C6), not a level; the national bed-size names are held in one size table shared with B2 (review C-10).
4. **Construction and material, second.** For mattresses the construction type (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) is the first thing guides explain [S21, S3, S8]; IKEA's own page says shoppers "prefer to start" with firmness, then sleeping position or material [S3]. For sofas John Lewis filters by cover and style [S7]. Trade codes split by material (wooden, metal, plastic) [S19]; Wikipedia keeps material in a separate list [S27]. Material is therefore an attribute, except for mattresses, where construction is the form.
5. **Style, period, maker.** These lead on resale sites only after the category (Seating, Tables, Case Pieces) is chosen: Mid-Century Modern, Art Deco, Creators, Period are facets [S12–S15].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the same four named levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form) and differ in the axis at the top.

### Candidate A (recommended): function first

Levels and one axis each (conventions C5):
- **Sub-family** — primary use named in the framework key: sit / lie (and be held in place) / work at / store in furniture / hold and organise loose items / show, divide or shelter. This is the level-1 axis.
- **Leaf** — axis stated per sub-family (C5(c)): B1.1 the form of seat (settee, armchair, side chair, stool or bench, task seat, floor or soft seat); B1.2 what is lain on or in (bed, mattress, bed base); B1.3 how the surface is used (dining, occasional, desk, folding or floor, mobile); B1.4 the case form (drawers, doors, shelves, chest); B1.5 how it holds (closed container, open rack or hook); B1.6 has no leaf (six kinds; `leaf = null`, C5(b)). Leaf codes are numbers only, `B1.<sub-family>.<leaf>` (review C-9).
- **Kind** — a generic type by structure or mechanism (sofa, sectional sofa, sofa bed; platform bed, bunk bed; hybrid mattress). Power source, technology and connectivity are attributes (C10); a mechanism that changes the form (recliner, sit-stand desk, sofa bed) may define a kind.
- **Named form** — a specific design below a kind, recorded with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2). **Maker-bound:** a designer or maker model or a product line (Eames Lounge Chair, CH24 Wishbone Chair, POÄNG). **Not maker-bound:** a generic cut or traditional type that many makers make (Chesterfield, Windsor chair, highboy, zaisu, kaidan tansu). Every named form sits under exactly one kind. Sizes, finishes, colourways, fabric covers and editions are attributes or variants (C2(b), C3), never named forms.

Sub-families: B1.1 Seating; B1.2 Sleeping & lying (beds, mattresses, bed bases; the key also puts the playpen, which holds a small child in place, with the cot); B1.3 Tables & work surfaces; B1.4 Storage furniture (case furniture, shelving, chests); B1.5 Containers & organisers (boxes, baskets, bags, racks, hooks, hangers); B1.6 **Display, division & shelter** (stands, fittings, partitions, pop-up shelters). *OWNER: the rename of B1.6 from "Display & division" to "Display, division & shelter" is applied because the single-kind sub-family could not hold the objects the key names (review B1-1); the owner confirms or refuses the rename (review Q-B1, listed in section 10, item 11).*

### Candidate B: room first

Levels: room (living, bedroom, dining, study, bathroom, outdoor) > furniture type > kind > named form. Used by Otto, Segmüller, Pepperfry and the Furniture Row glossary [S4, S8, S9, S26]; also by IKEA's second route [S1].
- Example kinds under it: Living room > sofa, armchair, coffee table, TV unit, bookcase; Bedroom > bed frame, mattress, wardrobe, chest of drawers, nightstand; Dining > dining table, dining chair, sideboard, bench.
- Where it breaks: a desk or a bench belongs in four rooms; a chest of drawers is bedroom at one shop and hall at another; the framework treats room and setting as modifiers (Appendix A) and requires one home per object (S1); outdoor furniture appears as a branch of its own in Google [S16], but the framework files garden furniture by what it is. Edge objects: dining chair used as desk chair; bar cart; storage bench; bathroom vanity (Google: Vanities under Cabinets & Storage [S16]).

### Candidate C: retail hybrid (IKEA-style families)

Levels: family (Sofas & armchairs; Tables & chairs; Beds & mattresses; Desks & desk chairs; Storage) > type > kind > named form [S1, S2].
- Example kinds: sofa, sleeper sofa, armchair, ottoman | dining table, dining chair, bench, bar stool, high chair, step stool | bed, mattress, nightstand, headboard, bed slats, mattress foundation | desk, desk chair, gaming chair.
- Where it breaks: each family mixes axes (a sofa bed sits with sofas but a futon has its own branch elsewhere; chairs are tied to tables, desk chairs to desks, so "chair" has three homes; nightstands ride with beds while the storage family holds dressers). It is a marketing grouping of things bought together, not of what the object is. Edge objects: desk chair vs dining chair; nightstand; bed slats (a part, kept B1 under S3 since it is a bed part); mattress topper (B2).

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds.** About 200 to 260 across the six sub-families: roughly 60 to 70 in seating, 25 to 30 in sleeping, 35 to 40 in tables, 50 to 60 in storage furniture, 25 to 35 in containers and organisers, and 10 to 15 in display, division and shelter. This is an estimate from the category lists of IKEA US (about 60 furniture and storage entries [S1]), Google (about 120 lines under Furniture, many of them accessories [S16]), Chairish (about 70 sub-types) [S14] and Wikipedia's lists [S27, S28]. The worked tree in section 3 holds 79 kinds, the ones attested here or named by the key; it is a sample, not the full list. Parts and accessories (table legs, replacement shelves, crib bumpers, sofa covers) are not kinds here; most follow S3 into the same category as the whole, or go to B2.

**Named forms.** Several hundred designs are the realistic first pass; thousands if every manufacturer product line is counted. Three reservoirs: designer classics (Wikipedia's list of chairs alone names a dozen or more designer chairs with designer and year [S28]; the makers' own pages add the CH22–CH26 series, CH07, CH445, CH468, CH101 [S30]); traditional named types (Windsor with its brace-back, Philadelphia, wheel-back, sack-back variants [S34]; Chesterfield; highboy and lowboy; tansu types and regional styles [S35, S36]); and retailer product lines (IKEA POÄNG, KALLAX, HEMNES, LISABO, VALEVÅG [S2, S3]). 1stDibs indexes "Designs" and "Design Series" as separate lists [S13]. Each named form carries `maker_bound`, and every product line is a row of the lines table, not a level. *OWNER: if one kind collects more than about 50 attested named forms (chairs are the likely case), they could be held in a register keyed by kind with only the iconic ones shown in the tree (review C-1d, Q-X9); the shared conventions do not yet adopt this.*

**Where depth concentrates.** Seating (chairs have the most named designs and the oldest design-collector market [S12, S28, S30]); mattresses (comparison by construction, firmness and size [S3, S21, S22]); case furniture (traditional forms recorded to sub-sub-type level on resale sites: chests of drawers split into chiffoniers, commodes, highboys, lowboys [S14]).

**Breadth of leaves (rough).** Broad: dining & side chairs, occasional tables, cupboards & cabinets, shelving. Medium: sofas & settees, armchairs & lounge chairs, beds, mattresses, desks & work tables, boxes & baskets. Narrow: floor & soft seating, bed bases, folding & floor tables, carts & islands, racks & hooks. The sub-family B1.6 has about six kinds and no leaf.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind is the level at which buyers compare quality or makers across items (C2(a)). Below that, distinctions are named forms or attributes. The number of makers is not the test; comparison is.

**Recommended stop for B1: the use-type kind, with one exception.**
- **Use-type kinds** (sofa, dining chair, dining table, desk, chest of drawers, wardrobe, bookcase, storage box). Evidence: IKEA, Wayfair and Container Store navigate and filter at this grain, and attributes (seat count, cover, size, finish, number of drawers) sit in filters beside it [S1, S2, S5, S10]; John Lewis lets shoppers filter sofas by seat count, cover and style rather than creating more categories [S7]. Quality comparison in sofas and chairs runs on construction attributes (fabric class by Martindale rub count [S20]) that apply across kinds.
- **Mattresses go one step deeper, by construction**, because the type is where buyers compare. The Sleep Foundation's type page links separate "best" guides for innerspring, memory foam and latex mattresses [S21]; IKEA runs separate landing pages for spring, foam and hybrid [S3]; Segmüller separates Taschenfederkern, Kaltschaum, Gelschaum and Viscoschaum [S8]. The recommended kinds are the Sleep Foundation's five (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) [S21]. The finer German splits (pocket spring, cold foam, gel foam, viscoelastic) would be attributes under those kinds. *OWNER: five kinds with the German splits as attributes, or the finer split as kinds (section 10, item 3); and whether a material-defined kind such as the latex mattress is accepted (Q-X5).* The floor mattress (shiki-futon) is a kind in this leaf by form (it lies on the floor and folds away), because the key and B2's boundary send it to B1; the quilt laid over it is B2. Firmness and size are attributes, never kinds [S22, S23, S25].
- **Beds** stop at structure type (platform, panel, bunk, four-poster, daybed, cot) [S26, S27]; headboard height, size and finish are attributes. Named models (Wishbone, POÄNG, HEMNES) are forms. A camp bed and a baby cot are different kinds with different words in the UK and the US; the labels follow the alias table.
- **Storage furniture** stops at the case type (chest of drawers, sideboard, wardrobe, bookcase, kitchen cabinet); the antique market compares highboy against lowboy against chiffonier [S14], so those traditional names are recorded as non-maker named forms of the kind chest of drawers, and Chairish's sub-sub-types are the evidence to attest them (*OWNER: section 10, item 4*). Fitted or freestanding is an installation attribute, because the same body is offered both ways (C12); built-in wardrobes and fitted kitchen cabinets are the same kinds with installation "fitted" (*OWNER: section 10, item 7*).
- **Containers** stop at container form (box, basket, bag, rack, hook, hanger). Volume (the Container Store sells boxes by quart size [S10]) is an attribute.
- **Designer classics** are compared against their reproductions, so maker and licence status are attached at the maker and line level (Vitra: "sole authorised manufacturer" [S31]; Vitsœ: "exclusive worldwide licensee" [S32]), not stored for any one object (C16).

**Shared rules applied.**
- *Rule T (C10):* power source, technology and connectivity are attributes. The sit-stand desk is defined by its height mechanism, not by its drive; a motorised or manual recliner is one kind. No B1 kind is defined by technology.
- *Rule R (C11):* the regional word names a kind only where no generic kind covers the form. Kotatsu has none in the tree (a heated low table; the key names it) and is a kind. Zaisu sits under floor chair, soban under tray table, and the tansu types under chest of drawers, kitchen cabinet and blanket chest, each tagged with region.
- *Rule M (C12):* the wall shelf is a kind apart from the bookcase because its body is made only for the wall; fitted and freestanding cabinets share a body, so installation is an attribute.

**Where the choice level differs inside B1:** mattresses (construction level) versus everything else (use-type level); kotatsu is the only regional kind, and collectors who compare tansu type against type are served by named forms of their use kinds with a region tag and a generated "by tradition" view (C2(c)) [S35, S36].

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: no tier entries for B1.** No source ranks sofas, tables or cabinets on one recognised tier scale. Tier would sit on makers, lines or named forms as tier entries, never on kinds (C4(a)), and none is proposed because no fetched source ranks furniture makers (C4(e)). What buyers rank by is carried as attributes with the standard named:
- **Fabric durability: attribute, spec-ranked.** Martindale rub-count classes (ISO 12947): decorative under 10,000; light domestic 10,000–15,000; general domestic 15,000–25,000; heavy domestic or light contract 25,000–40,000; contract 40,000 and above; severe contract 100,000 and above. The US Wyzenbeek test (ASTM D4157) gives double rubs with no reliable conversion [S20]. It applies to upholstered kinds and is stored as the attribute "fabric class" with the standard named (C4(b)). † The bands come from one guide page (S20 is a retail guide); a standards-body or second-region source is needed.
- **Mattress firmness: attribute, not a ladder.** A 10-point scale is common (1 extra soft to 9–10 extra firm) but "there is no universally accepted measurement" [S22]; the German H1–H5 grades "are not standardised" and are matched to body weight [S23]; Wikipedia notes a CEN "Hs" scale used in a 2003 trial [S24]. Because the scales are not standards measured on the object, the value is stored with the scale's name and is never compared across scales.
- **Contract versus domestic grade.** The rub-count classes above and the "market (domestic, commercial, professional)" modifier of Appendix A cover it [S20].
- **Licence status of the maker or line (not of an object).** Authorised, licensed, original-maker or reproduction maker is a field of the maker and line rows [S31, S32]; it is not a tier. "Vintage original" is a fact about one object and is not stored (C16). *OWNER: whether to record licence status at all (section 10, item 10; Q-X12).*
- **Price band.** One domain-wide scheme in USD (C4(d)), derived from a dated, sourced list price. † No dated list price was fetched in this batch, so no band is assigned.
- **Construction quality** (solid wood versus engineered wood, hardwood frame, spring system) appears in retailer text [S9] and stays an attribute.
- *OWNER: the shared typed tier object and one price-band vocabulary (Q-X7) are decided once for all categories.*

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommendation: mixed.** Incidental for mass-market furniture; **identity** for designer classics, vintage and antique furniture; **platform** for modular systems.
- **Identity.** On resale sites the maker or designer is a primary filter: 1stDibs "Creators" (for example Cassina) and Chairish "Shop by Brand" [S12, S13, S14]; Pamono's furniture navigation is by Seating, Tables, Storage [S15]. Carl Hansen & Søn and Vitra sell designs by named designers and say who is authorised [S30, S31].
- **Platform.** USM states that "each new development can be seamlessly integrated into existing furniture" [S33]; Vitsœ calls 606 a "kit of parts" and is the exclusive licensee of Rams's designs [S32]. Mattress and bed-frame fit is a size standard rather than a brand (ISO 24975) [S18]. These go in the compatibility ledger (C13): `platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `sources`. First rows: Vitsœ 606 (owner Vitsœ; modular shelving system) [S32]; USM Haller (owner USM; modular shelving system) [S33]; bed and mattress dimensions (owner ISO; standard ISO 24975; bed bases and mattresses) [S18]. Platforms are never levels.
- **Incidental.** IKEA, Nitori and Pepperfry sell own-label lines where the brand is a named form, not a reason to own [S1, S2, S6, S9].

**Maker ledger: yes, but limited** (*OWNER: scope, section 10, item 8*). The ledger uses the shared core schema (C1): `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class`; `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `known_for_kinds`; `scene_tags`; `price_band`; `sources`. The memo's earlier "known-for named forms" column is dropped; it is derived from the named-form ledger.
- *Scope (recommended):* makers of designer classics and modular systems, for example Vitra (house or manufacture, Switzerland) [S31], Carl Hansen & Søn (house or manufacture, Denmark) [S30], Vitsœ [S32] and USM [S33]. Mass-market and mattress houses (IKEA [S1, S2, S3], Nitori [S6]; retail own-label) enter only if the owner chooses the wider scope. Each row needs its own fetched source; the `maker_class` of each is proposed from the fetched pages and confirmed when the rows are built.
- *Lines table (C1):* `line_id`, `maker_id`, name, the kinds it spans, years, `sources`, and `built_by_maker_id` where the designer's licensee builds it. Proposed first rows: HEMNES, KALLAX and POÄNG (IKEA) [S2]; the CH22–CH26 series (Carl Hansen & Søn, Wegner) [S30]; Lounge Chair (Eames; built by Vitra for Europe and the Middle East) [S31]; 606 Universal Shelving System (Rams; built by Vitsœ) [S32]; USM Haller [S33].
- *B1 extension columns (optional, C1):* `designer` (on lines and named forms) and `licence_status` (authorised, licensed, original-maker, reproduction maker; maker or line level only; *OWNER: Q-X12*).
- *Tier entries (C4):* none proposed. *Variants (C3):* not filled for B1 unless the owner asks for depth in a scene; finishes, colourways and covers would be variants, never named forms. *School table (C1 addition):* not used.
- *Scene tags (C8):* the style names that resale sites use (Mid-Century Modern, Art Deco, Bauhaus, Biedermeier, Brutalist) are `scene_tags` with `core` or `adjacent` strength, tagged at maker, kind and named-form level without inheritance. † Each tag needs a definition source; the 1stDibs facets name the styles but do not define them [S12, S13].
- *Price band:* † see section 5.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommendation (C7): canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states; named forms by maker, then by year.**
- Kinds: the plain generic type first (sofa), then structural variants in increasing complexity (sectional, sofa bed, futon sofa). This matches how IKEA, John Lewis and the AAT list them [S1, S7, S17]. The secondary key by sub-family: seating, structural complexity; mattresses, construction in the Sleep Foundation's order (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) [S21], then the floor mattress; tables, formality of use (dining, occasional, desk, folding); storage furniture, number of closed compartments; containers, open to closed.
- Named forms: maker-bound forms by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested (CH24 1949/1950, Lounge Chair 1956, Panton 1967, 606 1960, Haller 1965 [S29–S33]), then alphabetically where no year is attested. Non-maker-bound forms follow the maker-bound ones, by attested year, otherwise alphabetically.
- Variants (C3) go by `variant_code` or year; none is filled for B1.
- Reason: a canonical-first order puts the thing most people own first; the maker-then-year order is the shared default (review C-10).

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A, function-first, four levels below the category.** Sub-family (six: Seating; Sleeping & lying; Tables & work surfaces; Storage furniture; Containers & organisers; Display, division & shelter) > leaf (stated per sub-family; B1.6 has none) > kind (generic type by structure or mechanism, stopping at the use type; mattresses stop at construction) > named form (designer or maker model, product line or traditional named type, with `maker_bound`). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Room, style, period, region, material, setting, user age, market, installation (fitted or freestanding), power source, size, firmness and finish are tags, attributes, filters or generated views. A maker ledger with a lines table and a compatibility ledger covers makers of designer classics and modular systems.
