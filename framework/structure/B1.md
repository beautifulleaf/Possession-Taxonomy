## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B1 Furniture & Storage.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, branch 5): *furniture and storage: made to sit, lie, work at, display, or store and organise things on or in, indoor or outdoor, or to hold a small child in place: seating, tables, beds and mattresses, bean bags, cots, high chairs and playpens, low tray-tables such as the soban, cabinets and fitted cabinetry, shelving, wardrobes, racks (wine, shoe, coat, magazine), hooks, hangers, boxes, baskets, shoe bags, chests and trunks, plant stands, display stands, counters and shop fittings, movable partitions, folding and camping furniture, pop-up gazebos, lap desks, rolling carts.* Category list line: "pieces and containers to sit, lie, work at, display, store and organise things, indoor or outdoor, fitted or not, including mattresses."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name B1:**
- Step 30 note on the branch: tool boxes and tool storage are C1.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): furniture, racks, shelves, hooks, hangers, boxes and other storage that hold several objects or stand in a room as furniture are never parts, "whatever they are made to hold" (B1); safes are B8. Cases and stands made to hold one object and travel or stand with it are parts of that object.
- Step 3 (groceries) note: a set or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value; "the basket or box kept afterwards is B1".
- Step 4 (G2) note: kitchen and wardrobe cabinetry is B1; pop-up gazebos are B1 (a built gazebo is G2).
- Step 30 note on B7: tables with a built-in heater (kotatsu, korsi) are B1.
- Step 27 (B9) note: humidors are B1.
- Step 14 (F4) note: a festival basket is a basket (B1); decorations are B4.
- Step 26 (E2) note: portable furniture is filed by what it does (B1), "camping" is a setting; sleeping bags and sleeping mats are E2.
- Step 30 order: B5, B6, B7, B8, B3 are tried before B1; B2 (bedding, mattress toppers, cushions, throws) and B4 (decor) after it. Garden furniture is B1 (note on C2); planters and plant pots are C2, plant stands are B1.
- Step 2 (H2): medical devices and hospital-type furniture go to H2 before B1 is reached; an ordinary object made in an adapted form is filed as that object.
- Step 13 (B10): animal housing and pet beds are B10.
- Step 17: anything worn on the body is not B1. Step 20: tapestries and textile hangings are F2.

**Museums and reference works.** The Getty Art & Architecture Thesaurus sorts furniture "by form or function" into seating furniture (single and multiple seating), sleeping and reclining furniture (beds, chaises longues, couches), storage and display furniture (case furniture, canterburies) and support furniture (tables, steps), plus screens [S17]. **Makers' own pages.** Carl Hansen & Søn presents the CH24 Wishbone Chair (1949, in production since 1950) among the CH22–CH26 series, the CH07 Shell Chair and CH445 Wing Chair, and says it also makes designs by Jacobsen, Mogensen, Klint and Kjærholm [S30]. Vitsœ (606 Universal Shelving System, Dieter Rams 1960) and USM (USM Haller, since 1965) sell modular systems whose parts only fit their own system [S32, S33]. An article on chair styles opens with the Panton chair, the first moulded plastic chair, released by Vitra in 1967 [S29].

**Where sources agree and where they split.**
- Agree: the top of the tree is a set of use families: seating, beds/sleeping, tables, storage (IKEA, Google, AAT, Wikipedia, Pamono, 1stDibs, Chairish all have them) [S1, S12, S14–S17, S27].
- Agree: style, period, region, material and maker are filters, not categories, on every resale site [S12–S15].
- Split: room versus function at the top (German and Indian retail lead with rooms; IKEA, the standards and the reference works lead with function) [S1, S4, S8, S9, S16, S17].
- Split on single objects: the nightstand (Beds & mattresses at IKEA [S1], Tables in Google [S16], Casegoods at Chairish [S14]); sleeper sofas and futons (sofas at IKEA and John Lewis [S1, S7], their own Futons branch at Google [S16]); desks (with desk chairs at IKEA [S1], with office furniture at Google [S16]); TV units (storage at IKEA, entertainment centers at Google).
- Split on mattresses: retailers and the Sleep Foundation file them with beds [S1, S21]; the Harmonized System files them with bedding (9404) rather than furniture (9403) [S19]; Wayfair gives them their own nav entry [S5]. The framework already settles this in favour of B1.
- Split on containers: IKEA, Wayfair ("Organization") and the Container Store treat boxes, baskets and bins as a separate storage-container range from furniture [S1, S5, S10].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **What the object is for (function/posture).** The first choice in the biggest stores, the standards and the reference works is sit, lie, work at or store: IKEA's four furniture families, Google's Chairs/Beds/Tables/Cabinets & Storage, the AAT's seating/sleeping/support/storage, Wikipedia's Seating/Sleeping/tables/storage [S1, S16, S17, S27]. This is also the wording of the framework key ("to sit, lie, work at, display, or store").
2. **Room, as a second route.** Room appears at IKEA, Wayfair, Chairish, the Container Store, Otto, Segmüller and Pepperfry as an alternative way in [S1, S4, S5, S8–S10, S14]. Under Appendix A, room is a setting, so it is a view or tag. It cannot be a level because the same kind lives in several rooms (a bench, a chest, a desk).
3. **Size and fit.** For beds the size name is decided early and is national (Twin/Full/Queen/King in the US, Single to Super King in Australia, numeric 90/140/160/180 × 200 in Northern Europe, "piazza" in Italy) [S3, S11, S25]; ISO 24975 exists to make frames, bases and mattresses fit [S18]. Size is an attribute, not a level.
4. **Construction and material, second.** For mattresses the construction type (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) is the first thing guides explain [S21, S3, S8]; IKEA's own page says shoppers "prefer to start" with firmness, then sleeping position or material [S3]. For sofas John Lewis filters by cover and style [S7]. Trade codes split by material (wooden, metal, plastic) [S19]; Wikipedia keeps material in a separate list [S27]. Material is therefore an attribute, except for mattresses, where construction is the form.
5. **Style, period, maker.** These lead on resale sites only after the category (Seating, Tables, Case Pieces) is chosen: Mid-Century Modern, Art Deco, Creators, Period are facets [S12–S15].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the same four named levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form) and differ in the axis at the top.

### Candidate A (recommended): function first

Levels and one axis each:
- **Sub-family** — primary use named in the framework key: sit / lie / work at / store in furniture / hold and organise loose items / show and divide.
- **Leaf** — the family of object by form (for example sofas & settees, dining & side chairs, case furniture).
- **Kind** — a generic type by structure or mechanism (sofa, sectional sofa, sofa bed; platform bed, bunk bed; hybrid mattress).
- **Named form** — a distinct recognised design: a designer/maker model (Eames Lounge Chair, CH24 Wishbone), a product line (POÄNG, KALLAX) or a traditional named type (Chesterfield, Windsor, highboy, kaidan tansu).

Sub-families: B1.1 Seating; B1.2 Sleeping & lying (beds, mattresses, bed bases); B1.3 Tables & work surfaces; B1.4 Storage furniture (case furniture, shelving, chests); B1.5 Containers & organisers (boxes, baskets, bags, racks, hooks); B1.6 Display & division (stands, fittings, partitions, pop-up shelters).

### Candidate B: room first

Levels: room (living, bedroom, dining, study, bathroom, outdoor) > furniture type > kind > named form. Used by Otto, Segmüller, Pepperfry and the Furniture Row glossary [S4, S8, S9, S26]; also by IKEA's second route [S1].
- Example kinds under it: Living room > sofa, armchair, coffee table, TV unit, bookcase; Bedroom > bed frame, mattress, wardrobe, chest of drawers, nightstand; Dining > dining table, dining chair, sideboard, bench.
- Where it breaks: a desk or a bench belongs in four rooms; a chest of drawers is bedroom at one shop and hall at another; the framework treats room and setting as modifiers (Appendix A) and requires one home per object (S1); outdoor furniture appears as a branch of its own in Google [S16], but the framework files garden furniture by what it is. Edge objects: dining chair used as desk chair; bar cart; storage bench; bathroom vanity (Google: Vanities under Cabinets & Storage [S16]).

### Candidate C: retail hybrid (IKEA-style families)

Levels: family (Sofas & armchairs; Tables & chairs; Beds & mattresses; Desks & desk chairs; Storage) > type > kind > named form [S1, S2].
- Example kinds: sofa, sleeper sofa, armchair, ottoman | dining table, dining chair, bench, bar stool, high chair, step stool | bed, mattress, nightstand, headboard, bed slats, mattress foundation | desk, desk chair, gaming chair.
- Where it breaks: each family mixes axes (a sofa bed sits with sofas but a futon has its own branch elsewhere; chairs are tied to tables, desk chairs to desks, so "chair" has three homes; nightstands ride with beds while the storage family holds dressers). It is a marketing grouping of things bought together, not of what the object is. Edge objects: desk chair vs dining chair; nightstand; bed slats (a part, kept B1 under S3 since it is a bed part); mattress topper (B2).

**Rejected axis: material or construction first.** Trade codes (HS 9403 by wooden/metal/plastic and by office/kitchen/bedroom use) and Wikipedia's second list split this way [S19, S27]; the framework and Appendix A bar it (material is a modifier). Edge objects: a metal-framed upholstered sofa; a USM Haller steel-and-panel sideboard next to a wooden sideboard; a rattan chair next to a rattan table.

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds.** About 200 to 260 across the six sub-families: roughly 60 to 70 in seating, 25 to 30 in sleeping, 35 to 40 in tables, 50 to 60 in storage furniture, 25 to 35 in containers and organisers, and 10 to 15 in display and division. This is an estimate from the category lists of IKEA US (about 60 furniture and storage entries [S1]), Google (about 120 lines under Furniture, many of them accessories [S16]), Chairish (about 70 sub-types) [S14] and Wikipedia's lists [S27, S28]. Parts and accessories (table legs, replacement shelves, crib bumpers, sofa covers) are not kinds here; most follow S3 into the same category as the whole, or go to B2.

**Named forms.** Several hundred designs are the realistic first pass; thousands if every manufacturer product line is counted. Three reservoirs: designer classics (Wikipedia's list of chairs alone names a dozen or more designer chairs with designer and year [S28]; the makers' own pages add the CH22–CH26 series, CH07, CH445, CH468, CH101 [S30]); traditional named types (Windsor with its brace-back, Philadelphia, wheel-back, sack-back variants [S34]; Chesterfield; highboy and lowboy; tansu types and regional styles [S35, S36]); and retailer product lines (IKEA POÄNG, KALLAX, HEMNES, LISABO, VALEVÅG [S2, S3]). 1stDibs indexes "Designs" and "Design Series" as separate lists [S13].

**Where depth concentrates.** Seating (chairs have the most named designs and the oldest design-collector market [S12, S28, S30]); mattresses (comparison by construction, firmness and size [S3, S21, S22]); case furniture (traditional forms recorded to sub-sub-type level on resale sites: chests of drawers split into chiffoniers, commodes, highboys, lowboys [S14]).

**Breadth of leaves (rough).** Broad: dining & side chairs, occasional tables, cupboards & cabinets, shelving. Medium: sofas & settees, armchairs & lounge chairs, beds, mattresses, desks & work tables, boxes & baskets. Narrow: floor & soft seating, bed bases, folding & floor tables, racks & hooks, partitions.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers across items. Below that, distinctions are named forms or attributes.

**Recommended stop for B1: the use-type kind, with one exception.**
- **Use-type kinds** (sofa, dining chair, dining table, desk, chest of drawers, wardrobe, bookcase, storage box). Evidence: IKEA, Wayfair and Container Store navigate and filter at this grain, and attributes (seat count, cover, size, finish, number of drawers) sit in filters beside it [S1, S2, S5, S10]; John Lewis lets shoppers filter sofas by seat count, cover and style rather than creating more categories [S7]. Quality comparison in sofas and chairs runs on construction attributes (fabric class by Martindale rub count [S20]) that apply across kinds.
- **Mattresses go one step deeper, by construction**, because the type is where buyers compare. The Sleep Foundation's type page links separate "best" guides for innerspring, memory foam and latex mattresses [S21]; IKEA runs separate landing pages for spring, foam and hybrid [S3]; Segmüller separates Taschenfederkern, Kaltschaum, Gelschaum and Viscoschaum [S8]. The recommended kinds are the Sleep Foundation's five (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) [S21]. The finer German splits (pocket spring, cold foam, gel foam, viscoelastic) would be attributes under those kinds; this is an owner decision (section 10). Firmness and size are attributes, never kinds [S22, S23, S25].
- **Beds** stop at structure type (platform, panel, bunk, four-poster, daybed, cot) [S26, S27]; headboard height, size and finish are attributes. Named models (Wishbone, POÄNG, HEMNES) are forms.
- **Storage furniture** stops at the case type (chest of drawers, sideboard, wardrobe, bookcase); the antique market compares highboy against lowboy against chiffonier [S14], so those traditional names are recorded as named forms of the kind chest of drawers, and Chairish's sub-sub-types are the evidence to attest them.
- **Containers** stop at container form (box, basket, bag, rack, hook). Volume (the Container Store sells boxes by quart size [S10]) is an attribute.
- **Designer classics** are compared against their reproductions, so maker and licence status must be attached at the named-form level (Vitra: "sole authorised manufacturer" [S31]; Vitsœ: "exclusive worldwide licensee" [S32]).

**Where the choice level differs inside B1:** mattresses (construction level) versus everything else (use-type level); tansu and kotatsu are regional kinds that carry their own named forms (kaidan, kuruma, mizuya) because collectors compare type against type [S35, S36].

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: preference (price band) for the category as a whole, with three spec-ranked attributes carried as modifiers.** No source ranks sofas, tables or cabinets on one recognised tier scale.

- **Fabric durability: spec-ranked.** Martindale rub-count classes (ISO 12947): decorative under 10,000; light domestic 10,000–15,000; general domestic 15,000–25,000; heavy domestic or light contract 25,000–40,000; contract 40,000 and above; severe contract 100,000 and above. The US Wyzenbeek test (ASTM D4157) gives double rubs with no reliable conversion [S20]. This applies to upholstered kinds and is a modifier ("fabric class").
- **Mattress firmness: weakly consensus-ranked.** A 10-point scale is common (1 extra soft to 9–10 extra firm) but "there is no universally accepted measurement" [S22]; the German H1–H5 grades "are not standardised" and are matched to body weight [S23]; Wikipedia notes a CEN "Hs" scale used in a 2003 trial [S24]. Carry as a modifier with the scale name, never as a level.
- **Contract versus domestic grade.** The rub-count classes above and the "market (domestic, commercial, professional)" modifier of Appendix A cover it [S20].
- **Authenticity for designer classics.** Licensed original, vintage original, and reproduction are different things to collectors; the makers state their authorised status [S31, S32]. Carry as an attribute on named forms (not attested here for reproductions beyond these statements; owner decision whether to record it).
- **Price band** and **construction quality** (solid wood versus engineered wood, hardwood frame, spring system) appear in retailer text [S9] and should stay as attributes.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommendation: mixed.** Incidental for mass-market furniture; **identity** for designer classics, vintage and antique furniture; **platform** for modular systems.
- **Identity.** On resale sites the maker or designer is a primary filter: 1stDibs "Creators" (for example Cassina) and Chairish "Shop by Brand" [S12, S13, S14]; Pamono's furniture navigation is by Seating, Tables, Storage [S15]. Carl Hansen & Søn and Vitra sell designs by named designers and say who is authorised [S30, S31].
- **Platform.** USM states that "each new development can be seamlessly integrated into existing furniture" [S33]; Vitsœ calls 606 a "kit of parts" and is the exclusive licensee of Rams's designs [S32]. Mattress and bed-frame fit is a size standard rather than a brand (ISO 24975) [S18].
- **Incidental.** IKEA, Nitori and Pepperfry sell own-label lines where the brand is a named form, not a reason to own [S1, S2, S6, S9].

**Maker ledger: yes, but limited.** Rows for makers of designer classics and modular systems (for example Vitra, Carl Hansen & Søn, Vitsœ, USM), plus a small set of mattress and mass-market houses if the owner wants them: maker, known-for named forms, designer, country, era, licence status, scene tag, price band. Each row needs its own attested source. A scene or tier column is not needed for most rows.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommendation: canonical form first, then variants by structural step; named forms by design year.**
- Kinds: the plain generic type first (sofa), then structural variants in increasing complexity (sectional, sofa bed, futon). This matches how IKEA, John Lewis and the AAT list them [S1, S7, S17] and avoids a spec order, which only suits mattresses.
- Mattress kinds: the Sleep Foundation's order (innerspring, foam, hybrid, latex, airbed) [S21], with firmness shown as an attribute column on the 10-point scale [S22].
- Named forms: by year of design or first production. The makers publish the years (CH24 1949/1950, Lounge Chair 1956, Panton 1967, 606 1960, Haller 1965) [S29–S33]; traditional types that have no single date follow the dated designs, alphabetical among themselves.
- Reason: a canonical-first order puts the thing most people own first; chronology suits named forms, which are mostly historical works.

## 9. Boundaries

Neighbouring categories and the closest cross-boundary objects, each with a precedence sentence drawn from the framework or proposed for it:

1. **B2 Soft Furnishings.** *A mattress and its base are B1; a mattress topper, pad or protector, pillow, duvet and loose seat cushion are B2.* (Framework: mattress toppers, cushions and bedding are B2. IKEA sells toppers on its mattress page [S3]; Google files mattress protectors under Linens & Bedding [S16].) *A cushioned seat sold as a piece (ottoman, pouf, bean bag) is B1.* Edge: sofa cushion sold separately is B2.
2. **B4 Decor.** *Mirrors, vases, picture frames and ornaments are B4 even when they stand on furniture; a dressing table, vanity or console table is B1.* *A festival or decorative basket kept for storage is B1; a decoration is B4.* Edge: decorative chest, planter-stand.
3. **B8 Home Safety & Security.** *A safe or lockable security cabinet made to protect valuables is B8; a locker, filing cabinet or lockable cupboard made to store things is B1.* Edge: gun cabinet (E4 or B8 owner call), medicine cabinet.
4. **C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment.** *A tool box, tool chest or tool storage is C1; a household chest, cabinet or shelving unit is B1 whatever it holds (S3).* *A workbench or workshop trestle is C1; a desk, drafting table or craft table for general use is B1* (open question: the framework does not name workbenches).
5. **B5 Kitchen & Dining.** *Food storage containers, lunch bags, bread bins and pantry canisters are B5; a freestanding or fitted kitchen cabinet, pantry unit, island, trolley, dining table, dining chair and bar stool are B1.* Edge: wine rack (B1 per key), wine fridge (B5).
6. **B6 Cleaning & Laundry.** *A laundry basket or hamper is B6; a storage basket, shoe bag or box is B1.*
7. **B7 Climate, Water & Energy.** *A kotatsu or any table with a built-in heater is B1; a bath, basin or vanity basin is B7, but a bathroom vanity cabinet without a basin is B1.*
8. **C2 Garden & Grounds.** *Garden furniture, loungers and outdoor storage boxes are B1; planters and pots are C2; a plant stand is B1.*
9. **E1 Toys & Games / E2 Sports.** *A pool, snooker, table-tennis or darts table is E2; a card, poker or general game table is B1; play structures and toy furniture sold as toys are E1; a child-size chair, table or bed sold as furniture stays B1 (user age is a modifier).* *Camping sleeping bags and mats are E2; camp cots, camp chairs, folding tables and air mattresses sold as beds are B1.*
10. **H1/H2 Health.** *A hospital bed, examination table, dentist or barber chair and other furniture made to treat or examine a person are H2; an ordinary bed or chair made in an adapted form is B1; a chair made mainly to massage is H1, a recliner with added vibration is B1.* (HS splits medical and barber chairs from other seats [S19]; Google puts medical furniture and salon chairs under Business & Industrial [S16]. The framework names neither barber nor massage chairs, so those two are open questions.)
11. **B10 Animal Equipment / F4 Religious Objects.** *A dog bed, cat tree or pet cage is B10; a coffin, casket, altar or shrine is F4; a hope chest, blanket chest or trunk is B1.*
12. **A4 Bags & Accessories.** *Luggage, rucksacks and bags that travel with a person are A4; a storage bag or tote that stays in a home is B1 only when it holds several objects (S3), otherwise it follows the object.*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A, function-first, four levels below the category.** Sub-family (six: Seating; Sleeping & lying; Tables & work surfaces; Storage furniture; Containers & organisers; Display & division) > leaf (family of object by form) > kind (generic type by structure or mechanism, stopping at the use type; mattresses stop at construction) > named form (designer/maker model, product line or traditional named type). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Room, style, period, region, material, setting, user age, market, installation (fitted or freestanding), size, firmness and finish are tags, filters or generated views. A maker ledger covers makers of designer classics and modular systems.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. Approve the six sub-families and the function-first axis against room-first retail (Candidate B) or the IKEA-style families (Candidate C).
2. Home for convertibles: sofa bed, futon and daybed in Seating (retailers' majority) or Sleeping? A one-sentence rule is needed (suggested: filed by the default state it is sold in; sofa bed and futon sit in Seating, daybed in Sleeping).
3. Mattress grain: the Sleep Foundation's five types as kinds with pocket spring, cold foam, gel foam and viscoelastic as attributes, or the German finer split as kinds.
4. Traditional type names (Chesterfield, Windsor, highboy, lowboy, kaidan tansu): named forms under a generic kind (recommended), or kinds.
5. Nightstand home (storage furniture recommended; IKEA files it with beds, Google with tables).
6. Sets and suites (dining set, bedroom suite, bunk-bed-with-desk): recommended as an attribute ("sold as a set") on the pieces, not kinds, since the framework files a set by its greatest item.
7. Fitted cabinetry and built-in wardrobes: kinds with "installation" as a modifier (framework key puts them in B1).
8. Maker-ledger scope: designer-classic and modular-system makers only (recommended), or also mass-market and mattress houses.
9. Open boundary calls: barber and dentist chairs, massage chairs, workbenches, jewellery boxes, picnic baskets, gun cabinets, fitting rooms/shop fittings versus G2.
10. Whether to record authenticity (licensed original, vintage original, reproduction) on named forms.

**Gaps in this research (for honesty):**
- No owner-community forum or wiki was fetched (the buying guides and glossaries stand in); a later round should read a furniture or mattress-review community and a woodworking community.
- China and France are thin: Tmall/JD pages and the Chinese GB/T 3324 page could not be fetched (the codeofchina page returned an error), and no French retailer page was fetched, so kind lists for those markets rest only on the Japanese, Indian, German and Australian evidence.
- Otto, Wayfair and Muji returned little (top navigation or consent text only); the House Beautiful chair-styles article was refused to WebFetch and only its first paragraph came back through Parallel.
- Named-form counts and kind counts in section 4 are estimates from category lists, not counted catalogues.
