## 0. Category

**Code and name.** F6 Natural History & Specimens (domain F, Collectibles, Media & Records). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md`, category list and placement key step 7.

**Membership test (step 7).** "A **natural object, not made or shaped by people beyond cleaning, cutting, polishing or preserving, kept as a specimen** for study, display or collection: minerals, rocks, fossils, meteorites, shells, element samples, unset gemstones and loose pearls, skeletal and taxidermy specimens, pinned insects, herbarium sheets?"

**Notes bound to step 7.**
- Tanned hides and leather are C4 as material and B2 as rugs.
- Unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6 whether natural, cultured or laboratory-grown, rough or cut: origin is a modifier (Appendix A lists origin: natural, cultured, laboratory-grown).
- A natural material sold for a use is filed by that use: firewood C8, garden stone C2, sponges, loofahs and pumice H1, animal chews B10, craft feathers, fleece and craft shells C4, dried flowers B4, religious relics and rudraksha beads F4.

**Rules that touch F6.**
- Step 1 comes first: a living animal (including live feeder insects and eggs for hatching) is out of scope. Step 6 sends a living plant to C3, so only a dead, pressed or mounted plant can reach F6 (the C3 memo says the same, `C3.md` P9). Step 7 comes before step 8 (parts follow the whole, S3) and before step 23 (science-education kits, C6), so a mineral or rock teaching set is F6 (`C6.md` section 9 already treats minerals, fossils and pinned insects as specimens, not instruments).
- S2: a kind is a generic type; a model, cut or iconic design is a named form; an individual item is never a kind. A particular mineral specimen, fossil or meteorite fragment is an individual; "amethyst", "ammonite" or "Gibeon iron meteorite" are generic types.
- S3 and its "never parts" list: a stand or case that holds one object and stands with it is a part of that object; boxes, trays, cabinets and racks that hold several objects are furniture and storage, B1.
- S5: test the object, not the motive. A stone sold as a healing stone, a birthstone or a decor piece is the same object as one sold to a collector.
- S4 and Appendix A: region and culture, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, motive, origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), how acquired, material, condition and edition are modifiers and never levels.

**Neighbouring decisions already taken in other stage-2 memos.** A3 treats a mounted or strung gem or pearl as jewellery and a loose one as F6 (`A3.md` section 9, item 1). C4 treats loose beads, findings and drilled stones for stringing as C4 and asks the owner to confirm drilled gemstone and pearl beads (`C4.md`, open question 6). B10 says taxidermy and skeletal specimens are F6 (`B10.md` line 199).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

**They agree on the top split by substance.** Eight of the retail and market sources open with the same three or four material branches: FossilEra (fossils, minerals, meteorites; S1 to S3), eBay.de (mineral, fossil, meteorite; S4), Geolitho (fossil, mineral, rock, extraterrestrial; S6), Tokyo Science (fossil, mineral, rock, meteorite; S9), Le Comptoir (minerals, fossils, gems, meteorites; S10), Paleomarket (S30), Bonhams (fossils, meteorites, minerals; S26) and the museum (minerals, rocks, meteorites; S24). Nobody opens with price, region or size.

**Species is the second step.** Where a seller goes below the material branch it lists species or varieties: FossilEra minerals (about 207 categories; S2), N's Mineral (hundreds of species in gojuon order; S7), GemSelect (S16), Cornerstone (kinds of rock; S25), Bonhams (minerals "alphabetically"; S26).

**They split on these objects.**
1. *Gems.* eBay.de has Edelstein beside Mineral (S4); Le Comptoir has Gemmes as its own catalogue (S10); Tokyo Science and the NHM keep gems with minerals (S9, S24); Geolitho has no gem type at all (S6). GIA divides by grading scheme, not by material: diamond, coloured diamond, coloured stone, pearl (S15).
2. *Amber.* FossilEra, Bonhams and UK Fossils file amber with fossils (S1, S26, S29); N's Mineral lists blue amber among its mineral entries (S7).
3. *Ammolite.* FossilEra sells it in both the fossil list (80) and the mineral list (72; S1, S2).
4. *Healing stones.* eBay.de has Heilstein as a sibling of Mineral (S4); Le Comptoir has a dictionary of lithotherapy (S10); Bonhams names a "metaphysical minerals" collection (S26). This is a motive, and under S5 and Appendix A it is a tag, not a branch.
5. *Geodes, polished spheres, eggs and pendants.* Mineraly treats presentation (crystals, rocks, ornament, objects, jewellery) as the top level (S8); FossilEra treats it as a facet over species (S2). The same polished sphere is "Objets" in one and "Polished" in the other.
6. *Rocks.* Geolitho, Tokyo Science and the NHM keep rocks as their own branch (S6, S9, S24); FossilEra has none; Mineraly's "Roches" holds aggregates and raw pieces (S8).
7. *Shells and biological specimens.* They have no branch in the mineral and fossil shops. Bonhams files conchology, coralogy, zoology and Lepidoptera in one lot group with curiosities (S26); shell dealers run their own sites by mollusc family (S20); skulls and taxidermy sit under home decor on Etsy and under "oddities" at Oddities For Sale (S36, S22).
8. *Element samples.* Only the specialist shop divides them, by form (cube, ampoule, encased, display) and keeps bullion apart (S31).
