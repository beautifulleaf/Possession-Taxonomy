## 0. Category

**Code and name.** F6 Natural History & Specimens (domain F, Collectibles, Media & Records). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md`, category list and placement key step 7.

**Membership test (step 7).** "A **natural object, not made or shaped by people beyond cleaning, cutting, polishing or preserving, kept as a specimen** for study, display or collection: minerals, rocks, fossils, meteorites, shells, element samples, unset gemstones and loose pearls, skeletal and taxidermy specimens, pinned insects, herbarium sheets?"

**Notes bound to step 7.**
- Tanned hides and leather are C4 as material and B2 as rugs.
- Unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6 whether natural, cultured or laboratory-grown, rough or cut: origin is a modifier (Appendix A lists origin: natural, cultured, laboratory-grown).
- A natural material sold for a use is filed by that use: firewood C8, garden stone C2, sponges, loofahs and pumice H1, animal chews B10, craft feathers, fleece and craft shells C4, dried flowers B4, religious relics and rudraksha beads F4.

**Rules that touch F6.**
- Step 1 comes first: a living animal (including live feeder insects and eggs for hatching) is out of scope. Step 6 sends a living plant to C3, so only a dead, pressed or mounted plant can reach F6 (the C3 memo says the same, `C3.md` P9). Step 7 comes before step 8 (parts follow the whole, S3) and before step 23 (science-education kits, C6), so a mineral or rock teaching set is F6 (`C6.md` section 9 already treats minerals, fossils and pinned insects as specimens, not instruments).
- Rule S2: a kind is a generic type; a model, cut or iconic design is a named form; an individual item is never a kind. A particular mineral specimen, fossil or meteorite fragment is an individual; "amethyst", "ammonite" or "Gibeon iron meteorite" are generic types.
- Rule S3 and its "never parts" list: a stand or case that holds one object and stands with it is a part of that object; boxes, trays, cabinets and racks that hold several objects are furniture and storage, B1.
- Rule S5: test the object, not the motive. A stone sold as a healing stone, a birthstone or a decor piece is the same object as one sold to a collector.
- Rule S4 and Appendix A: region and culture, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, motive, origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), how acquired, material, condition and edition are modifiers and never levels.

**Neighbouring decisions already taken in other stage-2 memos.** A3 treats a mounted or strung gem or pearl as jewellery and a loose one as F6 (`A3.md` section 9, item 1). C4 treats loose beads, findings and drilled stones for stringing as C4 and asks the owner to confirm drilled gemstone and pearl beads (`C4.md`, open question 6). B10 says taxidermy and skeletal specimens are F6 (`B10.md` line 199).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

**They agree on the top split by substance.** Eight sources (seven sellers and one museum) open with the same three or four material branches: FossilEra (fossils, minerals, meteorites; S1 to S3), eBay.de (mineral, fossil, meteorite; S4), Geolitho (fossil, mineral, rock, extraterrestrial; S6), Tokyo Science (fossil, mineral, rock, meteorite; S9), Le Comptoir (minerals, fossils, gems, meteorites; S10), Paleomarket (S30), Bonhams (fossils, meteorites, minerals; S26) and the museum (minerals, rocks, meteorites; S24). Nobody opens with price, region or size.

**Species is the second step.** Where a seller goes below the material branch it lists species or varieties: FossilEra minerals (about 207 categories; S2), N's Mineral (hundreds of species in gojuon order; S7), GemSelect (S16), Cornerstone (kinds of rock; S25), Bonhams (minerals "alphabetically"; S26).

**They split on these objects.**
1. *Gems.* eBay.de has Edelstein beside Mineral (S4); Le Comptoir has Gemmes as its own catalogue (S10); Tokyo Science and the NHM keep gems with minerals (S9, S24); Geolitho has no gem type at all (S6). GIA divides by grading scheme, not by material: diamond, coloured diamond, coloured stone, pearl (S15).
2. *Amber.* FossilEra, Bonhams and UK Fossils file amber with fossils (S1, S26, S29); N's Mineral lists blue amber among its mineral entries (S7).
3. *Ammolite.* FossilEra sells it in both the fossil list (80) and the mineral list (72; S1, S2).
4. *Healing stones.* eBay.de has Heilstein as a sibling of Mineral (S4); Le Comptoir has a dictionary of lithotherapy (S10); Bonhams names a "metaphysical minerals" collection (S26). This is a motive, and under S5 and Appendix A it is a tag, not a branch.
5. *Geodes, polished spheres, eggs and pendants.* Mineraly treats presentation (crystals, rocks, ornament, objects, jewellery) as the top level (S8); FossilEra treats it as a facet over species (S2). The same polished sphere is "Objets" in one and "Polished" in the other.
6. *Rocks.* Geolitho, Tokyo Science and the NHM keep rocks as their own branch (S6, S9, S24); FossilEra has none; Mineraly's "Roches" holds aggregates and raw pieces (S8).
7. *Shells and biological specimens.* They have no branch in the mineral and fossil shops. Bonhams files conchology, coralogy, zoology and Lepidoptera in one lot group with curiosities (S26); shell dealers run their own sites by mollusc family (S20); skulls and taxidermy sit under home decor on Etsy and under "oddities" at Oddities For Sale (S36, S22).
8. *Element samples.* Only the specialist shop divides them, by form (cube, ampoule, encased, display) and keeps bullion apart (S31).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**First: what natural substance it is.** Every seller and museum above opens with a material branch (section 1.6). The same order shows in the specialist guides: the Bones Vault guide opens with "what counts as a natural history specimen" and lists taxidermy, osteology, fossils, ivory and marine specimens (S33); FossilEra's meteorite page carries a buyer's guide that divides meteorites into stony, iron and stony-iron (S3); GIA's report types are divided by object, diamond, coloured diamond, coloured stone, pearl (S15).

**Second: the species or trade name inside the branch.** Minerals are listed by species or variety (S2, S7, S25); gems by species with trade-name sub-types (S16, S19); fossils by organism group and then taxon (S1 links separate pages for Spinosaurus, raptor and Carcharodontosaurus teeth); shells by family (S20: 320 families, 6,839 species); meteorites by class and then named fall (S3, S27).

**Third: form, size and grade, and these differ by branch.**

| Branch | What buyers filter or ask next | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral specimens | presentation (specimen, polished, pendant), size class (thumbnail to large cabinet), locality, a seller aesthetic score | S2, S23, S7, S6 |
| Gemstones | form (single, pair, lot, faceted, cabochon, rough), carat, colour, clarity grade, shape, treatment, lab certificate, origin | S17, S16, S18, S19, S15 |
| Pearls | the seven GIA factors; natural or cultured; variety (South Sea, Tahitian, akoya, freshwater) | S14, S15 |
| Fossils | organism group, taxon, whether prepared or polished | S1, S29, S26 |
| Meteorites | class and named fall, whether a slice or a whole stone | S3, S27, S26 |
| Shells | family and species, size, habitat, star grade, collector or bulk use | S20, S21 |
| Biological specimens | object type (mount, skull, skeleton), animal group, legal status and provenance | S33, S34, S22 |

**What does not come first.** Price comes late (FossilEra and Paleomarket offer price-ceiling filters only: S2, S30). Region appears as locality inside a species (S7 lists fluorite by country and has a Tsumeb Mine group) and as a Japanese-minerals group, never as the top branch. Presentation is first only at Mineraly (S8); FossilEra makes it a facet over species (S2). Motive (healing, decor, gift, education) appears as a separate branch only at eBay.de (Heilstein; S4), as a reference section at Le Comptoir (a dictionary of lithotherapy; S10) and as the educational-gift framing of the Taobao set (S38); each sells the same objects that sit in the material branches.

**Conclusion.** Substance is the first axis; species or trade name is the second; presentation, size, grade, locality and origin are attributes. The single exception that the framework text forces is gems: unset gemstones and pearls are F6 whatever their material (step 7 note), so the gem sub-family has to be divided by the grading scheme (diamond, coloured stone, pearl; S15), because the material species cut across the other sub-families (corundum is a mineral specimen and also ruby and sapphire).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates use the fixed level names (C5a): category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, at most four levels below F6. Modifiers named in Appendix A (region, era, price or tier, maker, scene, user, setting, motive, origin, material unless it is the form, condition, edition) stay off the tree in every candidate.

### Candidate A. Substance first (recommended)

- **Sub-family (level 1).** Axis: *which natural substance the object is*, in museum-and-trade order (minerals, rocks, fossils, meteorites, then gems and pearls, shells, preserved biological specimens, element samples). Two helper sub-families sit outside that axis: study sets and lots, and mounts, stands and cases (section 10, open questions 3 and 4).
- **Leaf (level 2), one stated axis per sub-family (C5c).** Minerals: chemical class (Strunz, 10 classes; S13). Rocks: rock class by origin (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic; S25). Fossils: the kind of organism or trace the fossil was (invertebrate, vertebrate, plant, trace and microbial, fossil resin; S1, S26). Meteorites: none (about 12 kinds; C5b). Gemstones and pearls: the grading scheme the trade applies (diamond, coloured stone, pearl; S15). Shells: the kind of animal that made the hard part (gastropod, bivalve, other mollusc, echinoderm and coral). Preserved biological specimens: how the specimen is preserved (mount, skeleton, pinned or dried, pressed, encased). Element samples, study sets and mounts: none.
- **Kind (level 3).** The trade name at which sellers open a category page and buyers compare quality: a mineral species or major variety, a rock type, a fossil group, a meteorite class, a gem species or variety, a shell family or popular group, a specimen type.
- **Named form (level 4, never maker-bound, `maker_bound: no`).** A variety or trade name under a mineral or gem; a taxon under a fossil group; a named fall under a meteorite class; a species under a shell group.
- **Attributes, tags and ledgers (not levels).** Presentation (specimen, geode, polished, tumbled, sphere, slab), size class, locality and mine, geological age, origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), treatment, lab report, grade scheme, legal status, provenance (collection), scene tags.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. *Quartz family.* The IMA-maintained Strunz scheme files the silica minerals among the oxides, whereas Dana files them among the silicates **(to verify: S5 and S13 were fetched, but the placement of quartz was not read on either page)**. A buyer looks for amethyst, agate and jasper under silicates or quartz. Mitigation: a generated A to Z view and an alias table (C9).
2. *Rocks that are also gems or minerals.* Lapis lazuli is a rock that Cornerstone's metamorphic kit lists (S25) and a gem material sold by gem shops (S7 lists it among species). Turquoise and jade have the same problem. One home: Minerals if the seller treats it as a species (N's Mineral does, S7), with a Rocks alias.
3. *Ammolite and amber.* Ammolite is in both fossil and mineral lists at FossilEra (S1, S2); amber is a fossil at FossilEra and Bonhams and a mineral entry at N's Mineral (S1, S26, S7). Recommended home: fossils (fossil shell, fossil resin).
4. *A polished sphere, egg, pyramid, heart or lamp.* Mineraly lists them all as "Objets" (S8). A sphere is cut and polished and stays natural (step 7); a lamp or a carved animal is a made object.

### Candidate B. Museum department first

- **Sub-family.** Axis: *the curatorial discipline* that holds the object: Mineralogy (minerals, gems, rocks, meteorites, ores; S24), Palaeontology, Zoology (shells, skeletons, taxidermy), Entomology, Botany (herbarium). The customs heading uses the same words, "zoological, botanical, mineralogical, anatomical, palaeontological" (S11), and Bonhams' lot order mixes them (S26).
- **Leaf.** Within Mineralogy: minerals, gems, rocks, meteorites; within Zoology: skeletal, taxidermy, shells; and so on.
- **Kind and named form.** As in A.
- Example kinds (12): quartz, fluorite, diamond, granite, iron meteorite, ammonite, trilobite, cowrie shell, fox skull, taxidermy bird mount, pinned butterfly, herbarium sheet.

**Where it breaks.**
1. *Pearls and corals.* A pearl is a mollusc product; the NHM keeps gems with minerals (S24) and GIA treats pearls as gems (S15).
2. *Amber and petrified wood.* Fossil resin and agatised wood fit palaeontology, botany and mineralogy equally; sellers file both with fossils (S1, S26).
3. *Element samples* have no department, and teaching sets span three.
4. A buyer does not hold "a zoology object": the branch is a museum's staff structure, not what a seller or collector asks first (section 2).

### Candidate C. Presentation first

- **Sub-family.** Axis: *how the piece is presented*: crystals and clusters, geodes, raw rock, polished and ornamental stones, fossils, meteorites, sets. This is Mineraly's top level (S8) and FossilEra's first facets (S2).
- **Leaf.** Species. **Kind.** Specimen type. **Named form.** Variety or locality.
- Example kinds (10): crystal on matrix, geode half, polished slab, tumbled stone, sphere, egg, palm stone, tooth, trilobite slab, iron meteorite slice.

**Where it breaks.**
1. The same amethyst appears as a geode, a point, a polished slab and a sphere, so a species is split over four sub-families.
2. Presentation has no home for shells, skulls, taxidermy, pearls or cut gems.
3. "Objets" and "Bijoux" in S8 are made goods (lamps, carved animals, pendants) that step 7 and A3 send out of F6.
4. Two sellers file the same polished stone in different places: a sphere or egg is "Objets" at S8 and "Polished" at S2, so presentation is not a stable key.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule: a kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.** Finer distinctions are named forms or attributes. Findings are by sub-family; no "best X" guide or comparison list was fetched in this batch, so the evidence is category pages, filter facets and grading standards.

**Minerals: the kind is the species or the major variety.**
- Evidence: FossilEra opens a category for each species or variety and then filters by presentation and price (S2); N's Mineral lists species in alphabetical order and makes locality and habit separate groups (S7); Bonhams sells minerals alphabetically (S26); Gem Rock Auctions keeps display specimens of a species in a separate line from gem rough (S18).
- Below the kind: locality or mine (S7: Tsumeb Mine, fluorite by country), habit ("octahedral" fluorite; S7), presentation (S2), size class (S23) and the seller's aesthetic score (S6) are attributes or tags, not named forms.
- Variety rule proposed for the owner: a variety is a kind when fetched sellers in at least two regions give it a category of its own. Amethyst passes (S2 US, S7 JP); Herkimer diamond, smoky quartz and rutilated quartz are listed only by S7 and stay named forms under quartz until a second region attests them.

**Gemstones: the kind is the gem species or the variety with its own trade name; grading is an attribute.**
- Evidence: GemSelect makes each species its own page with form tabs and colour sections (S16, S17); TOP STONE opens a sapphire category with trade-name sub-types (S19); GIA reports are separated by object (S15). N's Mineral merges ruby and sapphire in one category (S7), which is the only fetched split.
- Below the kind: colour, carat, shape, clarity, cut, treatment, origin and report are attributes; trade names that describe a look (cornflower blue; S19) are named forms; origin names (Montana, Kashmir; S19) are tags.
- Pearls: the kind is the pearl variety (South Sea, Tahitian; S14); the seven value factors are attributes.

**Fossils: the kind is the organism group or the part that buyers search (ammonite, trilobite, shark tooth, dinosaur tooth, petrified wood).**
- Evidence: FossilEra (S1), UK Fossils (S29) and Bonhams (S26) all open at this level.
- Below the kind: the taxon is a named form where sellers open a page for it (S1 links pages for Spinosaurus, raptor and Carcharodontosaurus teeth); formation, locality, preparation and size are attributes or tags.

**Meteorites: the kind is the class (iron, pallasite, chondrite group, lunar, Martian, tektite).** Evidence: S3, S7, S27. A named fall is a named form (Gibeon, Campo del Cielo, Admire; S3, S26); a fragment or slice of it is an individual.

**Shells: the kind is the family or the popular group (cowrie, cone, scallop); the species is a named form.** Evidence: S20 sorts by family and shows species and a star grade for each lot; S21 opens species groups and separates documented collector shells from bulk shells. Species depth (6,839) is loaded only on demand.

**Preserved biological specimens: the kind is the specimen type by animal group (bird mount, mammal mount, mammal skull, pinned butterfly).** Evidence: S33's table and S22 and S36 titles. The species is an attribute; legal status and provenance matter more than species when buyers compare (S33, S34).

**Element samples: the kind is the physical form (ampoule, cube, encased, crystal).** The element itself is a closed list of 118 and is stored as an attribute, because buyers compare form and purity and then collect the periodic table (S31).

**Does the choice level differ by sub-family?** Yes. It is the species in minerals and gems, the organism group in fossils, the class in meteorites, the family in shells, and the specimen type in biological specimens. The section 4 counts follow this.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended type: incidental.** Nature is the maker. Nothing in the fetched sources makes a dealer or preparer part of what is sought.

- **Certifiers matter at the top end.** GIA names the grading standard (S15, S14), and Japanese listings carry other labs' certificates (S19). These are `lab_report` attributes, with a short list of labs.
- **Provenance outranks makers.** Bonhams sells a named private collection as a lot group (S26). A `provenance` field (collection, former owner) is enough.
- **Taxidermy is the one place a name is sought.** "Fine art and antique: named studios, Victorian pieces" is a separate price tier at the Bones Vault guide (S33). An optional `studio` field is enough for now.
- **No maker ledger** is recommended. If the owner wants one, use the C1 core columns for certifiers (with a new `maker_class` value, certifier) and for taxidermy studios; nothing else in F6 needs it. Brand-and-maker stays an Appendix A modifier.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Minerals.** Inside each Strunz class, order kinds by the Strunz division number where the standard gives one (S13) and then alphabetically by English name. Reason: sellers list minerals alphabetically (S7, S26), the IMA scheme fixes the class order, and an A to Z view is generated anyway.
- **Rocks, fossils (inside a leaf), shells, biological specimens.** Alphabetical by English name, after the canonical form: the ordinary type first (granite before the special granites; ammonite before ammolite). No maker or year applies.
- **Meteorites.** The class order of the AMNH list (S27): chondrites, primitive achondrites, achondrites, stony-iron, irons; named falls alphabetically.
- **Gemstones.** Diamonds, then coloured stones in the order corundum, beryl, other species alphabetically, then pearls; a secondary key is the grading scheme (S15).
- **Named forms.** Alphabetical, because no maker (C7) or reliable first-introduction year exists.
- **Variants.** None is needed in F6 (C3). Individual pieces are items, and an "edition" does not occur.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A, substance first.** Category F6 › sub-family (which natural substance) › leaf (an axis stated per sub-family) › kind (the trade name at which buyers compare) › named form (variety, taxon, named fall or species; never maker-bound). Maximum depth four levels below the category. The leaf is skipped where a sub-family has about 12 kinds or fewer (meteorites, elements, sets, mounts; C5b). Ten sub-families:

1. F6.1 Minerals (leaf: Strunz class)
2. F6.2 Rocks (leaf: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic)
3. F6.3 Fossils and fossil resin (leaf: invertebrate, vertebrate, plant, trace and microbial, resin)
4. F6.4 Meteorites and impact glass (no leaf)
5. F6.5 Gemstones and pearls, unset (leaf: diamond, coloured stone, pearl)
6. F6.6 Shells and marine hard parts (leaf: gastropod, bivalve, other mollusc, echinoderm and coral)
7. F6.7 Preserved biological specimens (leaf: mount, skeletal, pinned and dried, pressed, encased)
8. F6.8 Element samples (no leaf)
9. F6.9 Study sets and lots (no leaf)
10. F6.10 Mounts, stands and cases for one specimen (no leaf)

**Structures that are not levels:** presentation, size class, locality and mine, geological age, origin, treatment, lab report, grade scheme, legal status, provenance, and the scene tags of section 7.

**Conventions C1 to C9, applied.**
- **C1 maker ledger.** None, because makers are incidental (section 6). If wanted: C1 core columns for certifiers and taxidermy studios only.
- **C2 kind or named form.** Kind = trade name at the choice level; every named form is `maker_bound: no`. Traditional or regional objects (Japanese minerals, Itoigawa jadeite; S7) are tags, never a leaf.
- **C3 variants.** Not used.
- **C4 tiers and bands.** No tier on a kind; GIA scales and the size convention are attributes; seller scales are platform tags; USD bands as C4d.
- **C5 levels.** Fixed names; the leaf axes are stated in section 3; F6.10 is the optional parts sub-family and F6.9 is a second helper sub-family for the owner to approve.
- **C6 size.** Always an attribute (`size_class`, carat, millimetres); user does not apply.
- **C7 order.** Section 8.
- **C8 scenes.** Section 7 list, pending a domain-wide vocabulary.
- **C9 alias and homonym table (seed).** amethyst = アメシスト = 紫水晶 [S7]; quartz = 水晶 [S7]; fluorite = フローライト = 蛍石 [S7]; iron meteorite = 鉄隕石 [S7] (also written 隕鉄; to verify, seen only in a search excerpt of Tokyo Science); stony meteorite = 石質隕石 [S7]; pallasite = パラサイト隕石 [S7]; rock = Gestein [S6]; gemstone = Edelstein [S4]; healing stone = Heilstein, mapped to a tag [S4]; tumbled stones = pierres roulées [S10]; polished eggs and spheres = œufs et sphères polis [S10]; fossil fish = Ichthys [S26]. A naming risk: FossilEra lists "tiger iron stromatolite" pieces under a mineral address (S1 listings). Homonyms: *diamond* (gem diamond against Herkimer diamond, a quartz; S7), *garnet* (group, species and gem), *coral* (fossil coral against coral skeleton; S1, S26), *amber* (fossil resin against the colour), *jade* (nephrite, jadeite, carved jade), *pumice* (specimen against H1 product).
