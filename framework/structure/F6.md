## 0. Category

**Code and name.** F6 Natural History & Specimens (domain F, Collectibles, Media & Records). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md`, category list and placement key step 7.

**Membership test (step 7).** "A **natural object, not made or shaped by people beyond cleaning, cutting, polishing or preserving, kept as a specimen** for study, display or collection: minerals, rocks, fossils, meteorites, shells, element samples, unset gemstones and loose pearls, skeletal and taxidermy specimens, pinned insects, herbarium sheets?"

**Notes bound to step 7.**
- Tanned hides and leather are C4 as material and B2 as rugs.
- Unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6 whether natural, cultured or laboratory-grown, rough or cut: origin is a modifier (Appendix A lists origin: natural, cultured, laboratory-grown).
- A natural material sold for a use is filed by that use: firewood C8, garden stone C2, sponges, loofahs and pumice H1, animal chews B10, craft feathers, fleece and craft shells C4, dried flowers B4, religious relics and rudraksha beads F4.
- Added to the step 7 note by the stage-2 decisions (CATEGORIES_v0_7.md): a mount of one animal prepared as in life is F6 however it is presented, a work combining animal parts with other materials is F2 (K-76); a cast, replica or carved figure is filed by what it is, and a gem simulant is not a gemstone (K-77); a single shell with its species identified is F6, a craft assortment C4, a drilled pearl or gem bead C4, strung A3, undrilled loose F6 (K-78); human specimens are OUT, ivory and CITES goods stay in with their status in the C17 ledger (K-79); a preserved or emptied specimen kept as a specimen is F6 (K-80).

**Rules that touch F6.**
- Step 1 comes first: a living animal (including live feeder insects and eggs for hatching) is out of scope, and so are **human remains (ashes, bones, hair and teeth kept as remains)**. Step 6 sends a living plant to C3, so only a dead, pressed or mounted plant can reach F6 (the C3 memo says the same, `C3.md` P9). Step 7 comes before step 8 (parts follow the whole, S3) and before step 23 (science-education kits, C6), so a mineral or rock teaching set is F6 (`C6.md` section 9 already treats minerals, fossils and pinned insects as specimens, not instruments).
- Rule S2: a kind is a generic type; a model, cut or iconic design is a named form; an individual item is never a kind. A particular mineral specimen, fossil or meteorite fragment is an individual; "amethyst", "ammonite" or "Gibeon iron meteorite" are generic types.
- Rule S3 and its "never parts" list, with the holder sentence (K-9): a holder, case, sleeve or stand made for one object follows it into that category's parts sub-family; a box, tray, cabinet or album made to hold several objects is B1.
- Sets and kits (S3a, K-1): a set of objects of one kind is filed as that kind, with a `unit` or `quantity` attribute; a mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed; a kit sold, standardised and compared as one object is a kind, and S3a names the rock and mineral teaching set among them.
- Rule S5: test the object, not the motive. A stone sold as a healing stone, a birthstone or a decor piece is the same object as one sold to a collector.
- Rule S4 and Appendix A: region and culture, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, motive, origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), how acquired, material, condition and edition are modifiers and never levels.
- C16: certificates, condition grades, provenance and serial numbers of one particular object are not stored. C17: legal classes (CITES, ivory and wildlife-trade regimes) go in the jurisdiction ledger, never on the tree.

**Neighbouring decisions already taken in other stage-2 memos.** A3 treats a mounted or strung gem or pearl as jewellery and a loose one as F6 (`A3.md` section 9, item 1). C4 treats loose beads, findings and drilled stones for stringing as C4 and K-78 settles drilled gemstone and pearl beads as C4 (`C4.md`, open question 6). B10 says taxidermy and skeletal specimens are F6 (`B10.md` line 199). Review F (boundary table) records F3/F6 (native gold, bullion bar, element cube), F4/F6 (relics, rudraksha) and F6/A3, F6/C4 as agreed.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

**They agree on the top split by substance.** Eight sources (seven sellers and one museum) open with the same three or four material branches: FossilEra (fossils, minerals, meteorites; S1 to S3), eBay.de (mineral, fossil, meteorite; S4), Geolitho (fossil, mineral, rock, extraterrestrial; S6), Tokyo Science (fossil, mineral, rock, meteorite; S9), Le Comptoir (minerals, fossils, gems, meteorites; S10), Paleomarket (S30), Bonhams (fossils, meteorites, minerals; S26) and the museum (minerals, rocks, meteorites; S24). Nobody opens with price, region or size.

**Species is the second step.** Where a seller goes below the material branch it lists species or varieties: FossilEra minerals (about 207 categories; S2), N's Mineral (hundreds of species in gojuon order; S7), GemSelect (S16), Cornerstone (kinds of rock; S25), Bonhams (minerals "alphabetically"; S26).

**They split on these objects.**
1. *Gems.* eBay.de has Edelstein beside Mineral (S4); Le Comptoir has Gemmes as its own catalogue (S10); Tokyo Science and the NHM keep gems with minerals (S9, S24); Geolitho has no gem type at all (S6). GIA divides by grading scheme, not by material: diamond, coloured diamond, coloured stone, pearl (S15).
2. *Amber.* FossilEra, Bonhams and UK Fossils file amber with fossils (S1, S26, S29); N's Mineral lists blue amber among its mineral entries (S7).
3. *Ammolite.* FossilEra sells it in both the fossil list (80) and the mineral list (72; S1, S2).
4. *Healing stones.* eBay.de has Heilstein as a sibling of Mineral (S4); Le Comptoir has a dictionary of lithotherapy (S10); Bonhams names a "metaphysical minerals" collection (S26). This is a motive, and under framework rule S5 and Appendix A it is a tag, not a branch.
5. *Geodes, polished spheres, eggs and pendants.* Mineraly treats presentation (crystals, rocks, ornament, objects, jewellery) as the top level (S8); FossilEra treats it as a facet over species (S2). The same polished sphere is "Objets" in one and "Polished" in the other.
6. *Rocks.* Geolitho, Tokyo Science and the NHM keep rocks as their own branch (S6, S9, S24); FossilEra has none; Mineraly's "Roches" holds aggregates and raw pieces (S8).
7. *Shells and biological specimens.* They have no branch in the mineral and fossil shops. Bonhams files conchology, coralogy, zoology and Lepidoptera in one lot group with curiosities (S26); shell dealers run their own sites by mollusc family (S20); skulls and taxidermy sit under home decor on Etsy and under "oddities" at Oddities For Sale (S36, S22).
8. *Element samples.* Only the specialist shop divides them, by form (cube, ampoule, encased, display) and keeps bullion apart (S31).
9. *Rough against cut gem material.* Gem Rock Auctions keeps Cabochons, Pairs and Rough Raw Gemstones (facet-grade rough) in lines apart from Mineral Specimens of the same species (S18); GemSelect and its sapphire page sell each species as faceted, cabochon, pairs or lots (S16, S17). Sellers therefore already separate a cut or cutting-grade stone from a display crystal of the same species; this is the basis of the placement test in section 3.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**First: what natural substance it is.** Most sellers and the museum above open with a material branch (section 1.6); Mineraly (presentation) and Etsy (decor) are the exceptions. The same order shows in the specialist guides: the Bones Vault guide opens with "what counts as a natural history specimen" and lists taxidermy, osteology, fossils, ivory and marine specimens (S33); FossilEra's meteorite page carries a buyer's guide that divides meteorites into stony, iron and stony-iron (S3); GIA's report types are divided by object, diamond, coloured diamond, coloured stone, pearl (S15).

**Second: the species or trade name inside the branch.** Minerals are listed by species or variety (S2, S7, S25); gems by species with trade-name sub-types (S16, S19); fossils by organism group and then taxon (S1 links separate pages for Spinosaurus, raptor and Carcharodontosaurus teeth); shells by family (S20: 320 families, 6,839 species); meteorites by class and then named fall (S3, S27).

**Third: form, size and grade, and these differ by branch.**

| Branch | What buyers filter or ask next | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Mineral specimens | presentation (specimen, polished, pendant), size class (thumbnail to large cabinet), locality, a seller aesthetic score | S2, S23, S7, S6 |
| Gemstones | form (single, pair, lot, faceted, cabochon, rough), carat, colour, clarity grade, shape, treatment, lab certificate, origin | S17, S16, S18, S19, S15 |
| Pearls | the seven GIA factors; natural or cultured; variety (South Sea, Tahitian; akoya and freshwater †) | S14, S15 |
| Fossils | organism group, taxon, whether prepared or polished | S1, S29, S26 |
| Meteorites | class and named fall, whether a slice or a whole stone | S3, S27, S26 |
| Shells | family and species, size, habitat, star grade, collector or bulk use | S20, S21 |
| Biological specimens | object type (mount, skull, skeleton), animal group, legal class (a C17 ledger entry for the kind, not a value stored per object) | S33, S34, S22 |

**What does not come first.** Price comes late (FossilEra and Paleomarket offer price-ceiling filters only: S2, S30). Region appears as locality inside a species (S7 lists fluorite by country and has a Tsumeb Mine group) and as a Japanese-minerals group, never as the top branch. Presentation is first only at Mineraly (S8); FossilEra makes it a facet over species (S2). Motive (healing, decor, gift, education) appears as a separate branch only at eBay.de (Heilstein; S4), as a reference section at Le Comptoir (a dictionary of lithotherapy; S10) and as the educational-gift framing of the Taobao set (S38); each sells the same objects that sit in the material branches.

**Conclusion.** Substance is the first axis; species or trade name is the second; presentation, size, grade, locality and origin are attributes. The single exception that the framework text forces is gems: unset gemstones and pearls are F6 whatever their material (step 7 note), so the gem sub-family has to be divided by the grading scheme (diamond, coloured stone, pearl; S15), because the material species cut across the other sub-families (corundum is a mineral specimen and also ruby and sapphire). Because a species can then have two kinds, the line between the mineral sub-family and the gem sub-family is set by an object test, the cut state of the piece (section 3), not by the seller's channel.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates use the fixed level names (C5(a)): category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, at most four levels below F6. Modifiers named in Appendix A (region, era, price or tier, maker, scene, user, setting, motive, origin, material unless it is the form, condition, edition) stay off the tree in every candidate.

### Candidate A. Substance first (decided)

- **Sub-family (level 1).** Axis: *which natural substance the object is*, in museum-and-trade order: minerals, rocks, fossils, meteorites, then gems and pearls, shells and other marine hard parts, preserved biological specimens, element samples. That is eight sub-families on the one axis. The ninth, F6.10, is the closing sub-family "Parts and accessories made for one specimen" and is exempt from the level-1 axis (C5(d); review F, X6). The earlier helper sub-family "Study sets and lots" is removed (it sat outside the axis and C5(d) exempts only the parts sub-family); its id F6.9 is retired and not reused (review F6-5; section 10).
- **Leaf (level 2), one stated axis per sub-family (C5(c)).** Minerals: chemical class (Strunz, 10 classes; S13; decided in F-24, provisional until the quartz placement is read, †). Rocks: rock class by origin (igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic; S25), with the value "mixed classes" for a set that spans them. Fossils: the kind of organism or trace the fossil was (invertebrate, vertebrate, plant, trace and microbial, fossil resin; S1, S26). Meteorites: none (about 12 to 16 kinds; C5(b): `leaf = null`; if the count passes about 16 the leaf axis would be stony, stony-iron, iron; S3, S28). Gemstones and pearls: the grading scheme the trade applies (diamond, coloured stone, pearl; S15). Shells and other marine hard parts: the kind of animal that made the hard part (gastropod, bivalve, other mollusc, echinoderm and coral). Preserved biological specimens: how the specimen is preserved (mount, skeletal and other hard parts, pinned and dried, pressed, encased, fluid-preserved). Element samples and parts: none (`leaf = null`).
- **Kind (level 3).** The trade name at which sellers open a category page and buyers compare quality: a mineral species or major variety, a rock type, a fossil group, a meteorite class, a gem species or variety, a shell family or popular group, a specimen type.
- **Named form (level 4, never maker-bound, `maker_bound: no`).** A variety or trade name under a mineral or gem; a taxon under a fossil group; a named fall under a meteorite class; a species under a shell group; a pearl trade name under a pearl kind. Each carries a region tag where it is a regional name (C11).
- **Placement test between F6.1 Minerals and F6.5 Gemstones and pearls (review F6-1; decided in F-4).** *A loose stone that is faceted or cut en cabochon, or rough sold as cutting material by weight, is F6.5; every other piece (crystal, cluster, specimen on matrix, geode, tumbled stone, slab, sphere, egg) is F6.1 under its species, even when the species is a gem.* F6.5 then holds a kind for every gem species sold cut (amethyst, garnet, tourmaline, topaz and the rest). The F6.1 kind and the F6.5 kind of one species share one `species_id` and one alias row (C9): they are two kinds by an object test, not by a seller's channel, so no object has two homes. Cut state is therefore the one presentation value that decides a home; all other presentation values stay attributes. Amber and ammolite are the exception: they stay in F6.3 whatever their cut state (F-4). A drilled bead of any gem or pearl is C4 (K-78).
- **Attributes, tags and ledgers (not levels).** Facets: presentation (specimen, geode, polished, tumbled, sphere, slab), size class, locality and mine, geological age, origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), treatment, UV reaction (fluorescent or not: an attribute under C10, not a kind), form of a gem (single, pair, lot, faceted, cabochon, rough), `part` (tooth, bone, claw), `unit` or `quantity` (single, set), `strunz_code` on each mineral kind, motive tags (`use:`) and scene tags. Ledgers: the grading-scale registry (section 5), the C17 jurisdiction ledger (CITES and other wildlife and ivory regimes), the small certifier and studio rows of the maker ledger (section 6). Not stored at all (C16): lab report numbers, certificate contents, provenance and collection names, condition, the legal status of one particular piece.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. *Quartz family.* The IMA-maintained Strunz scheme files the silica minerals among the oxides, whereas Dana files them among the silicates **(†: S5 and S13 were fetched, but the placement of quartz was not read on either page)**. A buyer looks for amethyst, agate and jasper under silicates or quartz. Mitigation: each mineral kind carries its `strunz_code` so that two builders file it the same way once the placement is read; a generated A to Z view and an alias table (C9).
2. *Rocks that are also gems or minerals.* Lapis lazuli is a rock that Cornerstone's metamorphic kit lists (S25) and a gem material sold by gem shops (S7 lists it among species). Turquoise and jade have the same problem. One home for the uncut piece: Minerals if the seller treats it as a species (N's Mineral does, S7), with a Rocks alias; a cabochon or faceted piece goes to F6.5 under the placement test above.
3. *Ammolite and amber.* Ammolite is in both fossil and mineral lists at FossilEra (S1, S2); amber is a fossil at FossilEra and Bonhams and a mineral entry at N's Mineral (S1, S26, S7). Decided (F-4): both stay in F6.3 (fossil shell, fossil resin), rough or cut, so a cabochon of amber or ammolite is F6.3 and not F6.5; the cut-state test does not apply to them.
4. *A polished sphere, egg, pyramid, heart or lamp.* Mineraly lists them all as "Objets" (S8). A sphere, egg, slab, palm stone or tumbled stone is cut and polished and stays natural (step 7, F6.1); a stone lamp (B3), a carved animal (F2 or B4) and a pendant (A3) are made objects and leave F6 (F-25).
5. *A species with two kinds.* Amethyst, garnet, tourmaline, topaz and corundum occur in F6.1 (crystals, specimens) and F6.5 (cut stones, cutting rough). A ruby crystal in marble matrix is F6.1 Corundum; a facet-grade tourmaline rough sold by weight is F6.5 Tourmaline; a faceted amethyst is F6.5 Amethyst; a tumbled garnet is F6.1 Garnet.

### Candidate B. Museum department first

- **Sub-family.** Axis: *the curatorial discipline* that holds the object: Mineralogy (minerals, gems, rocks, meteorites, ores; S24), Palaeontology, Zoology (shells, skeletons, taxidermy), Entomology, Botany (herbarium). The customs heading uses the same words, "zoological, botanical, mineralogical, anatomical, palaeontological" (S11), and Bonhams' lot order mixes them (S26).
- **Leaf.** Within Mineralogy: minerals, gems, rocks, meteorites; within Zoology: skeletal, taxidermy, shells; and so on.
- **Kind and named form.** As in A.
- Example kinds (12): quartz, fluorite, diamond, granite, iron meteorite, ammonite, trilobite, cowrie shell, fox skull, taxidermy bird mount, pinned butterfly, herbarium sheet †.

**Where it breaks.**
1. *Pearls and corals.* A pearl is a mollusc product; the NHM keeps gems with minerals (S24) and GIA treats pearls as gems (S15).
2. *Amber and petrified wood.* Fossil resin and agatised wood fit palaeontology, botany and mineralogy equally; sellers file both with fossils (S1, S26).
3. *Element samples* have no department, and teaching sets span three.
4. A buyer does not hold "a zoology object": the branch is a museum's staff structure, not what a seller or collector asks first (section 2).

### Candidate C. Presentation first

- **Sub-family.** Axis: *how the piece is presented*: crystals and clusters, geodes, raw rock, polished and ornamental stones, fossils, meteorites, sets. This is Mineraly's top level (S8) and FossilEra's first facets (S2).
- **Leaf.** Species. **Kind.** Specimen type. **Named form.** Variety or locality.
- Example kinds (10): crystal on matrix, geode half, polished slab, tumbled stone, sphere, egg, palm stone, tooth, trilobite slab, iron meteorite slice.

**Where it breaks.**
1. The same amethyst appears as a geode, a point, a polished slab and a sphere, so a species is split over four sub-families.
2. Presentation has no home for shells, skulls, taxidermy, pearls or cut gems.
3. "Objets" and "Bijoux" in S8 are made goods (lamps, carved animals, pendants) that step 7 and A3 send out of F6.
4. Two sellers file the same polished stone in different places: a sphere or egg is "Objets" at S8 and "Polished" at S2, so presentation is not a stable key.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule: a kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.** Finer distinctions are named forms or attributes. Findings are by sub-family; no "best X" guide or comparison list was fetched in this batch, so the evidence is category pages, filter facets and grading standards.

**Minerals: the kind is the species or the major variety, uncut.**
- Evidence: FossilEra opens a category for each species or variety and then filters by presentation and price (S2); N's Mineral lists species in alphabetical order and makes locality and habit separate groups (S7); Bonhams sells minerals alphabetically (S26); Gem Rock Auctions keeps display specimens of a species in a separate line from gem rough (S18).
- Below the kind: locality or mine (S7: Tsumeb Mine, fluorite by country), habit ("octahedral" fluorite; S7), presentation (S2), size class (S23) and the seller's aesthetic score (S6) are attributes or tags, not named forms.
- Variety rule (C11 level tie-break, CV-27): a variety is a kind when fetched sellers in two regions give it a category of its own and no generic kind covers its form; otherwise it is a named form and still in. Two regions decide level only, never inclusion. Amethyst passes (S2 US, S7 JP). Decided (F-23): amethyst, agate, jasper and opal are kinds, not named forms of quartz, because buyers compare them separately; the clause "no generic kind covers its form" is read as "a form that buyers compare separately", not as "the same chemistry and crystal form". Herkimer diamond, smoky quartz and rutilated quartz are listed only by S7: they are named forms under quartz, in on that one page, and become kinds only if sellers in a second region give them categories of their own.

**Gemstones: the kind is the gem species or the variety with its own trade name, for a cut stone or cutting rough; grading is an attribute.**
- Evidence: GemSelect makes each species its own page with form tabs and colour sections (S16, S17); TOP STONE opens a sapphire category with trade-name sub-types (S19); GIA reports are separated by object (S15); Gem Rock Auctions sells cabochons, pairs and facet-grade rough in lines apart from display specimens (S18). N's Mineral merges ruby and sapphire in one category (S7), so ruby as a kind of its own is not yet attested in two regions (†; this decides level only, CV-27).
- Below the kind: colour, carat, shape, clarity, cut, treatment and report type are attributes; trade names that describe a look (cornflower blue; S19) are named forms; locality names (Montana, Kashmir; S19) are region tags. The placement test of section 3 decides whether a piece of a gem species is F6.1 or F6.5.
- Diamond against fancy-colour diamond are two kinds (F-23) because GIA grades them on separate reports and scales (S15).
- Pearls: the kinds are saltwater and freshwater pearl (F-24, provisional until attested, †); the pearl trade names (South Sea, Tahitian; S14; Akoya, keshi, Kasumiga †) are non-maker named forms with region tags (C11); natural or cultured is an attribute; the seven value factors are attributes.

**Fossils: the kind is the organism group or the part that buyers search (ammonite, trilobite, shark tooth, dinosaur fossil, petrified wood).**
- Evidence: FossilEra (S1), UK Fossils (S29) and Bonhams (S26) all open at this level.
- Below the kind: the taxon is a named form where sellers open a page for it (S1 links pages for Spinosaurus, raptor and Carcharodontosaurus teeth); `part` (tooth, bone), formation, locality, preparation and size are attributes or tags.

**Meteorites: the kind is the class (iron, pallasite, chondrite group, lunar, Martian, tektite and impactite).** Evidence: S3, S7, S27. A named fall is a named form (Gibeon, Campo del Cielo, Admire; S3, S26); a fragment or slice of it is an individual.

**Shells and other marine hard parts: the kind is the family or the popular group (cowrie, cone, scallop; coral skeleton, sea urchin test †); the species is a named form.** Evidence: S20 sorts by family and shows species and a star grade for each lot; S21 opens species groups and separates documented collector shells from bulk shells. Species are named forms, harvested in stage 3 and uncapped (F-26, CV-7).

**Preserved biological specimens: the kind is the specimen type, by animal group where sellers split it (bird mount, mammal mount, mammal skull, pinned butterfly).** Evidence: S33's table and S22 and S36 titles. The species is an attribute; legal class (a C17 ledger entry) matters more than species when buyers compare (S33, S34). Fluid-preserved, egg, antler, jaw, tusk and feather kinds are held until a seller page shows them (†).

**Element samples: the kind is the physical form (ampoule, cube, encased, crystal).** The element itself is a closed list of 118 and is stored as an attribute (F-26), because buyers compare form and purity and then collect the periodic table (S31).

**Does the choice level differ by sub-family?** Yes. It is the species in minerals and gems, the organism group in fossils, the class in meteorites, the family in shells, and the specimen type in biological specimens. The section 4 counts follow this.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Type: incidental.** Nature is the maker. Nothing in the fetched sources makes a dealer or preparer part of what is sought.

- **Certifiers matter at the top end.** GIA names the grading standard (S15, S14), and Japanese listings carry other labs' certificates (S19, which names LOTUS and others). The labs are maker rows with `maker_class` = `certifier or grader` (the C1 class list in CONVENTIONS_v1; `maker_class` is multi-valued); the standards they apply are registry rows (section 5). A lab report of one stone is never stored (C16).
- **Provenance is not stored.** A named collection (Bonhams sells one as a lot group, S26) is a fact about an individual lot (C16). Nothing in F6 needs a provenance field. The earlier `provenance` and `lab_report` attributes are withdrawn.
- **Taxidermy is the one place a name is sought.** "Fine art and antique: named studios, Victorian pieces" is a separate price tier at the Bones Vault guide (S33). Studios are maker rows with `maker_class` = `taxidermy studio`; `known_for_kinds` is derived from the named-form ledger and is not stored (C1). The earlier plain `studio` field is withdrawn (a plain maker column on a named form duplicates the ledger, C1). A studio tier scheme (an editorial class, C4(c) iii) enters on one fetched source, S33 being the candidate; a second source from another region would add weight only (C4(e); †).
- **Ledger scope.** The F6 rows of the one domain-wide maker ledger hold the two classes above, uncapped, one fetched page admitting a row (CV-2, CV-5), with the C1 core columns and the optional extensions `authority_id` (not needed here) and `certifications` (not needed here). If one fetched page shows a lapidary, fossil preparer, mineral dealer or similar community, it takes an existing class ("artisan or atelier" or "independent") and is never dropped or made a kind. There are no line, tier or price-band rows unless a source supports them. The shared maker schema and the two classes are decided (CV-2). Brand-and-maker stays an Appendix A modifier. Issuing authorities (the issuer table of F3 and F5) do not apply.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Minerals.** Leaves follow the Strunz class order of S13. Inside each class, order kinds by the Strunz division number where the standard gives one (S13; the division numbers were not read, †) and then alphabetically by English name. Reason: sellers list minerals alphabetically (S7, S26), the IMA scheme fixes the class order, and an A to Z view is generated anyway.
- **Rocks, fossils (inside a leaf), shells, biological specimens.** Alphabetical by English name, after the canonical form: the ordinary type first (granite before the special granites; ammonite before ammolite). No maker or year applies (C7).
- **Meteorites.** The class order of the AMNH list (S27): chondrites, primitive achondrites, achondrites, stony-iron, irons; named falls alphabetically.
- **Gemstones.** Diamonds, then coloured stones in the order corundum, beryl, other species alphabetically, then pearls; a secondary key is the grading scheme (S15). In a coloured-stone species, the kind comes first and its named forms alphabetically.
- **Named forms.** These are all non-maker, so C7 (CONVENTIONS_v1) puts first-attested year first and alphabetical second; no first-introduction year is attested for any F6 named form, so they are alphabetical.
- **Variants.** None is needed in F6 (C3). Individual pieces are items, and an "edition" does not occur.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure: Candidate A, substance first.** Category F6 › sub-family (which natural substance) › leaf (an axis stated per sub-family) › kind (the trade name at which buyers compare) › named form (variety, taxon, named fall, species or pearl trade name; never maker-bound; uncapped, one fetched page each). Maximum depth is four levels below the category, and the leaf is skipped where a sub-family has about 12 kinds or fewer (meteorites, elements, parts; stored as `leaf = null`). A loose faceted, cabochon or cutting-rough stone is F6.5 and every other mineral piece is F6.1, except amber and ammolite, which stay in F6.3 (F-4); an unworked stone or fossil is F6 whatever it is kept for (F-17); amethyst, agate, jasper and opal are kinds, and diamond and fancy-colour diamond are two kinds (F-23); pearls are saltwater and freshwater kinds with region-tagged named forms (F-24); the rock and mineral teaching set is a kit kind (S3a); simulants, stone lamps, carved animals, pendants and replicas leave (K-77, F-25). Nine sub-families, F6.9 retired:

1. F6.1 Minerals (leaf: Strunz class)
2. F6.2 Rocks (leaf: igneous, sedimentary, metamorphic, mixed classes)
3. F6.3 Fossils and fossil resin (leaf: invertebrate, vertebrate, plant, trace and microbial, resin)
4. F6.4 Meteorites and impact glass (no leaf)
5. F6.5 Gemstones and pearls, unset (leaf: diamond, coloured stone, pearl; placement test against F6.1 by cut state)
6. F6.6 Shells and other marine hard parts (leaf: gastropod, bivalve, other mollusc, echinoderm and coral)
7. F6.7 Preserved biological specimens (leaf: mount, skeletal and other hard parts, pinned and dried, pressed, encased, fluid-preserved)
8. F6.8 Element samples (no leaf)
9. F6.10 Parts and accessories made for one specimen (C5(d); no leaf)

**Structures that are not levels:** presentation, size class, locality and mine, geological age, origin, treatment, UV reaction, form of a gem, part, quantity, the scale registry, the C17 ledger, and the motive and scene tags of section 7.

**Conventions C1 to C17, applied.**
- **C1 maker ledger.** Certifier or grader and taxidermy studio rows, uncapped, one fetched page each, with the C1 core columns and multi-valued `maker_class` (CONVENTIONS_v1). No line, tier or price-band rows unless a source supports them. Section 6.
- **C2 kind or named form.** Kind = trade name at the choice level; every named form is `maker_bound: no`. Traditional or regional objects (Japanese minerals, Itoigawa jadeite; S7) are tags, never a leaf; pearl trade names are named forms with region tags (C11).
- **C3 variants.** Not used.
- **C4 tiers and bands.** No tier on a kind; GIA scales and the size convention are attributes with registry rows; seller scales are dated platform tags; USD bands as C4(d) where a dated list price exists.
- **C5 levels.** Fixed names; the leaf axes are stated in section 3; F6.10 is the C5(d) parts sub-family; the former helper sub-family F6.9 is removed.
- **C6 size.** Always an attribute (`size_class`, carat, millimetres). A size class names a kind only when it changes what the object can be used with and a fetched source sells the sizes apart; nothing in F6 meets that test. User does not apply.
- **C7 order.** Section 8.
- **C8 scenes.** Section 7; every tag is held back until a definition source is fetched.
- **C9 alias and homonym table (seed).** Rows give `term`, language and script, target and relation (alias, regional name or homonym). Aliases: amethyst = アメシスト = 紫水晶 [S7]; quartz = 水晶 [S7]; fluorite = フローライト = 蛍石 [S7]; iron meteorite = 鉄隕石 [S7] (also written 隕鉄, †); stony meteorite = 石質隕石 [S7]; pallasite = パラサイト隕石 [S7]; spessartine = spessartite [S7, S16]. Regional names: rock = Gestein [S6]; gemstone = Edelstein [S4]; healing stone = Heilstein, mapped to a tag [S4]; tumbled stones = pierres roulées [S10]; polished eggs and spheres = œufs et sphères polis [S10]; fossil fish = Ichthys [S26]. Same species, two kinds: each F6.1 species kind and its F6.5 cut-stone kind share one `species_id`. A naming risk: FossilEra lists "tiger iron stromatolite" pieces under a mineral address (S1 listings). Homonyms: *diamond* (gem diamond against Herkimer diamond, a quartz; S7), *garnet* (group, species and gem), *coral* (fossil coral against coral skeleton; S1, S26), *amber* (fossil resin against the colour), *jade* (nephrite, jadeite, carved jade), *pumice* (specimen against H1 product), *pearl* (the object against the colour).
- **C10 technology.** UV reaction, fluorescence, treatment and laboratory growth are attributes, never kinds.
- **C11 regional names.** As C2; Itoigawa jadeite and Herkimer diamond are tag and named form; pearl trade names are named forms with region tags.
- **C12 mounting, C13 compatibility.** Not used.
- **C14 multi-use kinds.** Not used: a species with a F6.1 kind and a F6.5 kind is two kinds by an object test (cut state), not one multi-use kind.
- **C15 toy or real.** Fossil and skull replicas follow it: E1 if made as a toy or model kit, F2 if a sculpture (K-77).
- **C16 individual-item facts.** No report numbers, certificates, grades, provenance, collection names, condition or serial numbers are stored.
- **C17 jurisdiction ledger.** CITES, ivory and wildlife-trade classes are ledger rows by species kind or named form (source S34; further regimes †), never a tag on the tree. A customs heading (S11) is evidence only.

**Remaining risks.**
1. The quartz-family placement under Strunz has not been read (†): the order of the Oxides and Silicates leaves and the `strunz_code` values are provisional until attested (F-24).
2. The saltwater and freshwater division of pearls has no fetched page (†): the two pearl kinds are provisional until attested (F-24).
3. F6.4 Meteorites holds about 12 to 16 kinds, on the edge of the C5(b) leaf skip; if more are attested, the leaf axis is stony, stony-iron, iron.
4. A gem species has two kinds (F6.1 and F6.5) separated by cut state, with amber and ammolite as the stated exception; the share of species that need both is not counted.
5. The level of ruby, Herkimer diamond, smoky quartz and rutilated quartz (kind or named form) is provisional until sellers in a second region are seen (CV-27); all are in on one fetched page.
6. The kind that the salagrama named form sits under (ammonite is the working home) and the page that shows it sold are unattested (†).
7. Depth is large and uncapped: 6,240 mineral species and 6,839 shell species at one dealer; the counts of section 4 are estimates of the first harvest, so F6 may exceed 900 kinds.
8. The C17 ledger has only CITES (S34) fetched; ivory and other wildlife regimes are unattested (†).
9. The `fetch_status` values "failed" for S8 and S24 are automatic and appear wrong; the tool's pattern needs a fix before the column is relied on.
10. The mixed-class leaf of F6.2 holds the teaching kit although a kit can include fossils and minerals; the leaf axis is rock class and the kit is its one "mixed classes" kind.
