## 0. Category

**Code and name.** A1 Clothing (domain A, Worn & Carried).

**Membership test (placement key, step 17, first branch that fits after footwear is tried).** An object *worn on the body, or carried on the person or in hand as a personal accessory*, that is "a garment, headwear, glove, scarf, tie, belt, hosiery, uniform, costume, wearable baby sleeping bag, or **protective clothing, helmets and personal protective equipment made to protect the wearer from a hazard** (hard hat, motorcycle and cycling helmets and motorcycle riding gear, welding helmet, chainsaw trousers, safety glasses, respirator, ear defenders, hi-vis, life jacket)". Category line: "garments and wearable accessories of any material: everyday, traditional and subculture dress, underwear, uniforms, costume, team and fan jerseys, protective clothing, helmets and personal protective equipment."

**Precedence sentences in the framework that touch A1.**
- Step 17 note: a device whose main function is computing, communication, sensing or media (smartwatch, fitness tracker, earbuds) goes to D (step 24). A garment, shoe or accessory with heating or lights stays here.
- Step 17 note: wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties, plain clips and bands are H1.
- Step 17 note ("made for a sport"): garments made for the demands of one sport, dance or outdoor activity and not suited to everyday wear (ski jackets with powder skirts, swimming costumes, wetsuits, padded cycling shorts, equestrian riding hats, sport protective guards, fencing and kendo gear) go to E2. Running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers, leggings, team and fan jerseys and outdoor jackets stay here.
- Step 17 branch order: footwear is A2 before clothing is tried; ornaments worn on body, hair or clothing, including badges and patches worn as ornament, are A3; bags, wallets, umbrellas, non-corrective eyewear, hand fans, keyrings, fashion canes, baby carriers are A4. Tassels and cords worn on a cap or gown are A1 (A3 note).
- Step 9 note: garments of every kind, including lingerie and fetish wear, are A1 (H3 is for objects made for sexual stimulation or restraint).
- Step 14 note: religious dress and vestments are A1; objects for rites are F4. Occasion dress (wedding, christening, graduation) is filed as what it is: occasion is a modifier.
- Step 25 note: costumes are A1; toy crowns, wands, play make-up and other dress-up accessories made as toys are E1.
- Step 2 note: spectacles and contact lenses that correct vision, medical braces and supports are H2; an ordinary garment made in an adapted form for a disabled user is filed as that garment (adapted is a modifier).
- Step 10: armour made to protect against weapons is E4; protective clothing against hazards is A1.
- Step 28 and S3: fabric, leather as material, and notions sold loose (buttons, zips) are C4; towels, throws and swaddling cloths are B2.
- S4 / Appendix A: region, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, user (baby, child, adult, gender, size, ability), setting, market, occasion, motive, origin, how acquired, material, condition and edition never split an inner level; they become tags, views or ledgers.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects

- **Agree.** Garment form (jacket, trousers, dress) is the branch at the leaf; gender is the first split in shopping but never a branch in reference works; brand, size, colour, price are facets.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence of order.**
1. *Department or user first on every mainstream site.* VT (Women, Men, Designer, Kids), GR (Menswear, Womenswear), AS, ZA, UQ, ZZ and MC (レディース/メンズ) all open with it. The framework treats user as a modifier, so this becomes a generated view.
2. *Garment family and leaf second.* Every retail tree then branches on form (above).
3. *Fit and size third.* ZZ's kodawari filters start with sleeve length and length; ZA's top filters are Brand, Colour, Size, Price, Material, Length, Fit; MS puts top length first among filters then adds fit; VT2 puts Category, Size, Brand before Condition. Heddels' separate beginner's buying guide (https://www.heddels.com/buying-your-first-pair-of-raw-denim-the-beginners-guide, read only as a search excerpt, not fetched; lead) frames the order as: choose your silhouette, know your measurements, sanforized or not, pick the size.
4. *Maker or scene first for enthusiasts.* HD and DH open with makers; CC opens with Brand List; LR opens with substyle; GR's brand directory and Market facet run beside Category; MFA keeps scene guides apart from item guides.
5. *Spec for technical garments.* REI's guide says down is graded by fill power, a loft measurement, with fill weight the second number; GG says wool suiting is graded by Super numbers; DH/HD cite denim weight in ounces.

**Reading.** Buyers decide *form* first (what garment am I shopping for), then *fit and size*, then *maker or scene* where the market is identity-driven, then *spec* for technical or heritage garments. The scene or maker can pre-empt form only for people already inside a scene, and even there they buy a form from a maker (Lot. 1001 jeans from Warehouse). Form is therefore the stable branching axis; maker and scene are second-axis tags and ledgers.

### Candidate A (recommended): garment form in four levels

| Level | Axis (one per level, stated per sub-family under C5(c)) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | **Coverage and layer**, with the written tie-break below | Tops; Bottoms; Dresses, one-piece garments and sets; Outerwear; Underwear, hosiery, sleepwear and swimwear; Headwear and head protection; Hands, neck and waist wear; and, last and exempt from the axis (C5(d)), Parts and accessories made for one garment |
| Leaf | **Garment form** (the silhouette family a buyer names first); the leaf axis of each sub-family is in the table below | Jeans; Trousers; Jackets; Coats; Draped and wrapped garments |
| Kind | **Cut or type** that buyers treat as a different garment and compare on quality or maker (C2(a)) | Harrington jacket; chore coat; MA-1 flight jacket; kimono |
| Named form | A specific design below a kind, **maker-bound** (a model or line) or **not maker-bound** (a generic cut or traditional variant), field `maker_bound: yes \| no` (C2). Never an individual item, a specification, a print or a colourway | Levi's 501 (yes); Warehouse & Co. Lot. 1001 (yes); Dickies 874 (yes); Shetland sweater (no); furisode (no) |

**Tie-break written into the level-1 definition (review F3).** The words "coverage and layer" are read as follows.
- **Outerwear** = a garment made to be worn over other clothing and fastened or opened at the front (jacket, coat, vest, waistcoat), or made in cape form. Everything else is filed by coverage (Tops, Bottoms, Dresses and sets).
- **Knitwear exception.** A pullover sweater, a cardigan and any knitted vest are Knitwear (1.3) whatever their front. This is an editor's clause that closes the review's own cardigan and knit-vest cases; it does not widen Outerwear.
- **Shirt-like garments.** A garment sold under both names (shacket, overshirt) is Tops (1.2) if it has shirt construction (collar, yoke, button cuffs), else Outerwear. A zip-front fleece is Outerwear (4.1); a pullover fleece has no front opening and is Tops (1.4).
- **Underwear** = made to be worn under other clothing. A corset or bustier made to be worn visible is a top (kind "corset top", 1.1); a foundation corset is Underwear (5.1).
- **Swimwear and beachwear** = made to be worn in or beside water and not made for the demands of one sport (K-126): swimsuits, bikinis, swim trunks and beach cover-ups. Competition and sport swimwear (racing suits) and wetsuits are E2. Swimwear sits in leaf 5.4 whatever its coverage.

**Rules stated with the tree.**
1. **Name kinds by form, not by material (review F5).** The puffer jacket, puffer coat and puffer vest carry `fill: down | synthetic` and `fill_power`; the trucker jacket carries `fabric: denim | corduroy | …`; the rider's jacket carries `material: leather | …`. **Stated exception:** "fleece jacket" and "fleece pullover" keep the material in the name because the material is the form (a napped synthetic pile garment). Record this in each kind's definition.
2. **Jackets against coats by length (review F6).** A kind sits by its canonical length: hip length or shorter is 4.1, below the hip is 4.2. A kind keeps its home even when one model is longer or shorter. The puffer jacket is 4.1 and the puffer coat is 4.2.
3. **Uniforms and costumes are not a leaf (review F1).** Each uniform garment is filed by its form (a school blazer under blazer/sport coat, a chef's jacket under 4.1, uniform trousers under 2.2) and carries the tag `uniform: school | military | chef | …`. A costume is filed by its form where it has one (dress, jumpsuit, cape); the one kind "costume" holds outfits sold as a set with no single garment form. "Uniforms" and "Costumes" are generated views.
4. **Protective equipment is filed by body zone and form (review F2; decision A-4).** The hazard class is an attribute (`hazard_rating`); the design or voluntary standard met is `standard_met` with the standard named from a fetched source †, and a legal PPE class is a C17 row, never a level or a tag (C17). A "Protective equipment" view is generated. There is no protective sub-family and no protective leaf: sub-family 6 holds 12 kinds, so it stores `leaf = null` (C5(b), A-21), hats and caps first and the protective kinds after them by body zone from head to face (hard hat, helmets, bee veil, safety glasses, ear defenders, respirator, face mask). Every other protective garment (hi-vis vest, life jacket, chainsaw trousers, bee suit, protective gloves) sits in its ordinary leaf.
5. **Traditional dress is filed by form (review F4).** No leaf or sub-family is set by region. The kurta is in Shirts, the lehenga choli and ruqun are in Dresses and two-piece sets, the hakama is in Trousers, the haori is in Jackets; sari, kimono, yukata and the other wrapped lengths are in 3.4. Kimono is the kind; `formality` is an ordered attribute; furisode, houmongi, komon, tomesode and iromuji are non-maker named forms under it.
6. **Items outside the key's examples (decision A-6).** Gaiters and leg warmers are hosiery (5.2); the obi is a non-maker named form of the sash kind and the dupatta a draped length (3.4). Arm sleeves and arm warmers are A1 kinds in sub-family 7, ordered by body zone; an arm sleeve made for a sport is E2.
7. **Leaves with kinds named only by the key (†).** They stay in the tree so that every object the key sends here has one home (C5(d), review F2). They do not enter the data until a fetched page attests them (section 10).
8. **Historical styles (decision A-1).** A historical style made or sold as original or replica is a non-maker named form under its nearest generic kind, tagged `era: historical`; it is a kind only if sold as its own category (C2(c)). Each is recorded without a cap on one fetched page.
9. **Technology and power (CV-24, C10).** A heated, lit or cooling garment is the ordinary kind with an attribute; technology defines a kind only where the key names it or the form and installation change and a fetched source ranks the types apart.
10. **Swimwear (decision K-126).** Bikinis, swim trunks, swimsuits and beachwear are A1 (leaf 5.4); only competition and sport swimwear and wetsuits are E2. ## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type (convention C4): consensus-ranked at maker, line and named-form level through dated tier entries, spec-ranked material attributes with the standard named, platform segments as dated tags, and preference elsewhere.** No single tier ordering exists across clothing; the world uses different orderings at different grains.

**Where the tier lives (C4(a)).** A `tier` never sits on a kind. It sits on a maker, a line or a named form (or, under C4(b), a variant or plan row) as a **tier entry**: `tier_entry_id`, `subject_id`, `subject_type`, `scheme_id`, `value`, `rank`, `direction`, `scope`, `test_version`, `stale_after`, `source_url`, `date`, and a computed `disputed` flag. A subject may have several entries from different sources. A named form does not inherit its maker's tier unless an entry says so. Every scheme is registered once in the scheme registry with one of the six scale classes of C4(c); a scheme enters on one fetched source, and a second source or region only adds weight (C4(e)).

**Platform tags (C4(c), class vi), not tiers.** GR's **Market** facet (Core, Grails, Hype, Sartorial) and VT's Designer department are dated `platform_tag` values (platform, label, date of fetch). The memo's earlier reading of the Market facet as a "resale-native tier" is withdrawn. Resale condition facets (GR, VT, MC "Condition") are rows of the grading-scale registry (C16); no condition value of an object is stored.

**Spec-ranked (attributes with the standard named, C4(b)), not garment tiers.**
- *Denim:* fabric weight in ounces per square yard, selvedge, sanforized or unsanforized, slub and low-tension denim are defined terms (RD); Iron Heart 634S is 21 oz, Momotaro Classic 15.7 oz, Standard 14.7 oz (DH, HD). Attributes `denim_oz`, `selvedge`, `sanforized`. No named standard found; stored as a community-defined term.
- *Down:* fill power measures loft (cubic inches per ounce) and grades down quality; it is not warmth, because fill weight is the second number (REI †). Attributes `fill_power`, `fill_weight`. The test method that defines fill power is not named in the fetched excerpt †. A retailer band table runs about 550 to 600 for everyday down up to 850 to 900 for top grade, "retail convention rather than a formal grading standard" (retail glossary seen in a search excerpt, https://www.odsdesignerclothing.com/pages/glossary-fill-power, not fetched; lead).
- *Wool suiting:* Super numbers (100s, 120s, 150s and up) express fibre fineness; "Super" applies only to pure new wool by convention; a higher number is not simply a better suit (GG). Attribute `wool_super_s`; the source describes a convention, not a standard.
- *Size:* ISO 8559-1 and EN 13402 standardise body measurement and size designation (ISO); size is an attribute, never a tier (C6).
- *Hazard class of protective garments:* attribute `hazard_rating`; the EN or ISO standard met is the attribute `standard_met` with the standard named (C17), from fetched pages †; a legal PPE class is a C17 row.
- *Formality of traditional and tailored kinds:* an ordered attribute `formality` (GA ranks furisode and kurotomesode highest, then houmongi, komon, yukata), with sleeve length and price band attached. It is a convention of use, not a tier.

**Preference.** Silhouette, colour, print and fit: Zalando filters (Fit, Length, Pattern), ZZ's sleeve length, length and pattern filters.

**Price band (C4(d), CV-13, CV-14).** One domain-wide scheme in USD (0 free, under 50, 50 to 150, 150 to 500, 500 to 1,500, 1,500 to 5,000, 5,000 to 15,000, 15,000 to 50,000 and upward to over 5,000,000), derived from a dated, sourced list price; a band is never a tier. A1 uses the lower and middle bands and, for couture and archive pieces, the 15,000 to 50,000 band; the upper bands stay unused. It replaces the memo's earlier "$ to $$$$$ as DB uses": DB's own $-labels are stored as the source's scheme in a tier entry, not as the price band.

**Tier vocabulary (decided: CV-10, CV-12, C4).** There is no common label set and no common scale. Each entry carries its own source scheme, registered once with its scale class: a reviewer's or guide's grade (DB three tiers) and a community lineage (Osaka Five) are class iii, a maker's own ladder (Momotaro) is class iv, Grailed Market and Vinted Designer are class vi platform tags, measured specs are attributes (class i). The labels the sources suggest (mass, mid, premium or heritage, designer or luxury, archive or collectible) are values only inside the schemes that use them. Skill or level schemes are allowed as entries. Entries on a named form (maker ladders, vintage scores) follow C4(b).

**Views from attributes.** Views such as "heavyweight selvedge jeans" or "800+ fill power down" are generated from attributes (DH publishes a heavyweight selvedge jeans guide; seen in a search excerpt only, not fetched).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Order (convention C7, as amended by CV-21): canonical form first; then the secondary key the sub-family states; named forms non-maker first, then by maker, year and alphabet.**
- *Kinds inside a leaf:* the canonical kind first (the one most people own: jeans, dress, T-shirt), then the secondary key stated for the sub-family: **length, short to long** in Outerwear (4.1 before 4.2, since jackets and coats are now divided by length), **formality, high to low** in Suits and tailored sets and in Draped and wrapped garments (GA ranks furisode to yukata), and alphabetical otherwise.
- *Named forms inside a kind (C7, CV-21):* non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms, by maker (ledger `name` order), then first-introduction year as attested (the maker's own page), then alphabetically. Variants of a model are grouped under it and ordered by `variant_code` or year (Warehouse 1001, 1001XX "1947 MODEL" per HD; Levi's 501XX). Maker ladders (`line_rank`), newest-first and spec-ladder orders are generated views, not stored order.
- **Reason.** Canonical-first lets a placer land on the common case, which retailers also do (ZA lists chinos first under trousers, GR starts with Casual Pants); in sub-families 6 and 7 the stated key is hats and caps first and then body zone; chronology is how enthusiasts tell a model from its iterations (HD says Warehouse treats 1001 as Levi's treated the 501); formality is the consensus ordering for traditional and tailored garments (GA).
- **Do not order by** price, brand popularity or scene.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

### Final structure

A1 is divided in four levels below the category (convention C5): eight sub-families, of which seven follow coverage and layer under the written tie-break of section 3 (Tops; Bottoms; Dresses, one-piece garments and sets; Outerwear; Underwear, hosiery, sleepwear and swimwear, with the new swimwear leaf 5.4; Headwear and head protection and Hands, neck and waist wear, both without a leaf and with 12 and 8 kinds) and the eighth is the closing Parts sub-family (object served, 3 provisional kinds, no leaf); the first five have four leaves each, set by garment form, the kind is the cut or type buyers compare (deep in Outerwear, shallow in Tops, named by form not by material), and the named form is a specific design below a kind, maker-bound or not, with no cap. Protective gear, uniforms, costumes, traditional dress, scenes, regions, eras, users, occasions and sizes are tags, attributes and generated views, never branches; makers, lines, relations, schools, tier entries (per source, in registered schemes, never on a kind), named forms, variants, registers, aliases and scene tags live in the shared ledgers (C1 to C9) with the USD price band of C4(d). The worked tree has 108 kinds and 19 named forms and is an example, not a limit.

### Remaining risks

- **Sub-family 6 without a leaf rests on † kinds.** Eleven of its 12 kinds have no fetched page yet; if attestation adds many kinds the leaf-less reading is still allowed ("about 12" is a guide, A-21), and if some are not sold the sub-family only gets smaller; every object the key sends here keeps a home.
- **Swimwear leaf 5.4 is this memo's own placement.** K-126 puts bikinis, trunks and beachwear in A1 but names no leaf; the four kinds are †, and rash guards, board shorts and surf apparel are decided by the K-126 test in the harvest.
- **Deep outerwear kinds rest on JP and KR resale pages ([1R]).** They are in on one fetched page; whether each is a kind or a named form is provisional until a page from a second region is read (level only, C11).
- **Kimono types as non-maker named forms.** GT lists them as nodes; A-3 decided named forms, and the C11 tie-break (two regions selling one as its own category) could promote one in the harvest.
- **Kurti against kurta.** Kept as an alias; a separate kind needs sellers in two regions (C11) beyond MY.
- **Held-back scene tags.** Skate, techwear, lolita, Y2K, archive and K-fashion have no definition source yet (C8); the tags enter when a community-wiki page is fetched.
