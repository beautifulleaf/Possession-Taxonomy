# A1 Clothing — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md. Owner emphasis for A1: very deep variety in clothing, including the brands of different subcultures and scenes (workwear, streetwear, skate, techwear, goth, punk, lolita, ivy and trad, Japanese denim, outdoor and gorpcore, traditional dress worldwide), captured without splitting the tree by scene or brand. Sources fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_A1_sources.tsv; short keys below point to them.*

**One-line result.** Divide A1 by garment form in four levels (sub-family by coverage and layer, leaf by garment form, kind by cut or type, named form by maker model or iconic design). Carry scenes, regions, eras, tiers, users and occasions as tags and generated views, and carry makers in a maker ledger linked to named forms.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** A1 Clothing (domain A, Worn & Carried).

**Membership test (placement key, step 17, first branch that fits after footwear is tried).** An object *worn on the body, or carried on the person or in hand as a personal accessory*, that is "a garment, headwear, glove, scarf, tie, belt, hosiery, uniform, costume, wearable baby sleeping bag, or **protective clothing, helmets and personal protective equipment made to protect the wearer from a hazard** (hard hat, motorcycle and cycling helmets and motorcycle riding gear, welding helmet, chainsaw trousers, safety glasses, respirator, ear defenders, hi-vis, life jacket)". Category line: "garments and wearable accessories of any material: everyday, traditional and subculture dress, underwear, uniforms, costume, team and fan jerseys, protective clothing, helmets and personal protective equipment."

**Precedence sentences in the framework that touch A1.**
- Step 17 note: a device whose main function is computing, communication, sensing or media (smartwatch, fitness tracker, earbuds) goes to D (step 24). A garment, shoe or accessory with heating or lights stays here.
- Step 17 note: wigs, hairpieces, false eyelashes, artificial nails, hair ties, plain clips and bands are H1.
- Step 17 note ("made for a sport"): garments made for the demands of one sport, dance or outdoor activity and not suited to everyday wear (ski jackets with powder skirts, swimming costumes, wetsuits, padded cycling shorts, equestrian riding hats, sport protective guards, fencing and kendo gear) go to E2. Running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers, leggings, team and fan jerseys and outdoor jackets stay here.
- Step 17 branch order: footwear is A2 before clothing is tried; ornaments worn on body, hair or clothing, including badges and patches worn as ornament, are A3; bags, wallets, umbrellas, non-corrective eyewear, hand fans, keyrings, fashion canes, baby carriers are A4. Tassels and cords worn on a cap or gown are A1 (A3 note).
- Step 9 note: garments of every kind, including lingerie and fetish wear, are A1 (H3 is for objects made for sexual stimulation or restraint).
- Step 14 note: religious dress and vestments are A1; objects for rites are F4. Occasion dress (wedding, christening, graduation) is filed as what it is: occasion is a modifier.
- Step 25 note: costumes are A1; toy crowns, wands, play make-up and other dress-up accessories made as toys are E1.
- Step 2 note: spectacles and contact lenses that correct vision, medical braces and supports are H2; an ordinary garment made in an adapted form for a disabled user is filed as that garment (adapted is a modifier).
- Step 10: armour made to protect against weapons is E4; protective clothing against hazards is A1.
- Step 28 and S3: fabric, leather as material, and notions sold loose (buttons, zips) are C4; towels, throws and swaddling cloths are B2.
- S4 / Appendix A: region, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, user (baby, child, adult, gender, size, ability), setting, market, occasion, motive, origin, how acquired, material, condition and edition never split an inner level; they become tags, views or ledgers.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, wikis and glossaries

- MFA's wiki (MFA) separates **item guides** (Clothing, Footwear, Accessories) from **style guides by scene**: American Heritage/Workwear, American Sportswear, Western, British Countryside, British Football Casual, British subcultures 1950–1980, Goth Ninja, Gorpcore, Grungy, Heavy Metal, Japanese streetwear, Japanese-Western Prep, Preppy, Rockabilly, Streetwear. ### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **GT (Google product taxonomy).** Apparel & Accessories > Clothing has at least 18 children: Activewear, Baby & Toddler Clothing, Dresses, One-Pieces, Outerwear, Outfit Sets, Pants, Shirts & Tops, Shorts, Skirts, Skorts, Sleepwear & Loungewear, Suits, Swimwear, Traditional & Ceremonial Clothing, Underwear & Socks, Uniforms, Wedding & Bridal Party Dresses. Beside Clothing stand Clothing Accessories (belts, gloves, scarves and shawls, ties, hats, headwear, sashes, traditional accessories such as obis and tabi socks) and Costumes & Accessories. The level mixes axes: user age (Baby & Toddler), function (Activewear), occasion (Wedding), tradition (Traditional & Ceremonial, with kimonos split into bridal, casual, furisode, iromuji, komon, tomesode and houmongi) and garment form (Dresses, Pants). It shows what happens when a flat product feed needs one slot per product.
- **HTS (US Harmonized Tariff Schedule, chapter 61).** The chapter applies only to *knitted or crocheted* made-up articles (construction first; the chapter notes point to heading 6309 for worn clothing and heading 6212 for other goods, and woven apparel is outside this chapter; the woven chapter was not fetched). Headings then split by **user** (men's or boys' vs women's or girls' vs babies' under 6111) and garment (6101 overcoats, carcoats, capes, anoraks and windbreakers; 6103 suits and jackets; 6105 shirts; 6117 accessories), with fibre in subheadings (cotton, wool, man-made).
- **ISO 8559-1:2017 / EN 13402 (ISO/TC 133).** Sizing is its own standard: body measurement and size designation, not garment kinds. Size is therefore an attribute system outside the tree.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- FIT's Ivy Style exhibition (FIT) tells the story by maker and form: Brooks Brothers pioneered the sack suit and adapted the button-down Oxford cloth shirt and Shetland sweater from the English wardrobe. The garments are named forms credited to a maker; there is no category tree.
- WP (Wikipedia Category: Clothing by type) has 33 subcategories organised by garment family (Coats, Dresses, Gowns, Jackets, Jeans by type, Neckwear, Outerwear, Ponchos, Robes and cloaks, Saris, Shawls and wraps, Skirts, Sportswear, Suits, Tops, Trousers and shorts, Undergarments, Wedding clothing) plus Clothing brands by type. Its parent category also appears to carry sibling splits by country, culture, ethnicity, function and material (seen only in a search excerpt of the parent page, not fetched; treat as a lead).
- GA (a kimono reference guide) ranks kimono types by formality and TPO (time, place, occasion): furisode, kurotomesode and irotomesode, houmongi, komon, yukata; each carries sleeve length, occasion and price band as attributes.
- SV (a Korean heritage publication on Yale Ivy style) lists Ivy as a short canonical wardrobe tied to reference makers (J.Press, Brooks Brothers, L.L. Bean): suit, cotton trousers, tweed sport jacket, oxford-cloth button-down shirts, blucher oxfords, penny loafers, repp-stripe ties.
- AX (Arc'teryx's own naming scheme) is the maker's classification: activity collections (Climb, Trail, Ski & Snowboard, Everyday, Multi-Use, Veilance, System_A), category modifiers inside product names and product families (Satoro merino base layers, Thorium down mid layers).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects

- **Agree.** Garment form (jacket, trousers, dress) is the branch at the leaf; gender is the first split in shopping but never a branch in reference works; brand, size, colour, price are facets.
- **Split: sport and activewear.** GT puts cycling bibs, dance dresses, hunting clothing and motorcycle protective clothing under Activewear; ZZ files outdoor/sports separately; AS and ZA run Sport departments. The framework routes made-for-a-sport garments to E2 and motorcycle riding gear to A1, so sport is a boundary, not a family (section 9).
- **Split: traditional dress.** GT and MY give it a family of its own (occasion and region); WP and MC (kimono/yukata as a top-level men's category) show both models; AS and ZA have no equivalent because the market is Western. The same object (a haori) is a jacket by form and a "traditional" item by region.
- **Split: uniforms and workwear.** GT has Uniforms as a family (chef's jackets, school uniforms, sports uniforms); retailers file the same chef's jacket or coverall by form and sell it through a workwear edit.
- **Split: swimwear.** GR lists Swimwear under Bottoms, ZA and ZZ as their own family; the framework sends swimming costumes to E2.
- **Split: scene.** Scene is a navigation mode on specialist shops (LR gothic/ouji/J-fashion, 808 skate brand filter, KS scene collections), a guide type on community wikis (MFA), a resale title term (archive, y2k), and a retailer edit (AS). It is never the primary branch of a mainstream tree.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence of order.**
1. *Department or user first on every mainstream site.* VT (Women, Men, Designer, Kids), GR (Menswear, Womenswear), AS, ZA, UQ, ZZ and MC (レディース/メンズ) all open with it. The framework treats user as a modifier, so this becomes a generated view.
2. *Garment family and leaf second.* Every retail tree then branches on form (above).
3. *Fit and size third.* ZZ's kodawari filters start with sleeve length and length; ZA's top filters are Brand, Colour, Size, Price, Material, Length, Fit; MS puts top length first among filters then adds fit; VT2 puts Category, Size, Brand before Condition. Heddels' separate beginner's buying guide (https://www.heddels.com/buying-your-first-pair-of-raw-denim-the-beginners-guide, read only as a search excerpt, not fetched; lead) frames the order as: choose your silhouette, know your measurements, sanforized or not, pick the size.
4. *Maker or scene first for enthusiasts.* HD and DH open with makers; CC opens with Brand List; LR opens with substyle; GR's brand directory and Market facet run beside Category; MFA keeps scene guides apart from item guides.
5. *Spec for technical garments.* REI's guide says down is graded by fill power, a loft measurement, with fill weight the second number; GG says wool suiting is graded by Super numbers; DH/HD cite denim weight in ounces.

**Reading.** Buyers decide *form* first (what garment am I shopping for), then *fit and size*, then *maker or scene* where the market is identity-driven, then *spec* for technical or heritage garments. The scene or maker can pre-empt form only for people already inside a scene, and even there they buy a form from a maker (Lot. 1001 jeans from Warehouse). Form is therefore the stable branching axis; maker and scene are second-axis tags and ledgers.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates keep A1 as the category and respect Appendix A. Level names: *sub-family*, *leaf*, *kind*, *named form* (the maximum depth allowed below the category).

### Candidate A (recommended): garment form in four levels

| Level | Axis (one per level) | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | **Coverage and layer**: what part of the body the garment covers, and whether it sits next to the skin, as the main layer, over other clothes, or on head/hands/neck/waist | Tops; Bottoms; Dresses, one-piece garments and sets; Outerwear; Underwear, hosiery and sleepwear; Headwear and head protection; Hands, neck and waist wear |
| Leaf | **Garment form**: the silhouette family a buyer names first | Jeans; Trousers; Light jackets; Coats; Robes, wraps and draped garments |
| Kind | **Cut or type** that buyers treat as a different garment and compare on quality or maker | Harrington jacket; chore coat; MA-1 flight jacket; furisode |
| Named form | **A maker's model or an iconic design** (never an individual item) | Levi's 501; Warehouse & Co. Lot. 1001; Dickies 874; Angelic Pretty "Happy Cakes" jumperskirt |

Cross-cutting, off the tree: scene tags, region/tradition tags, era, tier, user, occasion, material and spec attributes, a maker ledger linked to named forms (sections 5 to 8).

**Where candidate A breaks (edge objects).**

3. *Uniform sets and costumes.* Defined by purpose (school, military, dress-up), not by form. They are listed as one leaf with kinds named by role, which is the one place the leaf axis bends.
4. *Swimwear and wetsuits.* Retail families (GR, ZA, ZZ) treat swimwear as an ordinary clothing family; the framework sends swimming costumes to E2.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type: consensus-ranked at maker, line and segment level, with spec-ranked material attributes on three families and preference elsewhere.** No single tier ordering exists across clothing; the world uses different orderings at different grains.

**Consensus-ranked (maker, line, segment).**
- GR's **Market** facet (Core, Grails, Hype, Sartorial) is a resale-native segment ordering placed beside Category (GR). VT lists Designer as a department beside Women, Men and Kids (VT); Zalando's men's landing page also shows a Designer department, but that page was seen only in a search excerpt (https://en.zalando.de/men/, not fetched; lead).
- DB sorts techwear and gorpcore brands by price ($ to $$$$$), by "3 Tiers", and by style, with a high-end design-led tier (Acronym, Arc'teryx Veilance), a "ninja" tier and a budget tier (DB).
- Makers publish their own ranks: Momotaro's lines Classic, Standard, Exclusive, Natural Indigo, 11 oz (HD); Arc'teryx's Veilance and System_A against its activity collections (AX).
- Prestige lineage is community-ranked: the Osaka Five (Full Count, Studio D'Artisan, Warehouse & Co. and others) are named as the founding group of the Japanese denim scene (HD, DH).
- **Formality** is a consensus rank for traditional dress: kimono types run from furisode and kurotomesode (highest) through houmongi to komon and yukata (casual), with sleeve length and price band attached (GA).

**Spec-ranked (material attributes, not garment tiers).**
- *Denim:* fabric weight in ounces per square yard, selvedge, sanforized or unsanforized, slub and low-tension denim are defined terms (RD); Iron Heart 634S is 21 oz, Momotaro Classic 15.7 oz, Standard 14.7 oz (DH, HD).
- *Down:* fill power measures loft (cubic inches per ounce) and grades down quality; it is not warmth, because fill weight is the second number (REI †). A retailer band table runs about 550 to 600 for everyday down up to 850 to 900 for top grade, "retail convention rather than a formal grading standard" (retail glossary seen in a search excerpt, https://www.odsdesignerclothing.com/pages/glossary-fill-power, not fetched; lead).
- *Wool suiting:* Super numbers (100s, 120s, 150s and up) express fibre fineness; "Super" applies only to pure new wool by convention; a higher number is not simply a better suit (GG).
- *Size:* ISO 8559-1 and EN 13402 standardise body measurement and size designation (ISO); size is an attribute system, not a tier.

**Preference.** Silhouette, colour, print and fit: Zalando filters (Fit, Length, Pattern), ZZ's sleeve length, length and pattern filters.

**How tier appears as a modifier, never a level.**
1. `tier` on the **maker ledger** row and on a **maker line** row (for example Momotaro Classic). Vocabulary to be set by the owner; the sources suggest at least: mass, mid, premium or heritage, designer or luxury, archive or collectible (GR Market, DB tiers).
2. `price_band` as a separate field ($ to $$$$$ as DB uses), so tier and price are not conflated.
3. Spec attributes on the kind or named form: `denim_oz`, `selvedge`, `sanforized`, `fill_power`, `fill_weight`, `wool_super_s`, `fibre_content`.
4. `formality` as an ordered attribute for traditional and tailored kinds (GA).
Views such as "heavyweight selvedge jeans" or "800+ fill power down" are generated from attributes (DH has such guides).

**Recommended type: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought, in every scene the owner named. Evidence:
- HD and DH organise Japanese denim by maker and by each maker's model codes, and DH groups makers by what they are known for. CC's first navigation is Brand List. GR, MS (3,708 brands on one outerwear page), MY (5,779+ brands in one women's ethnic-wear list) and VT ("Best selling brands") put a maker directory beside the category tree.
- MC and YA listing titles lead with the maker. YA links a separate "by brand" tree (`list2/23172`) beside the category tree †.

**Does A1 need a maker ledger? Yes.** Brand depth is the owner's stated priority, and no tree level can hold it (Appendix A). Recommended ledger columns, each row attested:

| Field | Note |
|---|---|
| maker id, name, native-script name | for example 株式会社ウエアハウス / Warehouse & Co.; aliases and renamings tracked |

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommended order: canonical form first; then a named secondary key by leaf; named forms by maker and chronology.**
- *Kinds inside a leaf:* the canonical kind first (the one most people own: jeans, dress, T-shirt), then related kinds. Secondary key by leaf: weight light to heavy in Outerwear (GR already contrasts "Light Jackets" with "Heavy Coats"), formality high to low in Suits and in Robes and wraps (GA ranks furisode to yukata), otherwise alphabetical.
- *Named forms inside a kind:* by maker, then by year of introduction, with variants of a model grouped under it (Warehouse 1001, 1001XX "1947 MODEL" per HD; Levi's 501XX).
- **Reason.** Canonical-first lets a placer land on the common case, which retailers also do (ZA lists chinos first under trousers, GR starts with Casual Pants); chronology is how enthusiasts tell a model from its iterations (HD says Warehouse treats 1001 as Levi's treated the 501); formality is the consensus ordering for traditional and tailored garments (GA).
- **Do not order by** price, brand popularity or scene.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

### Recommended structure

**A1 > sub-family > leaf > kind > named form** (maximum depth below the category: four levels).

1. **Sub-family axis: coverage and layer.** Seven sub-families (Tops; Bottoms; Dresses, one-piece garments and sets; Outerwear; Underwear, hosiery and sleepwear; Headwear and head protection; Hands, neck and waist wear). This is the order that every mainstream retailer and the trade schedule share (section 1).
2. **Leaf axis: garment form.** For example Jeans, Trousers, Light jackets, Coats, Robes wraps and draped garments.
3. **Kind axis: cut or type that buyers compare** (section 4b: deep in outerwear, shallow in tops).
4. **Named form axis: a maker's model or an iconic design**, tied to a maker-ledger row (sections 6 and 7).
5. **Off the tree, in separate tables:** scene tags and generated scene views; region/tradition tags and a generated "Traditional dress" view; era, tier, price band, user, occasion; spec attributes (denim ounces, selvedge, fill power, fill weight, Super number, fibre, formality); an alias table (JSK, sukajan, coverall); a maker ledger with a lines table; a named-form ledger.

**Summary values the owner asked for.** Choice level: kind = the cut or type buyers compare (jacket-level in outerwear, garment-level in tops); named form = a maker's model. Tier type: consensus-ranked for makers, lines and segments, with spec-ranked material attributes for denim, down and wool suiting. Maker centrality: identity; ledger yes. Scenes: tags and generated views, named forms only where the scene names a distinct form (cut-off vest, JSK/OP/SK aliases, sukajan).

### Owner decisions needed

1. **Adopt Candidate A** (coverage-and-layer sub-families) and generate occasion and scene views, or keep a function sub-family such as Google's? Candidate B breaks Appendix A (occasion, region).
2. **Traditional dress.** Keep it by form under Robes and wraps (sari, kimono, yukata) and in the form families (dirndl in Dresses, hakama and kurta in Bottoms or Tops), or give it a sub-family of its own as GT and MY do? A region/tradition view is provided either way. Where should the kurta and the haori go?
3. **Is "Robes, wraps and draped garments" a leaf under Dresses and sets, or an eighth sub-family?**
4. **Protective equipment (A1 by the key).** Place by body zone with a generated Protective view (recommended), or give it a sub-family (this mixes axes)? No fetched source gives hazard-class standards; they need sourcing.
5. **Sport boundary.** The key sends swimming costumes, wetsuits and padded cycling shorts to E2 while retailers treat swimwear as ordinary clothing, and the key files cycling helmets in A1 but equestrian hats in E2. Keep, or amend the key?
6. **Uniform sets and costumes.** Keep leaf 3.4 (defined by role) or file each garment by form with a uniform tag?
7. **Kind versus named form for Ivy and military specs** (OCBD, Shetland sweater, MA-1, M-65, sack jacket). Proposed rule: if several makers make the type it is a kind, if it is one maker's catalogue number it is a named form. Confirm.
8. **Tier vocabulary** (how many steps, what labels) and whether tier lives on maker, line or named form.
9. **Scene tag governance:** controlled vocabulary with definition sources (MFA style guides, Subculture Wiki), "core" or "adjacent" strength, tagged at maker, kind and named-form level without automatic inheritance.
