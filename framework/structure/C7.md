## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C7 Farm & Forestry Equipment.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 12): *made for **agricultural or forestry production**: tractors and anything made to be drawn by, mounted on or driven from a tractor; self-propelled farm and forestry machines; livestock handling, milking, shearing and slaughter equipment for herds and flocks; commercial crop, poultry and dairy equipment.* Note on the step: equipment for a household garden, allotment, pets, horses, or a few backyard hens or hives is C2 or B10, "including garden and compact lawn tractors"; animal feed in any quantity is B10. Category line: "tractors and their implements, self-propelled farm and forestry machines, livestock handling and milking equipment."

**Notes and precedence sentences elsewhere that touch C7:**
- Step 11 (G1) note: "A machine that carries its operator while it does other work, such as a tractor, ride-on mower or excavator, goes by that work." Trailers and horseboxes are G1 (step 11 is tried before step 12).
- Step 10 (E4) note: slaughter and stunning tools for livestock are C7.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): a part, attachment or consumable made for one kind of object goes to that object's category, so a tractor tyre, front loader or wheel weight follows the tractor into C7.
- Step 13 (B10): housing, feeding, tack, carriers, veterinary goods and identification microchips for animals are B10; animal feed in any quantity is B10.
- Step 29 (C2): mowers, trimmers, irrigation, pressure washers and snow clearers for grounds are C2.
- Step 32 (C1, C8): tools and machines that "lift, power or repair" are C1; building products, fencing and fuels are C8.
- Step 4 (G2): barns, stables and other buildings a person can enter are G2; Step 24 (D2): GPS units are D2.
- Appendix A: market (domestic, commercial, professional), user, setting, brand and maker, price and tier, era, region and scene are modifiers, never levels.
- Decided key sentences (v0.7): K-93 (Unimog, ATV, quad and utility vehicle G1; tool-carrier tractors C7), K-94 (carry-only trailers and wagons G1; wagons that load, mix, spread or process C7), K-99 (lawn and garden tractors without a rear power take-off and a Category 1 or larger three-point hitch are C2; two-wheel tractors follow the same test), K-100 (field irrigation C7), K-101 (herd and flock equipment, livestock EID stick readers and commercial hatchers C7; pet microchip scanners and small incubators B10), K-173 (chainsaws and pole saws C2; axes, wedges and log splitters C1; tractor-driven splitters and chippers C7), K-175 (pumps), K-194 (self-propelled dig, lift, load and move machines C1 unless a farm or forestry job), K-195 (fencing and electric-fence energisers C8).

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **EU Regulation 167/2013, Article 4** [S16]: legal categories T (wheeled tractors: T1, T2, T3, T4.1 high-clearance, T4.2 extra-wide, T4.3 low-clearance, each a or b by speed), C (track-laying), R (trailers R1–R4), S (interchangeable towed equipment S1, S2). The cuts are track width, unladen mass, ground clearance and design speed, not the buyer's power class.
- **HS 8432 and 8433** [S18][S17]: soil preparation (ploughs, disc harrows, other harrows/scarifiers/cultivators/rotovators, seeders and planters with no-till separate, manure spreaders, fertiliser distributors; sprayers excluded) and harvesting (mowers, haymaking machinery, balers, combine harvester-threshers, root and tuber harvesters, forage harvesters self-propelled or not, produce grading). Customs divides by machine function.
- **ISO/TC 23** [S19]: subcommittees for Tractors, Crop protection, Harvesting and conservation, Powered lawn and garden, Forestry machinery, Hand-held powered lawn/garden and forest machinery, Irrigation, Agricultural electronics, plus a milking machines working group.
- **Three-point linkage** [S15][S14]: ASAE S217 was replaced by ANSI/ASABE AD730:2009 with categories 1N, 1, 2N, 2, 3N, 3, 4N and 4 (the ISO 730 family).

**Museums and reference works.**
- **Museum of English Rural Life** [S23]: farm tools, implements and machinery; farm-based processing equipment; equipment for management of farm livestock; agricultural service industries. **Wikipedia** [S20][S21]: the list of agricultural machinery is grouped by job (tractor and power, soil cultivation, planting, fertilizer and pesticide dispensing, harvesting and post-harvest, hay making, loading); the tractor article classes tractors first by axles and wheels (two-wheel; four-wheel 2WD, front-assist, 4WD articulated; tracked) and then by use (standard, row-crop, utility, compact utility 20–50 hp, garden). **USDA Forest Service** [S26] and **FAO** [S28] describe forest and dairy machines by mechanism: feller bunchers by drive-to-tree versus swing-boom and by head (bunching, processing, felling); milking by bucket, pipeline and parlour, with parlours split into one unit per two stalls or one per stall, and rotaries, trigons and polygons apart.

**Where the sources agree.** All of them put the job in the crop or forest cycle ahead of the maker for implements (tillage, planting, spreading, spraying, harvest, hay, grain, livestock, forestry), and all give the tractor a head of its own. Forestry is a separate head in Mascus, traktorpool and Brazil [S1][S3][S7] and a separate ISO subcommittee [S19]. Hitch category and horsepower are the compatibility facts everywhere [S6][S8][S14].

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- **Tractor classes.** Deere's compact tops out at 65.9 HP and its utility starts at 50 [S12]; Tractor Iron's compact is 25–50 and utility 45–100+ [S13]; Wikipedia's compact utility is 20–50 [S21]; the law cuts by track width and mass [S16]; the French and German marketplaces cut by specialised design, not power [S3][S4]; Japan cuts by horsepower band and running gear [S10].
- **Loaders.** Front loaders sit under Traktoren in traktorpool but telehandlers sit under Hoflader/Bagger/Stapler [S3]; Mascus has telehandlers for agriculture under agriculture [S2]; Machinery Pete has Loaders and Lifts [S5].
- **Trailers and wagons.** Transport trailers, loader wagons, silage wagons, slurry tankers and bale carriers share traktorpool's Transportfahrzeuge head with Ladewagen listed also under Siliertechnik and Grünlandgeräte [S3]; the EU law counts trailers as R, a different category from tractors [S16].
- **Mulchers, chippers, winches.** The same machine is listed under vineyard, grassland, municipal and forestry heads [S3], and under forestry at Mascus [S1].
- **Animals and feed** are a head inside Agriaffaires' farm tree [S4], which the framework puts outside C7 (animals out, feed B10).
- **Parts.** Tyres, rims and tracks have their own top group in traktorpool and Agriaffaires [S3][S4]; the framework (S3) files them with the tractor.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**For implements and self-propelled machines the first choice is the job**: which stage of the crop, hay, livestock or forest cycle the machine does. Machinery Pete's mandatory first selector is Category (Tillage, Planting, Harvesting, Hay and Forage, Livestock Equipment) and only then Make [S5]; traktorpool's 305 categories are mostly job names (Bodenbearbeitung, Saat-/Pflanztechnik, Erntemaschinen, Grünlandgeräte) [S3]; the Indian implement list is by job with Tillage (524) far ahead [S8]; the HS headings split the same way (soil preparation, harvesting) [S18][S17]; the ISO committees likewise (crop protection, harvesting and conservation, forestry machinery, milking) [S19]; Wikipedia's list groups by job [S20].

**For tractors the first choice is size, then specialisation.** Deere's catalogue opens on four horsepower classes [S12]; Yanmar's lineup is ordered by horsepower band [S10]; Farm Trader lists Rated Engine Power and Drive Type as the first listing facets [S6]; Tractor Iron tells buyers to compare four numbers (engine HP, PTO HP, weight, 3-point lift) rather than the class label [S13]. The French and German marketplaces split tractors by specialised design first (vineyard, orchard, straddle, slope, forestry, Unimog) [S3][S4], and the EU law splits by track width, mass and clearance [S16]. (How the structure uses this: size is recorded as attributes and the design for a task is the kind, section 4b.)

**The compatibility check follows**: the implement must match the tractor's hitch category and power [S14][S8][S11]. Hitch category is a spec printed on every implement listing [S8] and a standard (AD730) [S15], so it works as a join between tractors and implements, not as a way of dividing either.

**Maker comes after type in marketplaces** (Make is the third selector at Machinery Pete [S5]; popular links at Mascus are by make within a type [S2]) **but first in communities** (TractorByNet forums are by maker [S25]; TractorData pages are by maker [S22]). Both are consistent with a type tree plus a maker ledger.

**For livestock equipment the first choice is the function**: handle, weigh, identify, milk, cool [S31][S30][S3]. Sources agree less on the boundaries of this group than on any other (section 1).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's limits: one stated axis per level, modifiers off the tree, at most four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; C5a). A sub-family holding about 12 kinds or fewer skips the leaf and the data stores `leaf = null` (C5b).

### Candidate A: by the job in the farm and forest cycle (recommended)

- **Level 1, sub-family: the job the machine does** (Tractors & carriers, Soil tillage, Seeding and planting, Crop care, Harvesting, Hay and forage, Post-harvest handling, Livestock and dairy, Forestry, and the closing sub-family of parts). Two sub-families are named for a role, not a crop-cycle job: "1 Tractors & carriers" holds the machines whose job is to power and carry implements, and "10 Parts and accessories made for one machine" is the closing S3 sub-family (C5(d), CV-17; review K7). Sub-family 10 is the only one exempt from the job axis.
- **Level 2, leaf (optional; one stated axis per sub-family, C5c):**

| Sub-family | Leaf axis | Leaves |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Tractors & carriers | running gear | Wheeled tractors; Articulated 4WD; Tracked; Two-wheel; Carrier |
| 2 Soil tillage | operation on the ground | Tillage and seedbed; Ground shaping and clearing |
| 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 | none (12 kinds or fewer; `leaf = null`) | none |
| 8 Livestock and dairy | stage of the herd, flock or dairy chain | Milking installations; Milk cooling and metering; Handling; Weighing; Identification and recording; Shearing; Slaughter and stunning; Poultry; Feeding |
| 10 Parts and accessories made for one machine | the machine served | Tractor fittings; Shearing machine parts |

- **Level 3, kind: the generic machine type buyers compare** (mouldboard plough, power harrow, disc mower, cattle crush). For wheeled tractors the kind is the **design for a task** (standard four-wheel, row-crop, narrow orchard and vineyard, high-clearance, slope, forestry). Size and power are attributes (`hitch_category`, `engine_hp`, `pto_hp`), never kinds (C6; Appendix A). The maker's own size label (sub-compact, compact, utility) is a field on the named form and a generated "size class" view.
- **Level 4, named form: a model or model line** (Yanmar YT3R, Gallagher TWR-5). Every named form seen in the sources is **maker-bound** (`maker_bound = yes`, C2a). No non-maker named form (a generic design that many makers build and buyers ask for by name) is attested for C7 yet. A maker's range that spans several kinds (Ponsse's cut-to-length range, Yanmar's YT series) is a line row (C1, review C K4), not a named form.

Fit with sources: Machinery Pete, traktorpool, Mascus, Farm Trader, the Brazilian and Indian lists, the HS headings, ISO committees and Wikipedia all open on job [S5][S3][S1][S6][S7][S8][S17][S18][S19][S20].

**Rules this candidate adopts.**
- **Carrier is an attribute (C12; review F35).** `carrier` = mounted, trailed or self-propelled. A self-propelled machine is its own kind only when no mounted or trailed machine does the same job (combine harvester, forest harvester, forwarder, skidder, feller buncher). Under it, "crop sprayer" and "forage harvester" are one kind each, with `carrier` recorded.
- **Multi-use kinds (C14).** A kind used in more than one place goes to (a) the leaf whose rule defines it, (b) the general leaf of the one family that holds all its uses, (c) a category-level multi-use sub-family. The flail mower and mulcher (uses in grassland, vineyard and municipal work, which are market settings, Appendix A) has one job, cutting standing vegetation, and goes under Hay and forage by (b); the forestry mulcher and stump grinder are separate kinds in Forestry [S1]. The forestry tractor goes under Tractors & carriers by (b), and Forestry lists it in a generated view. No multi-use sub-family is needed.
- **Parts follow the whole (S3; K7).** A fitting with no job of its own away from its host machine (front loader, wheel weights, tyres, tracks, guidance kit, shearing handpiece) goes to sub-family 10, leaf = the machine served. An attachment that does a job of its own (a combine header, a harvester head, a plough) keeps its job sub-family.

Where it breaks:
1. **Mulcher/flail mower** serves grassland, vineyard, municipal and forestry (traktorpool lists it four times [S3]); settled by C14(b) above.
2. **Slurry tanker** both carries and spreads: Crop care (spreading) wins over transport (section 9, P5).
3. **Front loader** is a tractor fitting but the loader is also a job (loading); S3 puts it in sub-family 10.
4. **Telehandler, skid steer, excavator** are sold in farm marketplaces [S2][S3][S5] but do no farm-specific job; they fall outside this tree (section 9, P7; K-194).

### Candidate B: by how the machine is carried and powered

- **Level 1: carrier and coupling** (tractor; mounted/3-point implement; trailed implement; PTO-driven; self-propelled machine; stationary installation).
- **Level 2: job. Level 3: kind. Level 4: named form.**

Fit: the EU law separates tractors (T, C) from trailers (R) and interchangeable towed equipment (S) [S16]; Deere keeps implements on a separate page [S12]; Farm Trader lists Tractor Connection (Linkage) [S6]; the Japanese article starts from 3-point versus 2-point link [S11].

Where it breaks:
1. **Sprayer, mower, baler** exist mounted, trailed and self-propelled; buyers compare them as one job (Mascus names "trailed sprayers" as a type [S2] while the Brazilian portal lists Pulverizador and Distribuidor Autopropelido [S7]), so B scatters one comparison set across several heads.
2. **Milking parlours, grain dryers, cattle crushes** are stationary and have no carrier at all.
3. **Rice transplanter, combine** are self-propelled but a buyer treats them as the job, not the carrier [S10].
4. The hitch category is a modifier that joins two kinds, not a way of dividing them. Candidate A keeps carrier and hitch category as attributes.

### Candidate C: by production system (crop, grassland, livestock, forestry, specialty)

- **Level 1: production system. Level 2: job. Level 3: kind. Level 4: named form.**

Fit: Machinery Pete has Specialty Crops, Hay and Forage and Livestock Equipment [S5]; traktorpool has Kartoffeltechnik, Obst- und Weinbau, Hopfentechnik, Gemüseanbau [S3]; Agriaffaires has Viti-vinicole and cultures spécialisées [S4].

Where it breaks:
1. **Tractor, sprayer, mulcher, loader** serve every system; traktorpool repeats Kompakttraktoren under four heads [S3], which breaks "one home".
2. **A potato planter** is a planter for a crop; its sibling is the precision planter, not the potato harvester.
3. **Crop** is not on the framework's modifier list as such but behaves like setting or scene (Appendix A): it is better as a generated view.
4. **Forestry** is the only system with its own machine kinds (harvester, forwarder, skidder), so C collapses to A plus a view.

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds.** About 200 (an estimate, not a count; the worked tree shows 105 as a seed). Basis: traktorpool's 305 categories [S3] contain roughly 100 that fall outside C7 (construction machines, wheel loaders and forklifts, trucks and trailers, lawn and garden items, model tractors) and several that repeat a kind under a second head; HS 8432 and 8433 name 9 and 11 six-digit subheadings [S18][S17] but the marketplaces split them far finer (Pflüge, Grubber, Scheibeneggen, Kurzscheibeneggen, Kreiseleggen, Fräsen and Packer are seven different headings under what HS calls soil preparation [S3][S18]). By sub-family: Tractors & carriers 10 to 13 (fewer than before, because horsepower classes are now attributes); Soil tillage 15 to 20; Seeding and planting 8 to 10; Crop care 25 to 30 (traktorpool alone lists 17 slurry and manure subcategories and 6 mechanical-weeding ones [S3]); Harvesting 25 to 30 (traktorpool names about 12 crop-specific harvesters, Wikipedia about as many [S3][S20]); Hay and forage 12 to 15; Post-harvest 10 to 12; Livestock and dairy 30 to 40 (more if feeding and housing equipment at herd scale is C7, section 9); Forestry 15 to 20 [S1][S3]; Parts and accessories made for one machine 10 to 15.

**Named forms.** Order of 10,000 or more. TractorData lists 280 makers, with 275 International Harvester models alone [S22]; TractorJunction shows 1,119 implements from one country [S8]; Yanmar's Japanese tractor lineup spans at least six series from 14.5 to 114 PS [S10]. Vintage models (Oldtimer-Traktoren 206 on one marketplace [S3]; TractorData's 1908-onwards span [S22]) multiply the tractor count. Named forms are harvested in stage 3 from the catalogues named in section 6 and grow without a cap, one fetched page per row (CV-7, C2(f)); the model sets of the tractor kinds live in registers linked to their kind (CV-45, C2(g)); colourways, sizes and SKUs are never named forms (review K10).

**Where depth concentrates.** Tractors (a small number of kinds, a very large number of maker model lines), combines and self-propelled sprayers, forestry machines (few kinds, high price, named forms only), and livestock weighing and handling (a few kinds with many indicator and crush models [S30]). Tillage and hay-making are wide with shallow depth: many kinds, few named forms that anyone tracks.

**Broad, medium and narrow leaves** (traktorpool listing counts, a single platform, shown only to indicate relative size [S3]):
- *Broad:* mowers (3,962), combine harvesters (3,128), cultivators (2,704), tipping trailers (2,578; G1), ploughs (2,112), balers (1,972).
- *Medium:* forage harvesters (884), loader wagons (788), potato lifters (624), feed mixer wagons (1,108), forestry winches (436), wood chippers (605).
- *Narrow:* milking robots (51), forest harvesters (64), forwarders (48), grape harvesters (19), carrot lifters (28), hop pickers (1), skidders (1).

## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers (C2a).** The evidence puts that level at the machine type, one step above the model, and at the design for a task for tractors. Named-form test (all categories): a non-maker named form is a design that many makers make and buyers ask for by name; a measuring mode, load or size class, fitting variant, active ingredient or material is an attribute.

- **Implements and machines: kind = machine type.** Marketplaces list, count and filter at this level: Pflüge, Grubber, Kreiseleggen, Fräsen, Düngerstreuer, Pressen are the leaves where hundreds to thousands of ads sit and where makers are compared [S3]; Indian implement pages carry a tractor power range and a maker on every listing and group by implement type [S8]; Japanese guides compare attachments by type with the main makers named for each [S11]; HS and ISO committees stop at the same level [S17][S18][S19]. Finer variants are facets on the listing, not separate heads: Farm Trader's Class, Cutting Width, Working Width and Tractor Connection (linkage or trailed) [S6]; Webbline's bale wrapper is listed with "Wrapper Type: Satellite", "Bale Type: Round and Rectangular", "Tractor Mounting: Trailed" [S6]. So reversible versus fixed mouldboard, mounted versus trailed, and working width stay as attributes of a kind (retail-facet test: a facet becomes a kind only when the object's form differs and a fetched source compares or ranks the types separately).
- **Carrier.** `carrier` is an attribute (mounted, trailed, self-propelled). A self-propelled machine is its own kind only where no mounted or trailed machine does the same job (rule in section 3; C10(b) and C12). The crop sprayer and the forage harvester are therefore one kind each; Mascus's "trailed sprayers" and the Brazilian "Pulverizador" and "Distribuidor Autopropelido" [S2][S7] are values of `carrier`.
- **Tractors: kind = design for a task.** The French and German marketplaces cut by specialised design (agricole, fruitier, vigneron, enjambeur, forestier, de pente, micro, Unimog) [S3][S4]; Wikipedia classes first by axles and wheels, then by use [S21]. These are physical forms that cannot replace one another without a different machine (C10(b)). The horsepower classes (Deere compact 22.4–65.9 and utility 50–260 [S12]; Tractor Iron compact 25–50 and utility 45–100+ [S13]; Wikipedia compact utility 20–50 [S21]; Yanmar bands [S10]) overlap between sources, and a model would take its kind from its maker's label. Size is always an attribute (C6), so it is not a level. Size is recorded as `hitch_category` (AD730 categories 1N, 1, 2N, 2, 3N, 3, 4N, 4 and Cat 0 and Limited Cat I [S14][S15]), `engine_hp`, `pto_hp`, operating weight and lift capacity [S13]. The maker's own label (sub-compact, compact, utility) is stored on the named form, and a generated "size class" view shows it. The EU categories T1 to T4.3, C, R and S [S16] are legal classes: they go to the jurisdiction ledger (C17), never the tree. Decided (C-6, option a): tractor kinds are designs for a task with hitch category and horsepower as attributes; size kinds by hitch category or by horsepower class are not used.
- **Self-propelled machines: kind = machine type, header or head is its own kind.** Combine harvesters and their headers are different headings with different buyers and prices in traktorpool, Agriaffaires and the Brazilian portal [S3][S4][S7]; Ponsse sells harvesters, forwarders and harvester heads as three product lines [S27]. Threshing type (Yanmar head-feeding versus conventional [S10]; axial rotor [S7]) is an attribute `threshing_type`, because no fetched source ranks combines by threshing type apart from the machine (C10(b)).
- **Livestock equipment: deeper than implements.** Handling, weighing and EID readers are separate shop categories at Te Pari [S30], and a weigh-scale indicator is compared by feature and maker (Gallagher, Tru-Test, Te Pari side by side [S30]) separately from the platform or load bars it works with [S30]; livestock EID stick readers are C7 too (K-101) and sit in their own leaf. Milking installations differ in physical form and in the building they need, and FAO ranks them separately (bucket, pipeline, parlour; one unit per two stalls or per stall; rotary, trigon, polygon; judged by cows per hour [S28]). They are therefore kinds under C10(b) and C12(a): bucket machine, pipeline installation, static parlour, rotary parlour, trigon parlour and polygon parlour. Stall layout inside a static parlour (abreast, herringbone) stays an attribute †.
- **Forestry: kind = machine type.** Mascus counts each separately (harvesters 1,844, forwarders 1,338, feller buncher 171, skidders 148) [S1]. The feller-buncher mechanism (drive-to-tree versus swing-boom [S26]) is an attribute `boom_type`: the fetched page describes both but no fetched source ranks them apart (C10(b)).
- **Where comparison sits above type.** Mulchers, mowers and sweepers are bought across five markets (agriculture, vineyard, municipal, forestry, grounds) [S3]; the kind stays one, with market as a view (C14b, section 3).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended: spec-ranked, with no tier on any kind (C4a).** Every source that orders machines orders them by a measurable number. Measured specifications are **attributes with their standard named** (C4b), not tier entries.
- **Tractors:** engine HP, PTO HP, operating weight and 3-point lift capacity [S13]; hitch category 0 to 4 with horsepower ranges [S14][S15]; Deere's class by horsepower [S12]; Yanmar's ascending band list [S10]; Farm Trader's Rated Engine Power facet [S6]; TractorData's hp-sorted brand pages [S22].
- **Implements:** the tractor power the implement needs (Power HP on every Indian listing [S8]), working or cutting width, seedbox capacity [S6].
- **Combines and harvesters:** horsepower and threshing type (Yanmar 14 to 138 PS combines, head-feeding versus conventional [S10]).
- **Milking and livestock:** cows per hour [S28], load-bar capacity and record capacity of indicators [S30].

**How it appears, never as a level or a kind tier:** numeric attributes on the named form (engine hp, PTO hp, lift kg, working width m, capacity); `hitch_category` as an attribute on both tractor and implement named forms, with the hitch standard as a row in the compatibility ledger (section 6), which is the join between them. A maker's size label (compact, utility) is a field on the named form, not a tier. Class boundaries overlap between makers [S12][S13], so a model keeps its maker's label and its own hp.

**Tier entries (C4a).** A tier entry (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) sits on a maker, line or named form (or, under C4(b), on a variant or plan row) and enters the data on one fetched source; a second source or region adds weight only (C4(e), CV-10). None is entered yet; the stage-3 harvest enters them as pages are fetched. Maker reputation (premium European or American lines versus value lines) is real in the forums [S25], but no fetched page gives a published ordering, so it is not entered as a tier. A maker's own range level (a technology or range ladder) may be entered with `scheme` = the maker's name. † Performance tests (the Nebraska Tractor Test Laboratory; OECD codes in EU 167/2013's type-approval schedule) were seen only in search excerpts and need a fetched page. If fetched, a test is registered once in the scheme registry as a third-party measured score (C4(c) class ii, with `test_version` and `stale_after`) and its results are tier entries on named forms, never values for one individual machine (C16); this is provisional until attested.

**Price band (C4d).** The domain-wide USD bands are derived from a dated, sourced list price. No fetched page for C7 gives a dated list price, so `price_band` stays empty. † A fetched list-price page is needed for each maker line before any `price_band` is entered; the shared USD scheme runs to over 5,000,000 (CV-14), which covers combines and forest harvesters.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended: platform** (the template's term for brand-led categories; not to be confused with a C13 compatibility platform), for tractors, self-propelled machines and forestry; **incidental** for generic implements and for livestock accessories.
- *Platform evidence.* Forums are organised by maker, with Buying/Pricing and Owning/Operating per maker [S25]; TractorData is maker first [S22]; marketplaces make Make a top selector [S5][S2]; Ponsse lists the machines, its own harvester heads and its information systems as one range [S27]; John Deere's iMatch quick hitch is Category I only and standardises hookup geometry for iMatch-spec implements [S14]; implements built for full Category I may not fit a Limited Category I tractor, which has narrower lift-arm spacing [S14].
- *Incidental evidence.* The three-point hitch is a published standard (ISO 730 family, now AD730) so implements work across makers if the category matches [S15][S14]; Te Pari sells Gallagher, Tru-Test and its own scale indicators side by side [S30]; the Japanese guide lists many makers per implement type [S11].

**Maker ledger: yes, one row per maker in the domain-wide ledger (C1; decision C-19).** Core columns: `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class` (multi-valued), `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `licensor_id`, `authority_id`, `scene_tags` (via the C8 tag table, section 7), `price_band` (computed, empty until a dated list price is fetched, section 5) and `sources` (at least one fetched URL; one page admits a maker, CV-5). `known_for_kinds` is not stored: it is derived from the named-form ledger (CV-1). `maker_class` takes the shared C1 list (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; retail own-label; licensee or diffusion; artisan or atelier; supplier) and, where a fetched page shows one, converter, builder or kit supplier (CV-2). Years active are needed for the vintage makers (`founded`, `ended`, `status`; International Harvester 1908–1985 in [S22]). The ledger is seeded from TractorData's 280 makers [S22] and the marketplace maker filters and lists [S1][S2][S3][S5][S11], taking existing catalogues first, and grows without a cap (CV-5; BRIEF). A maker whose kinds fall in C1 or C2 (for example a maker of towed tree-surgery chippers, or of walk-behind tillers) stays in the same ledger: no boundary drops a maker community. Platform notes (hitch categories supported, quick-hitch system) are not a maker column; they sit in the compatibility ledger below.

**Other ledgers (separate tables, not columns).**
- *Lines and collections:* Ponsse cut-to-length range (spans harvester, forwarder, harvester head), Yanmar YT series, Gallagher and Tru-Test weigh ranges, each with the kinds it spans, `line_rank`, `built_by_maker_id` and `licensor_id` where attested, and sources. A line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form.
- *Maker-relations and school tables (CV-3):* OEM, rebrand and licence rows are entered wherever a fetched page shows them; the school table is standing but no C7 tradition is seeded yet.
- *Registers (CV-45; C2(g)):* the model sets of the tractor kinds (TractorData's 280 makers, with 275 International Harvester models alone [S22]) live in registers linked to their kind, uncapped, with the named-form columns.
- *Tier entries (C4):* none yet; entered on one fetched source each as pages are fetched (section 5).
- *Variants (C3):* filled wherever a community compares variants, tractors included (CV-8), and grown without a cap: `variant_type` = model_year, generation, trim, engine, regional model or vintage, with the vintage-tractor community (section 7) the main user, and the maker's reference codes of models as `variant_code`. Filled in the stage-3 harvest.
- *Compatibility ledger (C13; CV-29):* `platform` rows with `platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `platform_type` (hitch category), `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `region`, `introduced`, `status` and `sources`, plus `compatibility_relation` rows (`from_id`, `relation`: supports, requires, fits, backward compatible with, adapts to; `to_id`; `sources`). Seed rows: three-point linkage categories (owner ASABE/ISO; kinds served standard four-wheel tractor, row-crop tractor, implement carrier, and every mounted implement kind; `standard_ref` ANSI/ASABE AD730:2009, ISO 730 family; [S15][S14]); Limited Category I (narrower lift-arm spacing [S14]); John Deere iMatch quick hitch (owner Deere; Category I only [S14]; typed as a hitch category by default, section 12). Platforms are never levels. The review's `type` and `openness` columns are not adopted: C13 of CONVENTIONS_v1 has `platform_type` and no `openness` column.
- *Jurisdiction ledger (C17; CV-35, CV-36):* EU Regulation 167/2013 categories T, C, R and S [S16] go here as rows (`subject_id`, `jurisdiction` = EU, `scheme`, `class`, `criteria`, `source_url`, `date`) against kinds, with the Article 4 criteria. ## 7. Scenes, cultures and views

All of these are generated views or tags. Scene tags (C8; CV-22 and the owner's depth rule) are set wherever a community exists, at maker, kind and named-form level without inheritance, each with a definition source. Two scenes are defined for C7: **vintage and antique tractor collecting** (core for vintage makers, tractor kinds and named forms; definition sources Yesterday's Tractors [S24] and the TractorByNet Vintage forum [S25]) and **small-acreage and compact-tractor owning** (core for compact tractors and their implements; definition sources TractorByNet [S25] and Tractor Iron [S13]). A tag whose definition source is not yet confirmed at the stage-3 harvest is held back (C8). Era and region are Appendix A modifiers.

- **Vintage and antique tractors.** Oldtimer-Traktoren, Tracteur ancien, Matériel de collection, Yesterday's Tractors and TractorByNet Vintage [S3][S4][S24][S25]. Not a distinct form: **a generated view, an era tag on named forms and the scene tag above**, with maker rows carrying year spans [S22]. Implements of the same era sit in Implement Alley [S24] and use the same kinds.
- **Specialty crops** (vine, orchard, hops, potatoes, vegetables). traktorpool has top groups for each [S3]; Machinery Pete has Specialty Crops [S5]; Agriaffaires has Viti-vinicole [S4]. Some forms are distinct (narrow orchard and vineyard tractor, high-clearance tractor, potato planter and lifter): those are **kinds**; the grape harvester and hop picker are further kinds seen but not placed (section 3). The crop heads themselves are a **generated view**.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommended: C7's shared order.** Kinds go canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states. In Tractors & carriers the canonical kind goes first (standard four-wheel tractor), then by increasing specialisation (row-crop, narrow orchard and vineyard, high-clearance, slope, forestry); in implement leaves the most generic kind first (mouldboard plough before specialist ploughs). Named forms go by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested; non-maker named forms, if any are attested, come first by first-attested year, then alphabetically (CV-21, C7). Variants go by `variant_code` or year. Ascending rated hp or capacity (every source that orders tractors does so: Yanmar's lineup 14.5 to 114 PS [S10], Deere's classes [S12], TractorData's hp-sorted pages [S22]) and chronology (TractorData also sorts by year [S22]) are offered as generated sort options, not the base order.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure: Candidate A.** C7 has ten sub-families by the job in the farm and forest cycle (Tractors & carriers, Soil tillage, Seeding and planting, Crop care, Harvesting, Hay and forage, Post-harvest handling, Livestock and dairy, Forestry, and the closing sub-family Parts and accessories made for one machine); leaves are optional and stated per sub-family (running gear for tractors, operation on the ground for tillage, stage of the herd, flock or dairy chain for livestock, the machine served for parts); kinds are machine types, designs for a task for wheeled tractors (size, power and hitch category are attributes), and herd-scale function and installation form for livestock equipment; named forms are maker-bound models and lines, harvested in stage 3 without a cap, with the tractor model sets in registers. **Maximum depth four levels below the category: sub-family, leaf (optional, `leaf = null` where a sub-family holds about 12 kinds or fewer, as a guide, A-21), kind, named form.** Most branches need three. The worked tree is a seed of 105 kinds; the estimate for the full set is about 200 (section 4).
- **Choice level:** machine type for implements and machines; design for a task for tractors (C-6); herd-scale function and installation form for livestock equipment.
- **Tier:** none on kinds; spec-ranked, carried as numeric attributes (engine hp, PTO hp, lift, width, capacity, `hitch_category`) with the standard named; tier entries only on makers, lines, named forms, variants or plans, each on one fetched source; none entered yet.
- **Makers:** platform for tractors, self-propelled machines and forestry; incidental for generic implements. One maker row per maker in the domain-wide ledger, seeded from TractorData and the marketplace maker filters (C-19) and grown without a cap; lines, maker-relations, school, compatibility (hitch categories), jurisdiction, alias and register tables as in section 6.
- **Scenes and views, not levels:** scene tags for vintage tractor collecting and for small-acreage and compact-tractor owning; generated views for crop (vine, orchard, hops, potato, rice), market (municipal, forestry), era (vintage), region (Japan paddy), precision and autonomous, forestry system (cut-to-length against whole-tree) and maker size class.

**Boundaries as decided.** C7 holds tractors with a rear PTO and a Category 1 or larger three-point hitch (whoever buys them) and the implements made for them; lawn and garden tractors without that PTO and hitch, and walk-behind tractors and tillers without it, are C2 (K-99). Field irrigation and its pumps are C7 (K-100, K-175); herd and flock equipment including livestock EID stick readers, feed mixers and commercial hatchers is C7 (K-101); fencing and energisers are C8 (K-195); tractor-driven splitters and chippers and forest-machine chippers are C7, hand-held chainsaws and tree-surgery chippers C2, axes and log splitters C1 (K-173, C7-O1); Unimog, ATV and utility vehicles are G1 and tool-carrier tractors C7 (K-93); excavators, skid steers, telehandlers and forklifts are C1 unless they do a farm or forestry job (K-194).

- Tier schemes and price bands are empty until pages are fetched (Nebraska Tractor Test Laboratory, dated list prices).
