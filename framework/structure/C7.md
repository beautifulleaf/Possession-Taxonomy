## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C7 Farm & Forestry Equipment.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 12): *made for **agricultural or forestry production**: tractors and anything made to be drawn by, mounted on or driven from a tractor; self-propelled farm and forestry machines; livestock handling, milking, shearing and slaughter equipment for herds and flocks; commercial crop, poultry and dairy equipment.* Note on the step: equipment for a household garden, allotment, pets, horses, or a few backyard hens or hives is C2 or B10, "including garden and compact lawn tractors"; animal feed in any quantity is B10. Category line: "tractors and their implements, self-propelled farm and forestry machines, livestock handling and milking equipment."

**Notes and precedence sentences elsewhere that touch C7:**
- Step 11 (G1) note: "A machine that carries its operator while it does other work, such as a tractor, ride-on mower or excavator, goes by that work." Trailers and horseboxes are G1 (step 11 is tried before step 12).
- Step 10 (E4) note: slaughter and stunning tools for livestock are C7.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): a part, attachment or consumable made for one kind of object goes to that object's category, so a tractor tyre, front loader or wheel weight follows the tractor into C7.
- Step 13 (B10): housing, feeding, tack, carriers, veterinary goods and identification microchips for animals are B10; animal feed in any quantity is B10.
- Step 29 (C2): mowers, trimmers, irrigation, pressure washers and snow clearers for grounds are C2.
- Step 32 (C1, C8): tools and machines that "lift, power or repair" are C1; building products, fencing and fuels are C8.
- Step 4 (G2): barns, stables and other buildings a person can enter are G2; Step 24 (D2): GPS units are D2.
- Appendix A: market (domestic, commercial, professional), user, setting, brand and maker, price and tier, era, region and scene are modifiers, never levels.

**Collectors and resale.**

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **EU Regulation 167/2013, Article 4** [S16]: legal categories T (wheeled tractors: T1, T2, T3, T4.1 high-clearance, T4.2 extra-wide, T4.3 low-clearance, each a or b by speed), C (track-laying), R (trailers R1–R4), S (interchangeable towed equipment S1, S2). The cuts are track width, unladen mass, ground clearance and design speed, not the buyer's power class.
- **HS 8432 and 8433** [S18][S17]: soil preparation (ploughs, disc harrows, other harrows/scarifiers/cultivators/rotovators, seeders and planters with no-till separate, manure spreaders, fertiliser distributors; sprayers excluded) and harvesting (mowers, haymaking machinery, balers, combine harvester-threshers, root and tuber harvesters, forage harvesters self-propelled or not, produce grading). Customs divides by machine function.
- **ISO/TC 23** [S19]: subcommittees for Tractors, Crop protection, Harvesting and conservation, Powered lawn and garden, Forestry machinery, Hand-held powered lawn/garden and forest machinery, Irrigation, Agricultural electronics, plus a milking machines working group.
- **Three-point linkage** [S15][S14]: ASAE S217 was replaced by ANSI/ASABE AD730:2009 with categories 1N, 1, 2N, 2, 3N, 3, 4N and 4 (the ISO 730 family).

**Museums and reference works.**
- **Museum of English Rural Life** [S23]: farm tools, implements and machinery; farm-based processing equipment; equipment for management of farm livestock; agricultural service industries. **Wikipedia** [S20][S21]: the list of agricultural machinery is grouped by job (tractor and power, soil cultivation, planting, fertilizer and pesticide dispensing, harvesting and post-harvest, hay making, loading); the tractor article classes tractors first by axles and wheels (two-wheel; four-wheel 2WD, front-assist, 4WD articulated; tracked) and then by use (standard, row-crop, utility, compact utility 20–50 hp, garden). **USDA Forest Service** [S26] and **FAO** [S28] describe forest and dairy machines by mechanism: feller bunchers by drive-to-tree versus swing-boom and by head (bunching, processing, felling); milking by bucket, pipeline and parlour, with parlours split into one unit per two stalls or one per stall, and rotaries, trigons and polygons apart.

**Where the sources agree.** All of them put the job in the crop or forest cycle ahead of the maker for implements (tillage, planting, spreading, spraying, harvest, hay, grain, livestock, forestry), and all give the tractor a head of its own. Forestry is a separate head in Mascus, traktorpool and Brazil [S1][S3][S7] and a separate ISO subcommittee [S19]. Hitch category and horsepower are the compatibility facts everywhere [S6][S8][S14].

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- **Tractor classes.** Deere's compact tops out at 65.9 HP and its utility starts at 50 [S12]; Tractor Iron's compact is 25–50 and utility 45–100+ [S13]; Wikipedia's compact utility is 20–50 [S21]; the law cuts by track width and mass [S16]; the French and German marketplaces cut by specialised design, not power [S3][S4]; Japan cuts by horsepower band and running gear [S10].
- **Loaders.** Front loaders sit under Traktoren in traktorpool but telehandlers sit under Hoflader/Bagger/Stapler [S3]; Mascus has telehandlers for agriculture under agriculture [S2]; Machinery Pete has Loaders and Lifts [S5].
- **Trailers and wagons.** Transport trailers, loader wagons, silage wagons, slurry tankers and bale carriers share traktorpool's Transportfahrzeuge head with Ladewagen listed also under Siliertechnik and Grünlandgeräte [S3]; the EU law counts trailers as R, a different category from tractors [S16].
- **Mulchers, chippers, winches.** The same machine is listed under vineyard, grassland, municipal and forestry heads [S3], and under forestry at Mascus [S1].
- **Animals and feed** are a head inside Agriaffaires' farm tree [S4], which the framework puts outside C7 (animals out, feed B10).
- **Parts.** Tyres, rims and tracks have their own top group in traktorpool and Agriaffaires [S3][S4]; the framework (S3) files them with the tractor.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**For implements and self-propelled machines the first choice is the job**: which stage of the crop, hay, livestock or forest cycle the machine does. Machinery Pete's mandatory first selector is Category (Tillage, Planting, Harvesting, Hay and Forage, Livestock Equipment) and only then Make [S5]; traktorpool's 305 categories are mostly job names (Bodenbearbeitung, Saat-/Pflanztechnik, Erntemaschinen, Grünlandgeräte) [S3]; the Indian implement list is by job with Tillage (524) far ahead [S8]; the HS headings split the same way (soil preparation, harvesting) [S18][S17]; the ISO committees likewise (crop protection, harvesting and conservation, forestry machinery, milking) [S19]; Wikipedia's list groups by job [S20].

**For tractors the first choice is size, then specialisation.** Deere's catalogue opens on four horsepower classes [S12]; Yanmar's lineup is ordered by horsepower band [S10]; Farm Trader lists Rated Engine Power and Drive Type as the first listing facets [S6]; Tractor Iron tells buyers to compare four numbers (engine HP, PTO HP, weight, 3-point lift) rather than the class label [S13]. The French and German marketplaces split tractors by specialised design first (vineyard, orchard, straddle, slope, forestry, Unimog) [S3][S4], and the EU law splits by track width, mass and clearance [S16].

**The compatibility check follows**: the implement must match the tractor's hitch category and power [S14][S8][S11]. Hitch category is a spec printed on every implement listing [S8] and a standard (AD730) [S15], so it works as a join between tractors and implements, not as a way of dividing either.

**Maker comes after type in marketplaces** (Make is the third selector at Machinery Pete [S5]; popular links at Mascus are by make within a type [S2]) **but first in communities** (TractorByNet forums are by maker [S25]; TractorData pages are by maker [S22]). Both are consistent with a type tree plus a maker ledger.

**For livestock equipment the first choice is the function**: handle, weigh, identify, milk, cool [S31][S30][S3]. Sources agree less on the boundaries of this group than on any other (section 1).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's limits: one stated axis per level, modifiers off the tree, at most four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). "Leaf" is used only where a family has four or more kinds; elsewhere kinds sit directly under the sub-family.

### Candidate A: by the job in the farm and forest cycle (recommended)

- **Level 1, sub-family: the job the machine does** (Power, Soil tillage, Seeding and planting, Crop care, Harvesting, Hay and forage, Post-harvest handling, Livestock and dairy, Forestry, Tractor fittings).
- **Level 2, leaf (optional): the machine family** (Combines, Balers, Milking installations); for tractors the axis is the running gear (wheeled, articulated 4WD, tracked, two-wheel, carrier).
- **Level 3, kind: the generic machine type buyers compare** (mouldboard plough, power harrow, disc mower, cattle crush); for wheeled tractors the kind is the consensus class or design (sub-compact, compact utility, utility, row-crop, orchard/vineyard, high-clearance, slope, forestry).
- **Level 4, named form: a model or model line** (Yanmar YT3R, Gallagher TWR-5).

Fit with sources: Machinery Pete, traktorpool, Mascus, Farm Trader, the Brazilian and Indian lists, the HS headings, ISO committees and Wikipedia all open on job [S5][S3][S1][S6][S7][S8][S17][S18][S19][S20].

Where it breaks:
1. **Mulcher/flail mower** serves grassland, vineyard, municipal and forestry; the job is "cut vegetation", so one kind under Hay and forage with crop and setting as views (traktorpool lists it four times [S3]).
2. **Slurry tanker** both carries and spreads: Crop care (spreading) wins over transport (section 9).
3. **Front loader** is a tractor fitting but the loader is also a job (loading); the S3 rule puts it with the tractor.
4. **Telehandler, skid steer, excavator** are sold in farm marketplaces [S2][S3][S5] but do no farm-specific job; they fall outside this sub-family list (section 9).

### Candidate B: by how the machine is carried and powered

- **Level 1: carrier and coupling** (tractor; mounted/3-point implement; trailed implement; PTO-driven; self-propelled machine; stationary installation).
- **Level 2: job. Level 3: kind. Level 4: named form.**

Fit: the EU law separates tractors (T, C) from trailers (R) and interchangeable towed equipment (S) [S16]; Deere keeps implements on a separate page [S12]; Farm Trader lists Tractor Connection (Linkage) [S6]; the Japanese article starts from 3-point versus 2-point link [S11].

Where it breaks:
1. **Sprayer, mower, baler** exist mounted, trailed and self-propelled; buyers compare them as one job (Mascus has "trailed sprayers" and John Deere self-propelled sprayers in one type list [S2]), so B scatters one comparison set across three heads.
2. **Milking parlours, grain dryers, cattle crushes** are stationary and have no carrier at all.
3. **Rice transplanter, combine** are self-propelled but a buyer treats them as the job, not the carrier [S10].
4. The hitch category is a modifier that joins two kinds, not a way of dividing them.

### Candidate C: by production system (crop, grassland, livestock, forestry, specialty)

- **Level 1: production system. Level 2: job. Level 3: kind. Level 4: named form.**

Fit: Machinery Pete has Specialty Crops, Hay and Forage and Livestock Equipment [S5]; traktorpool has Kartoffeltechnik, Obst- und Weinbau, Hopfentechnik, Gemüseanbau [S3]; Agriaffaires has Viti-vinicole and cultures spécialisées [S4].

Where it breaks:
1. **Tractor, sprayer, mulcher, loader** serve every system; traktorpool repeats Kompakttraktoren under four heads [S3], which breaks "one home".
2. **A potato planter** is a planter for a crop; its sibling is the precision planter, not the potato harvester.
3. **Crop** is not on the framework's modifier list as such but behaves like setting or scene (Appendix A): it is better as a generated view.
4. **Forestry** is the only system with its own machine kinds (harvester, forwarder, skidder), so C collapses to A plus a view.

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds.** About 200 (an estimate, not a count). Basis: traktorpool's 305 categories [S3] contain roughly 100 that fall outside C7 (construction machines, wheel loaders and forklifts, trucks and trailers, lawn and garden items, tyres and rims, model tractors) and several that repeat a kind under a second head; HS 8432 and 8433 name 9 and 11 six-digit subheadings [S18][S17] but the marketplaces split them far finer (Pflüge, Grubber, Scheibeneggen, Kurzscheibeneggen, Kreiseleggen, Fräsen and Packer are seven different headings under what HS calls soil preparation [S3][S18]). By sub-family: Power 12 to 15; Soil tillage 15 to 20; Seeding and planting 8 to 10; Crop care 25 to 30 (traktorpool alone lists 17 slurry and manure subcategories and 6 mechanical-weeding ones [S3]); Harvesting 25 to 30 (traktorpool names about 12 crop-specific harvesters, Wikipedia about as many [S3][S20]); Hay and forage 12 to 15; Post-harvest 10 to 12; Livestock and dairy 30 to 40 (more if feeding and housing equipment at herd scale is C7, section 9); Forestry 15 to 20 [S1][S3]; Tractor fittings 10 to 15.

**Named forms.** Order of 10,000 or more. TractorData lists 280 makers, with 275 International Harvester models alone [S22]; TractorJunction shows 1,119 implements from one country [S8]; Yanmar's Japanese tractor lineup spans at least six series from 14.5 to 114 PS [S10]. Vintage models (Oldtimer-Traktoren 206 on one marketplace [S3]; TractorData's 1908-onwards span [S22]) multiply the tractor count.

**Where depth concentrates.** Tractors (a small number of kinds, a very large number of maker model lines), combines and self-propelled sprayers, forestry machines (few kinds, high price, named forms only), and livestock weighing and handling (a few kinds with many indicator and crush models [S30]). Tillage and hay-making are wide with shallow depth: many kinds, few named forms that anyone tracks.

**Broad, medium and narrow leaves** (traktorpool listing counts, a single platform, shown only to indicate relative size [S3]):
- *Broad:* mowers (3,962), combine harvesters (3,128), cultivators (2,704), tipping trailers (2,578; G1 candidate), ploughs (2,112), balers (1,972).
- *Medium:* forage harvesters (884), loader wagons (788), potato lifters (624), feed mixer wagons (1,108), forestry winches (436), wood chippers (605).
- *Narrow:* milking robots (51), forest harvesters (64), forwarders (48), grape harvesters (19), carrot lifters (28), hop pickers (1), skidders (1).

## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers.** The evidence puts that level at the machine type, one step above the variant, and at the power class for tractors.

- **Implements and machines: kind = machine type.** Marketplaces list, count and filter at this level: Pflüge, Grubber, Kreiseleggen, Fräsen, Düngerstreuer, Pressen are the leaves where hundreds to thousands of ads sit and where makers are compared [S3]; Indian implement pages carry one tractor power range per implement type and a maker [S8]; Japanese guides compare attachments by type with the main makers named for each [S11]; HS and ISO committees stop at the same level [S17][S18][S19]. Finer variants are facets on the listing, not separate heads: Farm Trader's Class, Cutting Width, Working Width and Tractor Connection (linkage or trailed) [S6]; Webbline's bale wrapper is listed with "Wrapper Type: Satellite", "Bale Type: Round and Rectangular", "Tractor Mounting: Trailed" [S6]. So reversible versus fixed mouldboard, mounted versus trailed, and working width stay as attributes of a kind.
- **Tractors: kind = consensus class or design.** Deere, Tractor Iron, Wikipedia and Yanmar all compare makers inside a class, and the class names are shared enough to compare across makers even though the boundaries differ [S12][S13][S21][S10]. Below the class, buyers compare models by engine HP, PTO HP, weight and lift capacity [S13], which are named-form attributes. Horsepower band is therefore a spec on the named form, not a level; class names are soft kinds (section 10).
- **Self-propelled machines: kind = machine type, header or head is its own kind.** Combine harvesters and their headers are different headings with different buyers and prices in traktorpool, Agriaffaires and the Brazilian portal [S3][S4][S7]; Ponsse sells harvesters, forwarders and harvester heads as three product lines [S27].
- **Livestock equipment: deeper than implements.** Handling, weighing and EID readers are separate shop categories at Te Pari [S30], and a weigh-scale indicator is compared by feature and maker (Gallagher, Tru-Test, Te Pari side by side [S30]) separately from the platform or load bars it works with [S30]. Milking installations are compared by layout (bucket, pipeline, parlour; one unit per two stalls or per stall; rotary, trigon, polygon [S28]), so milking parlour layout is a kind-level distinction.
- **Forestry: kind = machine type.** Mascus counts each separately (harvesters 1,844, forwarders 1,338, feller buncher 171, skidders 148) [S1]; the feller-buncher mechanism (drive-to-tree versus swing-boom) is a variant a buyer picks by terrain [S26], so it is a kind or an attribute depending on how many makers differ; the evidence here does not settle it.
- **Where comparison sits above type.** Mulchers, mowers and sweepers are bought across five markets (agriculture, vineyard, municipal, forestry, grounds) [S3]; the kind stays one, with market as a view.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended: spec-ranked.** Every source that orders machines orders them by a measurable number.
- **Tractors:** engine HP, PTO HP, operating weight and 3-point lift capacity [S13]; hitch category 0 to 4 with horsepower ranges [S14][S15]; Deere's class by horsepower [S12]; Yanmar's ascending band list [S10]; Farm Trader's Rated Engine Power facet [S6]; TractorData's hp-sorted brand pages [S22].
- **Implements:** the tractor power the implement needs (Power HP on every Indian listing [S8]), working or cutting width, seedbox capacity [S6].
- **Combines and harvesters:** horsepower, header width and threshing type (Yanmar 14 to 138 PS combines [S10]).
- **Milking and livestock:** cows per hour [S28], load-bar capacity and record capacity of indicators [S30].

**How it appears as a modifier, never as a level:** numeric attributes on the named form (engine hp, PTO hp, lift kg, working width m, capacity); a hitch category tag on both tractor and implement (the join); a class label only as the kind name where the sources share it. Class boundaries overlap between makers [S12][S13], so a model gets its maker's label and its own hp, never a forced slot.

**Consensus-ranked elements.** Maker reputation (premium European or American lines versus value lines) is real in the forums [S25] but no fetched page gives a published ordering. It is not recommended as a tier; if wanted it is a price-band modifier with its source added later. A lead for attested performance tests (the Nebraska Tractor Test Laboratory; OECD codes named in EU 167/2013's type-approval schedule) was seen only in search excerpts, so it needs a fetched page before it is used.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended: platform**, for tractors, self-propelled machines and forestry; **incidental** for generic implements and for livestock accessories.
- *Platform evidence.* Forums are organised by maker, with Buying/Pricing and Owning/Operating per maker [S25]; TractorData is maker first [S22]; marketplaces make Make a top selector [S5][S2]; Ponsse sells the machine, head and information system as one system [S27]; John Deere's iMatch quick hitch is Category I only and standardises hookup geometry for iMatch-spec implements [S14]; implements built for full Category I may not fit a Limited Category I tractor, which has narrower lift-arm spacing [S14].
- *Incidental evidence.* The three-point hitch is a published standard (ISO 730 family, now AD730) so implements work across makers if the category matches [S15][S14]; Te Pari sells Gallagher, Tru-Test and its own scale indicators side by side [S30]; the Japanese guide lists many makers per implement type [S11].

**Maker ledger: yes.** Rows: maker, known-for kinds (tractors, combines, forage, tillage, seeding, livestock, forestry), country, years active (needed for the vintage makers: International Harvester 1908–1985 in [S22]), scene tags (vintage, specialty crop, small-holder Asia), price band, and platform notes (hitch categories supported, quick-hitch system). Named forms link to the maker row. Each row needs an attested page; the 280 makers in [S22] and the maker lists in [S1] and [S11] are a ready source of rows. Maker tier is left blank until a page that ranks makers is fetched.

## 7. Scenes, cultures and views

- **Vintage and antique tractors.** Oldtimer-Traktoren, Tracteur ancien, Matériel de collection, Yesterday's Tractors and TractorByNet Vintage [S3][S4][S24][S25]. Not a distinct form: **a generated view and an era tag on named forms**, with maker rows carrying year spans [S22]. Implements of the same era sit in Implement Alley [S24] and use the same kinds.
- **Specialty crops** (vine, orchard, hops, potatoes, vegetables). traktorpool has top groups for each [S3]; Machinery Pete has Specialty Crops [S5]; Agriaffaires has Viti-vinicole [S4]. Some forms are distinct (straddle tractor, grape harvester, hop picker, potato planter and lifter): those are **kinds**; the crop heads themselves are a **generated view**.
- **Rice and small-holder Asia.** Rice transplanter, puddling harrow, bund-plastering machine, head-feeding combine, power tiller, delta tractor [S9][S10][S11]. These are **distinct forms (kinds)** with a **region tag**; the rice, vegetable and dairy-pasture lineups are a view.
- **Forestry systems.** Cut-to-length (harvester plus forwarder, Ponsse's specialty [S27]) versus whole-tree (feller buncher plus skidder [S1][S26]) is a **generated view over forestry kinds**; the machines are kinds.
- **Hobby and small acreage.** Compact and sub-compact tractors with front loaders and mid-mount mowers, covered by the user and setting modifiers [S13][S12][S25]. **Tag and view, not a level.**
- **Municipal and winter service** (Kommunaltechnik 9,480; sweepers, snow blades, bank mowers [S3]): **view over implement kinds**; ownership by a council versus a farm is a market modifier.
- **Precision and autonomous farming** (GPS and precision-farming kit 509 on traktorpool [S3]; Yanmar's robot and auto tractors and auto combines [S10]; Iseki's smart machines [S9]). **Tag on named forms** (guidance, auto-steer, robot).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommended: canonical form first, then spec order, with chronology only for vintage.** Within a leaf, the most generic kind goes first (utility tractor before specialty tractors; mouldboard plough before specialist ploughs). Named forms go by maker series, then by ascending rated hp or capacity, because every source that orders tractors or machines does so by hp: Yanmar's lineup ascends 14.5 to 114 PS [S10], Deere's classes ascend by hp [S12], TractorData offers hp-sorted pages per maker [S22]. For vintage models chronology is the natural second order (TractorData also sorts by year [S22]); it should sit as an era sort option, not the base order.
