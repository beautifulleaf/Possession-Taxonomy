## 0. Category

**Code and name:** F2 Art (domain F, Culture, Records & Meaning).

**Membership test (placement key step 20):** "Made to be **looked at as a picture** on a flat or hung surface (paintings, drawings, prints, posters, art photographs, calligraphy, tapestries and textile hangings, artist trading cards), **or as free-standing sculpture**, including ornamental figurines of people and animals made in ceramic, glass, metal, stone, wood or resin, whatever the edition or price?" Category description: "pictures and free-standing sculpture, including ornamental figurines, whatever the edition or price."

**Notes and rules that touch F2 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):**
- Step 20 note: "figures of characters, action figures, vinyl and collectible-series figures, and bobbleheads are E1. Garden statues and gnomes are B4. Vases, bowls and other vessels are B4 or B5, however artistic."
- Step 19 note (F5): "photographic prints and slides of the owner's own life" are F5; "Photographs made as art, and posters, are F2." Step 5 (I2): the owner's photographs and digital artwork held as files are I2 ("the carrier decides: prints are F5"); NFTs are I2.
- S2: "an original painting under 'painting'"; individual items are never kinds. S5: "A signed football is a football; a collected teapot is a teapot; an antique chair is a chair; a commemorative plate is a plate." Step 28 note and S3 (C4): blank canvas, paint, clay are C4; a finished picture or sculpture is F2 (C4 memo precedence).
- Step 14 (F4) is tried before step 20: icons, shrines and devotional objects made for use in worship are F4. Step 15 (F3) is tried before step 20: "non-game trading cards" are F3, although step 20 lists "artist trading cards".
- Step 30 (B4): picture frames, wall mirrors, decorative bowls, garden ornaments, "decorative replica weapons" are B4. Tapestries and textile hangings are F2 (B1, B2 memos); rugs are B2.
- Appendix A: modifiers never split inner levels (region and culture, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, setting, motive, origin, material, condition, edition).

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

### 1.1 Reference works and thesauri
- **Getty AAT (AAT-VW).** Under Objects Facet > Visual and Verbal Communication > Visual Works > `visual works (works)` sits the node label `<visual works by material or technique>`. Its children include paintings, drawings, prints, photographs, sculpture, collages, mosaics, pastels, calligraphy, textile art (with tapestries and woven pictures beneath it), installations, mixed media works, new media art, time-based works and works on paper, plus craft-type work such as lacquerware, glassware and metalwork. Each of the five main types is subdivided by further node labels, each one axis: paintings by form, location or context, material or technique, and subject type; drawings by function, material or technique, maker and subject type; prints by form, function, and process or technique (and the status nodes original prints, interpretive prints, reproductive prints); photographs by form, function, technique, and subject or context; sculpture by form, function, location or context, material, technique and subject type (AAT-VW). This is the clearest published example of "one axis per level" and it does not put subject at the top.
- **AAT paintings (AAT-PA)** by material or technique: acrylic, bark, canvas, casein, encaustic, finger, fluorescent, frescoes, hide, ink resist, ink wash, oil (with oil sketches beneath), sand, sargas, tempera, theorems, Tuechleins, and watercolors (with aquarelles and gouaches beneath). Support (canvas, bark, hide) and binder (oil, tempera) share one node, a mixed axis.
- **AAT prints (AAT-PR)** by process or technique: collagraphs, color prints, digital prints (inkjet, laser, dye diffusion thermal transfer, thermal), embossed prints, intaglio prints, monoprints, photomechanical prints, planographic prints, relief prints, stencil prints, restrikes, rétirage prints and others such as vitreographs and nature prints.
- **AAT sculpture (AAT-SC)** by material: acroliths, chryselephantine, found object sculpture (with junk sculpture), glazed sculpture, ivories, jades, neon sculpture, scrimshaws, soft sculpture, terracottas, waxworks. A separate node, bronzes, sits beside the labels.
- **AAT photographs (AAT-PH)** by technique, in two sub-labels: picture-taking technique (aerial, chronophotographs, photobooth pictures, photomicrographs, snapshots, staged, stereoscopic) and processing or presentation technique (cased photographs, clichés-verre, contact prints, digital photographs, hand-colored, lenticular, photograms, photomurals).
- **Getty vocabularies (GETTY-V, AAT-PDF).** AAT holds generic terms (agents, work types, materials, styles, techniques); the Union List of Artist Names (ULAN) is a separate authority for named artists, studios and patrons. The generic/named split is the same as the framework's kind/maker split.
- **Graphics Atlas (GA)**, the Image Permanence Institute's process identifier, groups image-making processes in four blocks: pre-photographic (intaglio, planographic, relief), photomechanical (collotype, letterpress halftone, offset lithograph, photogravure, rotogravure, Woodburytype), photographic (albumen, ambrotype, bromoil, carbon, chromogenic, cyanotype, daguerreotype, gelatin and collodion printing-out paper, gum dichromate, instant, platinum, salted paper, silver dye bleach, silver gelatin, tintype and more) and digital (electrophotography, inkjet, thermal). Prints and photographs are one process family there.
- **Print Center New York glossary (PCNY)** separates techniques (chine-collé, collagraph, digital, drypoint, etching, intaglio, letterpress, monotype, offset, photogravure, screenprinting) from annotations (artist's proof, printer's proof, bon à tirer, hors commerce, edition, impression, proof, printer, publisher).
- **Artsy Art Genome (ART-G)** is a flat gene list with eight top groups (Artistic Disciplines, Subject Matter, Styles and Movements, Visual Qualities, Medium and Techniques, Time Periods, Geographic Regions, Materials). Its Medium and Techniques genes include oil painting, watercolor, pastel, tempera, encaustic, fresco, drawing, etching/engraving, lithograph, silkscreen, woodcut and linocut, monotype, digital print, photography, black-and-white photography, photogram, sculpture, soft sculpture, kinetic sculpture, polychrome sculpture, poster, hanging scroll, handscroll, East Asian calligraphy, NFT.

### 1.2 Standards and trade classification
- **Harmonized System chapter 97 (HS97).** 9701 paintings, drawings and pastels executed entirely by hand (collages, mosaics and similar decorative plaques are subheadings); 9702 original engravings, prints and lithographs, defined as "impressions produced directly … of one or of several plates wholly executed by hand by the artist, irrespective of the process or of the material employed by him, but not including any mechanical or photomechanical process"; 9703 original sculpture and statuary "in any material" (and the first 12 castings or reproductions). Frames of "a kind and of a value normal to those articles" are classified with the picture. Mass-produced reproductions and works of conventional craftsmanship are excluded from 9703. HS divides by discipline (three headings), splits original from reproduction, and treats material as irrelevant. Photographs are not in chapter 97 in the pages fetched.

### 1.3 Retailers and marketplaces (US/UK, France/EU, Germany, Japan, China, India)
- **Singulart (SGL-PA, SGL-PR, SGL-SC).** First split is Medium: Painting, Sculpture, Photography, Drawing, Print, Work on Paper, Textile, Digital, Other Media. Inside a medium the facets differ: Print has a technique facet (Giclée print, Lithography, Screen Printing, Relief Printing, Engraving, Monotype); Sculpture has Types (Freestanding, Wall-object, Outdoor, Bust) and a long materials list (bronze, steel, resin, wood, marble, ceramics, clay, stone, terracotta, plaster, neon tube, taxidermy and more); Painting has a technique list that mixes paints (acrylic, oil, gouache, tempera, watercolor) with materials added to paint (sand, plaster, resin, gold leaf). Themes, styles, size and price bands run across all media.
- **Saatchi Art (SAA).** Category row: Paintings, Photography, Drawings, Mixed Media, Sculpture, Collage, Printmaking, Digital, Installation. Photography has Style, Subject and Medium (Digital, Black and White, Color, Polaroid) facets, and limited and open edition prints.
- **Artsy (ART-C, ART-S).** Collect filters: Rarity (Unique, Limited Edition, Open Edition, Unknown Edition), Medium (Painting, Photography, Sculpture, Prints, Work on Paper, NFT), price, time period, colour, framed, signed. The help page lists the filter order: Artist, Rarity, Medium, Material, Price, Size, Availability, Ways to Buy, Artist Nationality, Location, Time Period, Color, Gallery, Framed, Signature.
- **Mojarto, India (MOJ).** Category tiles Serigraph (319), Folk Art (287), Painting (10,091), Sculpture (304); footer category list Digital Art, Drawing, Painting, Photography, Printmaking, Sculpture | 3D; named Indian artists are navigation items.
- **LUMAS, Germany (LUM).** Sells signed and limited editions of photographs and prints; its item categories are edition classes (Limited Edition, Open Edition, small open edition "Petites", small limited edition "Darlings"), with format (square, across, upright, panorama) and colour fields, and a separate image year and edition year.
- **Das Kunstlager, Germany (KUN).** Categories: Acryl & Ölgemälde, Collagen, Leinwanddrucke, Aquarelle, Metal Art, Fotokunst, Kunstdruck, Wandteppiche; its explainer divides "nach Gattung" by medium (oil, acrylic, watercolour, photo art/prints, mixed technique, sculpture).
- **Japan: Motokawa (MTK, MTK-T).** The shop is split into prints by technique (lithograph 346, silkscreen 1,000, paper screen 31, woodblock 226, copperplate 137, giclée 577, other 69), paintings by medium (watercolour 262, oil 318, acrylic 119, nihonga 37, pastel 106, ink-and-colour 60, mixed media 159, other 39), and parallel views by image subject, size, price, and famous domestic and overseas artists. Its technique guide lists lithograph, silkscreen, giclée, aquatint, copperplate, drypoint, embossing, etching, linocut, mezzotint, woodblock, monotype, paper screen, Ilfochrome, nihonga, gouache, pastel, oil, acrylic, and defines nihonga by materials (mineral pigment, animal glue on paper or silk) against yōga/oil.
- **Japan: This Is Gallery (TIG), Nikkei Art (NKA).** TIG counts paintings 28,238 (acrylic, pastel, pen drawing, nihonga, ink, watercolour, oil beneath), prints 1,951, sculpture and relief 1,084, art photography 946, collage, poster, postcard, crafts, illustration. Nikkei Art's categories are bronze, ceramics, glass, original paintings, prints, other paintings; its genre list names nihonga, oil, lithograph, silkscreen, woodblock, copperplate, reproduction and photo art.
- **China: Zhongyi (ZY) and the auction portal (MSJ).** Zhongyi's 2026 directory has 72 classes: Chinese painting by subject and technique (landscape ×10, figure ×10, flower-and-bird ×10), then mural, traditional and installation sculpture, lacquer painting, mixed material, mineral pigment, contemporary ink, new media, NFT, classical oil, acrylic, watercolour, pastel, traditional and edition prints, five script styles and seal carving, then subject classes. The portal's browse tags are 国画, 油画, 版画, 雕塑, 书法, 速写 (Chinese painting, oil, print, sculpture, calligraphy, sketch).

### 1.4 Auction houses, resale and collectors
- **Sotheby's and Christie's departments (SOT-D, CHR-D).** Mixed axes: some departments are medium-only (Prints; Photographs; Old Master Drawings; Digital Art), some are era and region (19th Century European Paintings, Impressionist and Modern, Post-War and Contemporary, Chinese Paintings Classical/Modern, Aboriginal Art, Orientalist Art), and some are form-and-region (European Sculpture and Works of Art, British Watercolours and Drawings 1550-1850). Christie's also lists Prints and Multiples, Comic Strips and Illustrations, Outsider Art, Portrait Miniatures, Japanese Art. Poly Auction Hong Kong (POLY) separates Modern and Contemporary Art from Fine Chinese Paintings and Calligraphy by tradition. Auction departments are sale segments, not a taxonomy.
- **Collector guides (SOT-PR, SOT-PH, SAF).** Prints are divided relief, intaglio, planographic (Sotheby's) and Relief, Intaglio, Planographs, Stencils (Saffronart), each with named techniques beneath (woodcut, linocut; engraving, etching, dry-point, mezzotint, aquatint; lithography, offset lithography; screenprint; monotype as the one unique print method). Photographs are described by process: gelatin silver, chromogenic, Cibachrome, dye-transfer, inkjet and archival pigment, and alternative processes (cyanotype, tintype, gum bichromate, Polaroid, ambrotype). Sotheby's states that giclée "is simply a neologism used to market inkjet prints" and that collectors prefer a more specific designation.
- **Resale and market data (RYK, MUT).** The Japanese buyback site lists genres paintings, nihonga, prints, ink painting, oil, watercolour, ukiyo-e, lithograph/silkscreen, abstract, contemporary, and values by artist. MutualArt indexes "900k+ artists" and filters them by media (Painting, Prints and Graphic Art, Works on Paper, Sculpture, Photography).

### 1.5 Where the sources agree and split
- **Agree:** five disciplines everywhere (painting, drawing, print, photograph, sculpture: AAT, HS97, Singulart, Saatchi, Artsy, Mojarto, MutualArt, Sotheby's) and, one level down, process or medium (oil, etching, gelatin silver, bronze).
- **Split 1, the edge disciplines.** Singulart adds Work on Paper, Textile, Digital; Saatchi adds Collage, Mixed Media, Installation; AAT has calligraphy, textile art, mosaics and pastels as visual-work types; Zhongyi puts calligraphy and seal carving in the same directory.
- **Split 2, pastel.** AAT lists pastels as a visual-work type beside drawings and paintings; HS97 puts "paintings, drawings and pastels" in one heading; Motokawa and Zhongyi file pastel with paintings; Singulart lists pastel among painting techniques.
- **Split 3, sculpture's axis.** Singulart offers both type (freestanding, wall-object, bust) and material; AAT has sibling nodes for form, material and technique; HS97 ignores material.
- **Split 4, East Asian painting.** Japan and China separate nihonga/guohua from oil, by materials and tradition (MTK-T, ZY); Western sources file ink wash as a technique of painting (AAT-PA).
- **Split 5, prints against photographs.** GA treats them as one process family; Sotheby's, Christie's, Singulart, Artsy and Saatchi keep separate sections; HS97 excludes photomechanical impressions from original prints.
- **Split 6, edition.** Artsy and LUMAS make edition class a first-class filter; AAT and HS97 draw an original/reproduction line; the framework says edition is a modifier.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence.**
1. Medium comes first in navigation. Singulart's Medium list is its top control (SGL-PA); Saatchi's category row (SAA); Mojarto's category tiles (MOJ); Kunstlager's menu (KUN); Motokawa's two shop halves, prints and paintings (MTK); TIG's category tree (TIG); Zhongyi's classes (ZY); Artsy's Medium filter (ART-C).
2. Maker comes first in filters. Artsy's filter order starts with Artist and then Rarity before Medium (ART-S). Motokawa lists famous artists (345 overseas, 240 domestic) and recommended artists (2,073) as shop sections beside the technique sections (MTK). MutualArt is organised around 900k artists (MUT). Mojarto's footer lists named artists (MOJ). The Japanese buyback site prices by artist (RYK).
3. Within a medium, buyers choose technique. Singulart tells print buyers to "narrow our art prints for sale further by print technique, edition size or price" (SGL-PR). Motokawa sells prints in six technique sections with counts (MTK). Sotheby's guide organises prints by technique (SOT-PR).
4. Edition and authenticity are secondary but explicit: Artsy Rarity, LUMAS edition classes, Saffronart's definition of a limited edition (signed, numbered, matrix destroyed) (ART-C, LUM, SAF), and HS97's "wholly executed by hand".
5. Subject, size and colour are views across media: Singulart themes and size bands (SGL-PA), Motokawa "image" and size sections (MTK), Saatchi style and subject (SAA), Artsy colour (ART-C).

**Reading.** The first axis is discipline (what kind of picture or object), the second is the process or medium inside it, and the maker and the edition are what a buyer filters by. This matches the framework: placement is by designed function (looked at), kinds are by medium, and maker, edition, subject, size and price are modifiers.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep F2 as the top. Levels below the category are named sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels at most).

### Candidate A (recommended): discipline, then process family, then medium, then named form
- **Sub-family** axis: the discipline of the work (painting, drawing, print, photograph, sculpture, and three small edge disciplines). Basis: AAT-VW, HS97, Singulart, Saatchi, Artsy, Mojarto, MutualArt agree on the first five.
- **Leaf** axis: the family of making process. Prints: relief, intaglio, planographic, stencil, unique, digital, photomechanical (SAF, SOT-PR, AAT-PR, GA). Photographs: silver, chromogenic and dye, pigment and digital, alternative and historic, camera-less and instant (GA, SOT-PH). Sculpture: how the form is made (cast, carved, modelled and fired, assembled and found, soft and light) (AAT-SC, SGL-SC). Painting and drawing need no leaf (see section 4).
- **Kind** axis: the specific medium or technique (oil painting, etching, gelatin silver print, bronze sculpture). This is where buyers compare (section 4b).
- **Named form**: a distinct format, branded process or traditional form inside a kind (ukiyo-e inside woodcut; Cibachrome/Ilfochrome inside silver dye bleach print; Polaroid inside instant print; Iris/Piezography inside inkjet art print).
- **Example sub-families and leaves (12 kinds in brief):** Painting > oil painting, acrylic painting, watercolour, gouache, ink wash painting, nihonga; Print > Intaglio > etching, mezzotint; Print > Planographic > stone lithograph; Photograph > Silver > gelatin silver print, daguerreotype; Sculpture > Cast > bronze sculpture. A fuller tree is below.
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. *Pastel* is a drawing medium to AAT, a painting medium to Motokawa and Zhongyi, and its own heading beside drawings in HS97. One home is needed (open question 3).
  2. *Monotype* is a print by every source (SOT-PR, PCNY, SGL-PR) but is unique; the leaf "unique and mixed" holds it, which mixes the edition idea into a process axis. It stays a leaf of one kind only because every source places it among prints.
  3. *Sculpture leaf versus kind.* "Cast", "carved", "modelled" are processes but kinds are written as materials (bronze, marble, terracotta): bronze is always cast, but "stone carving" is both a process and a material. The leaf has to be defined by process and each kind named by its usual material, and the build team must not re-split a kind by material (Appendix A allows material only where it is the form, as here).
  4. *Giclée reproduction of a painting.* It is an inkjet print (a kind), but HS97 and AAT call it a reproduction, not an original; authenticity class is a modifier, not a place (section 5).

### Candidate B: support or material first (work on paper, canvas, panel, textile, three-dimensional)
- Axis at the sub-family level: what the work is on or made of. Basis: Singulart Medium (Work on Paper, Textile, Digital, Other Media) and Artsy Medium (Work on Paper) (SGL-PA, ART-C); AAT lists canvas, bark and hide paintings as kinds (AAT-PA).
- Example kinds: work on paper > watercolour, pastel, etching, lithograph, gelatin silver print, charcoal drawing; canvas > oil painting, acrylic painting; textile > tapestry, embroidered picture; three-dimensional > bronze sculpture, terracotta.
- **Where it breaks:** a watercolour, a lithograph and a gelatin silver print would sit together although no buyer compares them; oil on paper and oil on canvas would split one medium; a photograph printed on canvas or aluminium would move sub-family with its support. Appendix A bars material as a level unless it is the form; here it is not.

### Candidate C: unique, limited, open (edition class) first
- Axis at the sub-family level: how many exist. Basis: Artsy Rarity, LUMAS item categories, HS97's split of 9701 (by hand, unique) from 9702 (impressions), AAT's original, interpretive and reproductive print nodes (ART-C, LUM, HS97, AAT-VW).
- Example kinds: unique > oil painting, drawing, monotype; limited edition > etching, lithograph, bronze cast; open edition > poster, inkjet reprint.
- **Where it breaks:** the same kind appears in two or three places (a photograph can be unique, limited or open; a bronze is limited by definition, and HS97 allows the first 12 casts as original); edition is a modifier in Appendix A and the key says "whatever the edition or price"; edition class is a property of an individual edition, not of the generic type.

## 4. Depth and size

**Rough counts (estimates).**
- Generic kinds: about 120 to 150 under Candidate A: paintings 12 to 15, drawings 8 to 10, prints 28 to 35 (AAT-PR alone lists about 30 process nodes; PCNY, SOT-PR and SGL-PR add the commonly sold ones), photographs 22 to 30 (GA lists about 40 processes in all across its four blocks), sculpture 20 to 28, textile 6 to 8, calligraphy 3 to 5, collage and mosaic 4 to 6.
- Named forms: about 150 to 300 under those kinds (trademarked or historic processes, formats and regional forms). Examples seen: Ilfochrome, Cibachrome, Iris, Piezograph, Imagegraph, Polaroid, ukiyo-e, Baxter print, paper screen.
- Depth is uneven. Paintings and drawings need three levels (sub-family, kind, named form). Prints, photographs and sculpture use four. Textile, calligraphy and collage are shallow (three levels).
- **Where depth concentrates:** prints (the largest list of well-defined process names, with a separate collector literature, SOT-PR, SAF, PCNY, AAT-PR) and photographs (GA). Sculpture is wide (many materials) but each kind is shallow.
- **Breadth.** Broad: oil painting, acrylic painting, watercolour, gelatin silver print, archival pigment print, screenprint, lithograph, bronze sculpture. Medium: etching, woodcut, mezzotint, chromogenic print, ceramic figurine, charcoal drawing. Narrow: daguerreotype, tintype, fresco (detached), encaustic, neon sculpture, East Asian calligraphy (outside East Asia).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops where buyers actually compare quality or makers.

**Recommendation: the kind is the medium or technique, not the sub-family and not the subject.**

Evidence for stopping at medium or technique:
1. Retail facets stop there. Singulart's print facet lists six techniques and no finer split (SGL-PR); Motokawa's six print sections and eight painting sections carry the counts (MTK); Artsy's Medium list is six items (ART-C) and its genes name individual techniques (ART-G).
2. Auction and collector literature goes to technique and no further for "what it is": Sotheby's print guide has one section each for woodcut, linocut, engraving, etching, dry-point, mezzotint, aquatint, lithography, screenprint, offset lithography and monotype (SOT-PR); its photograph guide has gelatin silver, chromogenic, Cibachrome, dye-transfer, inkjet, archival pigment and alternative processes (SOT-PH). Print buyers compare an etching with a lithograph on edition size and artist, not on subject.
3. Mezzotint is cited with a physical edition limit (50 to 80 impressions, MTK-T), which shows the technique itself bears on quality and rarity.
4. Buyers explicitly distrust coarser or branded labels: "collectors generally prefer a more specific designation" than giclée (SOT-PH). So "inkjet art print" is the kind and giclée is a name for it.
5. Nothing below technique is a compared quality grade. Subject, style, size and colour are views (SGL-PA themes, MTK image sections, SAA subject, ART-C colour).

**Differences by sub-family.**
- *Prints:* kind = technique (etching, not "intaglio print"). Retail sources group intaglio as copperplate or engraving (MTK, SGL-PR), but auctions and guides separate etching, drypoint, mezzotint and aquatint (SOT-PR, MTK-T). The finer level is recommended because the finer sources are the ones that compare quality.
- *Paintings:* kind = binder or medium. AAT nests gouaches under watercolors (AAT-PA) while Motokawa and Singulart list gouache as its own technique (MTK-T, SGL-PA), so gouache is shown as a kind beside watercolour; nesting it is an owner choice. Support and ground (canvas, panel, paper, silk) are attributes.
- *Photographs:* kind = process (gelatin silver, chromogenic, archival pigment, cyanotype). Brand-named processes (Cibachrome, Ilfochrome, Polaroid) are named forms under the process kind (SOT-PH, MTK-T).
- *Sculpture:* kind = usual material, because material is the form here (AAT-SC lists bronzes, ivories, jades, terracottas, waxworks as types; SGL-SC filters by material). Form (freestanding, wall-object, bust, relief, outdoor) is an attribute (SGL-SC Types) or a view, not a level.
- *Edge disciplines:* kind = the named object (tapestry, calligraphy, collage, mosaic).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: preference (market and maker driven). No spec-ranked or consensus-ranked tier exists for the kinds.** No source fetched orders oil painting above watercolour, or etching above lithograph, or gelatin silver above chromogenic. Price bands are the only generic ladder (Singulart: $500 and under, $500 to $2,000, $2,000 to $5,000, $5,000 to $20,000, $20,000 and up, SGL-PA; Mojarto has rupee bands, seen in a search excerpt only), and they are bands of price, not of quality.

What does behave like a graded quality dimension is a set of facts about the individual work or edition. They should be modifiers, never levels (Appendix A: price band and quality tier, brand and maker, edition, condition):
- **Authenticity class.** HS97 limits "original engravings, prints and lithographs" to impressions from plates "wholly executed by hand by the artist", excluding mechanical or photomechanical processes, and counts the first 12 castings as original sculpture. AAT has the print status nodes original, interpretive and reproductive prints, and restrikes and rétirages as their own nodes (AAT-VW, AAT-PR). Suggested modifier `authenticity_class`: original / interpretive / reproduction / restrike.
- **Edition type and size.** Artsy Rarity: unique, limited edition, open edition, unknown edition (ART-C). LUMAS: limited, open, small open ("Petites"), small limited ("Darlings") (LUM). Saffronart: limited edition = signed, numbered, matrix destroyed (SAF). PCNY: artist's proof, printer's proof, bon à tirer, hors commerce, impression, edition (PCNY). Suggested modifiers `edition_type`, `edition_size`, `proof_status`, `signed`.
- **Print date against image date.** LUMAS stores image year and edition year separately ("1962/2025" style); Artsy has a genome gene "Vintage Photo Prints" (LUM, ART-G). Suggested modifier `print_date` against `image_date`.
- **Process-dependent limits.** Mezzotint editions are limited by plate wear to about 50 to 80 impressions (MTK-T). This is a fact attached to the kind, not a tier.

**Appearance in the data.** Price band, edition and authenticity are attributes on the item type or named form; none is a level. The framework already says "whatever the edition or price" (key step 20).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought. Evidence: Artsy puts Artist first in its filter order (ART-S); Motokawa gives overseas, domestic and recommended artists their own navigation sections beside technique (MTK); MutualArt is an index of "900k+ artists" (MUT); the Japanese buyback site prices by artist (RYK); Sotheby's guide attaches technique to artist (Munch and woodcut, Rembrandt and Goya and intaglio, Hockney and lithography, Degas and monotype) (SOT-PR); Mojarto's footer lists named artists as navigation (search excerpt only).

**Maker ledger: yes, with a limit.** Kinds are generic and an individual work is never a kind (S2, BRIEF), so the artist belongs in a ledger and not in the tree. Recommended fields: maker, role (artist, printer, publisher, studio), known-for kinds, scene or movement tag, country, era, price band, authority identifier (ULAN, GETTY-V), source. Roles beyond "artist" are attested: PCNY defines printer and publisher as separate parties to an edition. Foundries and porcelain-figurine manufactories are not evidenced in this batch, and the framework itself names ornamental figurines.

**The limit.** The population is far too large to hold (MutualArt: 900k+ artists; ULAN is a separate authority, GETTY-V). The ledger should be seeded only with makers cited in the source literature as the exemplars of a kind or a named form (for example Munch for woodcut, SOT-PR) and should carry an external authority identifier instead of trying to enumerate the field. Whether to hold a ledger at all or only an authority link is an owner decision (section 10).

**Platform makers.** None. Brand-named processes (Cibachrome, Ilfochrome, Iris, Piezograph, Polaroid) are named forms, not makers of platforms (SOT-PH, MTK-T).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommended: by chronology of the process, oldest first, with the canonical modern medium named first only where the leaf has one dominant kind.**
- Prints are already explained in this order: relief is the oldest, intaglio appears in the 1400s, and planographic (lithography) is the most recent major method (SOT-PR); Saffronart's list runs Relief, Intaglio, Planographs, Stencils (SAF).
- Photographs have a chronology of invention that cataloguers use: the daguerreotype in 1839 and then the alternative processes, gelatin silver, colour and digital (SOT-PH); Graphics Atlas offers a Timeline browse beside its Browse and Search modes (search excerpt of its compare and search pages only, not seen on the fetched identification page).
- Paintings: no source gives an order. Singulart lists techniques alphabetically (SGL-PA); Motokawa lists by count (MTK). Recommend the chronology of the medium (fresco, tempera, encaustic, oil, watercolour, gouache, acrylic) with nihonga and ink wash by tradition.
- Sculpture: by material hardness or antiquity has no source; use alphabetical within a leaf unless the owner prefers otherwise.

Reason: chronology is attested for prints and photographs, is neutral to price, and does not rank the media.

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences are labelled (framework) where they restate CATEGORIES_v0_6.md and (proposed) where they are new.

1. **C4 Craft & Art Supplies.** Closest objects: blank canvas, paint tubes, pastels sold as materials, clay, a printmaking plate or woodblock kept after the edition. *Precedence (framework, with one proposed exception):* A finished painting, drawing, print or sculpture is F2, and the canvas, paints, pastels, clay and printmaking matrices it was made from are C4 (step 28 and S3); proposed exception: a carved block or plate sold to be looked at as a relief is F2. Saffronart states that in limited editions the matrix is destroyed after printing (SAF).
2. **D4 Cameras & Photography, F5 Documents & Personal Records, I2 Digital Media & Items.** Closest objects: a gallery print of a landscape, a family print, a photo file, unexposed film. *Precedence (framework; the test is proposed):* A photographic print made as art is F2, a print or slide of the owner's own life is F5, a photograph held as a file is I2, and unexposed film, photographic paper and darkroom goods are D4. Open point: "made as art" is a matter of the maker's intent. S5 tests the object, not the motive. The framework needs a test by object, for example whether the print is signed, numbered or catalogued as an edition (proposed).
3. **E1 Toys & Games and B4 Decor (figures and statues).** Closest objects: a porcelain shepherdess, a collectible-series vinyl figure, a garden statue, a plaster bust. *Precedence (framework):* An ornamental figurine of a person or animal is F2, a figure of a character, an action figure or a collectible-series or vinyl figure is E1, a garden statue or gnome is B4, and a vase or bowl is B4 or B5 however artistic. The ornament-versus-character line is the framework's, and no source fetched here tests it.
4. **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects.** Closest objects: a Buddha statue, an Orthodox icon, a Madonna oil painting. *Precedence (framework step 14; second clause proposed):* A picture or statue made for use in worship (icon, home-shrine image, devotional statue) is F4, and a work with a religious subject made to be looked at is F2: subject is not use.
5. **B2 Soft Furnishings, B4 Decor (frames and plates).** Closest objects: Aubusson tapestry, kilim wall hanging, picture frame, wall plate. *Precedence (framework, with the frame rule proposed):* An object woven or embroidered to hang and be looked at is a tapestry or textile hanging and is F2, a rug made to be walked on is B2 even if hung; a framed picture is F2 and the empty frame is B4, as HS97 treats frames of normal kind and value as part of the picture (HS97); a decorative plate made to hang is B4.
6. **Other edge objects (not full boundaries).**
   - Artist trading cards (F2 per step 20) against trading cards (F3 per step 15): *Precedence (proposed):* A hand-made card sold as an original artwork is F2, and a printed series card collected as a card is F3.
   - Artists' books and portfolios (Artsy genes "Artists' Books", "Books and Portfolios", ART-G) against F1: a bound book is F1, and a loose portfolio of prints is held as the prints.
   - Neon and light sculpture (AAT-SC, SGL-SC) against B3 lamps and B4 neon signs: made to give light is B3, made as sign is B4, made to be looked at as sculpture is F2.
   - Digital artwork and NFTs against I2: a physical print of a digital work is F2, the file or token is I2.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** F2 Art, then sub-family by discipline (Paintings, Drawings, Prints, Photographs, Sculpture, Textile and fibre hangings, Calligraphy, Collage mosaic and mixed work), then leaf by process family where one exists (prints, photographs, sculpture), then kind by medium or technique, then named form. **Maximum depth four levels below the category.** Paintings and drawings go to three, since their kinds are already the media. Edition, authenticity, price band, artist, subject, size, colour, scene and period are modifiers, tags, views or ledger fields.

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. Confirm sub-families by discipline, and whether textile hangings, calligraphy and collage-and-mosaic are separate sub-families or are folded into painting and drawing.
2. Confirm the kind level as medium or technique (etching, not "intaglio print"; "inkjet art print", with giclée as a named form).
3. Home for pastel: drawing (recommended, since pastel is dry pigment without a binder) or painting (Motokawa, Zhongyi file it there; AAT and HS97 name it beside both).
4. Installation, video, light and kinetic art, and sculpture that is also a lamp: in F2 as "free-standing sculpture", or out. Video art and NFTs are I2 by the key.
5. Reproductions: key step 20 includes posters and prints "whatever the edition". Confirm that offset posters, inkjet reproductions and factory ceramic figurines are F2 kinds, with `authenticity_class` as a modifier (HS97 and AAT separate original from reproduction and mass-produced work).
6. Maker ledger: hold a ledger of exemplar makers with an external authority identifier (recommended), or hold no artists and only link to an authority.
7. Format-defined kinds: portrait miniature, hanging scroll, handscroll and thangka-type formats. Kinds, named forms or attributes?
8. The "made as art" test between F2 and F5 for photographs, and between F2 and F4 for religious subjects; both need a rule that does not depend on motive.
