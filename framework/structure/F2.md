## 0. Category

**Code and name:** F2 Art (domain F, Culture, Records & Meaning).

**Membership test (placement key step 20):** "Made to be **looked at as a picture** on a flat or hung surface (paintings, drawings, prints, posters, art photographs, calligraphy, tapestries and textile hangings, artist trading cards), **or as free-standing sculpture**, including ornamental figurines of people and animals made in ceramic, glass, metal, stone, wood or resin, whatever the edition or price?" Category description: "pictures and free-standing sculpture, including ornamental figurines, whatever the edition or price."

**Notes and rules that touch F2 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_7.md):**
- Step 20 note: "figures of characters, action figures, vinyl and collectible-series figures, and bobbleheads are E1. Garden statues and gnomes are B4. Vases, bowls and other vessels are B4 or B5, however artistic. A map, including a single sheet from an atlas or a pictorial wall map, is F1; a print made as art that depicts a place is F2." (map sentence: K-142)
- Step 20 names six figurine materials: ceramic, glass, metal, stone, wood, resin. Each needs a home in the tree (section 3, F2.5).
- Step 19 note (F5; K-139, which also dropped "of the owner's own life" from the step-19 lead): "A photographic print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2; every other photographic print, slide or negative, whoever is shown, is F5. Posters are F2." Step 5 (I2): the owner's photographs and digital artwork held as files are I2 ("the carrier decides: prints are F5"); NFTs are I2.
- Step 7 (F6): "skeletal and taxidermy specimens" are F6; a natural object is F6 only when not made or shaped beyond cleaning, cutting, polishing or preserving. Step 7 note: "A mount of one animal prepared as in life is F6 however it is presented; a work that combines animal parts with other materials is F2. A cast, replica or carved figure is filed by what it is (F2 sculpture, E1 toy or kit) … Human specimens are OUT. Ivory and other CITES goods stay in; their legal status goes to the C17 ledger" (K-76, K-77, K-79).
- S2: "an original painting under 'painting'"; individual items are never kinds. S5: "A signed football is a football; a collected teapot is a teapot; an antique chair is a chair; a commemorative plate is a plate." Step 28 note and S3 (C4): blank canvas, paint, clay are C4; a finished picture or sculpture is F2 (C4 memo precedence).
- Step 14 (F4) is tried before step 20: icons, shrines and devotional objects made for use in worship are F4; its note now reads "Icons, thangkas and other devotional scroll paintings, altarpieces, devotional statues made for a shrine and crucifixes are F4 (old-master altarpieces included); a painting or sculpture with a religious subject made in another form is F2 or B4" and "a ketubah or baptism certificate is F5, or F2 when made as a picture" (K-108, K-112). Step 15 (F3) is tried before step 20: "non-game trading cards" and trophies are F3, although step 20 lists "artist trading cards".
- Step 30 (B4): picture frames, wall mirrors, decorative bowls, garden ornaments, "decorative replica weapons" are B4. Tapestries and textile hangings are F2 (B1, B2 memos); rugs are B2.
- Appendix A: modifiers never split inner levels (region and culture, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, setting, motive, origin, material, condition, edition). In F2 sculpture, material is the form (section 4b), as in AAT-SC.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

### 1.1 Reference works and thesauri
- **Getty AAT (AAT-VW).** Under Objects Facet > Visual and Verbal Communication > Visual Works > `visual works (works)` sits the node label `<visual works by material or technique>`. Its children include paintings, drawings, prints, photographs, sculpture, collages, mosaics, pastels, calligraphy, textile art (with tapestries and woven pictures beneath it), installations, mixed media works, new media art, time-based works and works on paper, plus craft-type work such as lacquerware, glassware and metalwork. Each of the five main types is subdivided by further node labels, each one axis: paintings by form, location or context, material or technique, and subject type; drawings by function, material or technique, maker and subject type; prints by form, function, and process or technique (and the status nodes original prints, interpretive prints, reproductive prints); photographs by form, function, technique, and subject or context; sculpture by form, function, location or context, material, technique and subject type (AAT-VW). This is the clearest published example of "one axis per level" and it does not put subject at the top.
- **AAT paintings (AAT-PA)** by material or technique: acrylic, bark, canvas, casein, encaustic, finger, fluorescent, frescoes, hide, ink resist, ink wash, oil (with oil sketches beneath), sand, sargas, tempera, theorems, Tuechleins, and watercolors (with aquarelles and gouaches beneath). Support (canvas, bark, hide) and binder (oil, tempera) share one node, a mixed axis.
- **AAT prints (AAT-PR)** by process or technique: collagraphs, color prints, digital prints (inkjet, laser, dye diffusion thermal transfer, thermal), embossed prints, intaglio prints, monoprints, photomechanical prints, planographic prints, relief prints, stencil prints, restrikes, rétirage prints and others such as vitreographs and nature prints.
- **AAT sculpture (AAT-SC)** by material: acroliths, chryselephantine, found object sculpture (with junk sculpture), glazed sculpture, ivories, jades, neon sculpture, scrimshaws, soft sculpture, terracottas, waxworks. A separate node, bronzes, sits beside the labels.
- **AAT photographs (AAT-PH)** by technique, in two sub-labels: picture-taking technique (aerial, chronophotographs, photobooth pictures, photomicrographs, snapshots, staged, stereoscopic) and processing or presentation technique (cased photographs, clichés-verre, contact prints, digital photographs, hand-colored, lenticular, photograms, photomurals).
- **Getty vocabularies (GETTY-V, AAT-PDF).** AAT holds generic terms (agents, work types, materials, styles, techniques); the Union List of Artist Names (ULAN) is a separate authority for named artists, studios and patrons. The generic/named split is the same as the framework's kind/maker split.
- **Graphics Atlas (GA)**, the Image Permanence Institute's process identifier, groups image-making processes in four blocks: pre-photographic (intaglio, planographic, relief), photomechanical (collotype, letterpress halftone, offset lithograph, photogravure, rotogravure, Woodburytype), photographic (albumen, ambrotype, bromoil, carbon, chromogenic, cyanotype, daguerreotype, gelatin and collodion printing-out paper, gum dichromate, instant, platinum, salted paper, silver dye bleach, silver gelatin, tintype and more) and digital (electrophotography, inkjet, thermal). Prints and photographs are one process family there.
- **Print Center New York glossary (PCNY)** separates techniques (chine-collé, collagraph, digital, drypoint, etching, intaglio, letterpress, monotype, offset, photogravure, screenprinting) from annotations (artist's proof, printer's proof, bon à tirer, hors commerce, edition, impression, proof, printer, publisher).
- **Artsy Art Genome (ART-G)** is a flat gene list with eight top groups (Artistic Disciplines, Subject Matter, Styles and Movements, Visual Qualities, Medium and Techniques, Time Periods, Geographic Regions, Materials). Its Medium and Techniques genes include oil painting, watercolor, pastel, tempera, encaustic, fresco, drawing, etching/engraving, lithograph, silkscreen, woodcut and linocut, monotype, digital print, photography, black-and-white photography, photogram, sculpture, soft sculpture, kinetic sculpture, polychrome sculpture, poster, hanging scroll, handscroll, East Asian calligraphy, NFT.

### 1.2 Standards and trade classification
- **Harmonized System chapter 97 (HS97).** 9701 paintings, drawings and pastels executed entirely by hand (collages, mosaics and similar decorative plaques are subheadings); 9702 original engravings, prints and lithographs, defined as "impressions produced directly … of one or of several plates wholly executed by hand by the artist, irrespective of the process or of the material employed by him, but not including any mechanical or photomechanical process"; 9703 original sculpture and statuary "in any material" (and the first 12 castings or reproductions). Frames of "a kind and of a value normal to those articles" are classified with the picture. Mass-produced reproductions and works of conventional craftsmanship are excluded from 9703. HS divides by discipline (three headings), splits original from reproduction, and treats material as irrelevant. Photographs are not in chapter 97 in the pages fetched. Customs headings are evidence only, never a legal class in the C17 ledger.

### 1.4 Auction houses, resale and collectors
- **Sotheby's and Christie's departments (SOT-D, CHR-D).** Mixed axes: some departments are medium-only (Prints; Photographs; Old Master Drawings; Digital Art), some are era and region (19th Century European Paintings, Impressionist and Modern, Post-War and Contemporary, Chinese Paintings Classical/Modern, Aboriginal Art, Orientalist Art), and some are form-and-region (European Sculpture and Works of Art, British Watercolours and Drawings 1550-1850). Christie's also lists Prints and Multiples, Comic Strips and Illustrations, Outsider Art, Portrait Miniatures, Japanese Art. Poly Auction Hong Kong (POLY) separates Modern and Contemporary Art from Fine Chinese Paintings and Calligraphy by tradition. Auction departments are sale segments, not a taxonomy.
- **Collector guides (SOT-PR, SOT-PH, SAF).** Prints are divided relief, intaglio, planographic (Sotheby's) and Relief, Intaglio, Planographs, Stencils (Saffronart), each with named techniques beneath (woodcut, linocut; engraving, etching, dry-point, mezzotint, aquatint; lithography, offset lithography; screenprint; monotype as the one unique print method). Photographs are described by process: gelatin silver, chromogenic, Cibachrome, dye-transfer, inkjet and archival pigment, and alternative processes (cyanotype, tintype, gum bichromate, Polaroid, ambrotype). Sotheby's states that giclée "is simply a neologism used to market inkjet prints" and that collectors prefer a more specific designation.
- **Resale and market data (RYK, MUT).** The Japanese buyback site lists genres paintings, nihonga, prints, ink painting, oil, watercolour, ukiyo-e, lithograph/silkscreen, abstract, contemporary, and values by artist. MutualArt indexes "900k+ artists" and filters them by media (Painting, Prints and Graphic Art, Works on Paper, Sculpture, Photography).
- **Figurines.** No figurine retailer, replacement dealer or collectors' club was fetched in batch S2_F2. The figurine kinds and makers in this memo rest on the key's own wording (step 20) and on the material lists of AAT-SC, SGL-SC and NKA; every figurine-specific name is marked † (section 10).

### 1.5 Where the sources agree and split
- **Agree:** five disciplines everywhere (painting, drawing, print, photograph, sculpture: AAT, HS97, Singulart, Saatchi, Artsy, Mojarto, MutualArt, Sotheby's) and, one level down, process or medium (oil, etching, gelatin silver, bronze).
- **Split 1, the edge disciplines.** Singulart adds Work on Paper, Textile, Digital; Saatchi adds Collage, Mixed Media, Installation; AAT has calligraphy, textile art, mosaics and pastels as visual-work types; Zhongyi puts calligraphy and seal carving in the same directory.
- **Split 2, pastel.** AAT lists pastels as a visual-work type beside drawings and paintings; HS97 puts "paintings, drawings and pastels" in one heading; Motokawa and Zhongyi file pastel with paintings; Singulart lists pastel among painting techniques.
- **Split 3, sculpture's axis.** Singulart offers both type (freestanding, wall-object, bust) and material; AAT has sibling nodes for form, material and technique, and its material node is the one that lists kinds (AAT-SC); Nikkei Art's sculpture categories are materials (bronze, ceramics, glass; NKA); HS97 ignores material.
- **Split 4, East Asian painting.** Japan and China separate nihonga/guohua from oil, by materials and tradition (MTK-T, ZY); Western sources file ink wash as a technique of painting (AAT-PA).
- **Split 5, prints against photographs.** GA treats them as one process family; Sotheby's, Christie's, Singulart, Artsy and Saatchi keep separate sections; HS97 excludes photomechanical impressions from original prints.
- **Split 6, edition.** Artsy and LUMAS make edition class a first-class filter; AAT and HS97 draw an original/reproduction line; the framework says edition is a modifier.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence.**
1. Medium comes first in navigation. Singulart's Medium list is its top control (SGL-PA); Saatchi's category row (SAA); Mojarto's category tiles (MOJ); Kunstlager's menu (KUN); Motokawa's two shop halves, prints and paintings (MTK); TIG's category tree (TIG); Zhongyi's classes (ZY); Artsy's Medium filter (ART-C).
2. Maker comes first in filters. Artsy's filter order starts with Artist and then Rarity before Medium (ART-S). Motokawa lists famous artists (345 overseas, 240 domestic) and recommended artists (2,073) as shop sections beside the technique sections (MTK). MutualArt is organised around 900k artists (MUT). Mojarto's footer lists named artists (MOJ). The Japanese buyback site prices by artist (RYK).
3. Within a medium, buyers choose technique. Singulart tells print buyers to "narrow our art prints for sale further by print technique, edition size or price" (SGL-PR). Motokawa sells prints in six technique sections with counts (MTK). Sotheby's guide organises prints by technique (SOT-PR). For sculpture, the choice inside the discipline is the material (SGL-SC materials facet; NKA bronze, ceramics, glass; AAT-SC).
4. Edition and authenticity are secondary but explicit: Artsy Rarity, LUMAS edition classes, Saffronart's definition of a limited edition (signed, numbered, matrix destroyed) (ART-C, LUM, SAF), and HS97's "wholly executed by hand".
5. Subject, size and colour are views across media: Singulart themes and size bands (SGL-PA), Motokawa "image" and size sections (MTK), Saatchi style and subject (SAA), Artsy colour (ART-C).

**Reading.** The first axis is discipline (what kind of picture or object), the second is the process, medium or material inside it, and the maker and the edition are what a buyer filters by. This matches the framework: placement is by designed function (looked at), kinds are by medium, and maker, edition, subject, size and price are modifiers.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep F2 as the top. Levels below the category are named sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels at most; C5(a)).

### Candidate A (decided: F2-O1): discipline, then process or material family, then medium, then named form
- **Sub-family** axis: the discipline of the work (painting, drawing, print, photograph, sculpture, and three small edge disciplines). Basis: AAT-VW, HS97, Singulart, Saatchi, Artsy, Mojarto, MutualArt agree on the first five.
- **Leaf** axis, stated per sub-family (C5(c)):
  - Prints: the family of printing process: relief, intaglio, planographic, stencil, other and hybrid processes, digital (SAF, SOT-PR, AAT-PR, PCNY).
  - Photographs: the process class: silver, chromogenic and dye, pigment and digital, alternative and historic, camera-less and instant (GA, SOT-PH).
  - Sculpture: the material family that makes the form: metal; stone; wood and other carved organic material; ceramic; glass and crystal; resin, plaster, wax and plastic; textile and soft; found and mixed; light (AAT-SC sculpture by material; SGL-SC materials facet; NKA bronze, ceramics, glass). Figurines share this axis with other sculpture.
  - Paintings, Drawings, Textile and fibre hangings, Calligraphy, and Collage and mosaic: no leaf; the data stores `leaf = null` (C5(b)).
- **Kind** axis: the specific medium, technique or material (oil painting, etching, gelatin silver print, bronze sculpture, porcelain and bone china figurine and sculpture). This is where buyers compare (section 4b).
- **Named form**: a distinct format, branded process or traditional form inside a kind, with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2(a)): ukiyo-e inside woodcut (`no`); Ilfochrome inside silver dye bleach print and Polaroid inside instant print (`yes`). Aliases (giclée, serigraph, aquarelle, C-print, Cibachrome) are C9 alias rows, not named forms; printer and inkset systems (Iris, Piezograph, Imagegraph) are a `printing_system` attribute (C10).
- **Example sub-families and leaves (12 kinds in brief):** Painting > oil painting, acrylic painting, watercolour, gouache, ink wash painting, mineral-pigment painting (nihonga); Print > Intaglio > etching, mezzotint; Print > Planographic > stone lithograph; Photograph > Silver > gelatin silver print, daguerreotype; Sculpture > Metal > bronze sculpture. The full tree is below.
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. *Pastel* is a drawing medium to AAT, a painting medium to Motokawa and Zhongyi, and its own heading beside drawings in HS97. One home is needed: pastel is a drawing (F-11), as AAT files it.
  2. *Monotype and monoprint* are prints by every source (SOT-PR, PCNY, SGL-PR, AAT-PR) but unique or near-unique. The leaf is now "Other and hybrid processes" (monotype, monoprint, collagraph), which keeps edition off the process axis; uniqueness is a kind-level fact of the monotype, not a leaf.
  3. *Figurine against sculpture.* A small bronze animal and a bronze statue, or a porcelain figurine and a unique porcelain sculpture, are the same material and making. They are one kind per material: size, edition, price and maker are attributes or ledger facts (C6, Appendix A). Figurines stay in the sculpture material leaves: there is no "Figurines" sub-family and no split of a figurine kind from the sculpture kind of the same material (F-2).
  4. *Giclée reproduction of a painting.* It is an inkjet print (a kind), but HS97 and AAT call it a reproduction, not an original; authenticity class is a scheme, not a place (section 5).
  5. *Posters.* A poster is filed by its printing process (a Mucha stone-lithograph poster is a stone lithograph; a gig poster is a screenprint) and carries `issue_form = poster`; a generated "Posters" view joins them. There is no poster kind and no poster leaf (F-10).

### Candidate B: support or material first (work on paper, canvas, panel, textile, three-dimensional)
- Axis at the sub-family level: what the work is on or made of. Basis: Singulart Medium (Work on Paper, Textile, Digital, Other Media) and Artsy Medium (Work on Paper) (SGL-PA, ART-C); AAT lists canvas, bark and hide paintings as kinds (AAT-PA).
- Example kinds: work on paper > watercolour, pastel, etching, lithograph, gelatin silver print, charcoal drawing; canvas > oil painting, acrylic painting; textile > tapestry, embroidered picture; three-dimensional > bronze sculpture, terracotta.
- **Where it breaks:** a watercolour, a lithograph and a gelatin silver print would sit together although no buyer compares them; oil on paper and oil on canvas would split one medium; a photograph printed on canvas or aluminium would move sub-family with its support. Appendix A bars material as a level unless it is the form; here it is not (it is the form only inside sculpture, where Candidate A uses it).

### Candidate C: unique, limited, open (edition class) first
- Axis at the sub-family level: how many exist. Basis: Artsy Rarity, LUMAS item categories, HS97's split of 9701 (by hand, unique) from 9702 (impressions), AAT's original, interpretive and reproductive print nodes (ART-C, LUM, HS97, AAT-VW).
- Example kinds: unique > oil painting, drawing, monotype; limited edition > etching, lithograph, bronze cast; open edition > poster, inkjet reprint.
- **Where it breaks:** the same kind appears in two or three places (a photograph can be unique, limited or open; a bronze is limited by definition, and HS97 allows the first 12 casts as original); edition is a modifier in Appendix A and the key says "whatever the edition or price"; edition class is a property of an individual edition, not of the generic type (C16).

### Worked example tree under Candidate A (64 kinds)

## 4. Depth and size

**Rough counts (estimates).**
- Generic kinds: about 120 to 150 under Candidate A: paintings 12 to 15, drawings 6 to 9, prints 26 to 33 (AAT-PR alone lists about 30 process nodes; PCNY, SOT-PR and SGL-PR add the commonly sold ones), photographs 22 to 30 (GA lists about 40 processes in all across its four blocks), sculpture 20 to 30 (nine material families; figurine kinds sit inside them), textile 6 to 8, calligraphy 2 to 4, collage and mosaic 4 to 6. The worked tree holds 64.
- Named forms: about 100 to 250 under those kinds (branded or historic processes, formats and regional forms) once aliases leave the list; an estimate, not a cap: named forms are harvested in stage 3 from existing catalogues first and grow without a cap, one attested row each (CV-7). Examples seen: Ilfochrome, Polaroid, ukiyo-e, paper screen, scrimshaw, junk sculpture.
- Lines and variants: figurine manufactories carry numbered series (lines) and model numbers (variants), filled wherever collectors compare models and grown without a cap (CV-7, CV-8); no source fetched gives a count †.
- Depth is uneven. Paintings and drawings use three levels (sub-family, kind, named form; `leaf = null`). Prints, photographs and sculpture use four. Textile, calligraphy and collage are shallow (three levels, `leaf = null`).
- **Where depth concentrates:** prints (the largest list of well-defined process names, with a separate collector literature, SOT-PR, SAF, PCNY, AAT-PR) and photographs (GA). Sculpture is wide (many materials) and its brand depth sits in the figurine manufactories' lines and model numbers, not in more kinds.
- **Breadth.** Broad: oil painting, acrylic painting, watercolour, gelatin silver print, archival pigment print, screenprint, stone lithograph, bronze sculpture, porcelain and bone china figurine and sculpture. Medium: etching, woodcut, mezzotint, chromogenic print, resin sculpture and figurine, glass and crystal sculpture and figurine, charcoal drawing. Narrow: daguerreotype, tintype, fresco (detached), encaustic, neon sculpture, wax sculpture, brush and pen calligraphy (outside East Asia).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops where buyers actually compare quality or makers.

**Decided (F2-O1): the kind is the medium, technique or material, not the sub-family and not the subject.**

Evidence for stopping at medium or technique:
1. Retail facets stop there. Singulart's print facet lists six techniques and no finer split (SGL-PR); Motokawa's six print sections and eight painting sections carry the counts (MTK); Artsy's Medium list is six items (ART-C) and its genes name individual techniques (ART-G); Singulart's sculpture materials facet (SGL-SC) and Nikkei Art's bronze, ceramics and glass categories (NKA) stop at material.
2. Auction and collector literature goes to technique and no further for "what it is": Sotheby's print guide has one section each for woodcut, linocut, engraving, etching, dry-point, mezzotint, aquatint, lithography, screenprint, offset lithography and monotype (SOT-PR); its photograph guide has gelatin silver, chromogenic, Cibachrome, dye-transfer, inkjet, archival pigment and alternative processes (SOT-PH). Print buyers compare an etching with a lithograph on edition size and artist, not on subject.
3. Mezzotint is cited with a physical edition limit (50 to 80 impressions, MTK-T), which shows the technique itself bears on quality and rarity.
4. Buyers explicitly distrust coarser or branded labels: "collectors generally prefer a more specific designation" than giclée (SOT-PH). So "inkjet print" is the kind and giclée is an alias of it (C9).
5. Nothing below technique is a compared quality grade. Subject, style, size and colour are views (SGL-PA themes, MTK image sections, SAA subject, ART-C colour).

**Differences by sub-family.**
- *Prints:* kind = technique (etching, not "intaglio print"). Retail sources group intaglio as copperplate or engraving (MTK, SGL-PR), but auctions and guides separate etching, drypoint, mezzotint and aquatint (SOT-PR, MTK-T). The finer level is recommended because the finer sources are the ones that compare quality. Issue form (poster) is an attribute; the kind is the process.
- *Paintings:* kind = binder or medium. AAT nests gouaches under watercolors (AAT-PA) while Motokawa and Singulart list gouache as its own technique (MTK-T, SGL-PA), so gouache is its own kind beside watercolour (F-11). Support and ground (canvas, panel, paper, silk) are attributes. *Precedence (proposed, review F2-3):* "A work whose image is built from pasted elements is a collage; a painting with added materials is mixed media."
- *Drawings:* kind = drawing medium; use (illustration, comic art) is a tag.
- *Photographs:* kind = process (gelatin silver, chromogenic, archival pigment, cyanotype). Maker-bound process names (Ilfochrome, Polaroid) are named forms under the process kind (SOT-PH, MTK-T); the earlier trade name Cibachrome is an alias of Ilfochrome.
- *Sculpture:* kind = material, because material is the form here (AAT-SC lists bronzes, ivories, jades, terracottas, waxworks as types; SGL-SC filters by material; NKA files bronze, ceramics and glass apart). One kind per material family product: a figurine and a sculpture of the same material are not split by size, edition or maker. *Precedence (proposed):* "Within one material, a figurine and a sculpture are one kind; size, edition, price and maker are attributes or ledger facts." Form (freestanding, wall-object, bust, relief, outdoor) is an attribute (SGL-SC Types) or a view, not a level.
- *Edge disciplines:* kind = the named object (tapestry, calligraphy, collage, mosaic).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: preference (market and maker driven). No spec-ranked or consensus-ranked tier exists for the kinds.** No source fetched orders oil painting above watercolour, or etching above lithograph, or gelatin silver above chromogenic, or porcelain above resin. No tier sits on a kind (C4(a)). Price bands are the only generic ladder; Singulart's bands ($500 and under to $20,000 and up, SGL-PA) and Mojarto's rupee bands (search excerpt only) are evidence of practice. The data uses the domain-wide C4(d) USD bands (CV-13, CV-14: "0 (free)", "under 50" and on up to "1,500,000–5,000,000" and "over 5,000,000"), derived from a dated, sourced list price, on maker, line and named-form rows; art uses the upper bands that most categories leave unused. A band is never a tier. A manufactory's own series ladder (for example a limited series above a general range) is C4(c) class (iv): `line_rank` in the lines table and, optionally, tier entries with `scheme` = the maker's name; it enters on the maker's one fetched page (CV-10, CV-12) †.

What does behave like a graded quality dimension is a set of facts about the individual work or edition. Under C16 and S2 the data never stores them for one work: it stores the vocabulary as a scale in the one grading-scale registry (C16, CV-34: `scale_id`, `name`, `owner`, `scale_type`, `steps`, `kinds_served`, `sources`) with the kinds it serves, and generated views use the scale. A scale enters on one fetched source (CV-10). The Artsy and LUMAS edition classes take `scale_type` = platform label; the authenticity scale takes `scale_type` = classification (R-2, section 12). Kind-level facts stay attributes on the kind.
- **Authenticity class (scheme).** HS97 limits "original engravings, prints and lithographs" to impressions from plates "wholly executed by hand by the artist", excluding mechanical or photomechanical processes, and counts the first 12 castings as original sculpture. AAT has the print status nodes original, interpretive and reproductive prints, and restrikes and rétirages as their own nodes (AAT-VW, AAT-PR). Scale `authenticity_class` (`scale_type` = classification: a class a kind or named form belongs to, never a grade of one object; R-2): original / interpretive / reproduction / restrike. Reproductions (offset posters, inkjet reproductions, factory figurines) stay in F2 under their process or material kinds, linked to this scale (F-12).
- **Edition class (scheme).** Artsy Rarity: unique, limited edition, open edition, unknown edition (ART-C). LUMAS: limited, open, small open ("Petites"), small limited ("Darlings") (LUM; a platform label, C4(c)). Saffronart: limited edition = signed, numbered, matrix destroyed (SAF). PCNY: artist's proof, printer's proof, bon à tirer, hors commerce, impression, edition (PCNY). Schemes `edition_type` and `proof_status`; edition size, signature and numbering are facts of one edition and are not stored (C16).
- **Print date against image date.** LUMAS stores image year and edition year separately ("1962/2025" style); Artsy has a genome gene "Vintage Photo Prints" (LUM, ART-G). These are facts of one print and are not stored (C16); "vintage print" is an era tag where it describes a named form.
- **Process-dependent limits (kind attributes).** Mezzotint editions are limited by plate wear to about 50 to 80 impressions (MTK-T); a monotype is unique by process. These are facts attached to the kind, not tiers.

**Appearance in the data.** Price band on maker, line and named-form rows (C4(d)); authenticity and edition classes as registry schemes linked to kinds; none is a level and none is a value on one work. The framework already says "whatever the edition or price" (key step 20).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought. Evidence: Artsy puts Artist first in its filter order (ART-S); Motokawa gives overseas, domestic and recommended artists their own navigation sections beside technique (MTK); MutualArt is an index of "900k+ artists" (MUT); the Japanese buyback site prices by artist (RYK); Sotheby's guide attaches technique to artist (Munch and woodcut, Rembrandt and Goya and intaglio, Hockney and lithography, Degas and monotype) (SOT-PR); Mojarto's footer lists named artists as navigation (MOJ, fetched page). For figurines the maker is a manufactory with numbered series: this is where F2 has the brand depth the owner asked for, but no figurine source was fetched (†).

**Maker ledger: yes, uncapped (C1; CV-2, CV-5, F-13).** Kinds are generic and an individual work is never a kind (S2, BRIEF), so the artist belongs in a ledger and not in the tree.
- *Core columns (C1, CV-1):* `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `licensor_id`, `authority_id`, `scene_tags` (C8), `price_band` (C4(d)), `sources`. `known_for_kinds` is not stored: it is derived from the named-form ledger. One ledger serves the whole domain (CV-2).
- *`maker_class`, multi-valued, from the one domain-wide class list (CV-2):* `artist`; `print studio or atelier`; `print publisher` (PCNY defines printer and publisher as separate parties to an edition); `foundry` (none evidenced †); `house or manufacture` (figurine manufactories †).
- *Authority and schools:* `authority_id` is a core column (ULAN for artists; GETTY-V). Traditions of makers (ukiyo-e print schools, East Asian painting and calligraphy lineages, all †) go in the standing C1 school table, and makers link to them by `school_id` (CV-3).
- *Lines (C1 lines table; CV-1, CV-7):* a manufactory's series that spans several kinds is a line row (`line_id`, `maker_id`, `name`, `kinds`, `line_rank`, `built_by_maker_id`, `licensor_id`, `years`, `sources`), with `built_by_maker_id` where another firm makes it. Seeds, all †: Lladró (porcelain figurines), Goebel's Hummel figurines (earthenware), Royal Doulton figures (bone china), Swarovski Silver Crystal (crystal). A line or design inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (Polaroid integral print, Ilfochrome).
- *Variants (C3, CV-8):* figurine model numbers (HUM numbers, Lladró model numbers, all †) as `variant_code`, `variant_type` = reference; colourway and limited-edition variants of one model where attested. Otherwise unused: an edition of one artwork is an individual work (S2, C16).

**No limit (F-13; the memo's exemplar ledger departed from by the owner's depth-and-breadth rule).** Artists go in the shared maker ledger with class `artist` and their ULAN `authority_id`, uncapped, not as an exemplar sample. The population is large (MutualArt: 900k+ artists; ULAN is a separate authority, GETTY-V), so the ledger is seeded first from existing catalogues and authorities (ULAN; artist indexes such as MutualArt, MUT; dealer artist lists such as Motokawa's, MTK; makers the literature names for a kind, such as Munch for woodcut, SOT-PR) and grows as far as the attested evidence goes; one fetched page admits a maker (CV-5). Figurine manufactories, print studios, print publishers and foundries enter the same way once a page attests them †.

**Platform makers.** None (C13 not used). Maker-bound process names (Ilfochrome, Polaroid) are named forms; printer systems (Iris, Piezograph, Imagegraph) are a `printing_system` attribute (C10).

**C9 alias and homonym table (seed; relations as CV-23).** giclée → inkjet print (marketing name: "a neologism used to market inkjet prints"; SOT-PH, SGL-PR, MTK); serigraph → screenprint (alias; MOJ, MTK-T); aquarelle → watercolour (alias; AAT-PA); C-print → chromogenic print (alias; SOT-PH); Cibachrome → Ilfochrome (alias, earlier trade name; SOT-PH, MTK-T); 日本画 nihonga → mineral-pigment painting (regional name; MTK-T); 国画 guohua → ink wash painting (regional name, broader: it also covers colour painting in the Chinese tradition; MSJ, ZY); 油画 → oil painting (MSJ); 版画 → Prints sub-family (MSJ); 銅版画 copperplate → Intaglio leaf (MTK); sumi-e → ink wash painting †. Iwa-enogu (岩絵具, the mineral pigment) is a C4 material term, not an alias of the painting kind.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Decided (C7, CV-20, CV-21): canonical kind first, then the secondary key the sub-family states; named forms non-maker first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically, then maker-bound by maker, first-introduction year and alphabet.**
- *Prints:* secondary key = chronology of the process, oldest first. Prints are already explained in this order: relief is the oldest, intaglio appears in the 1400s, and planographic (lithography) is the most recent major method (SOT-PR); Saffronart's list runs Relief, Intaglio, Planographs, Stencils (SAF). Leaves follow the same order, then other and hybrid processes, then digital.
- *Photographs:* secondary key = chronology of invention, which cataloguers use: the daguerreotype in 1839 and then the alternative processes, gelatin silver, colour and digital (SOT-PH); Graphics Atlas offers a Timeline browse beside its Browse and Search modes (search excerpt of its compare and search pages only, not seen on the fetched identification page).
- *Paintings:* no source gives an order. Singulart lists techniques alphabetically (SGL-PA); Motokawa lists by count (MTK). Recommend the chronology of the medium (fresco, tempera, encaustic, oil, watercolour, gouache, acrylic) with mineral-pigment painting and ink wash by tradition.

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences are labelled (framework) where they restate CATEGORIES_v0_7.md (several cross-memo sentences entered the key in v0_7 by decisions K-76, K-77, K-79, K-108, K-112, K-139 and K-142), (proposed) where they are new, and (cross-memo) where the domain-F review gives the same sentence to both memos.

1. **C4 Craft & Art Supplies.** Closest objects: blank canvas, paint tubes, pastels sold as materials, clay, mineral pigment (iwa-enogu), a printmaking plate or woodblock kept after the edition. *Precedence (framework, with one proposed exception):* A finished painting, drawing, print or sculpture is F2, and the canvas, paints, pastels, clay and printmaking matrices it was made from are C4 (step 28 and S3); proposed exception: a carved block or plate sold to be looked at as a relief is F2. Saffronart states that in limited editions the matrix is destroyed after printing (SAF). Easels and paint for pictures are C4; calligraphy and shodo brushes and pens, filed by the maker's first-named use, are C5 (K-170, K-172).
2. **D4 Cameras & Photography, F5 Documents & Personal Records, I2 Digital Media & Items.** Closest objects: a gallery print of a landscape, a family print, a stranger's cabinet card, a photo file, unexposed film. *Precedence (framework):* a photograph held as a file is I2, and unexposed film, photographic paper and darkroom goods are D4. *Precedence (framework, step 19 note, K-139; the step-19 lead no longer says "of the owner's own life"):* "A photographic print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2; every other photographic print, slide or negative, whoever is shown, is F5" (snapshots, studio portraits, cartes de visite, cabinet cards, real photo postcards are F5). The test is on the object (edition, signature or stamp), not the motive (S5). Every photographic print carries `process` and `mount_format` (CV-38).
3. **E1 Toys & Games and B4 Decor (figures and statues).** Closest objects: a porcelain shepherdess, a resin angel, a collectible-series vinyl figure, a resin film-character statue, a garden statue, a plaster bust. *Precedence (framework):* An ornamental figurine of a person or animal is F2, in any of the key's six materials; a figure of a character, an action figure or a collectible-series or vinyl figure is E1; a garden statue or gnome is B4; a vase or bowl is B4 or B5 however artistic. The ornament-versus-character line is the framework's, and no source fetched here tests it †.
4. **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects.** Closest objects: a Buddha statue, an Orthodox icon, a thangka, a Madonna oil painting. *Precedence (framework, step 14 note, K-108):* "Icons, thangkas and other devotional scroll paintings, altarpieces, devotional statues made for a shrine and crucifixes are F4 (old-master altarpieces included); a painting or sculpture with a religious subject made in another form is F2 or B4." The devotional forms are F4 by form; a Madonna easel painting is F2 and a garden figure B4: subject is not use. *Precedence (framework, step 14 note, K-112):* a ketubah or baptism certificate is F5, or F2 when made as a picture.
5. **B2 Soft Furnishings, B4 Decor (frames, plates, wall art).** Closest objects: Aubusson tapestry, kilim wall hanging, picture frame, wall plate, metal wall art, canvas print. *Precedence (framework, with the frame and wall-art rules proposed):* An object woven or embroidered to hang and be looked at is a tapestry or textile hanging and is F2, a rug made to be walked on is B2 even if hung; a framed picture is F2 and the empty frame is B4, as HS97 treats frames of normal kind and value as part of the picture (HS97); a decorative or commemorative plate is a plate (B5, display a tag; K-182, S5). Metal wall art and canvas prints (KUN "Metal Art", "Leinwanddrucke") are pictures on a hung surface and are F2, not B4: a printed image on metal or canvas is filed by its process with the support as an attribute, and a cut or forged metal relief is F2.5 Metal with `form = wall-object` (proposed, review F2-9(b)).
6. **F6 Natural History & Specimens.** Closest objects: a cased taxidermy fox, a composition of feathers and found objects, a carved jade animal, a polished sphere, a resin dinosaur-skull replica. *Precedence (framework, step 7 note, K-76):* "A mount of one animal prepared as in life is F6 however it is presented; a work that combines animal parts with other materials is F2" (filed under Found and mixed, kind 58). *Precedence (framework, step 7 note, K-77):* "A cast, replica or carved figure is filed by what it is (F2 sculpture, E1 toy or kit)"; so a replica of a fossil or skull is F2 when made as a sculpture and E1 when made as a toy or model kit. A stone only cut and polished into a slab, sphere, egg, palm stone or tumbled stone stays F6, and a carved animal leaves F6 for F2 or B4 (F-25). Ivory, bone and CITES-listed materials (kind 49) stay in, their status in the C17 jurisdiction ledger, never a flag on the tree; human specimens are OUT (step 7 note, K-79).
7. **F1 Media & Publications.** Closest objects: an antique engraved map sheet, a pictorial wall map, an art book, an artist's portfolio. *Precedence (framework, step 20 note, K-142):* "A map, including a single sheet from an atlas or a pictorial wall map, is F1; a print made as art that depicts a place is F2." Artists' books and portfolios (Artsy genes "Artists' Books", "Books and Portfolios", ART-G): a bound book is F1, and a loose portfolio of prints is held as the prints (agrees with F1).
8. **F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards.** Closest objects: an artist trading card, a printed series card, an art medal, a figure trophy. *Precedence (proposed; agrees with F3):* A hand-made card sold as an original artwork is F2, and a printed series card collected as a card is F3; an art medal struck or cast as a medal is F3. *Precedence (cross-memo, review boundary table):* a free-standing figure made with an award plate or inscription field is a trophy and F3; any other free-standing figure made to be looked at is F2.
9. **Other edge objects (not full boundaries).**
   - Neon and light sculpture (AAT-SC, SGL-SC) against B3 lamps and B4 neon signs: made to give light to a room is B3, made as a sign is B4, made to be looked at as sculpture is F2; light art is F2 when it is a free-standing object (F-12), and a light sculpture issued as a numbered or signed artwork edition is F2; an object with a lamp holder and cord, sold as a light (Akari models included), is B3 (R-25).
   - Digital artwork, video works and NFTs against I2: a physical print of a digital work is F2, the file or token is I2; a video work held as a file is I2 (F-12).

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure (Candidate A; F2-O1, F-2, F-10, F-11, F-12, F-13, CV-16, CV-17).** F2 Art divides first by discipline into eight sub-families (Paintings, Drawings, Prints, Photographs, Sculpture, Textile and fibre hangings, Calligraphy, and Collage, mosaic and mixed work), with a closing sub-family "Parts and accessories made for one artwork" that opens only when an S3 part is attested; Prints take leaves by printing-process family, Photographs by process class (the joined leaf "Camera-less and instant" kept), Sculpture by the material family that makes the form, and the other five sub-families store `leaf = null`; kinds are the medium, technique or material (64 in the worked tree, about 120 to 150 estimated), with pastel a drawing and gouache its own kind; named forms sit below with `maker_bound`, at most four levels below the category.
