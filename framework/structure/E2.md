## 0. Category

**Code and name:** E2 Sports, Fitness & Recreation (domain E, Play, Sport & Music).

**Membership test (placement key step 26):** "Made for a sport, dance, physical exercise, or an outdoor activity such as hiking, climbing, caving, angling, metal detecting or snow sports: balls, rackets, bats, cues, pool and snooker tables, darts and dartboards, table-tennis tables, bowling balls, fitness machines and fitness trampolines, boards, skates, kick scooters and skis, hang gliders and paragliders, tents and shelters, sleeping bags and sleeping mats, climbing, caving and navigation gear, fishing tackle and spearguns, metal detectors for treasure hunting, winter and water sports gear, combat-sport implements and their protective gear, airsoft and paintball markers, and garments, footwear and protective gear made for a sport (step 17)". Category description: "equipment for sport, dance, exercise and outdoor activities, and garments and footwear made for a sport. Inner structure: by sport or activity first, then by equipment type."

**Rules and notes that touch E2 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):**
- Step 26 note: "portable furniture, stoves, cookware, cool boxes and lanterns are filed by what they do, B1, B5 and B3, wherever they are used: 'camping' is a setting. Rucksacks and bags of every kind are A4, except cases made for one piece of equipment, which follow it under S3."
- Step 26 note: "games that are sports, including ball, racket, bat, stick, cue and target games and darts, are E2. Chess, draughts, go and other board games are E1."
- Step 17 note ("made for a sport"): "made for the demands of one sport, dance or outdoor activity and not suited to everyday wear: football boots, ski boots and ski jackets with powder skirts, climbing shoes and harnesses, ballet, tap and dance shoes, swimming costumes, wetsuits, padded cycling shorts, equestrian riding hats and sport protective guards, fencing and kendo gear. These go to step 26. Running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers, leggings, team and fan jerseys, and outdoor jackets stay here [A1 or A2]."
- Step 10 note (E4): blunted or padded implements made for a combat sport (fencing foils, epees, sabres, shinai, bokken, training weapons) and the protective gear for them are E2; airsoft and paintball markers are E2; bows and crossbows made for target shooting are E4; spearguns and other fishing tackle are E2; machetes and axes are C1 or C2.
- Step 11 note (G1): every boat, canoe, kayak, bicycle and aircraft is G1, including racing ones; "unpowered boards, skates, kick scooters, skis, sledges, hang gliders and paragliders are E2; powered paragliders are aircraft."
- Step 25 note (E1): children's play equipment, and trampolines made for play, are E1; fitness trampolines are E2.
- Step 2 note (H2): protective gear goes by what it is (step 17); massagers for comfort are H1; sports nutrition is H2.
- S3: parts follow the whole; cases, bags, covers and stands made for one object (a golf bag, a racket case) follow it; boxes and storage that hold several objects are B1; cleaning products B6; fuels C8. Never parts: anything worn on the body (step 17).
- S4 and Appendix A: region, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user (child, gender, size, ability), setting (indoor, outdoor, camping), market (professional), power source, material, condition and edition never split an inner level.

**Precedence sentences that already exist for E2 (restated, not new):** (1) A garment, shoe or protective guard made for the demands of one sport and not suited to everyday wear is E2; running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers, leggings, team jerseys and outdoor jackets are A1 or A2. (2) A sport's racket, bat, ball, stick, cue or board is E2 even when children use it; play equipment and toys are E1. (3) Camping stoves, cookware, lanterns, cool boxes and portable furniture are B-categories by what they do; tents, sleeping bags and mats are E2. (4) Bicycles, kayaks, canoes and boats are G1; unpowered boards, skates, kick scooters, skis and sledges are E2. (5) Weapons made to injure are E4; blunted combat-sport implements, airsoft and paintball markers and fishing tackle are E2.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (UK, Germany/Switzerland, Japan, China, India, US)

- **Decathlon UK (DEC-UK), "All Sports".** The site's own entry point is an A-to-Y list of about 100 sports and activities, from American Football, Archery, Badminton, Boxing and Camping to Wing Foil, Winter Sports and Yoga. Fishing is split into carp, coarse and match, predator, sea and spearfishing; there are also Dog Sports, Dance, Cross-Training, Disability Sports and Adaptive Clothing, Kids PE and Gym, and Country Sports. Equipment type comes inside each sport page. So the first axis is sport or activity, second equipment type.
- **Decathlon China (DEC-CN).** "Over 80 sports"; inside each sport the second level is a mix of equipment type and sub-activity: fishing is lure and freshwater casting, coarse (platform) fishing, sea fishing, fishing clothing, rod after-sales parts; skiing is ski clothing, thermal wear, helmets and goggles, protective gear, ski boots, skis-bindings-poles, repair parts; climbing is climbing shoes, quickdraws and protection, chalk and chalk bags, harnesses, ropes and slings; cue sports is table, cue, accessories; golf is clubs, balls and accessories, apparel, indoor golf.
- **Decathlon India (DEC-IN).** Sports footwear is listed by sport: running, walking, gym, trekking, cycling, basketball, cricket, hockey, football, tennis, badminton and squash, table tennis, climbing and mountaineering, skateboarding, horse riding, pool, snow. - **SportScheck, Germany (SC).** The main navigation is Damen, Herren, Kinder, Ausruestung, Sportarten, Marken. - **Super Sports Xebio, Japan (XEB).** The category list has two blocks. "Search by sport" (running, football and futsal, baseball and softball, basketball, volleyball, tennis, badminton, table tennis, yoga/fitness/training, swimming, athletics, handball, rugby, martial arts, other sports; golf; outdoor, camp, trekking, cycling; surf and snow; street sports; marine leisure) and "Category" (shoes, men's apparel, women's apparel, bags, caps, socks, fashion goods, balls, supports and taping, protein, supplements, dumbbells and training tools, seasonal goods, toys and stationery). ### 1.2 Trade classifications and product taxonomies

- **HS heading 9506 (HS).** The customs tree for "articles and equipment for general physical exercise, gymnastics, athletics, other sports (including table-tennis) or outdoor games" is sport-first for sports with a dominant implement and type-first elsewhere: 950611 skis (cross-country 95061110; snowboards 95061121; downhill 95061129; other 95061180), 950612 ski bindings, 950619 other ski equipment; 950621 sailboards; 950629 water-skis, surfboards and other water-sport equipment; 950631-39 golf clubs, golf balls, other golf equipment; 950640 table-tennis articles; 950651 lawn-tennis rackets; 950659 badminton and similar rackets; balls by type (950661 tennis, 950662 inflatable, 950669 cricket, polo and others); 950670 ice and roller skates; 950691 general exercise equipment (adjustable-resistance apparatus; other: gymnastic apparatus, kettlebells, medicine balls); 950699 other (cricket and polo equipment; the rest). The chapter note excludes sports clothing (Chapters 61 and 62), sports footwear other than skates (64) and sports headgear (65). So customs separates sport implements from sport garments, and splits by implement for a few sports and by function (exercise, balls) for the rest. (Customs headings are evidence only, not a source of kinds.)
- **Google product taxonomy, branch Sporting Goods (G-ATH, G-OUT, G-FIT).** Four children: Athletics (25 subcategories, 231 in total), Exercise and Fitness (26 subcategories, 56 in total), Indoor Games, Outdoor Recreation (17 subcategories, 458 in total, to level 7). Athletics and Outdoor Recreation are sport-first: American Football, Baseball and Softball, Boxing and Martial Arts, Fencing, Field Hockey and Lacrosse, Rugby, Soccer, Tennis, Track and Field, Wrestling; Boating and Water Sports (with Wetsuit Pieces, Diving and Snorkeling, Surfing, Swimming), Camping and Hiking, Climbing, Cycling, Equestrian, Fishing, Golf, Hunting and Shooting, Inline and Roller Skating, Outdoor Games, Skateboarding. Inside a sport the next level is equipment type, and Protective Gear, Gloves and apparel are sub-groups of the sport (Fencing Protective Gear: Gloves and Cuffs, Jackets and Lames, Masks; American Football Protective Gear: helmets, shoulder pads, girdles). Exercise and Fitness is type-first (Cardio Machines, Weight Lifting, Yoga and Pilates, Exercise Balls, Foam Rollers), because exercise is not one sport. These pages were read through a third-party mirror (WisePIM), not Google's own file (†: fetch Google's file if the owner allows).
- **Wikipedia (WIKI).** "Category:Sports equipment by sport" has about 35 subcategories each named for a sport (Badminton, Baseball, Cricket, Cue sports, Cycling, Golf, Hiking, Hockey, Hunting, Martial arts, Paintball, Skiing, Table tennis, Tennis, Water sports and others). The reference work is sport-first, then object.
- **GS1 Global Product Classification** has a "Sports Equipment" segment (page seen, June 2018 version; the classification itself was not read; search excerpt only; not used).

### 1.3 Standards bodies

- **UIAA (UIAA).** Safety standards for over 25 types of climbing and mountaineering equipment, each a numbered standard by equipment type (ropes 101 and 110, connectors 121, pitons 122, rock anchors 123, chocks 124, frictional anchors 125, rope clamps 126, pulleys 127, braking devices 129, ice anchors 151, snow anchors 154, snow pickets 155, avalanche shovels 156 and probes 157, crash pads 161, helmets including ski-mountaineering helmets 111). A standard attaches to an equipment type that may be used in several sports (climbing, mountaineering, ski touring).
- **FIE (FIE).** Fencing material rules: homologation is compulsory for blades, points, protective clothing (jackets, breeches, under-plastrons), masks, gloves, scoring apparatus and pistes; fabric must resist 800 N, mask bib 1600 N, glove cuff 350 N; separate rules for foil, epee and sabre. Here the standard defines the kinds for a whole sport, and homologation is a certification attribute of the kind.

### 1.4 Enthusiast buying guides (spec ladders and conventions)

- **Rackets.** TP (UK retailer) uses six aspects: swing type, head size, frame length, profile height, weight, balance; named head-size classes midsize (up to 97 sq in), mid plus (97 to 105), oversize (106 to 115), super oversize (116 and over); weight bands from "very heavy" to "ultra light" in grams; balance bands in cm. TCOM (US) gives 300 g and 100 sq in as the dividing lines between player's frames and "tweener" or game-improvement frames, and string patterns 16x19 and 18x20. BHQ (UK) classes badminton rackets by balance (head-heavy, even, head-light), weight in U units (3U about 85 to 89 g, 4U about 80 to 84 g, 5U/6U lighter), shaft flex and grip size, and maps maker families to balance (Yonex Astrox head-heavy, Arcsaber even, Nanoflare head-light; Victor and Li-Ning families). These are measured, spec-banded attributes of one kind.
- **Skis.** SKI-CZ (Czech rental shop, citing the Czech ski magazine SNOW.cz and Salomon) types skis by terrain: on-piste (easy recreational, sport, slalom, GS), all-mountain, freeride, freestyle, touring, and says three figures tell the type: width underfoot, sidecut radius, profile (camber/rocker). It warns that the lines are not fixed and each maker draws them differently. HS separates cross-country, downhill and "other" skis, snowboards and bindings. SC lists Skier (skis), Skihelme, Skibrillen, Skihandschuhe, Skistoecke, Skiprotektoren.
- **Ski boots.** EVO-SKI (US) says flex index runs from about 50 to 130+ (soft, medium, stiff, very stiff by ability), "not standardized between boot manufacturers". Flex is a number measured on the boot, on a maker's own scale.
- **Golf irons.** MGS and GL (US) use consensus categories: super game improvement, game improvement, players distance, players (cavity back), muscle back, blades, hybrid irons; GL says the categories are not exclusive (cavity back is a design that can appear in several categories); MGS says category lines blur. ALP splits golf by club type (driver, fairway wood, utility, iron set, single iron, wedge, putter), which HS and G-OUT collapse to "golf clubs".
- **Cricket bats.** PSC (Australia) describes Grades 1 to 4 as a willow-quality scale (grain count, blemishes), with a note elsewhere that the grading is a convention, not a standard (sportsgear24x7 text seen in a search excerpt only). Price and grade rise together.
- **Fishing rods.** FM (US) uses two spec scales: power (ultra-light, light, medium, medium-heavy, heavy) and action (extra-fast to slow), written as "MH Fast".
- **Wetsuits.** EVO-WS (US): thickness in mm written 3/2, 4/3, 5/4/3, mapped to water-temperature bands; types are full suit, springsuit/shorty, long john, top, bottom, rashguard.
- **Football boots.** PDS (UK retailer): soleplate/ground codes FG, SG, AG, MG, TF, IC (indoor) are an attribute of a boot; UNI (Denmark) shows the maker ladder: silos (adidas Predator, F50, Copa) and the maker's own levels (Elite, Pro, League, Club) each available in several soleplate codes.
- **Forums.** GolfWRX (equipment forum sections such as Tour and Pre-Release Equipment, Golf Balls, Putters, Long Driver's Lab, shaft, grips and clubmaking, rangefinders and wearables) divides golf equipment by equipment type inside one sport forum (search excerpt only; the fetch of the forum page returned nothing usable). Talk Tennis (Racquets forum) could not be read.

### 1.5 Collectors, resale and museums

- **Collectors (GHS, WTR).** Golf Heritage Society divides club collecting by era and make (Feather Ball era long-nose woods and blacksmith irons; Gutta Percha era; mass-produced clubs with makers, cleek marks and matched sets; patent clubs; classic clubs about 1930 to 1990), with putters as a separate path. The wood-tennis-racket collectors guide is organised by technological generation, maker, mark and patent, and a proposed grading of quality versus condition. Collectors' axes are era, maker and condition: all Appendix A modifiers, none a kind. Condition grades belong to one particular object and are not stored (C16).
- **Resale (EBAY, SLS).** See 1.1. Resale sites are sport-first with equipment type inside, plus condition.
- **Museums (NMAH).** The Smithsonian groups "Sports and Leisure": paper sports objects (programs, cards), leisure collections (camping vehicles and gear, video games, playing cards, sportswear, exercise equipment), toys. A museum divides by collection and material rather than a sport-first equipment tree, so it adds nothing to a structure for owned equipment.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

- **Agree:** every retailer, the customs tree (for racket, ski and golf sports), the Google branches Athletics and Outdoor Recreation, Wikipedia and the resale sites start from the sport or activity. Equipment type is the second level everywhere except fitness equipment, where the sport is "exercise" and type is first (G-FIT, HS 950691).
- **Split:** (a) garments and footwear. Customs and the framework's step 17 both pull sports clothing and footwear out of the equipment tree, while every retailer and Google keep them inside the sport (Decathlon India sells "Tennis Shoes", Google puts Fencing Jackets under Fencing). The sport-first retail tree is therefore not the E2 tree: it holds objects that the framework files in A1, A2, A4, B-categories and G1. (b) Activity grouping: eBay uses "Team Sports", "Water Sports", "Winter Sports", "Tennis and Racquet Sports", "Outdoor Sports"; Xebio uses sport lists plus "Outdoor", "Surf and Snow", "Street sports"; Decathlon uses a flat A-to-Z with "Winter Sports" and "Watersports" as extra pages; Google uses Athletics, Outdoor Recreation, Indoor Games, Exercise. No two sources use the same families, which means a family level is an editorial choice. (c) Spec ladders: racket specs are numeric; ski type and boot flex are explicitly non-standard; golf iron categories and cricket grades are conventions; only climbing (UIAA) and fencing (FIE) have standards bodies.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Sport or activity first, equipment type second, spec third.** Evidence:
- Navigation order: all of DEC-UK, DEC-CN, DEC-IN, SC, XEB, ALP, EBAY and SLS open with the sport; Google and Wikipedia do the same for Athletics and Outdoor Recreation. An owner cannot even describe a ski boot, fencing mask or football boot without the sport.
- Filter order: ALP's tree goes sport, equipment type, then spec (hard or soft ball, composite or wood; shakehand or penholder). XEB goes sport, then type (racket, strings, shoes, balls, bags), then variant (court surface; ball size; official ball). The buyer's questions in the guides follow the same order: "what racket" (TP, BHQ), "what ski type" (SKI-CZ), "what boot ground type" (PDS).
- Kinds are sport-specific: a tennis ball, a golf ball and a rugby ball are different things with different standards (HS has separate subheadings for tennis, golf and table-tennis balls); a racket for tennis is not interchangeable with one for badminton. Sport as the parent therefore keeps kinds generic (tennis ball) rather than forcing a cross-sport "ball" kind that no buyer shops for.
- The exception is fitness and recreation without a sport: Google puts Exercise and Fitness type-first (cardio machines, weights, yoga), and HS 950691 does the same. Buyers there choose by function (treadmill, kettlebell, yoga mat), so the leaf should be named for the activity ("Strength training", "Yoga and Pilates") and kinds follow.
- Spec is the third question: racket weight and balance, ski width, boot flex, wetsuit thickness, rod power and action. These are attributes of a kind, not further levels (see 4b and 5).
- A kind that several sports use is placed by the C14 tie-break (section 3), not by repeating it in each sport.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): activity family, sport, equipment type, named form

Level names are the fixed names of C5(a): sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels below the category, the named form counting as one).

| Level | Name | One stated axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | Kind of activity (an editorial grouping of sports that retailers and Google group together). The one exception is the last sub-family, "Multisport, fitness and protective gear" (C14c), whose leaf axis is function. |
| 2 | Leaf | The sport or activity the object is made for, read within each sub-family (C5c); the leaf axis of each sub-family is stated in the table below |
| 3 | Kind | Generic equipment type used in that sport (the ownable thing), including sport-specific garments, footwear and protective gear |
| 4 | Named form | A design below the kind, recorded with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2a): maker-bound = a model or a series inside one kind (Yonex Astrox, TaylorMade P790); not maker-bound = a generic design many makers build that buyers ask for by name (dome tent, tipi tent) |

A series that spans several kinds (a maker line) is a C1 lines row, not a named form. Model year, colourway and reference are C3 variants and never a level.

**Leaf axis per sub-family (C5c).** Sports and activities for all sub-families except the last. "General" leaves exist only where a kind is used by more than one leaf of the same sub-family (C14b); a general leaf is listed first.

**Parts of a sport's equipment (S3).** E2's leaf axis already names the host object's sport, so parts made for one object (tennis string, table-tennis rubber, ski binding, racket case, golf bag) are filed in the host's leaf as kinds, and this is stated here as the E2 definition. **OWNER:** C5(d) as drafted requires a closing "Parts and accessories" sub-family for domains A and H; the review proposes (X9) that a category whose leaf axis names the host may file parts in the host's leaf. That amendment is not yet in the conventions (section 10).

Sub-families (16, in alphabetical order, the multi-use one last): Air sports; Athletics, gymnastics and dance; Climbing, caving and navigation; Combat and marker sports; Cycling gear; Equestrian; Fishing; Golf, cue and target sports; Gym, fitness and exercise; Hiking, camping and trail; Racket and paddle sports; Rolling and board sports; Snow and ice sports; Team and ball sports; Water sports; Multisport, fitness and protective gear. Cycling gear and Equestrian hold about 12 kinds or fewer and store their kinds with `leaf = null` (C5b).

Why it fits: it is the order of every retail tree read (1.1), of Google's Athletics and Outdoor Recreation branches, of Wikipedia, and of the framework's own category description. Sport-specific garments and footwear sit inside the sport as kinds (football boot, ski boot, fencing jacket, climbing shoe, wetsuit), which matches how Decathlon India, Google and Xebio sell them.

Where it breaks, and the fix for each (four edge cases):
1. **A kind used by several sports.** A wetsuit serves surfing, diving, triathlon and kayaking; a ski helmet, goggles and gloves serve alpine skiing and snowboarding; a mouthguard serves boxing, rugby and hockey; a skipping rope serves boxing and fitness; crampons serve climbing and hiking. Fix, the **C14 tie-break**, applied in this order: (a) the leaf of the sport whose rule or standard defines the kind, if there is one (fencing mask: FIE; climbing helmet: UIAA); (b) the general leaf of the one sub-family that holds all its uses (wetsuit and swim goggles: Water sports, general; ski helmet and ski goggles: Snow and ice sports, general); (c) otherwise the category-level sub-family "Multisport, fitness and protective gear", with leaves by function (mouthguard, knee pad, shin guard, skipping rope, punch bag, crampons). A generated view lists the kind under every sport that uses it. Google's placement of wetsuits at the family level ("Boating and Water Sports > Boating and Water Sport Apparel > Wetsuits") agrees with rule (b).
2. **A sport with little in E2.** Running is among the biggest sport pages at every retailer, but the framework keeps running shoes in A2, running clothing in A1 and running bags in A4. Only spikes, starting blocks, hurdles and similar remain, so "Track and field" is a thin leaf here while a retailer's running page is large. The generated view (section 7) is what recreates the retailer's sport page.
3. **A multi-sport object.** Triathlon, "cross-training", "multisport" and "general fitness" objects do not belong to one sport (Decathlon lists Triathlon, Cross-Training and HIIT as separate sports). Fix: by C14 the object goes to the sub-family that holds all its uses, or to the multi-use sub-family. A leaf named "Multisport and cross-training" is created in the multi-use sub-family only if harvest finds kinds that C14(a) to (c) cannot place.
4. **A sport whose principal implement belongs elsewhere.** Archery bows and crossbows made for target shooting are E4 (step 10); kayaks, canoes and bicycles are G1; saddles and tack are animal equipment (B10). The sport leaf then holds only the E2 remainder (section 9).

### Candidate B: equipment type first, sport second

Levels: 1 role of the object in play (implement, ball or projectile, fixture such as net or table, protective gear, garment, footwear, machine, training aid); 2 sport; 3 kind; 4 named form. Examples: all balls under "ball", then football, tennis, rugby; all rackets under "racket", then tennis, badminton, squash.

Support: HS 9506 uses it for balls (950661 to 950669), skates (950670) and exercise apparatus (950691); Google Exercise and Fitness uses it; Xebio's second block ("shoes, apparel, bags, caps, socks, balls, supports") uses it.

Where it breaks: (1) a retail buyer does not look for "all balls"; (2) a football boot and a tennis shoe have nothing in common except being footwear, which the framework already files in A2; (3) kinds such as "fencing jacket" or "climbing harness" have no useful cross-sport siblings; (4) it repeats the framework's rule that function, not material or form class, is the axis. It remains useful as a generated cross-sport view ("all balls", "all protective gear").

### Candidate C: flat list of sports (no family level), then kind, then named form

Levels: 1 leaf = sport or activity (about 130); 2 kind; 3 named form. The activity families become generated views.

Support: DEC-UK and DEC-CN present a flat A-to-Z list; no two sources agree on the families (1.6), so a family level is arbitrary. Where it breaks: 130 leaves at one level is a long list for the owner and for navigation; fitness (not a sport) and "Camping" (a setting) sit awkwardly as sports; with no parent, the C14 general leaf and the multi-use sub-family have no home.

Recommendation: A, with C as the fallback if the owner prefers fewer arbitrary groupings. **OWNER:** the choice is an owner decision (section 10, item 1).

## 4. Depth and size

All counts are estimates built from fetched category counts and are marked as such.

- **Sports and activities (leaves).** DEC-UK lists about 100 and DEC-CN says "over 80"; adding sports that only specialist or regional sources list (pelota, teqball, wallball, Gaelic games, kendo, via ferrata) gives about 120 to 150 leaves, plus a few non-sport leaves (general fitness, multi-use function leaves, general-leaf entries).
- **Sub-families.** 16 (15 activity families and the multi-use sub-family).
- **Generic kinds.** Google's three E2-relevant branches hold 745 categories (Athletics 231, Outdoor Recreation 458, Exercise and Fitness 56; G-ATH, G-OUT, G-FIT), but many are parts, accessories, bags or apparel that S3 sends to the same place or to A4. Sport-specific garments and footwear (about 5 to 15 per sport for 40 or so sports) are not in Google's counts as separate kinds. Estimate: 900 to 1,500 kinds.
- **Named forms (models, series, iconic designs).** If recorded at maker-series level (Yonex Astrox, adidas Predator, TaylorMade P790), about 3,000 to 8,000 series across the category; if every model year were recorded it would be tens of thousands. Recommendation: record series as named forms or lines, and record model year, colourway and reference only as C3 variants where an owner-emphasis scene needs depth.
- **Where depth concentrates.** Golf (club types, shafts, and the largest set of named forms); football boots and other boots by ground or surface (ground codes); skiing and snowboarding (skis, boots, bindings); fishing (very many kinds of tackle; DEC-UK splits the sport into five); climbing (UIAA lists over 25 equipment types); camping and trail (tents, sleeping bags, mats, poles); racket sports (spec-banded attributes rather than kinds); fitness machines.
- **Broad leaves** (dozens of kinds): golf, football, skiing, fishing, climbing, camping and trail, baseball and softball, gym and strength, water sports (diving), cycling gear. **Medium** (8 to 20): tennis, badminton, table tennis, rugby, cricket, fencing, boxing, judo, skateboarding, ice skating, snowboarding, yoga. **Narrow** (1 to 7): darts, boules, pelota, teqball, kite sports, orienteering, slacklining.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule: a kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers (C2a).** Evidence for where that level sits in this category:

- **The equipment type inside a sport is the comparison level.** Buyers compare racket against racket (TP, BHQ, TCOM), ski against ski (SKI-CZ), ski boot against ski boot (EVO-SKI), football boot against football boot (UNI, PDS), cricket bat against cricket bat (PSC), fishing rod against fishing rod (FM), wetsuit against wetsuit (EVO-WS). The filter trees of ALP and XEB stop at the same level and offer the finer split as a filter (court surface, ball size, composite or wood).
- **Finer distinctions that stay attributes (not kinds), each with its scale named:**
  - racket head size, weight and balance classes, and badminton U-weight (TP, BHQ; band labels are shop conventions derived from the measured numbers);
  - ski use class (on-piste, all-mountain, freeride, freestyle, touring): SKI-CZ says the lines are not fixed and each maker draws them differently, and width, radius and rocker are the real figures; a second source from another region is needed (†);
  - golf iron category (game improvement, players distance, players, blade): GL says the categories overlap, and MGS says they blur (two US sources; a source from another region is needed, †);
  - cricket-bat willow grade 1 to 4 ("retailer convention", PSC; a second source is needed, †), rod power and action (FM), wetsuit thickness (EVO-WS), ski-boot flex index ("maker scale, not comparable across makers", EVO-SKI), football-boot ground code (PDS), ball pressure class or ball size (ALP, XEB);
  - motorised or manual drive (treadmill: C10, the body and use are the same kind), and mounting or setting (basketball hoop in-ground, wall-mounted or portable; pull-up bar doorway or wall; table-tennis table indoor or outdoor): installation attributes under C12, none a kind;
  - junior and ball sizes: size attributes (C6), with the standard named where one exists.
- **Where the kind level goes deeper than "equipment type":** golf clubs, where driver, fairway wood, hybrid, iron set, wedge and putter are compared separately (ALP, GL, MGS) and HS and Google collapse them to "golf clubs"; table tennis, where bat and rubber are bought and compared separately (ALP: rubber types); fencing, where foil, epee and sabre are three weapons with three rule sets (FIE); skis, where alpine, cross-country and snowboard are three different objects (HS 95061110, 95061121, 95061129).
- **Where the kind level is higher than the retail category:** balls. Retail pages split balls by ball size and pressure (XEB: size 5, 4, soft, beach; ALP: pressure and pressureless), but these are attributes of "tennis ball", "volleyball", "rugby ball".
- **OWNER:** whether touring skis are a separate kind (they are used with skins and touring bindings, so they pass the C2a comparison test; the review recommends a kind), as opposed to an ordinary alpine ski with a different width. The sources treat the difference as a use class, not a standard, so this memo keeps the attribute and flags it (section 10, item 2).
- **Traditional and historical forms (C2, C11).** Long-nose woods, blacksmith irons and wooden racket frames are, if kept, non-maker-bound named forms under driver, fairway wood, iron set and tennis racket, with an era tag. **OWNER:** whether they are recorded at all (section 10, item 9). A traditional sport object is a kind only when a fetched source sells it as its own category and its form differs from the generic kind (C2c, C11); none is proposed here beyond judo uniform, which XEB sells as its own category.

The choice level differs between sub-families: it is "equipment type" for most; "club type" for golf; "weapon" for fencing; "machine" for fitness; and "garment or footwear type for the sport" (football boot, ski boot, wetsuit, fencing jacket, climbing shoe) for sport-specific apparel.

## 5. Quality and tier

**No tier sits on a kind (C4a).** The category mixes four kinds of ordering. Each is recorded in the way the conventions fix, and none is a level.

**Price band (C4d).** One domain-wide scheme in USD (under 50, 50 to 150, 150 to 500, 500 to 1,500, 1,500 to 5,000, 5,000 to 15,000, over 15,000), derived from a dated, sourced list price on the maker, line or named-form row. The BHQ price guide maps to these bands. A platform's own label is a dated `platform_tag` (C4c); none is used here.

Recommended primary label for the category: **mixed: measured, banded attributes within a kind; maker ladders as tier entries on lines or named forms; certification attributes where a standards body exists.** Nothing orders kinds against each other across sports.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity, with small platform pockets.**
- Identity evidence: BHQ maps maker families to a spec profile (Yonex Astrox is head-heavy, Arcsaber even, Nanoflare head-light; Victor and Li-Ning have parallel families); UNI sells boots by maker silo (Predator for control, F50 for speed, Copa for touch) and level; ALP nests maker series under footwear (Nike Phantom and Mercurial, Mizuno Morelia, adidas Predator) and rackets by maker (Yonex, Wilson, Head, Babolat, Prince, Dunlop); MGS and GL cite iron models by maker (Titleist T150, TaylorMade P790, Mizuno Pro 243); collectors divide golf and tennis by maker and mark (GHS, WTR); Decathlon sells under its own sport-specific brands (DEC-CN pages show Btwin, Quechua, Domyos, Kiprun, Olaian, Perfly, Forclaz, Kalenji).
- **Platform pockets go to the C13 compatibility ledger (`platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `sources`); platforms are never levels or attributes.** Candidate rows, none attested by a fetched page in this batch (†, need sourcing before use): ISO 5355 alpine sole, ISO 9523 touring sole, GripWalk and tech (pin) touring inserts, serving ski boots and bindings; SPD, SPD-SL and Look Keo cleat systems, serving cycling shoes (E2) and pedals (G1); golf adapter-sleeve systems by maker, serving drivers and shafts.
- **Recommendation: yes, one maker ledger on the C1 core schema** (`maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`). Differences from the earlier draft: the retailer own-brand flag becomes `maker_class` = retail own-label, so Decathlon's per-sport brands (Btwin, Quechua, Domyos, Kiprun, Olaian, Perfly, Forclaz, Kalenji) are maker rows with `parent_id` = Decathlon; "sport leaves" is derived from `known_for_kinds`, not stored; "tier (consensus)" leaves the ledger (section 5). Because the same maker spans many sports (Yonex appears for badminton rackets in BHQ and tennis rackets in ALP; Wilson appears for tennis rackets in ALP and golf irons in GL), `known_for_kinds` links the maker to many leaves; the maker never becomes a level.
- **Lines, named forms and variants.** A maker's series spanning several kinds is a row in the lines table (`line_id`, `maker_id`, kinds spanned, tier entries, years, sources). A series inside one kind (Yonex Astrox, TaylorMade P790) is a maker-bound named form. Whether a given series (for example adidas Predator, which UNI shows on boots) spans kinds is settled at harvest from the maker's own page (†). A model year, colourway, collaboration or reference is a C3 variant (`variant_type` = reference, colourway, retro or re-issue, collaboration, limited edition), filled only where an owner-emphasis scene needs depth.
- Where a maker designs and another builds (own-label goods built by a supplier), record `built_by_maker_id` on the line (C1 addition).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Order sub-families alphabetically, with the multi-use sub-family last; order leaves alphabetically, with a general leaf first; order kinds by their role in play (the secondary key this sub-family states, C7); order named forms by C7.**

Leaves: every source with an A-to-Z list (DEC-UK, DEC-CN, SC) uses alphabetical order and no source gives a principled non-alphabetical order.

Kinds inside a sport leaf, in the role-in-play order (canonical form first):
1. the defining implement (racket, club, ski, board, bat, rod);
2. the object that is struck, thrown or ridden on (ball, shuttlecock, puck) and sport-specific fittings of the implement (strings, bindings);
3. fixtures (net, goal, table, board);
4. protective gear;
5. sport-specific garments;
6. sport-specific footwear;
7. training aids and other accessories that are kinds in their own right.

Reason: this is a recommendation, weakly supported. The sources do not agree on one order: XEB lists badminton as racket, strings, shoes, wear, and tennis as shoes, strings, balls, bags; ALP lists table tennis as bat, rubber, small goods, shoes, ball, bag, wear, tables; Google orders alphabetically; HS orders by implement, then balls, then skates, then other. The proposed order puts the object that defines the sport first, which is the one point on which all of them agree (the racket, club, ski or bat appears at or near the top wherever the list is not alphabetical). The tree in section 3 prints kinds alphabetically for readability; the role-in-play order is the stored order key.

**Named forms inside a kind (C7, with the draft amendment):** non-maker-bound forms come first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; maker-bound forms follow, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically. Spec order (head size, flex, weight) is a sort option in generated views, not a stored order. Variants go by `variant_code` or year. There is no "certified form first" rule: a certification is an attribute.

## 9. Boundaries

The closest cross-boundary objects and a precedence sentence for each. Sentences marked "(existing)" restate the framework; sentences marked "(proposed)" are new and need the owner unless marked as following a convention.

**E2 and A1 / A2 (sport garments, footwear and protective gear).** This is the largest overlap. Retailers sell tennis shoes, basketball shoes, golf shoes, cycling shoes, skateboard shoes, rash guards, ski goggles, cycling helmets and swimwear on the sport page (DEC-IN shoe list, SC, XEB), while the framework splits them by whether they are "made for the demands of one sport and not suited to everyday wear".
- P1 (existing). A garment or shoe made for the demands of one sport, such as a football boot, ski boot, climbing shoe, ballet shoe, wetsuit, swimming costume or padded cycling short, is E2; a running shoe, hiking boot, general trainer, legging, team jersey or outdoor jacket is A1 or A2.
- P2 (proposed). A helmet is filed by the hazard it was designed for, in the key's order: armour against weapons is E4 (step 10 comes before step 17); a helmet made for a single sport and listed with that sport's protective guards (riding hat, ski helmet, climbing helmet, ice-hockey and American-football helmet, fencing mask) is E2; motorcycle and cycling helmets and helmets for other hazards are A1 (named in the A1 branch). This closes a gap: the A1 branch names cycling helmets while the step 17 note names equestrian riding hats and "sport protective guards", and the framework does not say where ski, climbing and hockey helmets go. SC and G-ATH sell them with the sport. **OWNER:** sport-specific helmets in E2, or all protective headgear in A1 (section 10, item 3).
- P3 (proposed). Goggles follow the same rule: ski, swim and diving goggles and masks made for one sport are E2; sunglasses and other non-corrective eyewear are A4; safety glasses are A1. **OWNER:** same decision as P2.
- P4 (proposed, query). Court, field and gym shoes sold by sport (tennis, basketball, badminton, squash, table tennis, indoor court shoes) are E2 only when the framework test "not suited to everyday wear" holds; otherwise they are A2. The framework gives football boots and climbing shoes as E2 and "general trainers" as A2, but not court shoes. **OWNER:** where the line falls, including beachwear (section 10, item 4).

**E2 and A4 / S3 (bags and cases).**
- P5 (existing, restated). A bag of every kind is A4, except a case made to hold one piece of equipment, which follows it: a golf bag, racket case, ski bag and boot-and-helmet bag made for one set of equipment follow E2; a gym bag, duffel, hydration pack, chalk bag and sports backpack are A4. The chalk bag, quiver and boot bag are borderline cases the framework does not name.

**E2 and G1 (vehicles and boards).**
- P6 (existing). A bicycle, kayak, canoe or boat is G1; an unpowered skateboard, surfboard, stand-up paddleboard, ski, snowboard, sledge, kick scooter, hang glider or paraglider is E2; a powered board or scooter (an electric kick scooter) is G1. (Framework issue 4 below asks for this to be written as a note.)
- P7 (proposed). A part, accessory or paddle made for one vehicle follows the vehicle (kayak paddles, bike pumps, bike racks and bottle cages are G1); cycling clothing and cycling shoes are E2; a turbo trainer or indoor bike trainer that holds a real bicycle follows the bicycle (G1), while an exercise bike that is a complete machine is E2.
- P7b (proposed). Parachutes and skydiving gear (G-OUT lists Hang Gliding and Skydiving, with Parachutes) are not named by the framework; this memo suggests E2 (Air sports).

**E2 and E4 (weapons and target sports).**
- P8 (existing). A bow or crossbow made for target shooting is E4; a blunted foil, epee, sabre, shinai or bokken made for sport, the protective gear for it, and airsoft and paintball markers are E2; a sword made for fighting is E4.
- P9 (proposed). Archery arrows and bow accessories follow the bow to E4 (S3); archery targets, arm guards and finger tabs are worn or standing equipment and are E2; clay pigeons and clay throwers (G-OUT) are targets for shooting and are E2, while shooting rests and bipods follow the gun to E4. The framework does not mention archery, clay shooting or hunting accessories (decoys, blinds, game calls), which Google puts under "Hunting and Shooting" (G-OUT). **OWNER:** these homes, and whether a quiver goes to E4 with the bow (this memo's recommendation) or, as S3's "never parts: anything worn on the body" reads for a hip or back quiver, to A4 (section 10, item 5). Archery is a leaf only if the owner accepts P9.
- P9b (proposed, follows the key's order). Ear defenders and shooting glasses are A1 (step 17), whatever the use.
- P15 (proposed, jointly with E4). A blank-firing pistol made to start races is E2 (Athletics, gymnastics and dance > Track and field, starting pistol †); one made for self-defence or deterrence is E4.
- Slingshots, blowguns, boomerangs and shuriken (E4 question 9): **OWNER:** the boomerang is E2 (a throwing sport) or E1 (a toy); the others are E4 unless sold as toys (section 10, item 11).

**E2 and E1 (play and toys).**
- P10 (existing). Equipment for a sport is E2 whoever uses it, and junior sizes stay in the sport leaf; play equipment made to be played with as a toy (play-set rackets, foam balls, swing sets, play trampolines, table football) is E1. DEC-UK lists Table Football, Teqball and Dodgeball as sports; the framework files table football as E1 and the rest as E2.
- P16 (proposed, with E1; needs a fetched source for the cut, †). A fitness trampoline or rebounder (small frame, sold for exercise) is E2; a garden or play trampoline is E1.
- P17 (follows C15). A kite, flying disc or ball made for play by children that cannot do the real object's job to that object's own standard is E1; a ball, disc or kite made to a sport's standard (an official rugby ball, a traction kite) is E2. The real object in a child's size stays E2, size being an attribute (C6).

**E2 and B-categories (camping).**
- P11 (existing). A tent, shelter, sleeping bag or sleeping mat is E2; a portable table, chair or camp bed is B1; a camping stove or cookset is B5; a lantern is B3; a cool box is B-category by its function. **E2 and H1 / H2 (health and body-use).**
- P13 (proposed). A brace or support made to treat or support an injured joint is H2; padded guards worn to protect during a sport (knee pads for volleyball, shin guards, shoulder pads) are E2; a foam roller, resistance band or exercise recovery tool used in training is E2; a massager for comfort is H1; sports-nutrition powders, bars and gels are H2 (existing).

- P14 (existing, restated). ## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A):** sub-family (kind of activity), leaf (sport or activity), kind (generic equipment type, including sport-specific garments and footwear), named form (maker-bound series or model, or a not maker-bound design). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels**, as the framework allows (C5a).
