# E2 Sports, Fitness & Recreation — structure memo (research draft)

Stage 2 research memo for the owner. It describes how the world already divides sports equipment and sport-specific garments and footwear, and recommends a structure with evidence. It is not a decision. Every claim about how a source divides the category cites a page fetched in batch S2_E2; the same URLs, with a one-line description each, are in `verify/out/S2_E2_sources.tsv`. Where a fact was seen only in a search excerpt it is marked "(search excerpt only)". Framework: CATEGORIES_v0_6.md. Owner decisions: BRIEF.md. Counts of kinds and named forms are estimates and are marked as such.

Source tags used below (URLs are in the sources file): DEC-UK, DEC-CN, DEC-IN (Decathlon UK, China, India); SC (SportScheck, Germany); XEB (Super Sports Xebio, Japan); ALP (Alpen, Japan); EBAY; SLS (SidelineSwap); AMZ (Amazon US); WIKI (Wikipedia category); G-ATH, G-OUT, G-FIT (Google product taxonomy branches Athletics, Outdoor Recreation, Exercise and Fitness, read through WisePIM); HS (tariffnumber.com, HS heading 9506); UIAA; FIE; SKI-CZ (skiverleih.cz); TP (Tennisplanet); BHQ (Badminton HQ); TCOM (Tennis.com); MGS and GL (MyGolfSpy, GolfLink); PSC (PS Cricket); FM (Fishing Mood); EVO-SKI, EVO-WS (evo ski boot and wetsuit guides); PDS (Pro:Direct football boot guide); UNI (Unisport adidas boot guide); GHS (Golf Heritage Society); WTR (Wood Tennis Rackets collectors guide); NMAH (Smithsonian National Museum of American History).

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** E2 Sports, Fitness & Recreation (domain E, Play, Sport & Music).

**Membership test (placement key step 26):** "Made for a sport, dance, physical exercise, or an outdoor activity such as hiking, climbing, caving, angling, metal detecting or snow sports: balls, rackets, bats, cues, pool and snooker tables, darts and dartboards, table-tennis tables, bowling balls, fitness machines and fitness trampolines, boards, skates, kick scooters and skis, hang gliders and paragliders, tents and shelters, sleeping bags and sleeping mats, climbing, caving and navigation gear, fishing tackle and spearguns, metal detectors for treasure hunting, winter and water sports gear, combat-sport implements and their protective gear, airsoft and paintball markers, and garments, footwear and protective gear made for a sport (step 17)". Category description: "equipment for sport, dance, exercise and outdoor activities, and garments and footwear made for a sport. Inner structure: by sport or activity first, then by equipment type."

**Rules and notes that touch E2 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):**
- Step 26 note: "portable furniture, stoves, cookware, cool boxes and lanterns are filed by what they do, B1, B5 and B3, wherever they are used: 'camping' is a setting. Rucksacks and bags of every kind are A4, except cases made for one piece of equipment, which follow it under S3."
- Step 26 note: "games that are sports, including ball, racket, bat, stick, cue and target games and darts, are E2. Chess, draughts, go and other board games are E1."
- Step 17 note ("made for a sport"): "made for the demands of one sport, dance or outdoor activity and not suited to everyday wear: football boots, ski boots and ski jackets with powder skirts, climbing shoes and harnesses, ballet, tap and dance shoes, swimming costumes, wetsuits, padded cycling shorts, equestrian riding hats and sport protective guards, fencing and kendo gear. These go to step 26. Running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers, leggings, team and fan jerseys, and outdoor jackets stay here [A1 or A2]."
- Step 10 note (E4): blunted or padded implements made for a combat sport (fencing foils, epees, sabres, shinai, bokken, training weapons) and the protective gear for them are E2; airsoft and paintball markers are E2; bows and crossbows made for target shooting are E4; spearguns and other fishing tackle are E2; machetes and axes are C1 or C2.
- Step 11 note (G1): every boat, canoe, kayak, bicycle and aircraft is G1, including racing ones; "unpowered boards, skates, kick scooters, skis, sledges, hang gliders and paragliders are E2; powered paragliders are aircraft."
- Step 25 note (E1): children's play equipment, and trampolines made for play, are E1; fitness trampolines are E2.
- Step 2 note (H2): protective gear goes by what it is (step 17); massagers for comfort are H1; sports nutrition is H2.
- S3: parts follow the whole; cases, bags, covers and stands made for one object (a golf bag, a racket case) follow it; boxes and storage that hold several objects are B1; cleaning products B6; fuels C8.
- S4 and Appendix A: region, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user (child, gender, size, ability), setting (indoor, outdoor, camping), market (professional), power source, material, condition and edition never split an inner level.

**Precedence sentences that already exist for E2 (restated, not new):** (1) A garment, shoe or protective guard made for the demands of one sport and not suited to everyday wear is E2; running shoes, hiking boots, general trainers, leggings, team jerseys and outdoor jackets are A1 or A2. (2) A sport's racket, bat, ball, stick, cue or board is E2 even when children use it; play equipment and toys are E1. (3) Camping stoves, cookware, lanterns, cool boxes and portable furniture are B-categories by what they do; tents, sleeping bags and mats are E2. (4) Bicycles, kayaks, canoes and boats are G1; unpowered boards, skates, kick scooters, skis and sledges are E2. (5) Weapons made to injure are E4; blunted combat-sport implements, airsoft and paintball markers and fishing tackle are E2.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (UK, Germany/Switzerland, Japan, China, India, US)

- **Decathlon UK (DEC-UK), "All Sports".** The site's own entry point is an A-to-Y list of about 100 sports and activities, from American Football, Archery, Badminton, Boxing and Camping to Wing Foil, Winter Sports and Yoga. Fishing is split into carp, coarse and match, predator, sea and spearfishing; there are also Dog Sports, Dance, Cross-Training, Disability Sports and Adaptive Clothing, Kids PE and Gym, and Country Sports. Equipment type comes inside each sport page. So the first axis is sport or activity, second equipment type.
- **Decathlon China (DEC-CN).** "Over 80 sports"; inside each sport the second level is a mix of equipment type and sub-activity: fishing is lure and freshwater casting, coarse (platform) fishing, sea fishing, fishing clothing, rod after-sales parts; skiing is ski clothing, thermal wear, helmets and goggles, protective gear, ski boots, skis-bindings-poles, repair parts; climbing is climbing shoes, quickdraws and protection, chalk and chalk bags, harnesses, ropes and slings; cue sports is table, cue, accessories; golf is clubs, balls and accessories, apparel, indoor golf.
- **Decathlon India (DEC-IN).** Sports footwear is listed by sport: running, walking, gym, trekking, cycling, basketball, cricket, hockey, football, tennis, badminton and squash, table tennis, climbing and mountaineering, skateboarding, horse riding, pool, snow. Sport-specific garments and footwear are therefore sold by sport, not by garment type.
- **SportScheck, Germany (SC).** The main navigation is Damen, Herren, Kinder, Ausruestung, Sportarten, Marken. Sportarten is an A-to-Z list (American Football, Badminton, Basketball, Bergsteigen, Boxen, Camping, Eislaufen, Fussball, Handball, Inlineskating, Klettern und Bouldern, Laufen, Padel-Tennis, Radsport, Rugby, Schwimmen, Ski, Snowboarding, Tennis, Tischtennis, Volleyball, Wandern, Yoga and others). Ausruestung is again organised by sport (Ski: helmets, goggles, skis, gloves, poles, rucksacks, protectors; Laufen: glasses, gloves, watches and trackers, headlamps, bags, poles).
- **Super Sports Xebio, Japan (XEB).** The category list has two blocks. "Search by sport" (running, football and futsal, baseball and softball, basketball, volleyball, tennis, badminton, table tennis, yoga/fitness/training, swimming, athletics, handball, rugby, martial arts, other sports; golf; outdoor, camp, trekking, cycling; surf and snow; street sports; marine leisure) and "Category" (shoes, men's apparel, women's apparel, bags, caps, socks, fashion goods, balls, supports and taping, protein, supplements, dumbbells and training tools, seasonal goods, toys and stationery). Inside a sport the order is equipment type: for tennis, shoes by court surface, strings, balls, bags (racket cases for one, two or six rackets); for rugby, balls with an "official ball" class and size 3 and 4; for judo, uniform, belt, accessories. So a Japanese chain gives both a sport axis and a cross-sport garment-and-equipment axis side by side.
- **Alpen, Japan (ALP).** The online filter is sport first (running and athletics, football, baseball, basketball, volleyball, tennis, table tennis, rugby, handball, camp, trekking, ski, golf), then equipment type (bat, glove, ball, spikes, wear, bag and case, protective gear and training devices), then spec splits: baseball bats by hard-ball or soft-ball and composite, metal or wood; table-tennis rackets by shakehand or penholder and rubber types (inverted, pimples-out, long pimples, large-ball); golf by driver, fairway wood, utility, iron set, single iron, wedge, putter; tents by dome, two-room, one-pole (tipi), mountain; sleeping bags by three-season, summer, winter. It also nests maker and model series under shoes (Nike Pegasus, Mizuno Morelia, adidas Predator), which is where brand depth lives for footwear.
- **eBay Sporting Goods (EBAY).** Seventeen first-level groups: Boxing, Martial Arts and MMA; Camping and Hiking; Cycling; Fishing; Fitness, Running and Yoga; Golf; Hunting; Indoor Games; Other Sporting Goods; Outdoor Sports; Sports Mem, Cards and Fan Shop; Tactical and Duty Gear; Team Sports; Tennis and Racquet Sports; Water Sports; Winter Sports; Wholesale Lots. The list mixes single sports (Golf, Fishing), activity families (Team Sports, Water Sports, Winter Sports), a setting (Outdoor Sports), a market (Tactical and Duty, Wholesale Lots) and a motive (memorabilia).
- **SidelineSwap resale (SLS).** The home page opens with "Shop by Sport" (hockey, baseball, lacrosse, golf, softball, skiing, football, snowboarding, tennis), then popular categories by equipment type inside sport (baseball bats, hockey sticks and skates, lacrosse heads, golf drivers and iron sets, skis, snowboards, tennis racquets, softball bats, football helmets, hockey goalie gear) (search excerpt only for the lists; the fetch returned only the selling text).
- **Amazon US Sports and Outdoors (AMZ).** The fetch returned only product listings (agility hurdles, cones, a walking-treadmill pad, a sleeping pad); no category tree was readable. Not used as evidence of structure.

### 1.2 Trade classifications and product taxonomies

- **HS heading 9506 (HS).** The customs tree for "articles and equipment for general physical exercise, gymnastics, athletics, other sports (including table-tennis) or outdoor games" is sport-first for sports with a dominant implement and type-first elsewhere: 950611 skis (cross-country 95061110; snowboards 95061121; downhill 95061129; other 95061180), 950612 ski bindings, 950619 other ski equipment; 950621 sailboards; 950629 water-skis, surfboards and other water-sport equipment; 950631-39 golf clubs, golf balls, other golf equipment; 950640 table-tennis articles; 950651 lawn-tennis rackets; 950659 badminton and similar rackets; balls by type (950661 tennis, 950662 inflatable, 950669 cricket, polo and others); 950670 ice and roller skates; 950691 general exercise equipment (adjustable-resistance apparatus; other: gymnastic apparatus, kettlebells, medicine balls); 950699 other (cricket and polo equipment; the rest). The chapter note excludes sports clothing (Chapters 61 and 62), sports footwear other than skates (64) and sports headgear (65). So customs separates sport implements from sport garments, and splits by implement for a few sports and by function (exercise, balls) for the rest.
- **Google product taxonomy, branch Sporting Goods (G-ATH, G-OUT, G-FIT).** Four children: Athletics (25 subcategories, 231 in total), Exercise and Fitness (26 subcategories, 56 in total), Indoor Games, Outdoor Recreation (17 subcategories, 458 in total, to level 7). Athletics and Outdoor Recreation are sport-first: American Football, Baseball and Softball, Boxing and Martial Arts, Fencing, Field Hockey and Lacrosse, Rugby, Soccer, Tennis, Track and Field, Wrestling; Boating and Water Sports (with Wetsuit Pieces, Diving and Snorkeling, Surfing, Swimming), Camping and Hiking, Climbing, Cycling, Equestrian, Fishing, Golf, Hunting and Shooting, Inline and Roller Skating, Outdoor Games, Skateboarding. Inside a sport the next level is equipment type, and Protective Gear, Gloves and apparel are sub-groups of the sport (Fencing Protective Gear: Gloves and Cuffs, Jackets and Lames, Masks; American Football Protective Gear: helmets, shoulder pads, girdles). Exercise and Fitness is type-first (Cardio Machines, Weight Lifting, Yoga and Pilates, Exercise Balls, Foam Rollers), because exercise is not one sport.
- **Wikipedia (WIKI).** "Category:Sports equipment by sport" has about 35 subcategories each named for a sport (Badminton, Baseball, Cricket, Cue sports, Cycling, Golf, Hiking, Hockey, Hunting, Martial arts, Paintball, Skiing, Table tennis, Tennis, Water sports and others). The reference work is sport-first, then object.
- **GS1 Global Product Classification** has a "Sports Equipment" segment (page seen, June 2018 version; the classification itself was not read; search excerpt only).

### 1.3 Standards bodies

- **UIAA (UIAA).** Safety standards for over 25 types of climbing and mountaineering equipment, each a numbered standard by equipment type (ropes 101 and 110, connectors 121, pitons 122, rock anchors 123, chocks 124, frictional anchors 125, rope clamps 126, pulleys 127, braking devices 129, ice anchors 151, snow anchors 154, snow pickets 155, avalanche shovels 156 and probes 157, crash pads 161, helmets including ski-mountaineering helmets 111). A standard attaches to an equipment type that may be used in several sports (climbing, mountaineering, ski touring).
- **FIE (FIE).** Fencing material rules: homologation is compulsory for blades, points, protective clothing (jackets, breeches, under-plastrons), masks, gloves, scoring apparatus and pistes; fabric must resist 800 N, mask bib 1600 N, glove cuff 350 N; separate rules for foil, epee and sabre. Here the standard defines the kinds and the tier (homologated or not) for a whole sport.

### 1.4 Enthusiast buying guides (spec and consensus tiers)

- **Rackets.** TP (UK retailer) uses six aspects: swing type, head size, frame length, profile height, weight, balance; named head-size classes midsize (up to 97 sq in), mid plus (97 to 105), oversize (106 to 115), super oversize (116 and over); weight bands from "very heavy" (over 320 g) to "ultra light"; balance bands in cm. TCOM (US) gives 300 g and 100 sq in as the dividing lines between player's frames and "tweener" or game-improvement frames, and string patterns 16x19 and 18x20. BHQ (UK) classes badminton rackets by balance (head-heavy, even, head-light), weight in U units (3U about 85 to 89 g, 4U about 80 to 84 g, 5U/6U lighter), shaft flex and grip size, and maps maker families to balance (Yonex Astrox head-heavy, Arcsaber even, Nanoflare head-light; Victor and Li-Ning families). These are spec-ranked attributes of one kind.
- **Skis.** SKI-CZ (Czech rental shop, citing the Czech ski magazine SNOW.cz and Salomon) types skis by terrain: on-piste (easy recreational, sport, slalom, GS), all-mountain, freeride, freestyle, touring, and says three figures tell the type: width underfoot, sidecut radius, profile (camber/rocker). It warns that the lines are not fixed and each maker draws them differently. HS separates cross-country, downhill and "other" skis, snowboards and bindings. SC lists Skier (skis), Skihelme, Skibrillen, Skihandschuhe, Skistoecke, Skiprotektoren.
- **Ski boots.** EVO-SKI (US) says flex index runs from about 50 to 130+ (soft, medium, stiff, very stiff by ability), "not standardized between boot manufacturers".
- **Golf irons.** MGS and GL (US) use consensus categories: super game improvement, game improvement, players distance, players (cavity back), muscle back, blades, hybrid irons; GL says the categories are not exclusive (cavity back is a design that can appear in several categories); MGS says category lines blur. ALP splits golf by club type (driver, fairway wood, utility, iron set, single iron, wedge, putter), which HS and G-OUT collapse to "golf clubs".
- **Cricket bats.** PSC (Australia) describes Grades 1 to 4 as a willow-quality tier (grain count, blemishes), with a note elsewhere that the grading is a convention, not a standard (sportsgear24x7 text seen in a search excerpt only). Price and grade rise together.
- **Fishing rods.** FM (US) uses two spec scales: power (ultra-light, light, medium, medium-heavy, heavy) and action (extra-fast to slow), written as "MH Fast".
- **Wetsuits.** EVO-WS (US): thickness in mm written 3/2, 4/3, 5/4/3, mapped to water-temperature bands; types are full suit, springsuit/shorty, long john, top, bottom, rashguard.
- **Football boots.** PDS (UK retailer): soleplate/ground codes FG, SG, AG, MG, TF, IC (indoor) are an attribute of a boot; UNI (Denmark) shows the maker ladder: silos (adidas Predator, F50, Copa) and tiers (Elite, Pro, League, Club) each available in several soleplate codes.
- **Forums.** GolfWRX (equipment forum sections such as Tour and Pre-Release Equipment, Golf Balls, Putters, Long Driver's Lab, shaft, grips and clubmaking, rangefinders and wearables) divides golf equipment by equipment type inside one sport forum (search excerpt only; the fetch of the forum page returned nothing usable). Talk Tennis (Racquets forum) could not be read.

### 1.5 Collectors, resale and museums

- **Collectors (GHS, WTR).** Golf Heritage Society divides club collecting by era and make (Feather Ball era long-nose woods and blacksmith irons; Gutta Percha era; mass-produced clubs with makers, cleek marks and matched sets; patent clubs; classic clubs about 1930 to 1990), with putters as a separate path. The wood-tennis-racket collectors guide is organised by technological generation, maker, mark and patent, and a proposed grading of quality versus condition. Collectors' axes are era, maker and condition: all Appendix A modifiers, none a kind.
- **Resale (EBAY, SLS).** See 1.1. Resale sites are sport-first with equipment type inside, plus condition.
- **Museums (NMAH).** The Smithsonian groups "Sports and Leisure": paper sports objects (programs, cards), leisure collections (camping vehicles and gear, video games, playing cards, sportswear, exercise equipment), toys. A museum divides by collection and material rather than a sport-first equipment tree, so it adds nothing to a structure for owned equipment.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

- **Agree:** every retailer, the customs tree (for racket, ski and golf sports), the Google branches Athletics and Outdoor Recreation, Wikipedia and the resale sites start from the sport or activity. Equipment type is the second level everywhere except fitness equipment, where the sport is "exercise" and type is first (G-FIT, HS 950691).
- **Split:** (a) garments and footwear. Customs and the framework's step 17 both pull sports clothing and footwear out of the equipment tree, while every retailer and Google keep them inside the sport (Decathlon India sells "Tennis Shoes", Google puts Fencing Jackets under Fencing). The sport-first retail tree is therefore not the E2 tree: it holds objects that the framework files in A1, A2, A4, B-categories and G1. (b) Activity grouping: eBay uses "Team Sports", "Water Sports", "Winter Sports", "Tennis and Racquet Sports", "Outdoor Sports"; Xebio uses sport lists plus "Outdoor", "Surf and Snow", "Street sports"; Decathlon uses a flat A-to-Z with "Winter Sports" and "Watersports" as extra pages; Google uses Athletics, Outdoor Recreation, Indoor Games, Exercise. No two sources use the same families, which means a family level is an editorial choice. (c) Spec tiers: racket specs are numeric; ski type and boot flex are explicitly non-standard; golf iron categories and cricket grades are conventions; only climbing (UIAA) and fencing (FIE) have standards bodies.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Sport or activity first, equipment type second, spec third.** Evidence:
- Navigation order: all of DEC-UK, DEC-CN, DEC-IN, SC, XEB, ALP, EBAY and SLS open with the sport; Google and Wikipedia do the same for Athletics and Outdoor Recreation. An owner cannot even describe a ski boot, fencing mask or football boot without the sport.
- Filter order: ALP's tree goes sport, equipment type, then spec (hard or soft ball, composite or wood; shakehand or penholder). XEB goes sport, then type (racket, strings, shoes, balls, bags), then variant (court surface; ball size; official ball). The buyer's questions in the guides follow the same order: "what racket" (TP, BHQ), "what ski type" (SKI-CZ), "what boot ground type" (PDS).
- Kinds are sport-specific: a tennis ball, a golf ball and a rugby ball are different things with different standards (HS has separate subheadings for tennis, golf and table-tennis balls); a racket for tennis is not interchangeable with one for badminton. Sport as the parent therefore keeps kinds generic (tennis ball) rather than forcing a cross-sport "ball" kind that no buyer shops for.
- The exception is fitness and recreation without a sport: Google puts Exercise and Fitness type-first (cardio machines, weights, yoga), and HS 950691 does the same. Buyers there choose by function (treadmill, kettlebell, yoga mat), so the leaf should be named for the activity ("Gym and strength training", "Yoga and Pilates") and kinds follow.
- Spec is the third question: racket weight and balance, ski width, boot flex, wetsuit thickness, rod power and action. These are attributes of a kind, not further levels (see 5).

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): activity family, sport, equipment type, named form

| Level | Name | One stated axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | Kind of activity (an editorial grouping of sports that retailers and Google group together) |
| 2 | Leaf | The sport or activity the object is made for |
| 3 | Kind | Generic equipment type used in that sport (the ownable thing) |
| 4 | Named form | Series, model or iconic design (maker series are recorded here) |

Example sub-families (about 15 are proposed in section 10) and leaves: Racket and paddle sports (tennis, badminton, table tennis, padel, squash, pickleball); Team and ball sports (football, rugby, baseball and softball, cricket, basketball, hockey); Golf, cue and target sports (golf, disc golf, cue sports, darts, boules); Gym, fitness and exercise (cardio machines, strength training, yoga and Pilates); Combat sports (boxing, judo, fencing, kendo); Snow and ice sports (alpine skiing, cross-country skiing, snowboarding, ice skating); Water sports (surfing, diving and snorkelling, swimming); Rolling and board sports (skateboarding, inline and roller skating, kick scooters); Climbing and mountaineering; Hiking, camping and trail; Fishing; Air sports; Equestrian.

Why it fits: it is the order of every retail tree read (1.1), of Google's Athletics and Outdoor Recreation branches, of Wikipedia, and of the framework's own category description. Sport-specific garments and footwear sit inside the sport as kinds (football boot, ski boot, fencing jacket, climbing shoe, wetsuit), which matches how Decathlon India, Google and Xebio sell them.

Where it breaks (four edge objects):
1. **A kind used by several sports.** A wetsuit serves surfing, diving, triathlon and kayaking; a ski helmet, goggles and gloves serve alpine skiing and snowboarding; a yoga mat serves yoga, Pilates and stretching. Google puts wetsuits at the family level ("Boating and Water Sports > Boating and Water Sport Apparel > Wetsuits"). Fix: each family may have a "general (any sport in this family)" leaf, and the one-home rule puts the kind in exactly one place; sport views list it under each sport that uses it.
2. **A sport with little in E2.** Running is among the biggest sport pages at every retailer, but the framework keeps running shoes in A2, running clothing in A1 and running bags in A4. Only spikes, starting blocks, hurdles and similar remain, so "Running" is a thin leaf here while a retailer's running page is large. The generated view (section 7) is what recreates the retailer's sport page.
3. **A multi-sport object.** Triathlon, "cross-training", "multisport" and "general fitness" objects do not belong to one sport (Decathlon lists Triathlon, Cross-Training and HIIT as separate sports). Fix: leaves named "Multisport and cross-training" and "General fitness" are allowed.
4. **A sport whose principal implement belongs elsewhere.** Archery bows and crossbows made for target shooting are E4 (step 10); kayaks, canoes and bicycles are G1; saddles and tack are animal equipment (B10). The sport leaf then holds only the E2 remainder (section 9).

### Candidate B: equipment type first, sport second

Levels: 1 role of the object in play (implement, ball or projectile, fixture such as net or table, protective gear, garment, footwear, machine, training aid); 2 sport; 3 kind; 4 named form. Examples: all balls under "ball", then football, tennis, rugby; all rackets under "racket", then tennis, badminton, squash.

Support: HS 9506 uses it for balls (950661 to 950669), skates (950670) and exercise apparatus (950691); Google Exercise and Fitness uses it; Xebio's second block ("shoes, apparel, bags, caps, socks, balls, supports") uses it.

Where it breaks: (1) a retail buyer does not look for "all balls"; (2) a football boot and a tennis shoe have nothing in common except being footwear, which the framework already files in A2; (3) kinds such as "fencing jacket" or "climbing harness" have no useful cross-sport siblings; (4) it repeats the framework's rule that function, not material or form class, is the axis. It remains useful as a generated cross-sport view ("all balls", "all protective gear").

### Candidate C: flat list of sports (no family level), then kind, then named form

Levels: 1 leaf = sport or activity (about 130); 2 kind; 3 named form. The activity families become generated views.

Support: DEC-UK and DEC-CN present a flat A-to-Z list; no two sources agree on the families (1.6), so a family level is arbitrary. Where it breaks: 130 leaves at one level is a long list for the owner and for navigation; fitness (not a sport) and "Camping" (a setting) sit awkwardly as sports; with no parent, the framework's four-level maximum is under-used and the "general (any sport in this family)" leaf has no home.

Recommendation: A, with C as the fallback if the owner prefers fewer arbitrary groupings. The choice is an owner decision (section 10).

## 4. Depth and size

All counts are estimates built from fetched category counts and are marked as such.

- **Sports and activities (leaves).** DEC-UK lists about 100 and DEC-CN says "over 80"; adding sports that only specialist or regional sources list (pelota, teqball, wallball, Gaelic games, kendo, via ferrata) gives about 120 to 150 leaves, plus a few non-sport leaves (general fitness, multisport, general outdoor).
- **Sub-families.** About 15.
- **Generic kinds.** Google's three E2-relevant branches hold 745 categories (Athletics 231, Outdoor Recreation 458, Exercise and Fitness 56; G-ATH, G-OUT, G-FIT), but many are parts, accessories, bags or apparel that S3 sends to the same place or to A4. Sport-specific garments and footwear (about 5 to 15 per sport for 40 or so sports) are not in Google's counts as separate kinds. Estimate: 900 to 1,500 kinds.
- **Named forms (models, series, iconic designs).** If recorded at maker-series level (Yonex Astrox, adidas Predator, TaylorMade P790), about 3,000 to 8,000 series across the category; if every model year were recorded it would be tens of thousands. Recommendation: record series, not model years (model year is a view or an attribute).
- **Where depth concentrates.** Golf (club types, shafts, and the largest set of named forms); football boots and other boots by ground or surface (ground codes); skiing and snowboarding (skis, boots, bindings); fishing (very many kinds of tackle; DEC-UK splits the sport into five); climbing (UIAA lists over 25 equipment types); camping and trail (tents, sleeping bags, mats, poles); racket sports (spec-ranked attributes rather than kinds); fitness machines.
- **Broad leaves** (dozens of kinds): golf, football, skiing, fishing, climbing, camping and trail, baseball and softball, gym and strength, water sports (diving), cycling gear. **Medium** (8 to 20): tennis, badminton, table tennis, rugby, cricket, fencing, boxing, judo, skateboarding, ice skating, snowboarding, yoga. **Narrow** (1 to 7): darts, boules, pelota, teqball, kite sports, orienteering, slacklining.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule: a kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.** Evidence for where that level sits in this category:

- **The equipment type inside a sport is the comparison level.** Buyers compare racket against racket (TP, BHQ, TCOM), ski against ski (SKI-CZ), ski boot against ski boot (EVO-SKI), football boot against football boot (UNI, PDS), cricket bat against cricket bat (PSC), fishing rod against fishing rod (FM), wetsuit against wetsuit (EVO-WS). The filter trees of ALP and XEB stop at the same level and offer the finer split as a filter (court surface, ball size, composite or wood).
- **Finer distinctions that stay attributes (not kinds):**
  - racket head size, weight and balance classes (TP, BHQ);
  - ski use class (on-piste, all-mountain, freeride, freestyle, touring): SKI-CZ says the lines are not fixed and each maker draws them differently, and width, radius and rocker are the real figures;
  - golf iron category (game improvement, players distance, players, blade): GL says the categories overlap, and MGS says they blur;
  - cricket bat grade (PSC), rod power and action (FM), wetsuit thickness (EVO-WS), ski boot flex (EVO-SKI), football boot ground code (PDS), ball pressure class or ball size (ALP, XEB).
- **Where the kind level goes deeper than "equipment type":** golf clubs, where driver, fairway wood, hybrid, iron set, wedge and putter are compared separately (ALP, GL, MGS) and HS and Google collapse them to "golf clubs"; table tennis, where bat and rubber are bought and compared separately (ALP: rubber types); fencing, where foil, epee and sabre are three weapons with three rule sets (FIE); skis, where alpine, cross-country and snowboard are three different objects (HS 95061110, 95061121, 95061129).
- **Where the kind level is higher than the retail category:** balls. Retail pages split balls by ball size and pressure (XEB: size 5, 4, soft, beach; ALP: pressure and pressureless), but these are attributes of "tennis ball", "volleyball", "rugby ball".
- **Open point (owner):** whether touring, freeride or park skis are separate kinds because they differ in function (touring skis are used with skins and touring bindings), as opposed to ordinary alpine skis with a different width. The sources treat the difference as a use class, not a standard, so this memo recommends an attribute and flags it.

The choice level differs between sub-families: it is "equipment type" for most; "club type" for golf; "weapon" for fencing; "machine" for fitness; and "garment or footwear type for the sport" (football boot, ski boot, wetsuit, fencing jacket, climbing shoe) for sport-specific apparel.

## 5. Quality and tier

**No single tier type applies.** The category mixes three, and the memo recommends recording each as a modifier on the kind or named form, never as a level.

| Tier type | Where it applies | Source of the ordering | How it appears |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spec-ranked | racket head size, weight, balance (TP, BHQ, TCOM); ski width and sidecut (SKI-CZ); wetsuit thickness against water temperature (EVO-WS); rod power and action (FM) | measured numbers, but the class bands are shop or maker conventions | numeric attribute fields on the kind; a band label (for example 3U, mid plus) is derived |
| Consensus-ranked | golf iron categories (GL, MGS); cricket bat Grades 1 to 4 (PSC); ski boot flex (EVO-SKI, "not standardized between boot manufacturers") | community and retail convention; the same grade differs between makers | a "tier (consensus)" field with its source named; flex or grade is comparable only within one maker |
| Maker ladder | football boots: Elite, Pro, League, Club inside a maker silo (UNI); price bands in BHQ | the maker's own naming | a "maker tier" field on the named form, scoped to the maker |
| Certified or homologated | climbing equipment (UIAA standards, 25+ types); fencing material (FIE homologation, 800 N, 1600 N, 350 N); "official ball" classes for rugby (XEB) | standards body | a "certified to" attribute (standard number) on the kind or named form |

Recommended primary label for the category: **mixed, mainly spec-ranked attributes within a kind, consensus or maker-scoped tiers where the number is not standard, and certification flags where a standards body exists**. Nothing orders kinds against each other across sports. Price band is a separate modifier (BHQ gives a price-band guide).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity, with small platform pockets.**
- Identity evidence: BHQ maps maker families to a spec profile (Yonex Astrox is head-heavy, Arcsaber even, Nanoflare head-light; Victor and Li-Ning have parallel families); UNI sells boots by maker silo (Predator for control, F50 for speed, Copa for touch) and tier; ALP nests maker series under footwear (Nike Phantom and Mercurial, Mizuno Morelia, adidas Predator) and rackets by maker (Yonex, Wilson, Head, Babolat, Prince, Dunlop); MGS and GL cite iron models by maker (Titleist T150, TaylorMade P790, Mizuno Pro 243); collectors divide golf and tennis by maker and mark (GHS, WTR); Decathlon runs its own brand per sport (DEC-UK sport pages; the brand list is in a Wikipedia article seen in a search excerpt only).
- Platform pockets (not attested by a fetched page in this batch; they need sourcing before use): ski boot sole standards against binding type, cycling cleat systems against pedals, golf shaft-to-head fit. Where a maker sets compatibility, the compatibility belongs in an attribute of the part, not a level.
- **Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger.** Columns: maker, known-for kinds, sport leaves, scene tags, tier (consensus), price band, country, era, own-brand-of-retailer flag. Each row attested. Because the same maker spans many sports (Yonex: badminton, tennis, golf; Wilson: tennis, golf, baseball), the ledger links maker to many leaves; the maker never becomes a level. Retailer own-brands (Decathlon's per-sport brands) are a second flag.
