## 2. Findings by memo

Each finding gives its severity, the memo, the evidence (memo section, source id or framework rule) and an exact proposed fix. Findings that concern several memos are in section 3, and the shared conventions are in section 4.

### C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment

**F1. (high) The sub-family level cuts by power source and mounting, and mixes that with function.** C1, section 3, Candidate A level table: the sub-family axis is "how the equipment is driven, mounted and used". The first three sub-families (hand tools; hand-held power tools; workshop machines) are set by power source and by installation, and Appendix A lists both as modifiers ("power source and technology (manual, electric, gas, battery)"; "installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted)"). The other five are set by function (welding and site power; measuring; access and handling; storage) or by part status (accessories). C2 (Candidate B, break 1) rejects the same hand/power split by citing Appendix A. The mixed axis produces splits the memo itself lists, and some it does not:
- the mitre saw and tile saw (C1.2 or C1.3; edge 2);
- the soldering iron, glue gun and heat gun (C1.2 "Nail, Glue & Heat Guns" at Bunnings [S1], or C1.4 welding and soldering);
- the air compressor (C1.4 site power and air, or C1.3 machine);
- a hand plane and a power planer, which sit in different sub-families although buyers move between them (S17's "Making the Leap from Hand Tools to Machines").

The retail evidence for hand/power as the first cut is strong [S1, S4, S13–S17, S19], but the shared contract keeps power source off the tree.

*Fix:* replace the eight sub-families with the verbs of the C1 membership test, which is Candidate B extended to the non-action groups the memo already has:
- C1.1 Cutting & sawing;
- C1.2 Drilling, driving & turning (drills, drivers, screwdrivers, spanners, wrenches, sockets);
- C1.3 Shaping & finishing (planes, chisels, files, grinders, sanders, routers, lathes, mills);
- C1.4 Striking & fastening (hammers, mallets, nailers, staplers, riveters, pry bars);
- C1.5 Joining & heat (welders, soldering and brazing equipment, glue guns, heat guns);
- C1.6 Gripping & clamping;
- C1.7 Measuring, layout & testing;
- C1.8 Lifting, access & materials handling;
- C1.9 Power & air supply (generators, compressors, pumps);
- C1.10 Tool storage & carrying;
- C1.11 Accessories & consumables (S3).

Set the leaf to the tool family (saws, planes, sanders). Make each kind a generic type whose form differs, so that a panel saw, a circular saw and a cabinet table saw are three kinds in one leaf. Add two attributes, `drive` (manual | corded | cordless | pneumatic | hydraulic | engine) and `mount` (hand-held | bench | stationary). Generate the retail views "Hand tools", "Power tools" and "Workshop machinery" from them (convention K1). Tie-break for multi-action tools: file by the primary action the maker names. The oscillating multi-tool goes to Cutting, the rotary tool to Shaping and the heat gun to Joining & heat. If the owner prefers the retail cut, record it as an explicit, C1-only exception to Appendix A (owner question 1).

**F2. (medium) The memo files Japanese tools at two different levels, and names kinds by region.** In C1 section 4b (item 1) the dozuki, ryoba and azebiki are "each ... a kind", and striking and paring chisels are kinds. The worked tree and section 7 make the same saws and chisels (oire-nomi, usu-nomi, tateguya) named forms under "pull saw (nokogiri)" and "nomi, Japanese chisel". Kanna, nomi and genno are kinds labelled "Japanese" beside a Western bench chisel and smoothing plane. Region is an Appendix A modifier unless the form differs. *Fix:* name kinds generically by form, keep native names as aliases and tag the region (convention K8):
- saws: "double-edged pull saw" (alias ryoba), "backed pull saw" (alias dozuki), "flush-cut pull saw" (aliases azebiki, kugihiki);
- chisels: "bench (striking) chisel", "paring chisel", "mortise chisel", with oire-nomi, usu-nomi and tateguya-nomi as aliases of those kinds, and makers' and smiths' patterns as named forms;
- planes: "pull plane" (alias kanna) as a kind, because the pull stroke changes the construction, with awase-kanna as a named form;
- hammers: "double-faced hammer" (alias genno), with ryōguchi, funate and kataguchi as named forms.

Owner question 9 then becomes yes/no on this rule.

**F3. (medium) Objects in the membership test, and common tools, have no leaf.**
- Power nailers and staplers are a retail group at Screwfix and Bunnings [S1, S2], yet C1.2 has no fastening leaf.

- Automotive and bicycle workshop tools (torque wrench, OBD reader, chain tool) are not covered: S3 sends parts, attachments and consumables to the object, but says nothing of tools made to service it.

Add the precedence sentence: *"A tool made to service or repair a vehicle, bicycle or machine (jack, axle stand, torque wrench, OBD reader, chain tool) is C1; S3 sends only parts, attachments and consumables to the object they are made for."* The earth-moving machines are finding X2.

**F4. (low) One kind is named by material.** C1 tree C1.7 has "plastic toolbox" (Screwfix splits Plastic / Wheeled / Metal [S32]). *Fix:* "portable toolbox", "wheeled toolbox (tool trolley)" and "modular stackable tool case", each with `material` as an attribute (convention K9).

### C3 Plants & Living Cultures

**F12. (medium) The C3.1 leaf level is an ordered list of several axes, and the worked tree misfiles two plants.** C3 section 3, the "ordered key for growing-plant leaves", has twelve leaves set by different axes:
- lineage (orchids, carnivorous plants, cacti and succulents);
- a training form (bonsai);
- habitat (aquatic and bog);
- crop use (fruit and nut; vegetable and herb);
- a setting in disguise ("tropical foliage and flowering plants" is the houseplant department, defined in [S33] by use);
- growth form (trees, shrubs and climbers, perennials, annuals).

The memo says the one-axis rule "holds by definition" because of key order. The order does make placement deterministic, but one stated axis per level is a rule about the level, not about a tie-breaker. The worked tree also breaks the memo's own key in two places. The strawberry plant sits under "Vegetable & herb", although leaf 6 is "fruit and nut plants". Lavender is put there as a herb, although it is sold and grown mainly as an ornamental shrub (no source gives a herb test). Leaf 8 is the leaf the memo's own Candidate C critique forbids: "houseplant" is defined by use [S33].

*Fix (keeps Candidate A):*
1. State the axis as "horticultural group, as recognised by a register or a trade department", and give each leaf a written membership test that does not depend on where the plant is kept:
   - (4) cacti and succulents = Cactaceae and the genera on an attested succulent genus index (Mountain Crest [S22]);
   - (6) fruit and nut plants = grown for edible fruit or nuts, including strawberry and blueberry;
   - (7) vegetable and culinary herb plants = grown for edible leaves, roots, stems or seeds, or listed as culinary herbs on an attested herb list;
   - (8) rename to "tropical foliage and flowering plants (by genus list)" and define it by an attested list of families and genera (Araceae, Marantaceae, Bromeliaceae including Tillandsia, Gesneriaceae, Hoya, tropical Ficus, palms, tender ferns), not by "kept indoors".
2. Move the strawberry plant to leaf 6, and lavender to leaf 10 (shrubs) unless the herb list attests it.
3. Record the Tillandsia ruling in the leaf 8 list.

The owner's question on edible leaves (C3 question 2) stays.

**F13. (medium) Kind rank cannot be reproduced, and three sub-cases break the kind and named-form rules.**
- *Genus or species.* C3 section 4b rule (1) says "Where a seller or society gives a *species* its own page or filter ... the species is the kind". Nearly every species has a page somewhere, so two builders would set different ranks.
- *Spawn.* C3.4 "Fungal spawn" makes the carrier the kind (plug spawn, grain spawn) and the species ("shiitake, blue oyster") the named form. A species is not a model, cut, design or cultivar (BRIEF, level of generality), and the carrier rests on one maker's page [S13].
- *Roses and daffodils.* "Hybrid tea rose" is a kind (a rose class, section 4b "rose by class"), but daffodil divisions are attributes ("Division, cultivar"). These are two classification systems of the same kind, handled two ways.

*Fix:*
1. Replace rule (1) with a closed list, which the owner approves and which grows only by attested pages: genera whose kinds are species (proposed from the memo's own evidence: Philodendron, Monstera, Anthurium, Alocasia, Hoya, Nepenthes, Sarracenia, Ficus; each needs a society or register species page). Everywhere else the kind is the genus or the crop.
2. Make one kind "mushroom spawn" with `carrier` (plug, sawdust, grain, liquid culture) and `species` as fields, the same pattern as the memo's genus field. Named forms are named cultivated strains only.
3. Make "rose (Rosa)" the kind, with `rose_class` (hybrid tea, floribunda, climbing, shrub, miniature) as a controlled attribute, as daffodil division already is.

**F14. (medium) The state-sold rule is broken by plug plants, and the planted-pot rule differs from C2's.**
- C3.2 holds "Cuttings & plugs", but section 3 (break 1) sets the rule "rooted in a medium and alive = growing plant". A plug plant is rooted in a cell of compost.
- C3 P2 files a planted pot, kokedama or terrarium "as the plant when the plant is the item of greatest value". C2 section 9 files a plant "even when sold in a decorative pot or a planted basket" as C3 whatever the pot is worth. A cheap succulent in a costly glazed pot would get two answers.

*Fix:* rename the C3.2 leaf "Unrooted cuttings & divisions" and file plug plants, liners and rooted tissue-culture plantlets in C3.1 under their group. Adopt C2's wording on both sides: *"A living plant sold in a container, basket, kokedama ball or terrarium is C3 whatever the container's value; the empty container is C2."* (Question 27 in section 5.)

**F15. (low) Two living plant products have no home.** Cultivated turf (sod) is sold by area to lay as a lawn (Bunnings Turf Grass & Lawn Care [C2 S2]; C2 has only artificial turf). Live moss is sold for terrariums, kokedama and moss gardens (Engei moss balls [S18]). *Fix:* add "turf (cultivated sod)" and "moss" as kinds under C3.1 leaf 11 (perennials, grasses and garden ferns), with lawn seed staying in C3.2.

**F16. (low) The evidence is thin where the memo says it is.** Living cultures rest on one spawn maker [S13] and one ferment-culture shop [S14]. Aquatic plants have society and marketplace headings only and no kind. Yeast and brewing cultures were not researched. *Fix:* none to the structure. Keep the aquatic leaf empty until a retailer tree is fetched, and note that C3.4's kinds are single-source.

**F17. (summary) Usability check, 20 objects.** The 20 objects: potted Phalaenopsis, Monstera 'Albo Borsigiana', Ficus elastica 'Tineke', ficus bonsai, fig tree, Meyer lemon, strawberry plant, lavender, potted basil, Japanese maple, hydrangea, bamboo, tulip bulb, plug plants, seed potatoes, cut roses, cut Nordmann fir, Tillandsia, turf roll, shiitake plug spawn.

As the memo stands, **7 of 20 would split**:

- the Japanese maple and bamboo (no stated test between trees, shrubs and grasses);

With F12 to F15 applied (adding the leaf tests "tree = woody plant sold to grow on a single trunk" and "bamboo = grasses"), all 20 have one home.

### C4 Craft & Art Supplies

**F18. (high) Craft-first sub-families leave cross-craft objects, and three crafts, without a home.** C4 section 3 (Candidate A) divides by "the craft the object is for", and its edge list deals only with thread. Many common objects are made or sold for several crafts:
- craft scissors, rotary cutters, self-healing cutting mats and craft knives;
- glue guns and sticks, PVA and craft glue, spray mount;
- airbrushes (picture-making and model-making);
- the compressor sold with an airbrush (C4 or C1).

Embroidery hoops and Aida cloth fit both C4.2 Sewing & fabric and C4.3 Yarn & needlecraft. Polymer and air-dry clay fit C4.7 or C4.8. Three crafts within the test have no leaf. Spinning wheels and looms are named as narrow leaves in section 4 but placed nowhere (spinning and weaving are fibre crafts in the step 28 test). Printmaking (UNSPSC class [S17]; lino cutters, brayers, block-printing inks, presses) is picture-making but has no leaf.

*Fix:*
1. Add a sub-family **C4.9 General craft tools & adhesives**, with leaves "Cutting" (craft scissors, rotary cutter, cutting mat, craft knife), "Adhesives" (craft glue, glue gun, spray adhesive) and "Spraying" (airbrush). Add the rule: *"A tool or material made for one craft goes to that craft's sub-family; one made or sold for several crafts goes to C4.9."*
2. Add the precedence: *"A compressor made to drive an airbrush follows the airbrush (S3); a general workshop compressor is C1."*
3. Add a C4.3 leaf "Spinning & weaving" (spinning wheel, drop spindle, loom, unspun fibre) and a C4.1 leaf "Printmaking" (lino and wood-cutting tools, brayer, printing ink, printing press).
