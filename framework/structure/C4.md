## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose the **craft** first (what they are making), then the **role** of the object (material, tool, machine, fitting), then a **medium or type**, and only then grade, maker and price.
- Navigation: Michaels (Knit and Sew Shop, S6), Hobbycraft (Sewing, Knitting and Crochet as separate departments with Craft Type as a facet, S4 S5), buttinette (Wolle und Handarbeiten vs Stoffe und Nähzubehör vs Schmuckbasteln vs Papierbasteln, S8), Rayher (S9) and UNSPSC (S17) are craft-led. Blick and Sekaido are medium-led inside picture-making (S1, S10).
- Questions in buyer guides: "what are you making?" (Brother AU, S24; Singer "How to choose", S22); "what weight and what weave?" (S38); "is 8 ply the same as worsted?" (S35); "mechanical or computerized?" (S22, S23, S24); "which tannage, grade and weight for a wallet?" (S27); "what cone does my clay fire at?" (S37).
- Filter order: yarn is filtered by weight and fibre together (S5); needles by type then material (S36); sewing machines by category then type then features (S22); beads by category, form, brand and shape (S28). In none of these is price the first filter.
- The performance tier (artist or student) is chosen second, inside a medium (S19, S20); it belongs to a maker's range (a line), not to the kind (convention C4(a)).

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): craft family, then role, then kind, then named form

Levels (fixed names, C5(a)) and the single axis of each:
1. **Sub-family**: axis *the craft the object is for* (picture-making, sewing and fabric, yarn and needlecraft, leathercraft, beading and jewellery making, paper craft, clay and ceramics, model-making, and, added by K-169, candle and soap making, resin and casting, glass and mosaic, basketry, floristry). This is the wording of the C4 test. Two sub-families are exempt from this axis. **C4.14 General craft tools & adhesives** holds objects made or sold for several crafts: it is the category-level multi-use sub-family of C14(c), and its leaf axis is the operation (cut, stick, spray). **C4.15 Parts, accessories & consumables made for one machine or tool** holds the S3 items (C5(d), CV-17), and its leaf axis is the object served.
2. **Leaf**: axis *role in the craft*: material (colour, fabric, yarn, leather, clay), support or surface, hand tool or applicator, machine, fitting or notion. Each sub-family in the tree states its own leaf axis (C5(c)). Where a sub-family has 12 kinds or fewer and a single leaf would mix roles, the leaf is null (C5(b), CV-16): C4.6, C4.8 and the five crafts C4.9 to C4.13.
3. **Kind**: axis stated per leaf: *medium* for colour; *stitch job* for sewing machines; *product form* for yarn; *cloth name* for fabric; *tannage or type* for leather; *body class* for clay; *chemistry* for model paint; *operation* for needles, hooks and cutting tools; *textile process* for looms and wheels.
4. **Named form**: a specific design below a kind that a buyer can compare, either **maker-bound** (a model: Singer Heavy Duty 4511, Skutt KM 1027, Miyuki Delica 11/0) or **not maker-bound** (a design many makers make: Tunisian crochet hook, raku clay body), recorded in the field `maker_bound` (C2(a)). A maker's range or collection is never a kind or a level: a range inside one kind (Cotman Watercolour, Tamiya LP lacquer, KnitPicks Radiant, Knitcraft Everyday DK) is a maker-bound named form, and a range that spans several kinds is a row of the **lines table** (C1, C2(d)). Colourways, colour and part codes, pack sizes and sizes are attributes or sparse variants (C2(b), C3).

Maximum depth below the category is four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Kinds carry the attribute `drive` where it applies (manual, corded electric, cordless, engine, pedal or treadle; decision C-1: `drive` and `mount` are attributes with generated views) and never use power source or technology as a level (C10). Kiln `fuel`, `loading` and `chamber_size` are attributes: S12 and S13 sort kilns by them, but no fetched source compares the types as separate objects (C10(b)).

Individual colours, bead part numbers and paint codes (Tamiya XF-1, Humbrol numbers, Delica DB-0200) are items inside a range, not named forms; if an owner-emphasis scene needs them they are variants (C3).

## 4b. Choice level

A kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers (C2(a)). A named form is a design below it, maker-bound (a model) or not (a generic design many makers make); a measuring mode, size class, fitting variant or material is an attribute. A maker's range or collection is a line row (C1), never a kind. By sub-family:
- **Colours and drawing media: the kind is the medium** (watercolour, acrylic, oil, gouache, ink, coloured pencil, pastel). Buyers compare artist against student range and maker within a medium (S19, S20, S7). The grade belongs to the maker's range, so it is a tier entry on the line (section 5), not on the kind and not a level. Single colours are attributes (pigment, lightfastness class, series) or sparse variants, not kinds.
- **Brushes: the kind is the artist brush;** hair and shape are attributes. The fetched pages gave only department-level evidence (S1, S10, S30), so this is the weakest call in the memo †. It stays provisional until a brush-specific page is fetched (OWNER: review Q46e, yes/no).
- **Machines: the kind is the stitch job** (sewing, overlock, embroidery, longarm). Bernina explains that an overlock is a different machine, not a stitch setting (S23); Wikipedia names the four stitch families (S34). Mechanical against computerized, heavy duty, and domestic against industrial are attributes (`control`, `duty`, `market`; C10, Appendix A), not kinds. Models are the named forms (S22, S24). A "machines" leaf is allowed because these forms have no manual version of the same form (review K1).
- **Yarn: one kind per product form** (knitting and crochet yarn, crochet thread, floss, unspun fibre). Weight is an attribute shown as a facet beside fibre (S5): the same eight CYC classes cut across every fibre and maker (S2, S3, S35), and the two systems alias each other (DK = 8 ply = Double Knitting, Aran = 10 ply = worsted; S35, S4). Lines are rows of the lines table.
- **Fabric: the kind is the cloth name** (plain-weave cotton, denim, jersey). Buyers say "weight and weave" (S38); GSM is an attribute and fibre a tag. "Quilting" is a use, so it is an alias of the cloth, not part of the kind name (K9). buttinette's fabric filter mixes cloth names with fibres (Baumwollstoff, Bouclé, Canvas; S8), which shows the noise to avoid.
- **Needlecraft:** by technique, hand stitching with yarn or floss (knitting, crochet, embroidery, cross stitch, needlepoint) is C4.3 with its cloth (Aida, canvas) and hoops; constructing with fabric and thread, including machine embroidery, is C4.2. Needle and hook sizes are attributes (CYC ranges, S2).
- **Leather: the kind is the tannage or type** (vegetable-tanned, chrome-tanned, suede, hair-on), as Tandy divides it (S26). Grain grade and weight in oz are attributes (S27); tannery lines are rows of the lines table. "Vegetable-tanned leather" and "stoneware clay" are names where the material is the object, so the material may name the kind (K9).
- **Beads:** the kind is the bead form with material only where material is the form (seed bead, fire-polished glass bead, gemstone bead). Fire Mountain's fine part-number shapes (S28) are named forms where a model is meant, otherwise variants.
- **Clay: the kind is the body class** (earthenware, stoneware, porcelain, paper clay, air-dry, polymer), ordered by how and where it sets. Cone range is an attribute (S12, S37). Kiln fuel, loading and chamber size are attributes.
- **Model paint: the kind is the chemistry** (acrylic, enamel, lacquer), because chemistries are not freely mixable (S15, S16).
- **Cross-craft tools and materials (C4.14):** the kind is the tool or material (rotary cutter, craft glue, airbrush). The craft it is used for is a tag with a generated view.
- The choice level differs between sub-families: it is the medium in picture-making, the machine job in sewing, the product form in yarn and the body class in clay.

## 5. Quality and tier

Mixed. There is no single ordering across C4; each area has its own. Under C4(a) a `tier` never sits on a kind; it is a **tier entry** (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) on a maker, a line or a named form. The review's convention K5 adds a field `basis` (consensus | maker label | award) to separate sources of a tier (owner Q13a pending).
- **Artist against student colour: maker-labelled tier on lines.** Makers publish two ranges per medium (Winsor & Newton Professional against Cotman, S19; Sennelier "extra fine" against "fine", with about twice the pigment, S20). Retailers sell "student-grade to professional" (S7). Tier entries, `scheme` = artist/student, `basis` = maker label: Cotman = student and Professional Watercolour = artist (S19, Australia); Sennelier extra fine = artist and fine = student (S20, France). A second source from another region is on file for the scheme (S7, US) but not for Winsor & Newton (C4(e)). HS customs does not split the two (S18).
- **Measured scales are attributes with the standard named (C4(b)), not tier entries:**
  - lightfastness class I (excellent) to V (very weak), as cited by Sennelier (S20; the standard's number †);
  - yarn weight, CYC Standard Yarn Weight System categories 0 to 7 (S2);
  - clay cone number, cone 022 to 14 (S37), and kiln chamber size in cubic feet (S12);
  - fabric GSM class (S38) and leather weight in oz (S26, S27), which are units without a named standard.
- **Safety labelling is a certification attribute (C4(b)):** the ACMI "CL" seal and the ASTM D4236 chronic-hazard labelling duty (S7, S21). It sets a duty, not a quality tier; the scope of D4236 was read in a search excerpt †.
- **Leather grain grade** (full grain, top grain, corrected grain, genuine, bonded; S27) is recorded as an attribute `grain_grade` of a named form or line. S27 is a hobby guide and no standard was fetched †; if none is found it is re-filed as a consensus tier entry on lines.
- **Sennelier price series 1 to 6** (by pigment cost, S20) is a property of single colours. Colours are not stored, so only the scheme is recorded on the Sennelier line row.
- **Yarn weight and fabric GSM: attributes but not tiers.** They describe thickness or mass, not quality (S2, S38).
- **Sewing machines: preference.** Feature classes (mechanical, computerized, heavy duty) and price bands exist but no ordering of quality (S22, S23, S24).
- **Beads:** item-level grade words ("economy grade", "D grade"; S28) are not an industry ordering and are not stored (C16).
- **Model paint, notions, paper craft, cross-craft tools: no tier.**
- **Price band** is the domain-wide USD scheme (C4(d)), computed from a dated, sourced list price on maker and line rows. No dated list price was fetched, so no band is stored yet †.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity, with platform pockets.** The maker is part of what is sought in paints (a range is the product), model paints (numbered colours), beads (Miyuki, Matsuno, Czech, Preciosa-type shape numbers, S28), kilns and machines, and vintage sewing machines, where collectors organise the whole hobby by maker (S25). It is platform where the maker sets compatibility: sewing-machine feet and accessories carry Singer "Compatible Machine Type" codes (S22) and Bernina sells presser feet and accessories per machine through its own accessories search (S23); KnitPicks Options needle tips fit only their cables (S36). Maker is incidental for plain notions, generic fabric and paper. Model-paint chemistries that must not be mixed (S16) are a property of the kind (`chemistry`), not a platform.

**One maker ledger on the shared schema (C1).** Core columns: `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`. `known_for_kinds` points to kinds, not to a column of named forms (the named-form ledger is the source). Tannery and mill suppliers (Hermann Oak, Wickett and Craig) use the class `supplier` until the owner approves the review's proposed class "tannery or mill" (review K2, owner Q11). Seed rows from fetched pages: Winsor & Newton, Schmincke, Sennelier, Art Spectrum (S7, S19, S20); Singer, Husqvarna Viking, Pfaff, Bernina, Brother (S22, S23, S24); Gütermann, Hemline, Milward, Knitcraft, Trimits, Clover (S4, S5); KnitPicks (S36); Tamiya, Humbrol, Vallejo, Testors (S14, S15, S16); Miyuki, Matsuno (S28); Hermann Oak, Wickett and Craig (S27); Skutt, L&L, Paragon, Olympic (S12); Rohde, Potclays (S13); Laguna, Standard, Mammoth (S12).

**Separate tables, not columns.**
- *Lines* (`line_id`, `maker_id`, name, kinds spanned, `tier` entries, years, sources): the ranges listed in section 3 (K4).
- *Tier entries* (C4(a)): section 5.
- *Named forms* (C2) and *variants* (C3): models and designs only; variants only where an owner-emphasis scene needs depth.
- *Compatibility ledger* (C13): `platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `sources`. Seed rows: Singer machine-type accessory codes (kinds served: presser foot, machine needle; S22); Bernina machine-specific feet and accessories (S23); KnitPicks Options interchangeable needle system (kinds served: circular knitting needle; S36). Platforms are never levels.
- *Alias and homonym table* (C9): haberdashery (UK, S4) = notions (US, S6) = Kurzwaren und Nähzubehör (DE, S8); DK = Double Knitting = 8 ply (S4, S35); Aran = 10 ply = worsted (S3, S35); overlock machine = serger (US; S22, S23); the native script of Sekaido and Kiwa terms is held with the Japanese label. Relations: alias, regional name, homonym.

Under C8 a tag needs a definition source and a strength (`core` or `adjacent`); the fetched evidence is retailer facets only, so the tags are held back until a definition source is fetched †.
- **Regional vocabulary as aliases, not levels (C9, C11):** haberdashery = notions = Kurzwaren; DK = 8 ply; Aran, worsted and 10 ply; cone numbers against degrees (S37). A Japanese calligraphy set is a Sekaido top-level category (S10); whether it is C4 or C5 is an owner question (section 10).
- **User and occasion groupings are tags, not levels:** children's crafts and occasions are top-level in buttinette (S8) and Rayher (S9) and Singer filters by skill level (S22); user is a tag (C6) and skill level is a C4 level scheme, not a user; occasion is an Appendix A modifier.
- **Named form only when the scene names a distinct design:** Tunisian crochet hooks (S36, not maker-bound), Delica seed beads (S28, maker-bound) and raku clay (S12, not maker-bound) are named forms; the longarm quilting machine (S23) is a distinct kind. "Quilter's" or "cosplayer's" versions of a kind are not forms.
- **Era:** vintage and antique sewing machines (S25) are an era tag on the machine kind.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Kinds go canonical (most generic) form first, then the secondary key that the leaf states (C7):
- Yarn, fibre and needlecraft cloth: knitting and crochet yarn, crochet thread, embroidery floss, unspun fibre, needlecraft cloth. The CYC weight number (S2) orders a generated listing of yarn lines, not the kinds.
- Fabric: structure, with plain weaves first, then twills, then knits, then felt and other non-wovens (S38); GSM is an attribute and does not order kinds.
- Leather: Tandy's order, vegetable-tanned, chrome-tanned, suede, hair-on (S26).
- Clay: kiln-fired bodies by firing range, low to high (earthenware, stoneware, porcelain; S37), then paper clay, then bodies that set without a kiln (air-dry, polymer).
- Machines run from the plain sewing machine outward to overlock, embroidery and longarm (S34, S23).
- Model-making supplies follow Tamiya's workflow order: cut, shape, fill, mask, paint, finish (S15).
- Colours and drawing media run watercolour, acrylic, oil, gouache, inks, mediums, then drawing media, following Blick's department order where it is attested (S1).

Named forms go non-maker named forms first (by first-attested year, then alphabetically), then maker-bound forms by maker in the order of the maker ledger's `name`, then year, then alphabetically (C7 and the domain-G amendment). Variants go by `variant_code` or year.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A. Sub-family = the craft (picture-making; sewing and fabric; yarn and needlecraft; leathercraft; beading and jewellery making; paper craft; clay and ceramics; model-making), plus two exempt sub-families: C4.14 General craft tools & adhesives (multi-use, C14(c)) and C4.15 Parts, accessories & consumables (C5(d)). Leaf = role in the craft (material, support, tool, machine, fitting), or the operation in C4.14 and the object served in C4.15; null in C4.6 and C4.8. Kind = medium, stitch job, product form, cloth name, tannage, body class or chemistry as the leaf states. Named form = a model or design, maker-bound or not. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Maker ranges are lines, grade and tier are tier entries on lines and named forms, weight, GSM, cone, fibre, era, market and technology are attributes or tags, and makers go in the shared maker ledger. Role-first (Candidate B) and craft-list (Candidate C) layouts become generated views.
