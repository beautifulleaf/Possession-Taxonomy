# C4 Craft & Art Supplies — structure memo (research draft)

Research, not decision. Written from fetched pages only (38 sources, listed in `verify/out/S2_C4_sources.tsv`; keys S1 to S38 below are the row numbers of that file). Calls used: 16 search calls (several carried 2 to 4 queries), 38 fetches. No host refused a fetch. Evidence is strong for paints (grade tiers), yarn weights, sewing machines, leather, clay and kilns, beads and model paints. It is thin for paper craft (one store, S29), for oil paint and gouache (named in the C4 test and in search results only, no fetched category page), for Japanese retail below the top level (S10 and S11 gave only top-level lists), and for the resale market (no resale page fetched; see 1.3). Where a kind below carries "(key text)" it comes from the C4 membership test and was not seen in a fetched tree.

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C4 Craft & Art Supplies, in domain C Making, Growing & Work.

**Membership test (placement key step 28, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** made for **picture-making, textile, fibre, paper, bead, clay and model crafts**: sewing and embroidery machines, knitting and needlework tools, yarn, fabric, leather and tanned hides as material, and notions (buttons, zips; sold loose they are never parts under S3), paints, inks, pastels, charcoal, coloured pencils, crayons and markers made for making pictures, brushes, canvases, easels and sketchbooks, paints, glues and tools made for model-making, beading and jewellery-making tools including jeweller's saws, leatherworking tools such as leather punches, loose beads, craft feathers, fleece and shells, scrapbooking and paper-craft supplies, pottery tools and kilns. Note: pencils, pens and highlighters made for writing are C5. Tools for shaping wood, metal and stone, and for electronics work, are C1, whoever uses them. Step 28 is tried after steps 1 to 27 (so after E1 at step 25) and before C2 (29), the B household branches (30), C5 (31) and C1/C8 (32).

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch C4:**
- Step 7 note: tanned hides and leather are C4 as material and B2 as rugs; craft feathers, fleece and craft shells are C4; unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6; dried flowers B4; firewood C8.
- Step 8 / S3: a part or consumable made for one kind of object goes with that object, but buttons, zips and other notions sold loose are never parts (they stay C4); sewing-machine needles, feet and bobbins are parts of a C4 machine, so they stay in C4 anyway.
- Step 19 note: blank scrapbook albums are C4; used scrapbooks and kept diaries are F5; blank notebooks, diaries and greeting cards are C5.
- Step 20 note: a finished picture or free-standing sculpture is F2; the blank canvas, paint and clay are C4.
- Step 21: colouring and activity books are F1.
- Step 23 note: sewing tape measures are C4.
- Step 24 (D1): drawing tablets, 3D printers and printers are D1; computer-controlled cutting plotters are not named anywhere (see section 10).
- Step 25 (E1): scale models, model kits and miniatures (built or not) are E1; step 25 is tried before step 28.
- Step 18 (H1): grooming tools and cosmetics are H1.
- Steps 31 and 32: C5 writing and desk goods; C1 tools for wood, metal, stone and electronics; C8 paint, adhesives and fasteners made as building supplies.

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK, Germany, Japan, Australia, global marketplace)
- **Blick, US (S1).** Departments seen in the URL paths: painting, canvas, brushes, drawing, paper, furniture, studio, crafts, ceramics-sculpture, kids, books. Beneath them the A to Z index mixes a medium (Acrylic Paint, Artist Inks), a role (Acrylic Mediums, Acrylic Gessoes and Primers, Armature Wire), a support (Art and Illustration Boards), furniture and storage (Art Studio Furniture, Art Tables and Desks, Art Portfolios, Art Storage Cabinets), safety (Art Safety Supplies) and a user group (at-home learning, kids). Blick divides art materials first by medium or by role, not by craft.
- **Jackson's Art, US (S7).** Watercolour paint is one page of 239 items, "from student-grade to professional", sold by maker range (Schmincke Horadam, Sennelier, Art Spectrum, Jackman's own). Grade is a property of a maker's range, not a department. The page carries the ACMI "CL" (Caution Label) safety seal text, a labelling certification linked to the ASTM standard (S21).

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis
- **Ravelry (S3)** and **yarn.com (S35)**: yarn is classed by weight name (Thread, Cobweb, Lace, Light Fingering, Fingering, Sport, DK, Worsted, Aran, Bulky, Super Bulky, Jumbo), mapped to UK and Australian ply (4 ply = fingering, 8 ply = DK, 10 ply = worsted or Aran), to wraps per inch and to knit gauge.
- **Leather Craft Haven (S27)**: leather described on independent axes of tannage (vegetable, chrome, brain, combination), grade (full grain, top grain, corrected grain, genuine, bonded), weight (1 to 10 oz) and animal source; named tanneries (Hermann Oak, Wickett and Craig) signal quality.
- **Sewing With Ease (S38)**: "stop asking is it cotton and start asking what weight and what weave": 16 named cotton cloths by weave (poplin, lawn, twill, denim, canvas, jersey, flannel and others) and five GSM weight classes (under 100 to 400+).
- **KilnControl (S37)**: clay in three body classes (earthenware, stoneware, porcelain) ordered by cone number (cone 022 to 14).
- **Tamiya guide (S15)**: a tool-by-tool modelling workflow (cutting mat, side cutters, putty, masking, paints, brushes, airbrush, weathering, decals); paints split by chemistry, with a warning that lacquer must not top-coat acrylic or enamel.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market
- **ISMACS (S25)** organises vintage and antique sewing machines by manufacturer (the name on the machine), then by serial-number databases and models (Singer 66-18, Kimball and Morton "Lion", Wanzer, Pfaff, Gresham, Pope). For collectors the maker and the model are the identity, and age comes from a serial number (era is a modifier).
- No resale page for yarn stash, art materials or kilns was fetched. Etsy (S33) is the only marketplace page; it separates supplies from finished goods but gives no tier. This is a gap.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications
- **Craft Yarn Council Standard Yarn Weight System (S2)**: eight categories, 0 lace to 7 jumbo, with gauge per 4 inches and needle or hook size ranges ("guidelines only").
- **ASTM D4236 (S21)**: chronic-hazard labelling for art materials packaged for individual users (ICS code 97.195; the fetch returned only the catalogue entry, the scope text was read in the search excerpt of the same page). Jackson's shows the ACMI "CL" seal that certifies such labelling (S7). It sets a labelling duty, not a quality tier. Sennelier cites ASTM lightfastness classes I (excellent) to V (very weak) for pigments (S20).
- **HS heading 3213 (S18)**: "artists', students' or signboard painters' colours" in tablets, tubes, jars and pans, split only into "in sets" and "other". Customs treats student and artist colours as one heading.
- **UNSPSC family 60120000 (S17)**: Arts and crafts equipment, about 35 classes by craft or technique: art; canvases, films, boards and artists' papers; paint and mediums; art and craft cutting; picture framing; drawing; studio aids; printmaking; printing and drawing inks; art fabric; sewing, stitchery and weaving; candlemaking; wood crafts; basketry; stained glass; paper crafts and papermaking; mosaics; enamelling; maskmaking; beads; foam; chenille stem; ribbon; craft poms; leather craft; glitter; macramé; marbling; sequins and trims; cork; clay and ceramics; art metals; sculpture.
- **Google Product Taxonomy (S30, S31)**: Arts and Crafts (170 descendants) is split by role, not craft: Art and Craft Kits (candle, drawing and painting, fabric repair, incense, jewellery, mosaic, needlecraft, scrapbooking and stamping); Art and Crafting Materials (82: paper, fasteners and closures, paint-ink-glaze, shapes and bases, adhesives and magnets, crafting fibres [jewellery cord, thread and floss, unspun fibre, yarn], wire, embellishments and trims [beads, feathers, findings, loose stones, ribbons, sequins], filling, leather and vinyl, pottery and sculpting [clay, modelling dough, papier-mâché, plaster gauze, slips], textiles [crafting canvas, fabric, interfacing, printable fabric], wicks, wax); Tool Accessories (sewing-machine feet, needles, extension tables, replacement parts); Tools (58: colour mixing, cutting and embossing, measuring and marking, hoops, needles and hooks, textile craft machines [felting, hand looms, mechanical looms, sewing machines, spinning wheels], thimbles, thread and yarn tools); Craft Organization; Patterns and Molds (beading, needlecraft and sewing patterns). Model Making (kits, trains, rocketry) and Collectibles are siblings, not children.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split; gaps
- **Agree.** (1) Within a craft, every specialist store divides by role: material, tool, machine, fitting, finish (Tandy S26, Clay King S12, Hobbylinc S14, Fire Mountain S28, Hobbycraft S4 S5, buttinette S8). (2) Yarn weight, clay cone, leather weight and fabric GSM are measured specs shown as facets or tables (S2, S27, S37, S38). (3) Artist versus student is a property of a maker's range (S19, S20, S7). (4) Mechanical versus computerized is the standard machine split (S22, S23, S24, S6).
- **Split the same objects differently.** Google (S30) is role-first (all machines together: sewing machines beside spinning wheels and looms), while Michaels, Hobbycraft and buttinette are craft-first (S4, S5, S6, S8). UNSPSC (S17) is a flat list of crafts. Blick (S1) and Sekaido (S10) are medium-first for picture-making. Thread belongs to "Crafting Fibers" with yarn in Google but to haberdashery with notions at Hobbycraft (S4) and buttinette (S8). Kilns are by loading, fuel, size or use (ceramic, glass, knife) depending on the store (S12, S13). Beads are by material, form, maker or origin (S11, S28).
- **Gaps.** Spotlight (Australia), Joann, Yuzawaya's tree, Jackson's UK, Hobbycraft's art department and any Chinese, Indian or Spanish-language craft retailer were not fetched. The four languages covered are English, German, Japanese and (via maker pages) French.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose the **craft** first (what they are making), then the **role** of the object (material, tool, machine, fitting), then a **medium or type**, and only then grade, maker and price.
- Navigation: Michaels (Knit and Sew Shop, S6), Hobbycraft (Sewing, Knitting and Crochet as separate departments with Craft Type as a facet, S4 S5), buttinette (Wolle und Handarbeiten vs Stoffe und Nähzubehör vs Schmuckbasteln vs Papierbasteln, S8), Rayher (S9) and UNSPSC (S17) are craft-led. Blick and Sekaido are medium-led inside picture-making (S1, S10).
- Questions in buyer guides: "what are you making?" (Brother AU, S24; Singer "How to choose", S22); "what weight and what weave?" (S38); "is 8 ply the same as worsted?" (S35); "mechanical or computerized?" (S22, S23, S24); "which tannage, grade and weight for a wallet?" (S27); "what cone does my clay fire at?" (S37).
- Filter order: yarn is filtered by weight and fibre together (S5); needles by type then material (S36); sewing machines by category then type then features (S22); beads by category, form, brand and shape (S28). In none of these is price the first filter.
- The performance tier (artist or student) is chosen second, inside a medium (S19, S20).

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): craft family, then role, then kind, then named form

Levels and the single axis of each:
1. **Sub-family** — axis: *the craft the object is for* (picture-making, sewing and fabric, yarn and needlecraft, leathercraft, beading and jewellery making, paper craft, clay and ceramics, model-making). This is the wording of the C4 test.
2. **Leaf** — axis: *role in the craft*: colour or material, support or surface, applicator or hand tool, machine, fitting or notion (the split used inside a craft by S12, S14, S26, S28, S4).
3. **Kind** — axis stated per leaf: *medium or binder* for colour; *stitch job* for machines; *product form* for yarn; *cloth type* for fabric; *tannage* for leather; *body class* for clay; *chemistry* for model paint; *operation* for needles and hooks.
4. **Named form** — a maker's range, model or line (Winsor & Newton Cotman, Singer Heavy Duty 4511, Knitcraft Everyday DK, Skutt KM 1027, Miyuki Delica #11), only when the name denotes something a buyer can compare.

Where it breaks (edge objects):
1. **Thread.** Google files thread with yarn in "Crafting Fibers" (S30); Hobbycraft and buttinette file sewing thread with haberdashery and notions (S4, S8); embroidery floss is thread but belongs to needlecraft (S6). 2. **Cutting machines (Cricut-type).** Singer lists cutting machines and heat presses in the same collection as sewing machines (S22); Google puts "Craft Cutters and Embossers" in tools (S30). Craft family says paper craft or fabric; the role says machine. Placed in C4.6 by default; section 10 asks whether they are D1.
3. **Pottery wheel and kiln** serve one craft but are machines, while Google puts them outside Arts and Crafts altogether (no kiln or wheel node under Arts and Crafts in S31); here they sit in C4.7 as the test names kilns. Knife-making kilns (S12) are heat-treatment furnaces for metal and belong in C1.
4. **Craft kits** (needlecraft kit, jewellery-making kit, paint-by-numbers) bundle several kinds (S30 has a Kits branch; S5 has "Craft Kits" as a product type). A kit is not a kind; it needs a rule (section 10).

### Candidate B: role first, then craft (Google Product Taxonomy shape)

- Level 1 — axis *role*: Materials, Tools, Machines, Tool accessories, Kits, Patterns and molds, Organization [S30][S31].
- Level 2 — axis *material or tool family* (paper, fasteners, paint-ink-glaze, fibres, wire, trims, pottery and sculpting, textiles; cutting, measuring, needles and hooks, thread and yarn tools, textile craft machines).
- Example kinds (13): sewing machine, spinning wheel, mechanical loom, knitting needle, crochet hook, yarn, thread, fabric, interfacing, bead, jewellery findings, clay, art brush.
- Breaks: (1) one craft is scattered across four branches, so a knitter finds yarn under Materials > Crafting Fibers, needles under Tools > Needles and Hooks and a knitting machine under Tools > Textile Craft Machines; (2) a sewing machine sits beside a spinning wheel and a hand loom, which no sewing buyer compares; (3) Kits and Patterns are bundles or published works, not kinds; (4) Model making, pottery equipment and printmaking supplies fall outside it (S31 shows no kiln or wheel node under Arts and Crafts). Better kept as a **generated view** ("all machines", "all hand tools").

### Candidate C: flat list of crafts (UNSPSC family 60120000 shape)

- Level 1 — axis *craft or technique*, ~35 classes with no grouping [S17].
- Level 2 — axis *product family*.
- Example kinds (12): oil paint, canvas, sewing machine, ribbon, bead, leather, clay, kiln, mosaic tile, candle wick, basket cane, stained glass.
- Breaks: (1) no grouping, so "art", "fabric", "sewing and stitchery and weaving" and "ribbon making" all sit at one level; (2) several classes are tiny (maskmaking, craft poms, wiggle eyes) next to huge ones; (3) it has no place for model-making supplies or kilns beyond one "clay and ceramics" class; (4) crafts outside the C4 test (candle making, stained glass, mosaics, basketry, wood crafts, cork) are classes of their own, so it exposes the scope gap in section 10. Its list is the best check that Candidate A's eight sub-families do not miss a craft.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds:** about 250 to 350 under Candidate A (estimate; Google's Arts and Crafts holds 170 descendant nodes of which about 140 are materials and tools [S31], and UNSPSC lists about 35 crafts [S17]; this memo adds paint media, fabric types, machine kinds and leather types that Google does not split).
- **Named forms:** several thousand if every range and model counts. Evidence of scale: 146 sewing machines in one maker store (S22); 11,265 model-making supply items from 91 makers (S14); 31,546 bead products (S28); 932 yarns at one UK retailer (S5); Tamiya Color has over 400 products (S15). Individual colours, bead part numbers and paint codes (Tamiya XF-1, Humbrol numbers, Delica DB-0200) are items inside a range, not named forms.
- **Where depth concentrates:** paints (medium by grade by maker range), beads (form by maker by shape number, S28), yarn (weight by fibre by line), sewing machines (type by maker by model) and fabric (cloth by weight by fibre).
- **Broad leaves:** artist colour, yarn, fabric, beads and findings, notions. **Medium:** brushes, sewing machines, clay, leather, paper-craft materials, needles and hooks, model paint. **Narrow:** kilns, wheels, looms, spinning wheels, knitting machines, easels.

## 4b. Choice level

A kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. By sub-family:
- **Colour (picture-making): the kind is the medium** (watercolour, acrylic, oil, ink). Buyers compare artist against student range and maker within a medium (S19, S20, S7). Grade is a modifier; the maker's range is the named form. Single colours are attributes (pigment, lightfastness class, series), not kinds.
- **Brushes:** the kind is the artist brush; hair and shape are specs. The fetched pages gave only department-level evidence (S1, S10, S30), so this is the weakest call in the memo.
- **Machines: the kind is the stitch job** (sewing, overlock, coverstitch, embroidery, longarm). Bernina explains that an overlock is a different machine, not a stitch setting (S23); Wikipedia names the four stitch families (S34). Mechanical against computerized, heavy duty and industrial against domestic are modifiers; models are the named forms (S22, S24).
- **Yarn: one kind per product form** (knitting and crochet yarn, crochet thread, floss, unspun fibre). Weight is a spec attribute shown as a facet beside fibre (S5), not a level: the same eight CYC classes cut across every fibre and maker (S2, S3, S35), and the two systems alias each other (DK = 8 ply, Aran = 10 ply = worsted; S35). Lines are named forms.
- **Fabric: the kind is the cloth name** (quilting cotton, denim, jersey). Buyers say "weight and weave" (S38); GSM is a spec and fibre a tag. buttinette's fabric filter mixes cloth names with fibres (Baumwollstoff, Bouclé, Canvas; S8), which shows the noise to avoid.
- **Leather: the kind is the tannage or finish** (vegetable-tanned, chrome-tanned, suede, hair-on), as Tandy divides it (S26); grade, weight in oz and animal are attributes (S27); tannery lines are named forms.
- **Beads:** the kind is the bead form with material only where material is the form (seed bead, fire-polished glass bead, gemstone bead). Fire Mountain's fine part-number shapes (S28) are named forms.
- **Clay: the kind is the body class** (earthenware, stoneware, porcelain, air-dry, paper clay), and the cone range is its spec (S12, S37).
- **Model paint: the kind is the chemistry** (acrylic, enamel, lacquer), because chemistries are not freely mixable (S15, S16).
- The choice level differs between sub-families: it is the medium in picture-making, the machine job in sewing, the product form in yarn and the body class in clay.

## 5. Quality and tier

Mixed. There is no single ordering across C4; each leaf has its own.
- **Artist versus student colour: consensus-ranked.** Makers publish two ranges per medium (Winsor & Newton Professional against Cotman, S19; Sennelier "extra fine" against "fine", with about twice the pigment, S20). Retailers sell "student-grade to professional" (S7). Sennelier adds a price series 1 to 6 for artist colours by pigment cost and cites ASTM lightfastness I to V (S20). HS customs does not split the two (S18). Shown as a **grade modifier** (student, artist) on the kind and on each named range; lightfastness class and series are attributes of colours.
- **Leather grade: consensus-ranked** (full grain, top grain, corrected, genuine, bonded; S27); weight in oz is a spec.
- **Clay and kilns: spec-ranked** by cone temperature (cone 022 to 14; earthenware cone 015 to 1, stoneware cone 2 to 12, porcelain cone 10 to 13; S37), with kiln chamber size as a second spec (S12).
- **Yarn weight and fabric GSM: specs but not tiers.** They describe thickness or mass, not quality (S2, S38).
- **Sewing machines: preference.** Feature classes (mechanical, computerized, heavy duty) and price bands exist but no ordering of quality (S22, S23, S24).
- **Beads:** item-level grade words ("economy grade", "D grade"; S28), not an industry ordering.
- **Model paint, notions, paper craft: no tier.**

All of these appear as modifiers (grade, cone class, weight class, GSM class), never as levels.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity, with platform pockets.** The maker is part of what is sought in paints (a range is the product), model paints (numbered colours), beads (Miyuki, Matsuno, Czech, Preciosa-type shape numbers, S28), kilns and machines, and vintage sewing machines, where collectors organise the whole hobby by maker (S25). It is platform where the maker sets compatibility: sewing-machine feet and accessories carry Singer "Compatible Machine Type" codes (S22) and Bernina sells presser feet and accessories per machine through its own accessories search (S23); KnitPicks Options needle tips fit only their cables (S36); model paint chemistries must match (S16). Maker is incidental for plain notions, generic fabric and paper.

**Recommend a maker ledger**, with maker, known-for kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country and era, each row attested. Seed rows from fetched pages: Winsor & Newton, Schmincke, Sennelier, Art Spectrum (S7, S19, S20); Singer, Husqvarna Viking, Pfaff, Bernina, Brother (S22, S23, S24); Gütermann, Hemline, Milward, Knitcraft, Trimits, Clover (S4, S5); KnitPicks (S36); Tamiya, Humbrol, Vallejo, Testors (S14, S15, S16); Miyuki, Matsuno (S28); Hermann Oak, Wickett and Craig (S27); Skutt, L&L, Paragon, Olympic (S12); Rohde, Potclays (S13); Laguna, Standard, Mammoth (S12).

## 7. Scenes, cultures and views

- **Craft traditions as tags and views:** quilting, embroidery and cross stitch, amigurumi, weaving, macramé, felting (S4, S5, S8); diamond painting and punch needle (S4); cosplay and comic or manga drawing (Sekaido lists comic supplies, S10). These are generated views by tag, not levels and not named forms.
- **Regional vocabulary as aliases, not levels:** haberdashery (UK, S4) = notions (US, S6) = Kurzwaren and Nähzubehör (Germany, S8); DK = 8 ply (S35); Aran/worsted/10 ply (S3, S35); cone numbers against degrees (S37). Japanese calligraphy supplies are a Sekaido top-level category (S10), a tradition-specific object set (see section 10).
- **User and occasion groupings are tags, not levels:** children's crafts and occasions are top-level in buttinette (S8) and Rayher (S9) and Singer filters by skill level (S22); these map to user and occasion modifiers.
- **Named form only when the scene names a distinct form:** Tunisian crochet hooks (S36), Delica seed beads (S28), raku clay (S12) and longarm quilting machines (S23) are distinct forms; "quilter's" or "cosplayer's" versions of a kind are not.
- **Era:** vintage and antique sewing machines (S25) are an era tag on the machine kind.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Order by **spec where a standard exists, otherwise canonical (most generic) form first**. Yarn kinds are ordered lace to jumbo by CYC number (S2); clay by cone range, low to high (S37); fabric by GSM class (S38); leather by weight (S27). Machines run from the plain sewing machine outward to overlock, coverstitch, embroidery and longarm (S34, S23). Model-making supplies follow Tamiya's workflow order: cut, shape, fill, mask, paint, finish (S15). Paint media run watercolour, acrylic, oil, ink, mediums, following Blick's department order (S1).

## 9. Boundaries

Neighbours, closest cross-boundary objects and precedence sentences:

**C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment**
- Objects: hobby knife, side cutters, jeweller's pliers, a leather punch, wood-burning pen (S30), soldering iron for jewellery, rotary tool, knife-making kiln (S12).
- Precedence: *A tool made for model-making, beading, jewellery making, leatherworking, sewing or paper craft is C4 even when a workshop owner buys it; a general-purpose cutting, shaping, drilling or soldering tool, and any tool for shaping wood, metal or stone or for electronics work, is C1 whoever uses it.*
- Precedence: *A kiln made for firing clay or fusing glass is C4; a furnace for heat-treating metal is C1.*

**C5 Office & Stationery**
- Objects: drawing pen against writing pen, fineliner, sketchbook against notebook, calligraphy brush, ink and inkstone (S10), comic-drawing nibs (S10), colouring books.
- Precedence: *Pencils, markers and inks made for drawing or making pictures are C4; pens, pencils and highlighters made for writing are C5; a sketchbook is C4 and a blank notebook or diary is C5; colouring books are F1.*

**E1 Toys & Games**
- Objects: plastic model kit, kids' craft kit (S8, S9), paint-your-own ceramics, diamond-painting kit (S4), craft kit (S5).
- Precedence: *A scale model or model kit, built or not, is E1 (step 25 is tried first); the paint, cement, putty, airbrush and tools sold for building and finishing models are C4.*

**A3 Jewellery and F6 Natural History (and F2 Art)**
- Objects: strung necklace, loose bead, drilled gemstone strand (S28), loose pearl, unset gemstone, finished painting, sculpture, blank canvas.
- Precedence: *A finished necklace or bracelet is A3; loose beads, findings, wire and cord sold for making jewellery are C4; an unset gemstone or loose pearl is F6 whether or not it is later used in jewellery.*
- Precedence: *A finished painting, drawing or sculpture is F2; the paint, canvas, clay and glaze it was made from are C4.*

**Other close calls**
- Brush: *A brush made for painting a picture or applying glaze is C4; a brush made for applying makeup is H1.*
- Paint and glue: *Paint made for artists, crafts or models and glue made for paper craft or model-making are C4 (step 28); wall paint, masonry paint and general-purpose or construction adhesives are C8.*
- Drawing tablet and plotter: *A drawing tablet is D1; a sewing machine, embroidery machine or craft cutting machine is C4 unless the owner moves cutters to D1 (section 10).*
- Fabric and wadding: *Fabric, felt and wadding sold by the metre or as craft material are C4; finished curtains, rugs and throws are B2.*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A. Sub-family = the craft (picture-making; sewing and fabric; yarn and needlecraft; leathercraft; beading and jewellery making; paper craft; clay and ceramics; model-making). Leaf = role in the craft (colour or material, support, tool, machine, fitting). Kind = medium, stitch job, product form, cloth type, tannage, body class or chemistry as the leaf states. Named form = maker range, model or line. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Grade, weight, GSM, cone, fibre, era, market and technology are modifiers, tags or spec attributes; makers go in a maker ledger. Role-first (Candidate B) and craft-list (Candidate C) layouts become generated views.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. **Scope of "crafts".** Every source includes crafts that the C4 test omits: candle making and wicks, resin and casting, stained glass, mosaics, wood crafts, basketry, floristry and soap (S17, S30, S8, S9). Under the current key they would drop to B4, C8 or X0. Add a ninth sub-family "other hobby crafts", or leave them out?
2. **Craft kits and sewing patterns.** A kit bundles several kinds, a pattern is a published instruction (F1 or I2). Recommend: a kit is filed by its dominant material; a paper sewing pattern goes to F1 and a PDF to I2. Confirm.
3. **Cutting plotters** (Cricut-type) and heat presses (S22, S30): C4 or D1?
4. **Calligraphy supplies** (brush, ink stick, inkstone; S10): C4 (medium for pictures) or C5?
5. **Art-studio furniture and storage** (tables, taborets, portfolios, cabinets, S1): B1 under S3's "never parts" rule, with only easels in C4?
6. **Drilled gemstone and pearl beads** against F6's "loose pearls and unset gemstones": confirm beads for stringing are C4.
7. **Craft paint for decorating objects** (as opposed to picture-making) and craft glue: confirm C4 over C8.
8. **Yarn weight and fabric GSM** as spec attributes rather than levels, and weight aliases (DK, 8 ply, Double Knitting) stored once.
9. **Brush choice level** (round, flat, hair type): needs a brush-specific source before it is fixed.

**Framework conflicts noticed (for the next revision, not applied):** the C4 test omits the crafts in decision 1; the C4 test lists "pottery tools and kilns" but no wheels, slab rollers or pugmills; the "easels" entry sits beside art-studio furniture that falls to B1; "sewing tape measures" is a note under C6 but not in the C4 test.
