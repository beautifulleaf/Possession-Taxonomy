## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B6 Cleaning & Laundry, domain B Home.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, branch 2): *made to **clean, wash or dry** the home, its surfaces or textiles, or to collect waste: vacuum cleaners, brooms, mops, washing machines, dryers, irons, airers, laundry baskets, cleaning products and tools, waste bins and bin bags.* Category line: "everything made to clean, wash or dry, cleaning products, waste bins". The branch is tried second, after B5 Kitchen & Dining and before B7, B8, B3, B1, B2 and B4.

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch B6:**
- S3: parts follow the whole, so a vacuum bag, filter, belt or battery goes to the vacuum's category (B6); **cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8, whatever they are used in**.
- Step 1: services such as cleaning, laundry and dry-cleaning visits, care and repairs are OUT; only the objects used are in.
- Step 7 note: sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for a use are H1 (natural material filed by use); cleaning sponges and scourers are tools made to clean, so B6.
- Step 17/A4: garment bags are luggage (A4); wheeled luggage is A4.
- Step 18: towels of every kind are B2; grooming mirrors, massagers and body scales are H1.
- Step 24 note: a connected household object such as a robot vacuum is filed by its task at step 30 (so B6), because connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 29 (C2): pressure washers, pool cleaning equipment, leaf blowers and compost bins are C2.
- Step 30, B5 branch: dishwashers, kitchen timers and oven gloves are B5 (washing-up products are still cleaning products, B6).
- Step 30, B1 branch: boxes, baskets, racks and hooks are B1, but laundry baskets are named in the B6 branch, which is tried first.
- Step 30, B4 note: air fresheners and home fragrance are B4 (B4 memo proposes: made to scent the air B4, made to remove odour or clean a surface or fabric B6).
- Step 4 note: installed machines keep their own category; a central vacuum unit is therefore B6 and its fixed piping is C8.
- Step 32: tool boxes, workshop machines and trade supplies are C1/C8.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Machines: form (type) first.** Currys, idealo and Kakaku all put the type facet first after price and maker [S1, S2, S4]. Home Depot and Lowe's start the guide with "how you will use it" and then list the types [S5, S8]. Consumer Reports fixes the comparison group by type before it scores anything [S15]. For washers the first question is drum or vertical, then drying or no drying [S17, S30], then capacity by household size [S17]. Floor type and pets (suitable for) come next at Currys [S1]; budget then dock features are the robot buyer's second and third choice [S14, S16, S31].

**Consumables: task first.** dm, wilko, Home Bargains and Askul sort by what is being cleaned or done (laundry, cleaning, washing-up, kitchen, toilet, windows) [S20, S21, S22, S19]. Within laundry, the physical form (powder, liquid, capsule) comes next [S22, S35].

**Tools: head or head form.** Mops are chosen by head type and by dry versus wet job [S33, S6, S28].

**Not the first axis:** maker (a facet, never the tree) [S1, S2, S4]; price (a facet or a guide chapter) [S14, S1]; power source (cordless/corded sits inside the type facet but is secondary to form) [S1].

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): what is cleaned or cared for > product type > kind > named form

- **Level 1, sub-family (axis: what is cleaned or cared for):** floors; hard surfaces and windows; textiles (laundry); dishes; waste. Five sub-families.
- **Level 2, leaf (axis: product type, as a shopper's category names it):** vacuum cleaners, floor washers, carpet cleaners, mops, brooms; cloths, brushes, window cleaners, steam cleaners, chemical cleaners; washing machines, dryers, irons and steamers, airers, laundry baskets, laundry products; washing-up products; bins and bin bags.
- **Level 3, kind (axis stated per leaf):** vacuum body form; washer loading axis; dryer moisture-removal method; iron steam source; mop head form; bin opening mechanism; detergent physical form.
- **Level 4, named form:** model lines and iconic designs (Henry, Dyson V8 to V16, Roomba, Beat Wash).

**Where it breaks:**
1. *Handheld car vacuum or mattress vacuum:* cleans a seat or a bed, not a floor, yet every retailer files it with vacuum cleaners [S1, S5]. The sub-family "floors" is then really "dry and wet suction", and the framework text itself keeps vacuum cleaners together.
2. *Steam cleaner or all-purpose spray:* serves floors and hard surfaces both. Placed in the surfaces sub-family; a floor-only cleaner is in floors.
3. *Robot vacuum-and-mop:* a floor machine that also mops; kept as one kind, with the mop and dock features as attributes [S14, S4].
4. *Dishwasher tablets:* the dishwasher itself is B5, so the sub-family "dishes" holds only consumables and sponges.
5. *Dryer technology as the kind axis:* collides with Appendix A "power source and technology" (see section 10).

### Candidate B: object role first (appliance, implement, product, receptacle) > task > kind

- **Level 1 (axis: role of the object):** appliances; hand implements; consumable products; receptacles and fittings.
- **Level 2 (axis: task):** floor, surface, laundry, waste.
- **Level 3 (axis: form).**
- **Supports:** retailers and trade split appliances from consumables: Currys, idealo, expert.de and Kakaku sell appliances in separate departments from dm and Kakaku daily goods [S1, S2, S3, S18, S21]; HS puts every consumable in 3402 and vacuum cleaners in 8508 [S23, S24].
- **Breaks:** the appliance/implement split is a power-source split (a motor-driven mop against a string mop), which Appendix A forbids as an inner level; a steam mop is both [S28]; Wilko, Home Bargains and Askul sort the other way, task first [S19, S20, S22]; spin mop with bucket, carpet sweeper and window vac are on the line; the same task (laundry) would be spread over four sub-families.

### Candidate C: process (what is removed) > product type > kind

- **Level 1 (axis: soil process):** remove dry soil (vacuum, sweep, dust, lint); wet-clean (mop, scrub, wash, wipe); launder (wash, dry, press); dispose (bin).
- **Supports:** the EU definitions divide vacuum cleaners as dry, wet and wet-and-dry [S12]; the framework wording "clean, wash or dry ... collect waste".
- **Breaks:** robot vacuum-and-mop and wet-dry vacuums sit in two processes [S4, S12]; Kakaku and Currys count wet-dry as a feature rather than a family [S1, S4]; a detergent belongs to laundry and to wet cleaning; no retailer sorts this way.

**Comparison:** Candidate A matches the key's own three objects of the verb (the home and its surfaces, textiles, waste), the wilko/Home Bargains/dm/Askul trees, and keeps appliances and consumables for the same task together, so a shopper for laundry sees washer, dryer, iron, airer, detergent in one place. Its cost is that a vacuum cleaner stands in "floors" though it also cleans stairs and seats. Candidate B follows the department stores of electrical retail but violates Appendix A. Candidate C is the most principled in cleaning science and the least used.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: spec-ranked for appliances where a regulated or measured scale exists, consensus-ranked for robot vacuum docks and price tiers, and preference for consumables, tools and bins.**

- **Spec-ranked (washers and dryers):** EU A to G energy class, energy per 100 cycles, rated capacity in kg, water per cycle, programme duration, spin-drying class and noise on the label [S25]; Kakaku uses capacity bands by household (1 to 2 people 5 to 9 kg, 3 to 4 people 8 to 11 kg, 5 and over 11 kg) [S17].
- **Spec-ranked (vacuums, partly):** EU 666/2013 sets limits for mains dry vacuum cleaners from 1 September 2017: annual energy under 43 kWh, input power under 900 W, dust pick-up on carpet at least 0.75 and on hard floor at least 0.98, dust re-emission at most 1 %; sound power and motor lifetime are declared [S12]. Battery, robot, wet and central cleaners are outside it [S12]. HEPA classes H12 to H14 (99.5 to 99.995 % separation) are the allergy ladder at idealo [S2]. Declared suction (Pa) is not comparable across brands [S14]. Consumer Reports scores only within a type [S15].
- **Consensus-ranked (robots):** Vacuum Wars price tiers (ultra-budget under $300 up to premium $1,300 to $1,600 and over) and a dock ladder from charging dock to auto-empty to mop washing, drying and refilling [S14]; RTINGS tiers best, upper mid-range, mid-range, budget, cheap [S16]. These are test-site consensus, not a standard.
- **Preference:** mops, brooms, cloths, chemical cleaners, bins and laundry products. There is no grade; brand and price are the only order.

**As a modifier, never a level:** store `tier` with a typed source (label class, measured score, or test-site tier) and keep spec fields beside it: energy class, rated capacity kg, spin class, noise dB, annual kWh, dust pick-up, HEPA class, declared suction (marked non-comparable), dock level (1 charging, 2 auto-empty, 3 mop wash and dry), price band. The ordering is only valid within a kind (Consumer Reports and Vacuum Wars both say so) [S14, S15].

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity for appliances; incidental for tools, bins and generic chemicals; light identity for household-name consumables.**
- Maker is a primary facet and the named lines carry the maker: Dyson V-series, Numatic Henry, Roomba, Miele, Bosch Unlimited at idealo and expert.de [S2, S3]; 20-plus brands at Currys [S1]; maker pages with signature technologies (Panasonic auto-dosing, Hitachi, Toshiba ultrafine bubbles, Sharp, AQUA, Haier) in the Kakaku guide [S17]; Chinese buyers search by Roborock, Ecovacs, Xiaomi, Dreame, Yunjing [S31].
- Consumable houses are shelf-level brands: Ariel, Bold, Fairy, Lenor, Microban, Vileda, Flash and Beldray at wilko; Cif, Astonish and Cillit Bang at Home Bargains [S20, S22]; Frosch and Denkmit at dm [S21].
- Platform dependence: vacuum bags, filters, belts and batteries fit only certain models (parts follow the whole under S3), so a `fits` field is needed on parts. No platform at the kind level.

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger** scoped to appliance makers (vacuum, robot, washer, dryer, iron) and the major consumable houses, with columns maker, known-for kinds, scene tags (for example commercial cleaning, premium, budget), tier, price band, country, era and an attestation URL. Named forms carry the maker; kinds do not.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then by scale or autonomy, then named forms by maker lineage and date.**
- Vacuums run from the full-size forms to the portable to the autonomous, as Consumer Reports orders them (large: canister, upright; small: stick, handheld, robotic) [S15].
- Washers: front-load, top-load, then combinations and twin-tub; dryers by the order Wikipedia gives, from vented through condenser to heat pump [S10, S34].
- Named forms follow the maker's own generation sequence (Dyson V7, V8, V10, V11, V12, V15, V16; Hoover Constellation after Model O) [S2, S9].
- Consumables go by physical form (powder, liquid, capsule), and tools by head form, because that is the retailer's line order [S22, S33].

## 9. Boundaries

(framework) = restates an existing note; (proposed) = new, needs the owner.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A):** category B6 > sub-family (what is cleaned or cared for: floors; hard surfaces and windows; textiles; dishes; waste) > leaf (product type) > kind (form or mechanism, stated per leaf) > named form (model line or iconic design). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels**, as the template allows. Attributes (power source, bag or bagless, capacity, run time, HEPA class, energy class, spin class, noise, dock level, mop system, bio or non-bio, scent), tags (region, market, user such as pet or allergy, scene, era, price band) and a maker ledger sit beside the tree.

**Reasons:** it follows the verb in the framework test (the home and its surfaces, textiles, waste); it matches the task-first trees of wilko, Home Bargains, dm and Askul [S19–S22] and keeps the washer, dryer, iron, airer and detergent together; it stops each kind at the level where Consumer Reports, Kakaku, Currys and Lowe's compare [S1, S5, S15, S17]; and it moves power, bag type, user and market to attributes, as Appendix A requires.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. Approve "what is cleaned or cared for" as the sub-family axis and the five sub-families, or choose Candidate B or C.
2. **Technology as a kind axis.** Appendix A lists "power source and technology" as a modifier, while the stage-2 instruction omits it from the forbidden list. Recommended: power source (cordless, corded) and bag type stay attributes; a mechanism that changes the object's form and what buyers compare stays a kind (robot, wet-and-dry, heat pump dryer, impeller washer). The alternative is to make heat pump, condenser, agitator and impeller attributes of one "tumble dryer" and one "top-load washer".
3. **Robot vacuum:** one kind inside vacuum cleaners (recommended, as Currys and Lowe's do [S1, S5]) or a leaf of its own (Kakaku [S4]).
4. **Stick and handheld:** two kinds (Currys, Lowe's, recommended) or one (Home Depot, Chinese guide) [S1, S5, S8, S32].
5. **Washer-dryer:** a kind of washing machine (recommended) or a separate leaf (EU label, China) [S25, S30].
6. **Parts and accessories** (vacuum bags, filters, belts, batteries, nozzles): confirm they stay in B6 as parts under S3, with a `fits` field, and decide whether they get kinds or only a parts leaf.
7. **Consumables:** confirm cleaning and laundry products as kinds (leaf by product class, kind by physical form) and brands as ledger rows, not forms. The framework already puts cleaning products in B6.
8. Boundary rulings in section 9, especially: shoe care (B6 or A2), dish racks (B5 or B1), moisture absorbers (B7), bathroom dryer unit and heated airer (B6), fabric freshener against room spray (B6 against B4), household brush against trade brush (B6 against C1), textile dye (C4).
9. **Antique and hand-wash tools** (washboard, wash copper, carpet beater): include as kinds under a hand-wash leaf, or leave to a later pass.
10. **Scope of the maker ledger:** appliance makers only, or major consumable houses too.

**Framework conflicts to log (not applied here):**
- The B6 branch names laundry baskets, but S3 and the B1 branch file baskets and boxes under B1; the order of branches means B6 wins, but the S3 "never parts" sentence should say so.
- Appendix A lists power source and technology as modifiers; the kind rules for robot, heat pump and impeller (decision 2) need a rule.
- Washing-up products and dishwasher cleaners are B6 while the dishwasher is B5; one precedence sentence would prevent drift.

**Gaps in this research.**
- **No collector or resale source** (vintage vacuums, antique laundry tools) was fetched; section 7 treats hand-wash and historic tools only from Wikipedia's lists [S10, S11].
- **Not reached:** Amazon and marketplace trees; German and Japanese consumable trees beyond dm and Askul; French, Spanish, Indian and Brazilian retail; JD and Tmall (the platforms returned no category text; logged as blocked hosts by earlier batches); Wirecutter and Which? (appeared in search only); the EU ecolabel for detergents; any laundry-detergent standard.
- **Search-excerpt content in fetched pages:** the Zhihu robot guide [S31], the expert.de Waschen list [S3] and the idealo Produkttyp counts beyond the top three [S2] were read from search excerpts of pages that were also fetched, because the fetch excerpt was truncated; they should be re-read in the data phase.
- **Names marked † in section 3** need a fetched attestation. A four-URL Parallel fetch (Wikipedia vacuum, washing machine, dryer, Home Depot) timed out once; each was fetched later in a smaller call.
- No host refused a request; the Parallel fetch timeout was the only failure.
