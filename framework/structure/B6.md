## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B6 Cleaning & Laundry, domain B Home.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, branch 2): *made to **clean, wash or dry** the home, its surfaces or textiles, or to collect waste: vacuum cleaners, brooms, mops, washing machines, dryers, irons, airers, laundry baskets, cleaning products and tools, waste bins and bin bags.* Category line: "everything made to clean, wash or dry, cleaning products, waste bins". The branch is tried second, after B5 Kitchen & Dining and before B7, B8, B3, B1, B2 and B4.

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch B6:**
- S3: parts follow the whole, so a vacuum bag, filter, belt or battery goes to the vacuum's category (B6); **cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8, whatever they are used in**.
- Step 1: services such as cleaning, laundry and dry-cleaning visits, care and repairs are OUT; only the objects used are in.
- Step 7 note: sponges, loofahs and pumice sold for a use are H1 (natural material filed by use); cleaning sponges and scourers are tools made to clean, so B6.
- Step 17/A4: garment bags are luggage (A4); wheeled luggage is A4.
- Step 18: towels of every kind are B2; grooming mirrors, massagers and body scales are H1.
- Step 24 note: a connected household object such as a robot vacuum is filed by its task at step 30 (so B6), because connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 29 (C2): pressure washers, pool cleaning equipment, leaf blowers and compost bins are C2.
- Step 30, B5 branch: dishwashers, kitchen timers and oven gloves are B5 (washing-up products are still cleaning products, B6).
- Step 30, B1 branch: boxes, baskets, racks and hooks are B1, but laundry baskets are named in the B6 branch, which is tried first.
- Step 30, B4 note: air fresheners and home fragrance are B4 (B4 memo proposes: made to scent the air B4, made to remove odour or clean a surface or fabric B6).
- Step 4 note: installed machines keep their own category; a central vacuum unit is therefore B6 and its fixed piping is C8.
- Step 13: things made for keeping, feeding, housing, carrying, training or caring for animals are B10, so cat litter (made for the animal's toilet) is B10 (review C-7 row 31); a cleaner made for pet stains is a cleaning product and stays B6 under S3.
- Step 30, B7 branch: baths, showers, basins, toilets, water heaters, softeners and their salt or cartridges are B7 (softener salt follows its machine under S3); dishwasher salt and rinse aid are cleaning products, B6.
- Step 32: tool boxes, workshop machines and trade supplies are C1/C8.

**Enthusiast and review communities.** Vacuum Wars (robot vacuums, 150 to 200-plus tested) groups by need (mop combos, pet hair, large homes) and by price tier, and treats dock automation as the main feature ladder [S14]. RTINGS (95-plus robots) names tiers best, upper mid-range, mid-range, budget and cheap, and explains that bump-navigation robots sit under about $200 and LiDAR mapping arrives at $200–300 [S16]. Consumer Reports divides vacuums into large (canister, upright) and small (stick, handheld, robotic) and says an Overall Score is comparable only within a type, except canister against upright [S15]; its steam mops are a separate rated type from traditional mops [S28]. Zhihu writers compare by budget band and base-station features [S30, S31].

**Collectors and the resale market.** No collector or resale source was fetched (vintage Hoover and Kirby machines, antique washing tools and mangles exist as collecting niches but were not reached before the cap). Wikipedia names iconic forms such as Hoover's 1954 Constellation and the Kirby "Domestic Cyclone" lineage [S9].

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.** EU Regulation 666/2013 defines vacuum cleaner types: dry, wet, wet-and-dry (more than 2.5 L of liquid), battery operated, robot (mobile part plus docking station), central, hybrid, water filter (more than 0.5 L of water), and general-purpose, hard-floor and carpet by nozzle; it excludes wet, wet-and-dry, battery, robot, industrial and central cleaners and floor polishers from its limits [S12]. IEC 60312-1 (dry household vacuum cleaners; marked replaced, committee TC 59/SC 59F "Surface cleaning appliances") shows the dry/household split and excludes battery cleaners [S13]. EU Regulations 2019/2023 and 2019/2014 treat washing machines and washer-dryers together, with an A to G label for energy per 100 cycles, rated capacity in kg, water, programme duration, spin-drying class and noise, and exclude machines under 2 kg [S25]. HS 8508 splits vacuum cleaners by motor and capacity (850811 motor not above 1,500 W and receptacle not above 20 L, 850819 other, 850860 other than self-contained motor, 850870 parts), not by upright, stick or robot [S24]. HS 3402 puts laundry detergent, dishwashing liquid, household cleaners, fabric softener and stain removers in one heading, split only by retail or bulk [S23].

**Museums and reference works.** Wikipedia divides vacuum cleaners into upright (direct-fan or fan-bypass), canister/cylinder, wet/dry (shop vac), extraction, pneumatic, backpack, cyclonic, central and robotic, with the Scooba robot wet vacuum as an "other" [S9]; dryers into tumble (incl. washer-dryer and laundry center), ventless (spin, condenser, heat pump, mechanical steam compression), convectant, solar, microwave, ultrasonic, hybrid and the Japanese bathroom clothes-drying unit [S10]; mops into dry/dust, wet/moist, pre-moistened, microfibre, sponge and cloth [S33]; washing machines into top-loading and front-loading [S34]; laundry detergent into powder and liquid forms, with pods in a separate article [S35]; and lists "cleaning tools" as one flat alphabetical list that mixes tools, machines and methods (broom, besom, carpet beater, chamois, dishwasher, feather duster, hataki, lint remover, microfibre cloth, mop, parts washer, pressure washing, vacuum cleaner, washing machine, wire brush) [S11]. The robotic vacuum has its own article [S36].

**Where sources agree.** (a) The vacuum family is upright, canister/cylinder, stick, handheld, robot, with wet-dry/shop, central and carpet extractor as further types [S1, S5, S8, S9, S15, S32]. (b) Washing machines split by the axis of the drum (horizontal front-load against vertical top-load) in every region [S7, S17, S30, S34]. (c) Dryers split by how moisture is removed [S10]. (d) Irons split into iron, steam station and garment steamer [S26, S27]. (e) Consumables are kept apart from appliances in dm, Kakaku, wilko-style departments, and sorted by task: laundry, cleaning, washing-up, waste [S18, S20, S21, S22].

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- **Robot vacuum:** a type of vacuum at Currys and Lowe's [S1, S5], its own category at Kakaku [S4], outside the ecodesign scope in the EU text [S12] and not distinguished by HS [S24].
- **Stick and handheld:** two kinds at Lowe's and Currys, one "cordless and stick" kind at Home Depot [S8], and merged into "small" at Consumer Reports [S15]. The Chinese guide uses three forms, horizontal, upright and handheld, with no separate stick [S32].
- **Cordless, bagless, pet:** are presented as vacuum "types" at Currys [S1] but are power, dust-handling and user attributes under Appendix A.
- **Wet-and-dry:** a vacuum type in Wikipedia and the EU text [S9, S12], a feature at Currys [S1], and a separate product line (Waschsauger) at idealo [S2].
- **Washer-dryer:** a type of drum or vertical washer at Kakaku [S17, S18], a separate label class in the EU [S25] and a separate category from the washer-plus-dryer set in China [S30].
- **Sewing machines:** grouped with washers and irons at expert.de [S3], but C4 in the framework.
- **Washing-up and dishwasher products:** sit with cleaning at wilko and Home Bargains [S20, S22] and in their own department at dm [S21].
- **Bathroom clothes-drying unit** (Japan): a dryer type in Wikipedia [S10] but fixed ventilation equipment in a building trade sense.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Machines: form (type) first.** Currys, idealo and Kakaku all put the type facet first after price and maker [S1, S2, S4]. Home Depot and Lowe's start the guide with "how you will use it" and then list the types [S5, S8]. Consumer Reports fixes the comparison group by type before it scores anything [S15]. For washers the first question is drum or vertical, then drying or no drying [S17, S30], then capacity by household size [S17]. Floor type and pets (suitable for) come next at Currys [S1]; budget then dock features are the robot buyer's second and third choice [S14, S16, S31].

**Consumables: task first.** dm, wilko, Home Bargains and Askul sort by what is being cleaned or done (laundry, cleaning, washing-up, kitchen, toilet, windows) [S20, S21, S22, S19]. Within laundry, the physical form (powder, liquid, capsule) comes next [S22, S35].

**Tools: head or head form.** Mops are chosen by head type and by dry versus wet job [S33, S6, S28].

**Not the first axis:** maker (a facet, never the tree) [S1, S2, S4]; price (a facet or a guide chapter) [S14, S1]; power source (cordless/corded sits inside the type facet but is secondary to form) [S1].

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): what is cleaned or cared for > product type > kind > named form

OWNER: approve "what is cleaned or cared for" and the five sub-families (review Q-B6), or choose Candidate B or C. The memo keeps Candidate A.

- **Level 1, sub-family (axis: what is cleaned or cared for; never the room, which is a setting):** B6.1 floors; B6.2 surfaces, windows and objects; B6.3 textiles (laundry); B6.4 dishes; B6.5 waste. Five sub-families. Codes are numbers only: `<category>.<sub-family>.<leaf>` (C-9), for example B6.3.2.
- **Level 2, leaf (axis stated per sub-family, C5(c)):**
  - B6.1 Floors: product type as a shopper's category names it (vacuum cleaners, floor washers and extractors, parts, mops, brooms, floor products).
  - B6.2 Surfaces, windows and objects: product type; chemical cleaners are a leaf whose kinds go by soil or action; a care-products leaf goes by the object cared for.
  - B6.3 Textiles: product type (washing machines, dryers, irons, airers and baskets, detergents, other textile-care products).
  - B6.4 Dishes: leaf = null (C5(b)), because the sub-family holds about six kinds or fewer; its kinds go by use (hand washing, machine washing).
  - B6.5 Waste: product type (bins, bags and liners, waste machines).
- **Level 3, kind (axis stated per leaf in the tree below):** vacuum body form and use; washer drum arrangement and function; dryer moisture-removal method; iron and steamer type; mop head form; bin form; detergent physical form; soil or action for chemical cleaners.
- **Level 4, named form:** model lines and iconic designs (Henry, Dyson V8 to V15, Roomba, Beat Wash). Each is recorded with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2(a)); every example below is maker-bound. Lines that span kinds are rows of the line table (C1, C-5), with `series_id` on the members.

**Where it breaks:**
1. *Handheld car vacuum or mattress vacuum:* cleans a seat or a bed, not a floor, yet every retailer files it with vacuum cleaners [S1, S5]. The sub-family "floors" is then really "dry and wet suction". Harmless: the leaf decides the home, so the handheld vacuum has one home (B6.1.1) and a generated view lists it by what it cleans.
2. *Steam cleaner or all-purpose spray:* serves floors and hard surfaces both. One home each under C14(b): steam cleaner in B6.2.3, multi-purpose cleaner in B6.2.4; a floor-only cleaner is B6.1.6.
3. *Robot vacuum-and-mop:* a floor machine that also mops; kept as one kind, with the mop and dock features as attributes [S14, S4].
4. *Dishwasher tablets:* the dishwasher itself is B5, so the sub-family "dishes" holds only consumables.
5. *Dryer and washer technology as the kind axis:* settled by rule T (C10) as follows: vented against ventless is a kind (the duct); condenser against heat pump, sensor against pedal, and cordless against corded are attributes; robot and steam mop are kinds because their form differs. Agitator against impeller waits on the owner (decision 2).
6. *Sub-family 2 is partly an "objects" sub-family:* shoe care, car-wash and descaler products do not clean a surface; they sit in B6.2.5 so that every object the key and S3 send to B6 has one home.

### Candidate B: object role first (appliance, implement, product, receptacle) > task > kind

- **Level 1 (axis: role of the object):** appliances; hand implements; consumable products; receptacles and fittings.
- **Level 2 (axis: task):** floor, surface, laundry, waste.
- **Level 3 (axis: form).**
- **Supports:** retailers and trade split appliances from consumables: Currys, idealo, expert.de and Kakaku sell appliances in separate departments from dm and Kakaku daily goods [S1, S2, S3, S18, S21]; HS puts every consumable in 3402 and vacuum cleaners in 8508 [S23, S24].
- **Breaks:** the appliance/implement split is a power-source split (a motor-driven mop against a string mop), which Appendix A forbids as an inner level; a steam mop is both [S28]; Wilko, Home Bargains and Askul sort the other way, task first [S19, S20, S22]; spin mop with bucket, carpet sweeper and window vac are on the line; the same task (laundry) would be spread over four sub-families.

### Candidate C: process (what is removed) > product type > kind

- **Level 1 (axis: soil process):** remove dry soil (vacuum, sweep, dust, lint); wet-clean (mop, scrub, wash, wipe); launder (wash, dry, press); dispose (bin).
- **Supports:** the EU definitions divide vacuum cleaners as dry, wet and wet-and-dry [S12]; the framework wording "clean, wash or dry ... collect waste".
- **Breaks:** robot vacuum-and-mop and wet-dry vacuums sit in two processes [S4, S12]; Kakaku and Currys count wet-dry as a feature rather than a family [S1, S4]; a detergent belongs to laundry and to wet cleaning; no retailer sorts this way.

**Comparison:** Candidate A matches the key's own three objects of the verb (the home and its surfaces, textiles, waste), the wilko/Home Bargains/dm/Askul trees, and keeps appliances and consumables for the same task together, so a shopper for laundry sees washer, dryer, iron, airer, detergent in one place. Its cost is that a vacuum cleaner stands in "floors" though it also cleans stairs and seats. Candidate B follows the department stores of electrical retail but violates Appendix A. Candidate C is the most principled in cleaning science and the least used.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: spec ladders (attributes with the standard named) for washers, dryers and vacuums where a regulated or measured scale exists; tier entries (consensus) for robot vacuum models; preference, with no tier, for consumables, tools and bins.**

Under C4(a) a tier never sits on a kind. It sits on a maker, a line or a named form as a tier entry (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`); a spec ladder measured on the object is an attribute with its standard named (C4(b)).

- **Spec ladders, stored as attributes (washers and dryers):** EU A to G energy class, energy per 100 cycles, rated capacity in kg, water per cycle, programme duration, spin-drying class and noise on the label, with Regulations 2019/2023 and 2019/2014 named as the standard [S25]. Kakaku's capacity bands by household (1 to 2 people 5 to 9 kg, 3 to 4 people 8 to 11 kg, 5 and over 11 kg) are a buying guide for the capacity attribute, not a tier [S17].
- **Spec ladders, stored as attributes (vacuums, partly):** EU 666/2013 sets limits for mains dry vacuum cleaners from 1 September 2017: annual energy under 43 kWh, input power under 900 W, dust pick-up on carpet at least 0.75 and on hard floor at least 0.98, dust re-emission at most 1 %; sound power and motor lifetime are declared [S12]. Battery, robot, wet and central cleaners are outside it [S12]. The limits and the scope exclusions are legal classes of the EU and go in the jurisdiction ledger (C17), not on the tree. HEPA classes H12 to H14 (99.5 to 99.995 % separation) are the allergy ladder at idealo [S2], an attribute with the class named. Declared suction (Pa) is stored only as a non-comparable attribute [S14]. Consumer Reports scores only within a type [S15].
- **Tier entries (robot vacuum models):** Vacuum Wars price tiers (ultra-budget under $300, budget $300 to $600, low-mid $600 to $1,000, high-mid $1,000 to $1,300, premium $1,300 to $1,600 and over) [S14] and RTINGS tiers (best, upper mid-range, mid-range, budget, cheap) [S16] are entered as `scheme` values on named forms, each with its source and date. Two US test sites are one region; the Chinese guide [S31] ranks by budget band and base-station features but names no scheme, so a second-region scheme is missing (C4(e)). Treat these as test-site consensus, not a standard. A Consumer Reports Overall Score is a measured score entered the same way, valid only within a type [S15].
- **Dock level** (1 charging, 2 auto-empty, 3 mop wash and dry) is a feature ladder named by Vacuum Wars [S14]; it is stored as an attribute with that scheme named, not as a tier.
- **Preference:** mops, brooms, cloths, chemical cleaners, bins and laundry products. There is no grade; the only order is maker and price band.

**Price band** is the domain-wide USD scheme (C4(d)): under 50, 50 to 150, 150 to 500, 500 to 1,500, 1,500 to 5,000, 5,000 to 15,000, over 15,000, derived from a dated, sourced list price. The Vacuum Wars price tiers above keep their own source scheme and are not converted into price bands.

**As a modifier, never a level:** the tier entries and spec attributes sit beside the tree. The ordering is valid only within a kind (Consumer Reports and Vacuum Wars both say so) [S14, S15]. No tier is stored for a bin: the only bin source is one guide for kitchen bins and it grades nothing [S29].

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger in the shared C1 schema.**

The known-for named forms are derived from the named-form table, not stored as a column.
- *Scope.* OWNER: appliance makers only (vacuum, robot, washer, dryer, iron), or also the major consumable houses (review Q-X6). The memo keeps both, as drafted. Named forms carry the maker; kinds do not.
- *Compatibility.* The C13 ledger starts with the vacuum-part platforms (bag, filter, belt and battery fits). Its rows need fetched fit tables †.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds:** canonical form first, then the secondary key the leaf states (C7). **Named forms:** by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested. **Variants:** none in B6.
- Vacuum kinds, secondary key scale or autonomy, as Consumer Reports orders them: large (canister, upright), then small (stick, handheld, robotic) [S15]; wet-and-dry, central and backpack follow.
- Washers: front-load, top-load, twin-tub, then washer-dryer. Dryers, secondary key installation: vented tumble, ventless tumble, spin, bathroom unit, following Wikipedia's order from vented to ventless [S10, S34].
- Named forms within a maker follow the maker's own generation sequence where years are attested (Dyson V8, V10, V11, V12, V15; Hoover Constellation after Model O †) [S2, S9]; where no year is attested, alphabetical.
- Detergents go by physical form (powder, liquid, capsule), other leaves by product class, and mops by head form, because that is the retailer's line order [S22, S33].

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A):** category B6 > sub-family (what is cleaned or cared for: floors; hard surfaces and windows; textiles; dishes; waste) > leaf (product type) > kind (form or mechanism, stated per leaf) > named form (model line or iconic design). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels**, as the template allows. Attributes (power source, bag or bagless, capacity, run time, HEPA class, energy class, spin class, noise, dock level, mop system, bio or non-bio, scent), tags (region, market, user such as pet or allergy, scene, era, price band) and a maker ledger sit beside the tree.

**Reasons:** it follows the verb in the framework test (the home and its surfaces, textiles, waste); it matches the task-first trees of wilko, Home Bargains, dm and Askul [S19–S22] and keeps the washer, dryer, iron, airer and detergent together; it stops each kind at the level where Consumer Reports, Kakaku, Currys and Lowe's compare [S1, S5, S15, S17]; and it moves power, bag type, user and market to attributes, as Appendix A requires.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. Approve "what is cleaned or cared for" as the sub-family axis and the five sub-families, or choose Candidate B or C.
2. **Technology as a kind axis.** Appendix A lists "power source and technology" as a modifier, while the stage-2 instruction omits it from the forbidden list. Recommended: power source (cordless, corded) and bag type stay attributes; a mechanism that changes the object's form and what buyers compare stays a kind (robot, wet-and-dry, heat pump dryer, impeller washer). The alternative is to make heat pump, condenser, agitator and impeller attributes of one "tumble dryer" and one "top-load washer".
3. **Robot vacuum:** one kind inside vacuum cleaners (recommended, as Currys and Lowe's do [S1, S5]) or a leaf of its own (Kakaku [S4]).
4. **Stick and handheld:** two kinds (Currys, Lowe's, recommended) or one (Home Depot, Chinese guide) [S1, S5, S8, S32].
5. **Washer-dryer:** a kind of washing machine (recommended) or a separate leaf (EU label, China) [S25, S30].
6. **Parts and accessories** (vacuum bags, filters, belts, batteries, nozzles): confirm they stay in B6 as parts under S3, with a `fits` field, and decide whether they get kinds or only a parts leaf.
7. **Consumables:** confirm cleaning and laundry products as kinds (leaf by product class, kind by physical form) and brands as ledger rows, not forms. The framework already puts cleaning products in B6.
8. Boundary rulings in section 9, especially: shoe care (B6 or A2), dish racks (B5 or B1), moisture absorbers (B7), bathroom dryer unit and heated airer (B6), fabric freshener against room spray (B6 against B4), household brush against trade brush (B6 against C1), textile dye (C4).
9. **Antique and hand-wash tools** (washboard, wash copper, carpet beater): include as kinds under a hand-wash leaf, or leave to a later pass.
10. **Scope of the maker ledger:** appliance makers only, or major consumable houses too.

**Framework conflicts to log (not applied here):**
- The B6 branch names laundry baskets, but S3 and the B1 branch file baskets and boxes under B1; the order of branches means B6 wins, but the S3 "never parts" sentence should say so.
- Appendix A lists power source and technology as modifiers; the kind rules for robot, heat pump and impeller (decision 2) need a rule.
- Washing-up products and dishwasher cleaners are B6 while the dishwasher is B5; one precedence sentence would prevent drift.

- **No collector or resale source** (vintage vacuums, antique laundry tools) was fetched; section 7 treats hand-wash and historic tools only from Wikipedia's lists [S10, S11].
