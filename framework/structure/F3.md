## 0. Category

**Code and name:** F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards (domain F, Culture, Records & Meaning).

**Membership test (placement key step 15):** "Money, postage or an award in physical form: coins, banknotes and bullion (in circulation or not), postage stamps and postal stationery, medals and decorations, trophies, cups, shields and award plaques, regalia and insignia of office (maces, chains of office, gavels), tokens and scrip, non-game trading cards."

**Notes and rules in the framework that touch F3 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):**
- Step 15 notes: "paper certificates and diplomas are F5. Casino and poker chips are E1. Collectible figures, dolls and model vehicles are E1. A commemorative plate is a plate."
- Step 25 (E1): "board, card, dice and tile games (including trading card games)", "casino and poker chips" and "tabletop and coin-operated games" are E1. So a card made to be played in a game system is E1 and only a non-game trading card is F3.
- Step 19 (F5): "a document issued to prove identity, status, ownership or achievement (... certificate, deed, diploma, share certificate, identity and access badges); a card, pass or token issued to give access to an account, service, place or stored value (payment, gift, transit, membership and key cards); ... picture postcards used or not, autographs, kept tickets and programmes".
- Step 20 (F2): artist trading cards are F2; step 17 note on A3: "badges and patches worn as ornament" are A3 and "identity and access badges are F5"; step 14 note: "anything worn as an ornament, including amulets worn as pendants, rings or bracelets, is A3".
- Key order matters: step 14 (F4), then step 15 (F3), then step 17 (worn: A-categories), step 19 (F5), step 25 (E1). A worn medal therefore reaches F3 before A3.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): cases, covers and stands made to hold or protect **one** object follow it; storage that holds several objects is B1.
- S5 (test the object, not the motive): a signed football is a football; a commemorative plate is a plate.
- Appendix A: region, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, market, occasion, motive (collecting, investment, memorabilia), material, condition and edition (limited, signed) never split an inner level.
- BRIEF.md: individual unique items are out (a particular card is not a kind); "money as collectible objects (coins, banknotes)" is in; cryptocurrency holdings are out and NFTs are I2 (not F3); a kept ticket stub is a keepsake paper (F5).

**Precedence sentences that already exist for F3 (restated, not new):** (1) A paper certificate or diploma is F5, not F3. (2) Casino and poker chips are E1, not F3 tokens. (3) Collectible figures, dolls and model vehicles are E1, even when sold in card-style "collectible series". (4) Trading card games are E1; only a trading card that is not part of a game system is F3. (5) A commemorative plate is a plate (B4 or B5), not a medal or plaque. (6) Pins, badges and patches worn as ornament are A3; identity and access badges are F5.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

F3 is not one hobby. The sources show five fields that rarely share a shop: **numismatics** (coins, notes, tokens, medals, with bullion next door), **philately**, **orders, medals and decorations** (called "militaria" or "phaleristics" by dealers), **awards** (trophies and plaques) and **card collecting**. Regalia of office has no market of its own.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities and societies

- **Numista (S1).** The community catalogue (548,572 types and 978,373 varieties on the fetched page) has the most developed generic tree found, which this memo uses as a backbone. Level 1: Coins, Tokens, Medals, Banknotes, Paper exonumia. Coins are split by **issue status** (standard circulation, circulating commemorative, non-circulating, collector, emergency coinage with siege, official necessity and merchant-token children, local, patterns, contemporary counterfeits, proto-coins). Tokens are split by **purpose** and go four deep (Tokens > Service tokens > Transportation tokens > Public transportation tokens), with Bullion (Rounds, Bars) filed under Tokens. Medals are split by purpose (award, commemorative, art, membership, religious, souvenir, collection, advertising, replicas, fantasy). Banknotes are split by status (standard circulation, circulating commemorative, non-circulating, local, emergency, non-issued, patterns and trials, contemporary counterfeits, ATM test notes). Paper exonumia holds banknote-like items (replicas, fantasy, souvenir, demonstration, play money, movie money), vouchers, postal orders, travelers cheques, coupons. The same page puts **issuer, currency, denomination, year, composition, shape, technique, size, weight, mint, series, tag** (theme tags such as Animals, Sports, Olympic Games) as **filters**, not tree levels. My count of the fetched tree is about 155 nodes: roughly 11 under Coins, 74 under Tokens, 35 under Medals, 10 under Banknotes and 26 under Paper exonumia.
- **IBNS (S24).** The banknote society's guide says banknotes are "catalogued by types and varieties, and these are the way in which banknotes are sold and collected"; collectors choose by theme (butterflies, bridges), by vanished countries, by occupation notes; catalogues exist for a country, for old issues, or for a theme "such as banknote errors or replacement notes"; "other paper instruments" are listed apart from banknotes; essays, trials, proofs and specimens have a section of their own in the guide's contents (search excerpt only).
- **US Coin Shows exonumia guide (S36).** Its eight parts are Civil War tokens, trade tokens and good-for pieces, US Mint medals and Congressional Gold Medals, challenge coins, elongated coins and wooden nickels, transit and tax tokens, plus buying at shows. This is the collector's cut of exonumia, by **issue class**, not by metal.
- **The Stamp Forum (S35).** Collectors choose among Scott (worldwide, 6 volumes), Michel, Yvert and Gibbons catalogues by what they collect; Gibbons is described as a ladder from simplified "Stamps of the World" to Concise to Commonwealth to Regional volumes; Colnect is named as the identification tool. So the unit collectors use is the **catalogue number**, and the catalogue is chosen by **country or region**.
- **Philately reference (S27, S28).** Wikipedia lists the material: stamps, covers, postal stationery (stamped envelopes, postal cards, letter sheets, aerogrammes, wrappers), revenue stamps, cinderellas (labels that look like stamps), maximum cards, sheets and miniature sheets; and the collecting approaches: traditional, thematic, postal history, aerophilately, astrophilately, maximaphily. It names Scott, Stanley Gibbons, Yvert et Tellier, Zumstein and Michel as worldwide catalogues and Facit, Fischer, Sassone and Sakura among national ones.
- **Trading card reference (S26).** Wikipedia gives three types: sports cards (by sport), non-sports or entertainment cards (comic book, historic events, humor, merchandise and toys, music, movie and television, nature and animals), and collectible card games.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market (grading and price guides)

- **Coin grading (S25, S15, S6).** The Sheldon scale runs 1 (Poor) to 70; 1-58 are circulated grades, 60-70 are Mint State, and proofs are marked PR or PF; it was devised in 1949 for large cents and adapted by the ANA. German and Japanese dealers show PCGS and NGC grades in titles and shelves (S15, S6).
- **Banknote grading (S23, S24).** PMG uses a 70-point scale derived from the Sheldon scale, with EPQ (original paper), Star (eye appeal) and NET (problem note) designations; IBNS publishes its own grade standards (Good to Uncirculated), says "the grade of an item is determined by its greatest defect", and gives separate wording for polymer notes.
- **Card grading (S21, S22).** PSA grades 1-10 with PSA 10 Gem Mint (centering to about 55/45 front), qualifiers such as MK for marks, refuses trimmed or recoloured cards, and also grades tickets, packs, comic books and magazines, and video games on separate tabs; its page says pins and coins are graded without half points. Beckett presents card and memorabilia grading.
- **Medal precedence (S20).** The British "order of wear" is the collector's and dealer's ordering: gallantry and distinguished-service decorations, then campaign medals, then coronation and jubilee medals, then long service and good conduct, efficiency awards, then foreign awards; the page says "precedence outranks chronology" and that unofficial commemoratives are outside it.
- **Stamp condition.** No fetched page gave a stamp condition scale (mint, hinged, used, centering). This is a gap; section 5 treats stamp condition as unsourced.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Harmonized System chapter 97 (S32, S33).** Heading 9704 is "postage or revenue stamps, stamp-postmarks, first-day covers, postal stationery (stamped paper) and the like, used or unused". Heading 9705 is collections and collectors' pieces of archaeological, ethnographic, historical, zoological, botanical, mineralogical, anatomical, paleontological or numismatic interest; its numismatic branch is split by age (over 100 years or not). Heading 9706 is antiques over 100 years. Australia's additional notes say legal-tender coins are not 9705, and that commercially produced commemoratives are not collectors' pieces unless age or rarity later makes them so (S33). The trade order therefore places **stamps and postal items** (9704) apart from **collectors' coins** (9705); new legal-tender coins fall outside 9705. Medals, tokens, banknotes and bullion have their own headings elsewhere in the HS (for example 4907 for unissued notes is named in the 9704 text), which were not fetched.
- **Google product taxonomy (S31).** Under Arts & Entertainment > Hobbies & Creative Arts > Collectibles: Collectible Coins & Currency (Collectible Banknotes, Collectible Coins), Collectible Trading Cards, Postage Stamps, Seal Stamps, Sports Collectibles (autographed paraphernalia, fan accessories). Under Party & Celebration > **Trophies & Awards**: Award Certificates, Award Pins & Medals, Award Plaques, Award Ribbons, Trophies. In the lines returned for the Collectibles and Trophies branches there is no node for tokens, scrip, orders and decorations, bullion or regalia.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **British Museum (S34).** The Department of Money and Medals holds coins, coin weights, tokens, dies, "the national collection of paper money, ranging from 14th-century Chinese banknotes to the euro", "commemorative and art medals from the Italian Renaissance", and "political and promotional badges". The museum renamed the department in 2024 from Coins and Medals to Money and Medals to cover paper money (search excerpt only for the renaming date). So the museum groups **money, tokens and medals**, not stamps.
- **UK honours (S29).** Wikipedia divides British awards into **honours** (usually orders of chivalry or merit: Garter, Bath, Order of Merit, Royal Victorian Order, British Empire), **decorations** (specific deeds: Victoria Cross, George Cross, Military Cross, Distinguished Flying Cross) and **medals** (service in an operation, long service, good conduct: Imperial Service Medal, British Empire Medal). This is the standard cut for orders and decorations.
- **Ceremonial regalia (S30).** The mace article treats maces as parliamentary, civic, academic, judicial and ecclesiastical (the last "more correctly described as staves"), with swords and sceptres as companion regalia; it does not mention chains of office.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects

**Agree.**
- Stamps and postal items are always a separate field from coins and notes (S27, S32, S31, S7, S9).
- Coins, banknotes, tokens and medals are one family in the specialist tools (Numista S1, British Museum S34, eBay S2, Numismatic News S3, MA-Shops S15, Rakuten S6).
- Card shops separate trading card games from the rest (S11, S26) and sell accessories separately (S11; S12 by search excerpt).
- Grading is a numeric scale derived from or modelled on a published standard: Sheldon 1-70 for coins (S25) and notes (S23), a 1-10 PSA scale for cards (S21).
- The unit of sale and study is the **type** in a catalogue, not the single specimen: Numista types (S1), Scott/Michel/Gibbons numbers (S28, S35, S16 uses Michel "MiNr"), Pick for notes (S24).

**Split the same objects differently.**
- **Bullion.** Numista files bars and rounds under Tokens (S1); eBay and Numismatic News make Bullion a top-level group by metal (S2, S3); APMEX and BullionByPost make it a shop organised by metal, then bar or coin, then mint (S4, S5); Rakuten puts "precious metals and ingots" beside medals (S6); Daikichi files gold coins and ingots under gold (S8). Bullion coins (Sovereign, Britannia, Krugerrand: S5) are shelved with bars, not with collector coins.
- **Medals.** Numista files medals as a sibling of coins by purpose (S1); eBay and Numismatic News file medals inside Exonumia with tokens (S2, S3); UK dealers file campaign medals as military collectibles (S13); Rakuten files "medals" and "orders and insignia" as two separate shelves (S6); awards shops sell medals with trophies (S17, S18).
- **Region and era versus type.** US marketplaces and Rakuten open coins by region or era (S2, S3, S6); Numista opens by issue status and keeps region as a filter (S1).
- **Sport or subject versus form.** Card shops open on sport and league (S11, S12); Wikipedia opens on three card types (S26).
- **Format versus issue class in stamps.** The Chinese dealer shelves sheets, small sheets, souvenir sheets and booklets apart from the issue-class shelves (S9); Japan Post shelves booklets and special stamps separately from definitives (S7); Wikipedia lists sheets and miniature sheets with stamps (S27).
- **Collectors' coins versus legal-tender coins.** HS 9705 and Australian notes exclude current legal-tender coins from "collectors' pieces" (S33), while Numista and every dealer treat circulating and collector coins as one family (S1).
- **Cards in the grading universe.** PSA and Beckett grade sports, non-sport and trading-card-game cards together (S21, S22; Beckett's three groups are search excerpt only), while the framework sends trading card games to E1 (step 25).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

There is no single first axis. Evidence by field:

- **Coins and notes.** Retail navigation is by **region or era** (eBay: US, Canada, Ancient, Medieval, World: S2; Numismatic News: by denomination then series, and by country: S3; Rakuten: by Japanese era and by region: S6) and, for bullion, by **metal** (S2, S3, S4, S5). The specialist catalogue instead opens with **what kind of issue it is** (circulation, commemorative, collector, emergency, pattern, counterfeit) and holds issuer and year as filters (S1). Both are real; region, era and metal are Appendix A modifiers, so only the issue-status cut can be a level.
- **Stamps.** Collectors and catalogues choose by **issuing country** first (Scott, Michel, Yvert, Gibbons regionals: S35, S28), then by the issue (a catalogue number) and by **theme** (S27). Postal shops and Chinese dealers shelve by **issue class** (definitive, commemorative, special) and **format** (sheet, miniature sheet, booklet) (S7, S9, S10). Country and theme are modifiers; issue class and postal item type (stamp, stationery, cover, revenue label) are generic.
- **Medals and decorations.** Dealers open with **purpose** (campaign, long service, gallantry: S13, S20, S29) or with **period and regime** (S14). Purpose is generic; period and regime are modifiers.
- **Trophies.** Retailers open with **form** (cups, shields, plaques, crystal, medals: S17, S18) and offer sport as a second route (S17).
- **Trading cards.** Retailers open with **sport or league** (S11, S12), Wikipedia with **three card families** (S26), graders with **sports, gaming, non-sports** (Beckett, search excerpt only). Sport is a content subject: the framework rule that content genre never divides media, games, software catalogues or subscriptions argues for treating it as a tag or generated view. The generic, non-subject cuts in the evidence are **product form** (single card, pack, box, set) and **card build** (base, insert, autograph).
- **Grade.** Across coins, notes, cards and bullion, buyers' second question is always **grade or condition** (S21, S23, S25, S15), which is a condition modifier of one individual item. Under C16 no grade value is stored; the grading scales are recorded once in a scale registry (section 5).

**Questions buyers ask** (from the structure of the shops): which issuer and year (S1, S3), what grade and who graded it (S15, S6), which mint or refiner (S4, S5), which catalogue number (S16, S35), which sport and brand (S12). None of these is a kind.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates obey the framework: modifiers (region, era, price or tier, maker, scene, condition, edition) never split an inner level, and a kind is a generic type a person can own. They differ in what the first cut is.

### Candidate A — Issuing function, then standing or form of the issue (recommended)

**Levels and axes** (the axis is stated once per level; leaf and kind axes are named per sub-family because the sources cut each family on its own natural axis, as the BRIEF allows):

| Level | Axis | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family (9, plus a parts sub-family) | what the object was issued as: coin, paper money, bullion, token or scrip, postage and postal item, honour or medal, award, card, insignia of office | The five fields in section 1 and the Numista level 1 (S1), HS 9704 versus 9705 (S32), Google nodes (S31). |
| Leaf | the **standing or form of the issue** within the family: for coins, notes and tokens, whether the issue is official and circulating, official and non-circulating, local or emergency, or a copy (S1); for postage, the postal item type (stamp, stamp unit, stationery, cover, fiscal label: S27, S32); for medals, the purpose class (honour, service, commemorative, award, membership: S1, S29, S13); for awards, the form (S17, S18); for cards, the product form (single, sealed product, set: S11); for parts, the object served | as shown |
| Kind | the generic piece or class a person owns inside the leaf (for example standard circulation coin, proof set, stamped envelope, campaign medal, cup) | as shown |
| Named form | the **series, issue or iconic design** with its own name and its own grade market: Morgan dollar, Machin definitives, Victoria Cross (a card product line such as Topps Flagship is a line row, not a named form) | S3 series with date ranges; S35 Machin; S29 named awards |

Bullion, trophies and regalia store `leaf = null` (C5(b): about 12 kinds or fewer, and a leaf would repeat the kind); coins, banknotes, tokens, postage, medals, cards and parts keep a leaf. **Maximum depth below the category: four** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). The parts sub-family F3.10 closes the category (C5(d)) and is the only one exempt from the level-1 axis.

**Everything else is a tag, attribute or ledger field**: issuer or country, year, denomination and currency, metal and fineness, weight, grading scale (in a registry; no grade value is stored, C16), catalogue number, theme, sport or franchise, edition, mint or manufacturer, issue class, postal service, finish, unit, product form, material.

### Candidate B — Collecting discipline first

Four top groups named after the communities: **numismatics** (coins, notes, tokens, medals, bullion), **philately**, **honours and awards** (orders, decorations, trophies, regalia), **card collecting**. This follows the specialist societies and tools (IBNS S24, Numista S1, British Museum S34, The Stamp Forum S35) and the HS split (S32). Below it the same leaves and kinds as Candidate A. It differs from A in grouping 9 sub-families into 4 parents and in putting **medals with money** (as Numista S1, British Museum S34 and Baldwin's, which sells "medals" beside coins, S13 do) rather than with honours. It adds a level or removes the sub-family level, and it gives no home to trophies (no discipline) or tokens that are also awards.

### Candidate C — Region and era first, denomination as kind (the US marketplace pattern)

eBay and Numismatic News open coins with US, Canada, Ancient, Medieval, World (S2) and by country (S3); Rakuten opens Japanese coins by era (S6); Numismatic News then lists US kinds by denomination and series (Dimes, Quarters, Half Cents: S3). This would make "dime", "tetradrachm" or "yen coin" kinds. It breaks Appendix A (region and era become levels), and it makes each denomination a country-bound kind: Numista's own filters keep **issuer, currency and denomination apart from the tree** (S1), which is the evidence for keeping them as attributes. Region and era would be generated views.

### Where each candidate breaks (three or four edge objects each)

- **A.** (1) **Panda 1 oz gold coin** (S9 files it as Gold and silver coins, panda and zodiac coins; bullion dealers shelve Sovereign, Britannia and Krugerrand with bars, S5): legal tender struck in both a bullion and a collector finish, so needs the finish-and-packaging rule in section 9. (2) **Sports finisher's medal** (S17 and S18 sell medals beside trophies; Numista files "sport awards" under medals, S1): one home needed. (3) **Challenge coin** (Numista: membership medal: S1; US Coin Shows treats it as exonumia: S36): coin-shaped but a medal. (4) **Zero-euro souvenir banknote** (S1 paper exonumia): looks like a note, is not legal tender.
- **B.** (1) **Bullion**: Numista files it under tokens (S1), metal dealers shelve it as its own shop (S4, S5), so no discipline owns it. (2) **Orders and decorations**: numismatic dealers (S13) and militaria dealers (S14) both sell them. (3) **Trophies**: no discipline. (4) **Cards graded alongside coins** (PSA grades coins and pins: S21): the grader crosses disciplines.
- **C.** (1) **Euro coins** issued by 20 states share one currency and one design series. (2) **Ancient coins of one empire struck in many cities** (S3 lists Roman Imperial and Roman Provincial separately). (3) **Zodiac coins, stamps and notes** (S9) belong together by theme, not by region or era. (4) A **dime** struck in 1900 and a **dime** struck in 2020 are one kind under C but two quite different markets.

### Recommended worked example under Candidate A (60 kinds after the conformance pass)

Sub-families are numbered F3.1 to F3.10. Named forms carry `maker_bound` (C2): `yes` when the design belongs to one issuer or maker (Morgan dollar, Victoria Cross, Machin definitives, the RMB series), `no` when many issuers or makers strike the same type of design (Civil War token, Hard Times token, elongated coin). ## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: consensus-ranked** for coins, banknotes and cards (published grading scales run by standard-setters and grading companies); **spec-ranked** for bullion (fineness and weight); **preference** for trophies, tokens, regalia and parts; **unsourced** for stamps (no fetched page gives a stamp condition scale †). A grade describes one particular item, so it is never stored on a kind, named form or variant (C16); the taxonomy records the scales only.

- **Coins — consensus-ranked.** The Sheldon scale runs from 1 (Poor) to 70; 1-58 are circulated grades, 60-70 are Mint State, proofs carry PR or PF, and the Mint State adjectives (Select, Choice, Gem, Superb Gem, Perfect) are "commonly used but unofficial" (S25). PCGS and NGC grades appear in dealer titles and shelves (S15, S6). PSA says pins and coins are graded without half points and that one uniform standard is hard to apply to them (S21).
- **Banknotes — consensus-ranked.** PMG uses a 70-point scale derived from the Sheldon scale, with adjectival bands (Gem Uncirculated 65 to 70 down to Good 4), the EPQ designation for original paper (required from 65), the Star designation for eye appeal and NET for problem notes (S23). IBNS has its own grade standards (the fetched extract shows the lowest grades, Fair and Good), applies "the grade is determined by the greatest defect", and gives a separate wording for polymer notes (S24).
- **Cards — consensus-ranked.** PSA 10 Gem Mint is the top of a 1-10 scale with centering tolerance about 55/45 front and 75/25 back, qualifiers such as MK (marks), and a refusal to grade altered cards (S21). Beckett grades four subgrades (centering, corners, edges, surface) into an overall grade, per a search excerpt only (S22 shows no table on the fetched page) †.
- **Bullion — spec-ranked.** The buyer's tests are metal, fineness and weight (S4, S5), which are attributes with their standard named (C4(b)); the standard that defines fineness is not in a fetched page †. The **refiner or mint** is a maker row (S4, S5). Refiner accreditation is the C1 extension `certifications`, not a tier, and the BullionByPost LBMA statement is a search excerpt only †. A search excerpt of BullionByPost shows "Grade B" silver coins and "pre-owned" gold bars: a seller's label, which would be a dated platform tag (C4(c)) once fetched †. The fetched extract names the refiners Metalor and Umicore (S5).
- **Medals and decorations — ranked by precedence, preference by condition.** The British order of wear is an official rank of awards (S20); it ranks the award, not the specimen, and it is one country's scheme, so it is a generated view and not a tier entry (C4(e) asks for a second region). The fetched pages give no condition scale for medals. An auction catalogue in a search excerpt says condition uses "conventional numismatic terms" (search excerpt only) †.
- **Stamps — unsourced.** Catalogue prices are given for unused and used stamps (Stanley Gibbons catalogue, a Wikipedia search excerpt; not fetched), and the philately page lists "paper, printing, gum, perforation" as the things studied (S27). No fetched page gave the mint-never-hinged, hinged and used ladder or centering grades †.
- **Trophies, tokens, regalia, parts — preference.** Retailers sell by size, material and price (S17, S18).

**How a tier appears.** Never as a level and never on a kind (C4(a)). (1) Each grading scale is recorded once in a **grading-scale registry** (proposed in the domain F additions to C16; OWNER: question 14): `scale_id`, name, owner, `scale_type` (condition grade; measured spec under C4(b); platform label under C4(c)), `steps`, `kinds_served`, `sources`. First rows: Sheldon 70 (S25), PMG 70 (S23), PSA 1-10 (S21), IBNS grade standards (S24), Beckett (S22, search excerpt †). Kinds link to the scales that apply; no grade value, grader certificate or holding record is stored, because individual items are out (BRIEF, C16). (2) For bullion, `fineness` and `weight` are attributes with their standard named. (3) A tier entry (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) sits only on a maker, a line or a named form, and enters only with a fetched source, and a second from another region where available (C4(e)); none is sourced for F3 yet. (4) Price band: the domain-wide USD bands of C4(d), derived from a dated, sourced list price and attached to maker and line rows only; none is sourced yet †, and a price that follows the metal price (bullion) has no stable list price, so its band stays empty until a source gives a rule †. No fetched source gives a conversion between the 70-point scale and PSA's 10-point scale, so the scales are never converted.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity, with the maker split into issuer and manufacturer.**

- **Issuer as identity.** Numista keeps separate catalogue pages for **issuers, mints, printers, engravers and designers, ruling authorities and issuing entities** (S1), and filters by mint and printer; APMEX and BullionByPost shelve bullion by **mint or refiner** (S4, S5); the US Mint, Royal Canadian Mint, Perth Mint and Royal Mint have their own APMEX shelves (S4). For stamps and notes the issuing country or administration is the first thing collectors ask (S35, S28), but that is a region modifier.
- **Manufacturer as identity (cards).** The Japanese shop names product by manufacturer and line (EPOCH, BBM: S11); the US shop filters by brand (Topps, Panini, Upper Deck, search excerpt of S12 †). The card manufacturer is part of what is bought.
- **Publisher of the numbering system as a platform for identification.** Scott, Michel, Yvert, Gibbons, Pick and Numista numbers are how types are cited and sold (S28, S35, S24, S1, S16 "MiNr"). They are identifiers, not makers. A search excerpt (Grokipedia) says Scott protects its numbering by copyright and licenses limited use; this was not fetched and the rights position must be checked before numbers are stored †.
- **Incidental.** Trophy makers, civic regalia makers and token makers are examples only (S17, S18). Medallists and die-sinkers matter for art medals (S34) but no fetched page ranks them.

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger on the shared C1 schema, plus a separate issuer table.**
- *Core columns (C1):* `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class`; `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `known_for_kinds` (kind ids); `scene_tags` (empty until each tag has a definition source, C8 †); `price_band` (C4(d)); `sources` (at least one fetched URL). This replaces the earlier fields `role`, `tier` and `era`: the role becomes `maker_class`, a tier never sits on a maker as a column (C4(a); tier entries are a separate table), and era is a tag on the named form.
- *`maker_class` for F3 (multi-valued, because a mint can also be a refiner):* `mint`, `refiner`, `banknote and security printer`, `medallist or engraver`, `card manufacturer`, `awards maker`, `certifier or grader` (PCGS, NGC, PMG, PSA, Beckett: S15, S6, S21, S22, S23). These values are proposed in the domain F additions to C1 and await approval. OWNER: question 12.
- *Extensions:* `authority_id` (Numista, VIAF or ISNI where they exist †); `certifications` (refiner accreditation such as the LBMA Good Delivery list †: the only fetched mention is a search excerpt, and it is not a tier).
- *Issuer table, shared with F5 (not makers):* `issuer_id`, name, country, type (state, central bank, postal administration, transit authority), years, sources. Named forms that are issuer-bound point to an issuer row; a mint, printer or manufacturer that made them points to a maker row. The issuer role links to the Appendix A region modifier.
- *Lines table (C1; review C's K4):* a maker's line that spans kinds is a line row (`line_id`, `maker_id`, name, kinds spanned, years, `tier` entries, sources). Card product lines (EPOCH and BBM: S11; Topps Flagship and Panini Select: S12 †) are line rows, not named forms. A line or design inside one kind (a bullion coin design, the Victoria Cross) is a named form, and the Morgan dollar is issuer-bound.
- *Not columns:* known-for named forms are derived from the named-form ledger, not stored in a column (C1). Grades, serial numbers and certificates are not stored (C16). Catalogue numbers are an identifier on the named form, not a `variant_code` (C3), and are held until the licensing check †.
- *Alias rows (C9):* arcade token (S1) → E1; telephone card (S3 exonumia) → F5; good-for piece = trade token (S36); scrip (S3); 特殊切手 = special stamp (S7) †; Ganzsache = postal stationery (S16); MiNr = Michel number (S16); Notgeld † (no fetched page in this batch). Script forms need a fetched source for the term itself †.
- Each maker named in a row needs its own fetched source.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Axis: canonical form first (C7), then the secondary key each sub-family states; named forms by issuer or maker, then first issue year; medals by a generic purpose ladder.**

- **Kinds inside a leaf** follow the order the standard tools already use: Numista lists coins from standard circulation to counterfeits and banknotes from standard circulation to ATM test notes (S1), so the ladder from **ordinary issue to special issue to copy** is the canonical order. Postage runs from the definitive (the everyday stamp) to the commemorative or special-issue stamp, then units, stationery, covers and fiscal labels (S7, S9, S27).
- **Named forms inside a kind** go by issuer (issuer table name) or maker (maker ledger `name`), then by **first issue year** as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested (C7). Within one issuer this gives the date-ranged series of the US list (Barber 1892-1916, Mercury 1916-1945, Roosevelt 1946-present: S3) and the sequence of Chinese issue codes (S9, S10). Catalogue numbers are the identifier that dealers and collectors cite (Michel "MiNr": S16; the four worldwide catalogues: S28) and are not an order key. That Scott numbers run roughly in order of issue is from a search excerpt only †.
- **Medals and decorations** run by a **generic purpose ladder**: honour (order), gallantry decoration, campaign, long service and good conduct, commemorative, art and souvenir, award, membership. The ladder is the British order of wear (S20) and the British honours split (S29) with the national names removed. Each country's own order of wear (S20: gallantry and distinguished-service decorations, campaign medals, coronation and jubilee, long service and good conduct, efficiency, foreign awards; "precedence outranks chronology") is a **generated view**, not the tree order.
- **Trading cards** have kinds in product-form order (base card, insert or autograph card, product-issued card, sealed card product). Card product lines are line rows ordered by maker, then first year (the Japanese shop's year shelves, such as "2026 professional baseball cards", are from a search excerpt of S11 †).
- **Parts** run by the object served (coin and note, stamp, medal, card), then by part type.
- **Reason:** collectors and cataloguers locate a named form by issuer, series and date, and a medal by its purpose class; the national order of wear answers a different question and is kept as a view.

## 9. Boundaries

The closest neighbours are A3 (worn items), E1 (games), F5 (documents and cards), F2 (art), and F4 (religious objects). Smaller edges are B4, B1, E4, E2, F6 and I2. The precedence sentences below test the object (S5), not the motive or the market. Sentences marked "proposed" come from the domain F review and the shared conventions, and await owner approval (questions in section 10).

- **A3 Jewellery — badges, pins, rings and worn medals.**
  *Precedence: a medal, order or decoration made as an award or commemoration is F3 whether it is worn, mounted on a bar or kept in a case; a pin, badge or patch worn as ornament and not issued as an award is A3; an identity or access badge is F5. A medal made for devotion with a chain or bail to be worn is A3 (see F4 below). A crown that is regalia of an office is F3; a crown or tiara worn as an ornament is A3; a toy crown is E1 (proposed, review F3-8).*
  OWNER: a coin or medal fitted with a bail or mount to be worn as jewellery, which review A files in A3 by its form while step 15 comes before step 17 (question 24). OWNER: championship rings (Crown sells Rings under awards: S17; step 15 does not name rings, so a ring reaches A3 at step 17 as the key is written) (question 18).
  Closest objects: championship ring, club or lapel pin (S17 "Pins"; S31 "Award Pins & Medals"), medal bar (a part: F3.10; S14), challenge coin carried on the person (F3.6; S1, S36).
- **E1 Toys & Games — card games, chips, game tokens, play money.**
  *Precedence: a card made to be played in a game system is E1 even when graded, signed or never played; a non-game sports or entertainment card is F3. Casino and poker chips, and a token made to be used up in one coin-operated game or machine, are E1 (that game's consumable, S3); a token issued to stand for money or a service (transit, toll, deposit, trade, wage, tax) is F3.*
  OWNER: play, prop and souvenir money (S1 files play money and movie money under paper exonumia; step 25 sends a game's own play money to E1; the proposed split is game money E1 and prop or souvenir notes F3) (question 20).
  Closest objects: Pokémon and Magic cards (S11 separates trading card games; S19 invites valuations of Pokémon and Magic cards beside stamps, coins and medals; PSA's services page says it grades "vintage, modern or TCG" cards: search excerpt only †), character cards and stickers (S11), arcade and game-machine tokens (S1 files them as tokens; step 25 names coin-operated games), play money (S1).
- **F5 Documents & Personal Records — cards, certificates, postcards, covers, ephemera.**
  *Precedence (proposed, review X2 and F5-5): payment, gift, telephone, transit, membership and key cards are F5 whether live, used or expired. A token of metal, plastic or other material issued to stand for money or a service is F3. Paper issued to circulate as money (scrip, emergency money, Notgeld) is F3; a voucher or gift certificate redeemable only with its issuer is F5. Postal stationery with a printed stamp, and a cover issued by a postal administration or prepared as a first-day cover, are F3; any other used envelope, letter or picture postcard is F5, whatever stamps it carries. A share certificate or bond, an award certificate and a diploma are F5. An advertising or trade card issued by a business is F5; a card issued as part of a numbered series by a card manufacturer is F3.*
  OWNER: whether any cover that has passed through the post is F3 (question 17). The earlier wording of this memo ("collected for itself", "kept for its stamps and postal markings") tested motive and is withdrawn.
  Closest objects: maximum card (S27, S9), stock and bond certificates (S2 "Stocks & Bonds, Scripophily"), travelers cheques and postal orders (S1 paper exonumia), telephone cards (S3 lists them as exonumia: alias row to F5), award certificate (S17, S31), advertising trade card (F5-5).
- **F2 Art — artist trading cards, sculpture and art medals.**
  *Precedence: an artist trading card is F2; an art medal struck or cast as a medal is F3; a free-standing figure made to be looked at is F2 and a figure made with an award plate or inscription field is F3.*
  Closest objects: art medal (S1, S34), upright award with a figure (S17 "Sculptures"), medallic plaquette (S34 mentions art medals from the Italian Renaissance).
- **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects — religious medals and charms.**
  *Precedence (proposed, review X1): a medal made for devotion (a saint's or scapular medal, a Miraculous Medal) is F4 when loose and A3 when it is made with a chain or bail to be worn; a medal struck to commemorate or to reward, whatever its subject, is F3. Whether a medal is blessed is a ritual act, not a property of the object, and is not a test.*
  Closest objects: religious medals and spiritual tokens (S1 files both as medals and tokens), good-luck tokens and charms (S1). A charm made to be worn is A3 (step 14 note); OWNER: a good-luck or spiritual token that is carried, not worn, and not made for a rite has no stated home (added to the framework-conflict list).
- **E2 Sports and B4 Decor — trophies, plaques and plates.**
  *Precedence: a trophy, cup, shield or award plaque is F3 whatever sport it names; sports equipment that is signed or won is still the equipment (S5); a commemorative plate is a plate; a wall plaque with no award function is B4.*
  Closest objects: championship belt (Trophy Deals search excerpt only †; OWNER question 18), signed ball (S5), commemorative plate (framework), glass cup award (S17).
- **B1 Furniture & Storage — albums and holders.**
  *Precedence (S3; proposed review X5): a capsule, slab, toploader, sleeve or case made to hold one coin, note, stamp, medal or card follows it into F3.10; an album, binder, stock book, tray or coin tube made to hold several is B1 when empty, whatever it is made to hold.*
  Closest objects: coin tubes, holders, albums (S3 "Services & Supplies"; S6 supplies shelf with albums, holders, hinges), PSA slab (S21), card display cases (DICK'S sells slab display cases: search excerpt only †).
- **E4 Weapons & Armour — ceremonial swords.**
  *Precedence: a sword, staff or mace carried as insignia of office and not made to fight is F3; a weapon made to fight, including a collectible sword, is E4 (Google files Collectible Swords under Collectibles: S31).*
  Closest objects: sword of state (S30 "Great Sword of Dublin"), sceptre (S30), drum-major mace (S30).
- **F6 Natural History & Specimens — native gold, bars and element samples.**
  *Precedence (agreed in the F6 memo and the domain F review, no new source): a bullion bar, round or coin is F3; a nugget or specimen of native gold kept as a natural object, and an element sample such as an element cube, are F6.*
  Closest objects: gold nugget, element cube (F6 memo).
- **I2 Digital Media & Items — digital cards and tokens.**
  *Precedence: a card or coin that exists only as a token or file is I2; the printed card and the struck coin are F3; cryptocurrency holdings are out (BRIEF), so eBay's "Virtual Currency" category (S2) and the crypto-key banknote kind in Numista (S1) add nothing here beyond the note itself.*
  Closest objects: digital trading card NFT, "crypto-key embedded note" (S1).

**Bullion against coin (replaces the former pricing test; review F3-4, proposed).** *A coin struck in a mint's bullion programme, in the standard bullion finish, is a bullion coin (F3.3); the same design struck as proof, reverse proof or another collector finish, or issued in a mint's presentation packaging, is a collector coin (F3.1 Non-circulating legal-tender coin); a worn circulation coin sold for its silver content is a standard circulation coin with a `metal_content` attribute.* Finish and packaging are properties of the issue that the mint states, so the Panda and the Britannia, which are struck both ways, have each object in one home. This needs a fetched mint page that states the finish (†).

**Medal against trophy (proposed).** *A disc medal, wherever sold, is a medal (F3.6); a cup, shield, plaque, upright award, bowl or rosette is a trophy or award (F3.7).*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A, issuing function then standing or form of the issue.** Ten sub-families: nine by issuing function (F3.1 Coins; F3.2 Banknotes & paper money; F3.3 Bullion; F3.4 Tokens & scrip; F3.5 Postage & postal items; F3.6 Medals & decorations; F3.7 Trophies & awards; F3.8 Trading cards; F3.9 Regalia & insignia of office) and F3.10 Parts and accessories made for one coin, note, stamp, medal or card (C5(d), exempt from the level-1 axis). Leaves are by the standing, form or purpose of the issue (bullion, trophies and regalia store `leaf = null`, C5(b)); kinds are generic pieces inside a leaf; named forms are series, issues and iconic designs with a `maker_bound` flag. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); coins, banknotes, bullion and regalia often use three. Region, era, metal, denomination, sport, franchise, grade, edition and maker are never levels. Candidates B and C remain available as **generated views** over the same data (by collecting discipline; by region and era).
