## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J5 Pantry Staples, domain J (Groceries & Cellar).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, placement key step 3, branch 5): *pantry staples: rice, grains, flours, oats and breakfast cereals, dried and fresh unfilled pasta and noodles, gnocchi, pulses, nuts and seeds, dried fruit, dried vegetables, dried mushrooms and dried seaweed, oils, cooking fats and vinegars, spices, dried herbs and salt, sugar, honey (including honeycomb) and syrups, sweet and savoury spreads, sauces, condiments, stocks and pastes from any source, coconut milk and cream, pickles and preserves, tinned and jarred fruit, vegetables, pulses and tomatoes, baking ingredients (raising agents and dried yeast, baking chocolate, unbaked doughs and pastry), tofu, tempeh, seitan and other plant proteins and meat alternatives.* Category line: "grains, flours and cereals, pasta and noodles, pulses, nuts and seeds, dried fruit and vegetables, oils, fats and vinegars, spices and salt, sugar, honey and spreads, sauces, condiments and stocks, pickles and preserves, tinned goods, baking ingredients, tofu and meat alternatives."

**Notes and precedence sentences in the framework that touch J5:**
- Step 3 note: Culinary describes these foods as eaten; here each is filed once, as the product as bought, divided by product and by the quality of the ingredient, and Culinary links to it.
- Step 3 note: "A product is filed by what it is, not by what it contains: sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes and cooking fats are J5 whatever they are made from (fish sauce, oyster sauce, stock cubes, lard, dripping), except butter and ghee (J3)."
- Step 3 note: nuts, seeds, dried fruit, and dried or cured meat and fish are groceries even when sold salted, roasted or as snacks (dried and cured meat are J1 and fish J2; nuts, seeds and dried fruit are J5).
- Step 3 note: soups, stews, curries and pasta dishes in tins, jars or pouches are ready meals (step 1, OUT); pasta and cooking sauces in jars, with or without meat, are sauces (J5).
- Step 3 note: wines and spirits made for cooking or flavouring (mirin, cooking wine, bitters, extracts) are J5.
- Step 3 note: a set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value, and is OUT if that item is a prepared food; the basket or box kept afterwards is B1.
- Step 1: bakery, confectionery, chocolate bars, crisps, crackers, popcorn and other snack products, ready meals, soups, filled pasta and dumplings are prepared foods (OUT); curing, smoking, fermenting, drying, salting, pickling, tinning, freezing or plain cooking of one main ingredient does not make a food prepared (pickles and kimchi, jams and honey stay groceries).
- Step 3 note: a plant sold still growing is C3; vitamins, supplements and sports nutrition are H2; areca nut, khat, kratom and the other step-27 products are B9; food made for animals (bird seed, fat balls, hay, feed) is B10.
- Step 27 note: hemp seeds and hemp oil sold as food are J5; CBD products are B9.
- J6 branch: "coffee, tea and cocoa as beans, leaves, powders or capsules, and other leaves and flowers brewed as infusions" are J6, so cocoa powder is J6.
- Step 6 note and `_framework_conflicts.md` (C3 flag): proposed rule that sprouting seed sold for eating is J5, for growing C3.
- Branch order inside step 3: meat J1, fish J2, dairy and eggs J3, fresh and frozen fruit and vegetables J4, then J5, coffee and tea and cocoa J6, drinks J7. Anything that is not clearly a branch above J5 falls to J5 if it is on the pantry list.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (UK, Germany, France, India, US specialist)

- **UK, Waitrose Food Cupboard [S1].** Fourteen sibling shelves: Breakfast Cereal; Crisps, Snacks & Nuts; Chocolate & Sweets; Biscuits & Crackers; Tins, Cans & Packets; Rice, Pasta & Pulses; Sugar & Home Baking; Jam, Honey & Spreads; Dried Herbs, Oils & Vinegar; Condiments, Dressings & Marinades; Jarred Goods, Pickles & Olives; Desserts; Cooking Sauces & Meal Kits. The top level mixes crop (rice, pasta), function (baking, condiments), preservation (tins, jars) and snack status. Below it, Rice is split into Long Grain, Basmati, Wholegrain/Brown/Wild, Risotto & Paella, Oriental & Sushi, Dessert Rice and Microwaveable Rice [S2] (variety and use, plus a pack format). Oils & Vinegars is split into Olive Oil, Vegetable Oil, Sunflower Oil, Seed & Nut Oil, Chilli & Infused Oil, Balsamic Vinegar, Wine Vinegar, Rice Vinegar, Malt Vinegar and Cider Vinegar [S3] (oil by plant, vinegar by raw material, balsamic as its own shelf).
- **Germany, EDEKA24 [S4].** Lebensmittel is cut into Backen & Desserts (Margarine & Butterschmalz, Backzutaten, Mehl-Backpulver-Hefe, Nüsse, Zucker & Zuckerersatz), Frühstück (Honig, Konfitüre, Nuss- und Schokocreme), Würzmittel & Brühen (Öle, Brühen, Essig, Feinwürzmittel, Gewürze-Kräuter, Salatkräuter, Salz, Würzmischungen), Soßen (Fix-Produkte, Fertige Soßen, Fonds-Soßenbinder, Grillsaucen, Ketchup-Mayonnaise, Salatdressing, Senf-Meerrettich, Tomatensoße-Pesto, Würzsaucen), Beilagen (Reis, Nudeln, Samen-Saaten, Hülsenfrüchte, Knödel-Kartoffelprodukte) and Konserven (Gemüse, Obst, Oliven-Peperoni, with fish and meat tins). Flour sits with baking, honey with breakfast, nuts both with snacks and with baking. Within Nudeln the products are listed by cut and maker (Barilla Fusilli, Delverde Penne Rigate, De Cecco Tagliatelle No 203), so a German buyer navigates pasta by cut then by maker.
- **France, Carrefour épicerie salée [S16].** Eleven sub-rayons: Apéritifs et Chips; Huiles, Vinaigres et Vinaigrettes (Huiles, Huiles d'olive, Vinaigres, Vinaigres balsamiques, Vinaigrettes); Pâtes; Sauces chaudes; Sauces froides; Conserves et Bocaux; Plats cuisinés; Riz, Purées et Féculents; Cornichons et condiments; Sel, Épices et Bouillons; Soupes et Croûtons. Sugar, flour and baking aids are a separate rayon (Sucres, Farines et Aide à la pâtisserie), and cuisine rayons (Saveurs asiatiques, italiennes, indiennes) cut across. Facets are quality labels (AOP, IGP, Label Rouge, Bio), origin and diet.
- **India, BigBasket Foodgrains, Oil & Masala [S11].** One 9,272-item node holds rice, atta, flours and sooji, dals and pulses, millets, edible oils and ghee, whole and powdered spices and masalas, sugar and salt, dry fruit and nuts, sauces and cooking pastes. Facets show how Indian buyers think: Oil Type (olive, mustard, groundnut, sesame, coconut, rice bran, pomace, extra virgin); Dal Type (toor, moong, urad, masoor, chana, rajma); Grain Type (split or whole); Millets (ragi, jowar, bajra, foxtail, kodo); Age of the Rice; rice lines by cultivar (Sona Masoori, Ponni, Basmati, Matta, Idli); Powdered Spices as blends (biryani, garam, sambar, rasam). Spices, oils and grains share one tree, unlike UK and German trees.
- **US, Chinese-American store myAsianStore [S12].** Groceries opens with Soy Sauces & Condiments (light, dark, thick and specialty soy, oyster sauce, hoisin, chili oil, vinegars), Instant Noodles & Rice Dishes, Pantry Staples (beans, grains, hot-pot bases, soup mixes, cooking sauces) and collections Cereal/Grain Flour, Bean Paste, Preserved Vegetables, Peanuts, Sugar, Seasoning, Canned Goods. Condiments and sauces are one group; pickled vegetables are a staple.
- **Specialist retail [S9, S10, S20].** DeLallo sells each pasta cut in semolina, whole-wheat, organic and gluten-free versions [S9]. Rancho Gordo sells heirloom beans by named variety and separates Grains and Seeds, Herbs and Spices, Pantry Goods, Chiles and Sauces [S10]. Burlap & Barrel, a direct-trade spice seller, filters by Spice Type (chilis and paprikas, cinnamon, onions and garlic, salt and pepper, blends, extracts), Format (whole, ground, blend), Cuisine, Uses, Flavor and Heat [S20].

### 1.2 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Codex GSFA food categories [S13].** Numbered families relevant here: 02 Fats and oils; 04 Fruits and vegetables (including pulses, seaweeds, nuts and seeds), cut by processing state (fresh, frozen, dried, in vinegar/oil/brine, canned, purees and spreads such as peanut butter, fermented); 06 Cereals (including breakfast cereals and, at 06.8, soybean products: tofu, semi-dehydrated and fermented bean curd); 11 Sweeteners including honey (refined and raw sugars, brown sugar, syrups, table-top sweeteners); 12 Salts, spices, soups, sauces, salads, protein products (12.1 salt, 12.2 herbs and spices, 12.4 mustards, 12.6 sauces, 12.9 soybean sauces); 15 processed nuts as snacks. Codex divides by processing state and function, and puts tofu under cereals and pulse products.
- **Japan, Standard Tables of Food Composition [S5].** Eighteen groups ordered by source organism then processing: 1 Cereals, 3 Sugars and sweeteners, 4 Pulses, 5 Nuts and seeds, 14 Fats and oils, 17 Seasonings and spices (subgroups seasonings, spices, other); fruit, vegetables, mushrooms and algae are separate groups; confectionery, beverages and prepared foods are separate. Four-level internal structure (large, medium, small, subdivision) with a five-digit food number.
- **USDA FoodData Central [S14].** SR categories: Cereal Grains and Pasta; Fats and Oils; Legumes and Legume Products; Nut and Seed Products; Spices and Herbs; Sweets; Soups, Sauces, and Gravies; Baked Products. Spaghetti is recorded as "Pasta, dry, enriched" vs "Pasta, dry, whole grain".
- **USDA AMS grade standards for nuts [S24].** Separate voluntary standards per nut and per form: almonds in shell and shelled, Brazil nuts, filberts, pecans in shell and shelled, pistachios, walnuts, mixed nuts, peanuts by Virginia, Runner and Spanish type, peanut butter. The graded unit is nut species by form (in shell or shelled).
- **EU and IOC olive oil rules [S6].** Eight categories (extra-virgin, virgin, lampante, refined, olive oil composed of refined and virgin, pomace, crude pomace, refined pomace); only four reach consumers. Graded by physico-chemical limits (acidity 0.8 or 2 percent) and sensory panel (Reg. 2022/2104).
- **Soy sauce, JAS 1703 [S22, S23].** Five types (koikuchi about 80 percent of output, usukuchi, tamari, saishikomi, shiro), three manufacturing methods, three grades (Tokkyu, Jokyu, Hyojun) set by total nitrogen, colour number and extract per type, with Tokusen and Cho-tokusen for 10 and 20 percent more nitrogen.
- **Balsamic vinegar [S17, S18].** Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena DOP: Affinato (12 years or more) and Extravecchio (25 years or more), cooked must only. Aceto Balsamico di Modena IGP: wine vinegar and must, with Invecchiato (3 years or more) and Riserva (5 years or more) regulated labels.
- **Saffron, ISO 3632-1:2025 [S15, S19].** Two ladders: ISO laboratory Categories I, II, III and traditional market cuts (Super Negin, Negin, Sargol, Pushal, Konj).
- **German flour type numbers [S21]** are ash content in mg per 100 g of dry flour (405, 550, 1050), with separate series for spelt (630, 812, 1050) and rye (815, 997, 1150); wholemeal has no type number.

### 1.3 Enthusiast guides, reference works and market reports

- **Rice [S8]:** Serious Eats groups varieties by subspecies (indica, japonica) because it predicts more than grain length. **Pasta [S9, S4]:** long, short, tube, stuffed, cooked in broth (pastina), stretched, dumpling-like, special shape; over 400 shapes. **Japanese noodles [S33]:** by base (wheat: udon, kishimen, somen, ramen; buckwheat: soba; starch: harusame, kuzukiri). **Legumes [S27]:** Clovegarden groups by genus (Phaseolus American beans, Vigna beans, lentils, peas, chickpeas, fava, soybeans, peanuts) with dal (split) and gram (Indian names) as cross-cutting terms. **Sugar [S25]:** demerara, muscovado, turbinado, sucanat, jaggery, piloncillo differ by crystal size, moisture and molasses. **Honey [S26, S7]:** floral source is called the single most important factor; manuka carries UMF, MGO, NPA and KFactor ratings. **Salt [S32]:** by crystal and job (table/iodised, kosher, fine and coarse sea, flake, fleur de sel, kala namak, pickling); kosher has no density standard, so brand matters. **Canned tomatoes [S34]:** San Marzano is a variety, a protected name (DOP) and a paid label; "San Marzano style" is not protected in the US.
- **Market report [S28]:** the German retail panel separates plant-based meat from tofu, tempeh and seitan, which it treats as traditional foods not sold as analogues.
- **Consensus rankings [S29, S30, S31]:** Japan's grain inspection association ranks 144 production-area by cultivar rice entries against a Koshihikari blend in five ranks (Toku-A 43 in the 2025 crop); the Olive Oil Times World Ranking scores brands and producers from competition medals.

### 1.4 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** the same twelve shelf families appear everywhere: rice and grains, flour, pasta and noodles, pulses, nuts and seeds, oils, vinegars, spices and salt, sugar and honey, sauces and condiments, tinned and jarred, baking. Oils are cut by plant, vinegars by raw material, pulses by species, nuts by species, sugar by refining and region name, honey by floral source.
- **Split:** (a) flour: with grains in India and Japan [S11, S5], with baking in the UK, Germany and France [S1, S4, S16]; (b) honey: with sweeteners in Codex and Japan [S13, S5], with jam and breakfast in the UK and Germany [S1, S4]; (c) nuts: snack shelf [S1, S4] vs ingredient group [S5, S24]; (d) tinned food: by preserving medium in the shop [S1, S16] vs by crop in Codex and Japan [S13, S5]; (e) tofu: soybean product under cereals [S13], chilled vegetarian (a Tesco page was not fetched †), plant-protein retail category [S28]; (f) soy sauce: a standards class of five types [S22] vs "Soy Sauces & Condiments" in shops [S12]; (g) spices and herbs: one shelf with oils (Waitrose [S1]) vs one with salt and stock (Carrefour [S16], Codex 12.1 and 12.2 [S13]); (h) cuisine rayons [S16] cut across every family.
- **Collectors and resale:** no fetched source. Aged balsamic [S17], manuka honey [S7], saffron [S19] and competition-winning oils [S31] are bought as quality goods, but no resale market was reached. Pantry goods are consumed, not kept; the "kept" cases are few and sit on quality ladders.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose the **ingredient or product family first** (the shelf line: rice, pasta, oil, spice) and then one of three second choices depending on the family:

1. **Variety or type, for crops sold whole:** rice by variety and use [S2, S8, S11], beans by named variety [S10, S27], nuts by species [S24], honey by floral source [S26], sugar by refining and origin name [S25]. Evidence: Waitrose Rice children, BigBasket rice and dal facets, Rancho Gordo's named bean list, USDA standards per species, "single most important factor" for honey.
2. **Use, for goods that are interchangeable in kind but not in job:** flour by type number and bake [S21], salt by crystal size and task [S32], pasta by sauce fit [S9], noodles by dish [S33]. Evidence: the German guide is organised "what each type is really good for", the salt guides are organised "choose by the job".
3. **Legal or measured grade, where a standard exists:** olive oil category [S6], soy sauce grade [S23], balsamic designation and age [S17, S18], saffron ISO category [S15, S19], manuka UMF/MGO [S7], nut grade [S24]. These are read off the label, not argued.

Filter order in the shops follows this: Carrefour opens with rayon, then brand, then quality labels (AOP, IGP, Label Rouge, Bio), origin and diet [S16]; BigBasket opens with brand, price, then product-specific facets (oil type, dal type, grain type, age of rice) [S11]; Waitrose has only dietary and brand facets over a deep category tree [S1]. Region, diet, organic status and pack size are facets everywhere and never levels, which matches Appendix A. Cuisine (Saveurs asiatiques, Internationale Küche) is a navigation lens [S16, S4], not a division of the goods. Preservation (tin, jar, pickle) is a shelf in shops [S1, S16] but a processing state in Codex and Japan [S13, S5]; in the recommended structure it is a leaf axis inside one sub-family (preserved fruit and vegetables) and a pack attribute elsewhere, never a sub-family of its own.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (C5a; at most four levels below J5, the named form counting as the fourth). The candidates differ at the sub-family level, which is where the sources split.

### Candidate A (recommended): ingredient or product family

- **Sub-family axis (one axis, C5c):** *what the product is made from or what it is*: the ingredient family (rice and grains, pasta and noodles, pulses, nuts and seeds, fruit and vegetables as preserved, oils and fats, spices and salt, sweeteners) or the product family (spreads, sauces and condiments, baking ingredients, plant proteins). Preservation is not a sub-family axis: a tin or jar of the same product is a pack attribute (JC4(c), proposed), and preserving process is the leaf axis of the one sub-family whose kinds are crops kept by a process (J5.10). J5.11 Baking ingredients is the product family whose members are named by their job in the bake; it holds only products that no other family holds (raising agents, setting agents, flavourings, baking chocolate, unbaked dough), and flour, sugar and nuts stay in their own families.
- **Leaf axis (stated per sub-family, C5c):** J5.1 grain and its form as sold (ordered key in the tree); J5.2 dough base; J5.3 pulse species group; J5.4 plant group of the nut or seed; J5.5 kind of product (plant oil, solid fat, vinegar); J5.6 form of the seasoning (single spice, herb, blend, mineral); J5.7 sweetener type; J5.8 kind of spread; J5.9 product type as standards and shelves class it (ordered key); J5.10 preserving process; J5.11 function in the bake; J5.12 how the protein food is made (ordered key).
- **Kind:** the generic type at which buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). **Named form:** a cultivar, a cut, a protected name or a maker's line, with `maker_bound` yes or no (C2a).
- **Sub-families and leaves** (numbers kept from the first draft where the content is unchanged):
  - J5.1 Rice, grains & flours: Rice; Other grains, pseudo-cereals & starch pearls; Oats & breakfast cereals; Flours & meals.
  - J5.2 Pasta & noodles: Durum wheat pasta; Egg pasta & egg noodles; Soft-wheat noodles; Buckwheat noodles; Rice & starch noodles; Other-flour pasta & noodles; Gnocchi. (Fresh or dried is the attribute `storage_state`, not a leaf.)
  - J5.3 Pulses: Beans; Lentils; Peas; Chickpeas & other pulses. Dried and tinned pulses of one species are one kind; the tin is a pack attribute.
  - J5.4 Nuts & seeds: Tree nuts; Coconut; Peanuts; Seeds; then the final kind "Mixed nuts" with `leaf = null` (**OWNER:** JC5(c), Q15).
  - J5.5 Oils, fats & vinegars: Plant oils; Animal & solid cooking fats; Vinegars.
  - J5.6 Spices, herbs & salt: Single spices; Dried herbs; Spice blends & seasoning mixes; Salt.
  - J5.7 Sugars, honey & syrups: Sugars; Honey; Syrups & molasses.
  - J5.8 Spreads, pastes & dips: Sweet spreads; Nut & seed butters; Savoury spreads, pastes & dips.
  - J5.9 Sauces, condiments & stocks: Soy sauces; Other fermented seasonings; Cooking & pasta sauces; Table sauces, condiments & dressings; Stocks & bouillon; Cooking & seasoning pastes.
  - J5.10 Preserved fruit & vegetables (fruit, vegetables, edible fungi and seaweed, as J4 defines produce): Dried; Tinned & jarred; Pickled in vinegar; Salted, brined & fermented; Preserved in oil & spices.
  - J5.11 Baking ingredients: Raising agents & yeast; Flavourings, extracts & colourings; Setting agents, starches & thickeners; Baking chocolate, doughs & mixes.
  - J5.12 Tofu, tempeh & meat alternatives: Tofu & bean-curd products; Tempeh & fermented beans; Seitan & wheat gluten; Plant-based meat analogues.
  - No "parts and accessories" sub-family (C5d applies to domains A and H): groceries have no parts; tools such as pasta machines and salt grinders go to B5 under S3 and the key.
- **Remaining breaks (each settled by a sentence in section 9):** (1) **Dried apricots against almonds**: dried fruit sits in J5.10 (a crop kept by drying) and nuts in J5.4 (the key lists nuts and seeds as their own family); a dried chestnut or dried coconut stays in J5.4 (P3). (2) **Tomato products**: whole peeled and chopped tomatoes, passata and purée are all heat-processed in the pack, so they are one block of kinds in J5.10 "Tinned & jarred" (the review's "one leaf"); a separate tomato leaf would put a crop on the process axis. (3) **Pesto and other herb pastes**: a spread or dip (J5.8) or a sauce (J5.9); the review's X2 sentence names pesto as J5.8, while Culinary files pesto as a sauce (SV-J2-5) (**OWNER:** Q14). (4) **Fermented foods**: kimchi and sauerkraut are J5.10 (a crop kept by fermenting); miso, doenjang, gochujang and doubanjiang are J5.9 (a paste used to season); natto and tempeh are J5.12 (a fermented bean eaten as a protein body) (P9).

### Candidate B: Codex-style processing state

Sub-families by state of the food: fresh-type dry goods, dried, preserved in liquid, fermented, extracted (fats, sugars), blended. Matches Codex 04 and 12 [S13] and the Japanese processing-ordered subgroups [S5]. **Breaks:** (1) flour and pasta are "dried and milled" like spices; (2) a dried bean and a dried mushroom sit together but a bean and a lentil flour do not; (3) honey (an extract?) and sugar (extracted) split from jam (preserved); (4) soy sauce (fermented) separates from tofu (set) though both are soybean products. It would regroup the shop shelf, which the owner will not meet in any buying guide, and it makes storage and processing state a level everywhere, against JC4 (proposed).

### Candidate C: kitchen use

Sub-families by job: Staples to cook, Fats and acids, Seasoning, Sweet side, Baking, Preserved, Protein. Matches Waitrose's mixed top level [S1] and the salt and flour guides' "choose by the job" [S21, S32]. **Breaks:** (1) **flour** is baking in the UK and a staple in India; (2) **nuts** serve snacks, baking and salad; (3) **honey** is sweet side or spread; (4) **chickpeas** are staple, tinned and ingredient of hummus. Uses are many-to-one and conflict with the key rule that modifiers such as motive and occasion never divide.

## 5. Quality and tier

J5 is a **mixed** category: most leaves have a legal or measured ladder (spec-ranked in the sense of C4b), two have a consensus ranking, and the rest are preference with price band.

**Spec or legal ladders (attributes with the standard named, never tier entries on kinds; each ladder is a row of the proposed `standard` table, JC1(a), **OWNER:** Q8):**
- Olive oil legal category: extra virgin (free acidity up to 0.8 percent), virgin (up to 2 percent), lampante, refined, blends, pomace; EU Reg. 2022/2104 and IOC trade standard [S6].
- Soy sauce grade by total nitrogen, extract and colour per type: Tokkyu, Jokyu, Hyojun; Tokusen and Cho-tokusen [S23]; JAS 1703 [S22].
- Balsamic age classes: DOP Affinato (12 years or more) and Extravecchio (25 years or more) [S17]; IGP Invecchiato (3 years) and Riserva (5 years) [S18]. These are the consortia's rules; the `standard` row links to the `designation` row whose rules set it.
- Saffron: ISO 3632-1:2025 Categories I, II, III [S15, S19]; the market cuts Negin, Sargol, Pushal are a trade vocabulary for the physical cut, recorded as the attribute `cut` [S19].
- Manuka honey: UMF (four markers, UMF Honey Association), MGO (mg/kg) and NPA are measured ladders [S7]. KFactor is one maker's own scheme (Wedderspoon, S7) and goes in as a tier entry with `scheme = KFactor (Wedderspoon)`, never compared with UMF (H-review detail on maker ladders, proposed).
- Nuts: USDA voluntary grades by nut and form [S24].
- Flour: type number as ash content [S21] (the name of the German standard itself is not on the fetched page †).
- Rice age (months since harvest) and grain type split or whole [S11]: attributes without a published standard.

**Consensus ladders (dated tier entries, C4a: `subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`):** Japan's rice taste ranking, Toku-A / A / A-prime / B / B-prime against a Koshihikari blend reference, 144 area-by-cultivar entries tasted in the 2025 crop, 43 of them Toku-A [S29]; the association itself warns that the rank is not an evaluation of a given bag and covers only main brands [S30]. Its subject is the cultivar named form with the production area recorded as `origin`. The Olive Oil Times World Ranking scores brands and producers from competition Gold and Silver awards with recency bonuses (brands with five or more awards; producers with 12.5 points or more) [S31]; its subjects are maker and line rows. Both are dated rankings, not stable properties. Under C4(e) each scheme needs a second source from another region before entry; neither has one yet (section 10.2).

**Preference with price band:** pasta, sugar, salt, spice, preserved goods, sauces, spreads, nuts (beyond grade), vinegar (beyond balsamic), baking aids. Tier data for these would be platform tags (a retailer's own-label tiers such as Carrefour Classic' and Carrefour Bio, visible in S16, as dated `platform_tag` under C4(c)) and the price band.

**Price band (C4d).** The domain-wide USD item bands (under 50, 50 to 150, and so on) are written for items, and a 1 kg bag of rice or a 500 ml bottle of oil never leaves the first band. A unit price is the meaningful band for J5: the shops themselves show per-kg prices [S1, S4]. The memo keeps its recommendation of a `unit_price_band` instead of the item band. **OWNER:** Q9: the review's JC2 proposes one J scheme in USD per kg (solids) or per litre (liquids), from a dated, sourced list price, with bands set at first data load; the first draft wrote it per 100 g or 100 ml, which is the same scheme at another scale.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: incidental for commodities, identity for a defined set of leaves, platform for none.**
- Incidental: rice, flour, sugar, salt (most), oils below extra virgin, pulses, tinned vegetables, vinegar, stock. Makers are shown as brands on shelves [S1, S4, S16]; the buyer picks the product first.
- Identity: extra virgin olive oil (the ranking lists producers and brands by name [S31]); traditional balsamic (acetaie under the consortium [S17]); soy sauce breweries (JAS certification is per factory; the mark is optional [S23]); direct-trade spice sellers (Burlap & Barrel's farm sourcing [S20]); heirloom beans (Rancho Gordo's own varieties [S10]); manuka honey (UMF Honey Association licensees [S7]); saffron importers with ISO reports [S19]; artisan pasta (maker lines by die and drying; the shelf lists Barilla, De Cecco, Delverde by cut [S4]); kosher salt (Diamond Crystal vs Morton behave as different ingredients [S32]).
- Platform: none. Nothing in J5 sets compatibility, so the C13 ledger has no J5 rows. Protected names and certification marks are quality designations, not compatibility.

**Recommendation.**
- **Maker ledger:** sparse, C1 core columns, limited to the identity leaves above. `maker_class` takes only C1's values (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; retail own-label; artisan or atelier; supplier) and `parent_id` records groups. The first draft's new classes (producer, estate or acetaia, brewery, brand, importer, own-label) are removed from `maker_class` (X6, a C1 correction). The supply-chain fact goes in the J column `supply_role` (**OWNER:** Q17, JC3): grower or growers' marketing body; mill; processor or packer; cannery; cooperative; importer or trader; retail own-label. Two J5 roles have no JC3 value yet, and are proposed for the list: "vinegar maker (acetaia)" and "soy and ferment brewery" (JC3's "brewery" means beer). Certifications (JAS factory mark, UMF licence, organic) sit on the named form or product as attributes, not on the maker.
- **Standards and designations (JC1, proposed; **OWNER:** Q8).** The first draft's single "designation ledger" mixed protected names with grading standards (J5-4). They are split. `designation` rows (protected names of origin or method; `authority` = the consortium, never a maker): Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena DOP [S17], Aceto Balsamico di Modena IGP [S18], San Marzano DOP [S34]. Each binds one kind, so each is a non-maker named form of that kind and carries its `designation_id` (JC1(c)(1)). `standard` rows (measured or legal grading schemes, whose values sit on products as attributes): the EU olive-oil categories [S6], JAS 1703 soy-sauce grades [S22, S23], ISO 3632-1 [S15], the USDA nut grades [S24], the German flour type numbers [S21], the UMF and MGO scales [S7] and the balsamic age classes [S17, S18].
- **Tier entries:** the S29 and S31 rankings (section 5) and KFactor, with `scheme`, `value`, `source_url` and `date`.
- **Variants (C3):** not needed (no colourways or editions); crop-year releases of a rice or oil, if ever wanted, would be `variant_type` year rows. Left empty.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Canonical form first, then the secondary key that the sub-family states (C7):
- Rice, grains, flours: canonical (most common shelf kind: long-grain), then basmati, jasmine, medium- and short-grain, risotto, paella, glutinous, red and black, flattened, as the Waitrose list runs [S2] with the unlisted kinds after; flours by grain, wheat first, then by type number ascending [S21].
- Pasta and noodles: canonical shape family, then length from long to short to soup; noodles by base (wheat, buckwheat, starch) as in S33.
- Pulses and nuts: canonical (most common), then size or colour where the source states it.
- Preserved fruit and vegetables: tomato kinds first in Tinned & jarred (the most common tinned product on the fetched shelves [S16, S34]), then alphabetically by English label.
- Oils: olive oil first, then other plant oils by commonness; vinegars: wine vinegar first, balsamic after; named forms of balsamic by age class (IGP, Invecchiato, Riserva, DOP Affinato, DOP Extravecchio) [S17, S18].
- Soy sauce: koikuchi first (80 percent of output [S22]), then usukuchi, tamari, saishikomi, shiro; within a type by grade (Tokkyu, Jokyu, Hyojun) [S23].
- Sugars by colour from light to dark; honey by floral source alphabetically after the canonical clover or wildflower.
- Named forms: by maker (ledger name), then first-introduction year if attested, then alphabetical (C7); non-maker named forms (cultivars, cuts) alphabetically by their **English label**, with the native-script name as a C9 alias (J5-7; the first draft ordered them in their native language). The domain-G amendment to C7 (non-maker named forms first) applies if the owner adopts it.
Reason: buyers recognise the canonical form first and compare away from it. Chronology is not a useful key for commodities.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure.** Candidate A: category J5 -> 12 sub-families by ingredient or product family (one axis: what the product is made from or is) -> leaves (axis stated per sub-family; no sub-family skips the leaf level; preserving process is the leaf axis of J5.10 only) -> kinds at the variety, type or standards level of section 4b -> named forms (cultivar, cut, protected name, maker line). Maximum depth below the category: four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form), no deeper. Quality ladders sit on attributes (legal or measured grades with the standard named) or on dated tier entries (rice ranking, olive oil ranking, KFactor), never on kinds. A sparse maker ledger covers identity leaves; protected names go in a `designation` table and grading schemes in a `standard` table; regional names go in the alias table; storage state, pack (tin, jar, pouch), diet, organic and pack size are attributes. The level-1 restructure (J5.4 Nuts & seeds and J5.10 Preserved fruit & vegetables) and the rice kinds were applied in this pass as corrections (C5(c); kinds that overlapped) and need the owner's confirmation (Q13).
