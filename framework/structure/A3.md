## 0. Category

**Code and name:** A3 Jewellery (domain A, Worn & Carried).

**Membership test (CATEGORIES_v0_7, key step 17, third branch):** an ornament worn on the body, hair or clothing: rings, necklaces, bracelets, body jewellery, brooches, pins, badges and patches worn as ornament, hair ornaments, cufflinks, worn amulets.

**Notes that bind (S1b):** identity and access badges are F5; smart rings are D2; medical alert bracelets and tags are H2; tassels and cords worn on a cap or gown are A1. Step 14's note: anything worn as an ornament, including amulets worn as pendants, rings or bracelets, is A3 (so a cremation pendant or a cross pendant is A3), while prayer beads and rudraksha beads are F4. Step 7's note: unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6 whether natural, cultured or laboratory-grown. Step 28: loose beads and jewellery-making tools are C4. Step 15: medals and decorations are F3. Step 16: watches are A5; smartwatches D2. Step 17's own note: plain hair clips, bands and ties, and wigs, are H1. Step 8 (S3) is tried before step 17: a part or accessory made for one kind of object follows that object.

**Appendix A modifiers that apply here and so may never be a level:** region and culture, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, user (gender, child, adult), occasion (wedding, festival), motive (heirloom, memorial), origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), material, condition, edition.

**Precedence sentences touching A3:** see section 9.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and the traditional-jewellery literature

- **Body jewellery:** the buyer's questions are placement, gauge, length or inner diameter, then material (https://www.body-jewelry.com/blog/piercing-size-guide.html). The Association of Professional Piercers lists style by placement ("rings for some areas, straight or curved bars for others, nostril screw or labret stud for specific placements") and sets a materials standard (ASTM F138 / ISO 5832-1 steel, implant titanium, 14k+ gold, platinum, niobium, lead-free glass; gold-plated, filled and vermeil not acceptable for fresh piercings) (https://safepiercing.org/jewelry-for-initial-piercings). Gauge is a thickness attribute from wire gauge, identical across materials (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_jewellery ; BM25 chart above).
- **Indian bridal jewellery** is divided **head to toe by body part**, not by material: bindi, maang tikka (Rajasthani borla, Maharashtrian mandoria), nath (nose ring), karn phool / earrings (jhumka, chandbali), haar and mangalsutra, bajubandh (upper-arm band), bangles (regional: shankha pola, chuda), haath phool or aarsi (rings joined by chains to a bracelet), kamarbandh (waist), payal (anklets, silver because gold on the feet is held inauspicious) (https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/wedding-traditions/solah-shringar.html). A retailer guide groups Indian jewellery as hair and head, ear, neck, hand and arm, waist (https://www.mirraw.com/blogs/2025/12/25/the-ultimate-guide-to-indian-jewelry-history-types-regional-styles-bridal-traditions). Techniques (kundan, polki, meenakari, filigree) appear as embellishments, not categories. These two pages are the only fetched attestation for most Indian bridal forms; one further Indian retail category page is needed (**†**, section 10).
- **African beadwork** is described by cultural group and by the **object worn**: Zulu "love letters", armbands, waistbands, anklets, bandoliers and the long white *ucu* neck strand (https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/articles/16600); Maasai collars and neck rings that change with age grade, Yoruba beaded crown (https://bowers.org/index.php/collection/collections-blog/the-social-beadwork-the-use-of-beads-in-sub-saharan-africa); Zulu love-letter belt, Maasai beaded necklace, Fang anklet and Yoruba bracelet currency (https://umaine.edu/hudsonmuseum/online-exhibits-2/body-adornment-ethnographic-jewelry-from-around-the-world/africa). Bead colour is a symbol vocabulary, not a form (https://www.kirtilals.com/blog/african-maasai-zulu).

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- **1stDibs:** facets are stone, period (centuries and decades), gender, stone cut, carat weight and a **jewelry type** filter splitting "fine jewelry" (321,324 listings) from "fashion jewelry" (32,874); each maker has its own creator page (https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry).
- **oldthing.ch (Switzerland):** the tree is **material-first** (Goldschmuck, Silberschmuck, Perlenschmuck, Modeschmuck und Luxus-Accessoires, Edelsteine) and repeats the same form list (Anhänger, Armbänder, Armreifen, Broschen und Nadeln, Ketten, Ohrringe, Ringe, Sets) under each material; antique jewellery is split by period (Barock, Rokoko, Art Deco) and folk jewellery by culture (https://oldthing.ch/de/Silberschmuck/Ketten?ic=48). This is the one source that puts material above form, and it shows the cost: five copies of one form list.
- **Costume jewellery collectors** identify by **maker mark** (Trifari, Coro, Miriam Haskell, Eisenberg, Weiss) and date by mark features (copyright symbol after the mid-1950s, wartime sterling 1942 to 1947, patent numbers) (https://californiavintagebuyers.com/guides/costume-jewelry-makers-marks).

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Customs (HS / EU Combined Nomenclature, chapter 71):** 7113 articles of jewellery of precious metal or metal clad with precious metal (7113.11 silver, 7113.19 other precious metal, 7113.20 clad); 7116 articles of pearls or stones; **7117 imitation jewellery** (7117.11 cufflinks and studs of base metal, 7117.19 other base metal, 7117.90 other). Note 9 defines "articles of jewellery" as small objects of personal adornment (rings, bracelets, necklaces, brooches, earrings, watch-chains, fobs, pendants, tiepins, cufflinks, dress-studs, religious or other medals and insignia) and also pocket articles such as snuff boxes and prayer beads; note 11 keeps dress-combs, hair-slides and hairpins out of imitation jewellery (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2019/1776/annex/I/chapter/71/adopted/data.xht?view=snippet&wrap=true). Trade law divides by **material**, because duty depends on it. Customs classes go in the jurisdiction ledger (C17), not on the tree.
- **UK Hallmarking Act 1973:** fineness standards by parts per thousand; carat table 9 = 375, 12 = 500, 14 = 585, 15 = 625, 18 = 750, 22 = 916.6; platinum minimum 950 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1973/43/data.html).
- **ISO 8653:2016** defines and measures ring sizes (sizes 41 to 76) and is the only ISO jewellery form standard found (https://www.iso.org/standard/65408.html).
- **GIA** grades loose natural diamonds on the 4Cs; the report attaches to the stone, not to a mounted piece (https://www.gia.edu/gem-lab-service/diamond-grading).
- **APP** (above) is the body-jewellery materials standard.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **Wikipedia, List of jewellery types** groups by **site on the body**: hair ornaments, arms, hands, body, feet, "special functions" (signet, thumb ring), components (cameo, findings, locket, medallion, pendant), plus separate lists of gem and non-gem materials (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_jewellery_types).
- **Hudson Museum, Bowers Museum and Museums Victoria** (above) group ethnographic jewellery by culture, then object.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

**Agree:** the core forms are ring, necklace/chain, bracelet, earring, pendant, brooch/pin; every retailer navigates by these first (Tanishq, CTF, Etsy, CHRIST, Cartier). Reference works and Indian sources group the same forms by body site. Metal, stone, karat, period, price and occasion are always **filters beside the form**, except at oldthing and in customs law.

**Split the same objects differently:**
- Pendant and charm: categories of their own (Tanishq pendants; CTF pendants and charms) or a component (Wikipedia: pendant, locket, medallion listed under components).
- Bangle versus bracelet: one group at Tanishq ("bracelets & bangles") but separate at CTF ("Bracelets / Anklets" and "Bangles"); anklets join bracelets at CTF. Cartier lists rigid models such as Love as "bracelets".
- Watches sit inside "jewelry" at Etsy and 1stDibs ("Jewelry & Watches"); A5 owns them here.
- Gold coin and bar are listed under jewellery at CTF; F3 owns them here. "Display ornament/gift" (CTF) is B4 here. Jewellery storage (Etsy, CHRIST) is B1 here.
- Silver: "fine" at Borsheims and precious at customs (7113), but also the base of "demi-fine" (sterling, vermeil) at Borsheims. The fine / demi-fine / fashion line is a retail convention, not a standard.
- Body jewellery is its own category (Etsy, Wikipedia) although it includes ear jewellery (plugs, tunnels, studs).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Buyers choose by form first, then narrow by metal, stone, price and occasion.** Evidence:
- Every retailer's top-level navigation is a form list (Tanishq types, CTF categories, Etsy categories, CHRIST product type †). Metal, occasion and collection come after (Tanishq, CTF pages above). Cartier's navigation is **form (rings, bracelets, necklaces, earrings) crossed with named collections** (Love, Trinity, Juste un Clou, Panthère) that repeat under every form (https://www.cartier.com/en-sg/jewellery/collections).
- Buyer questions in body jewellery start from **placement**, because placement fixes the form, gauge and length (APP; body-jewelry.com; BM25).
- Indian tradition and museum reference lists start from **body site** (head, ear, nose, neck, arm, hand, waist, foot).
- Resale markets start from **maker and period** (1stDibs creator pages; costume-jewellery maker marks) or **material** (oldthing).
- Spec facets that follow: ring size (ISO 8653; CHRIST ring-size facet), chain length, gauge, karat, carat weight, clarity (Tanishq).

So: form (what it is) is the first choice at retail; body site is the first choice in reference and in piercing; maker is the first choice in the resale and collector market. Gem and metal are filters in every retail source.

## 3. Candidate structures

Three candidates were tested. Candidate A is recommended. In every candidate, **metal, gem, fineness, price tier, era, region, maker, occasion and user are tags, attributes or ledgers, never levels** (Appendix A; C4, C6).

### 3.1 Candidate A (recommended): site of wear > form family > kind > named form

| Level | Name | One stated axis (within each sub-family, C5(c)) | Count (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | the part of the body or dress the piece is made to adorn (head and hair; ear; neck and chest; arm and wrist; hand and finger; torso, waist and clothing; leg and foot), **plus one fitting-defined class** (sub-family 3, see the exception below), **plus the parts sub-family** that C5(d) requires | 9 |
| 2 | Leaf | form family: the word a shop or museum uses as its category (earrings, necklaces, pendants and charms, bangles, brooches and pins). In sub-family 3 the leaf axis is the fitting form (barbell, ring, stud or labret, navel curve, stretched-ear piece). A sub-family with 12 kinds or fewer may store `leaf = null` (C5(b)); the data stores the kind without a dummy leaf. | 22 in the worked tree (about 25 est. in full) |
| 3 | Kind | generic type of piece (hoop earring, tennis bracelet, signet ring, maang tikka) | about 110 to 140 (est.) |
| 4 | Named form | a specific design below the kind: **maker-bound** (Cartier Love bangle, Pandora Moments charm bracelet) or **not maker-bound** (borla, ucu strand, nath), recorded with `maker_bound: yes / no` (C2(a)) | no cap (CV-7); many hundreds expected |

Four levels below the category, with the named form as the fourth (C5(a)).

**Sub-family 3 holds every ornament made to be worn through a piercing other than an earring made for the earlobe**: nose, lip, brow, tongue, navel, nipple and intimate-site jewellery, cartilage barbells and labrets, and every stretched-ear piece (plug, tunnel, taper), **whoever sells it**. Sub-family 2 (Ear) holds earrings made for the lobe (hook, post, hoop, clip-on, jhumka, chandbali) and ear cuffs and climbers. The nose pin (Tanishq "nosepin"), the nose stud (BM25) and the nath are therefore one case: sub-family 3. The test is the fitting the piece is made for, not the sales channel (a channel test would be a market modifier, Appendix A). This replaces the draft's "sold through jewellers goes to its site" sentence, which had no nose site to go to and contradicted the edge-object list. Fitting attributes (post, hook, clip-on, threaded or threadless, flat-back, gauge, length, inner diameter) are attributes, never kinds.

**Exception, decided (A-13).** The level-1 sub-families are sites, except sub-family 3, which is defined by a fitting. One barbell is worn in the ear cartilage, brow, tongue and nipple, so it cannot be split by site; all sources divide piercing jewellery by form and gauge, not site (BM25, BodyCandy, body-jewelry.com, APP, Wikipedia), and Etsy and Wikipedia keep it apart from earrings. C5(d) lets only the parts sub-family depart from the level-1 axis, so this is a second, stated exception, adopted. Residual edge, decided: a small ring or stud sold for "the ear" with no non-lobe placement named is an earring (sub-family 2); the lobe is the default fitting.

**Bangle test (used for the Love bracelet and similar models).** A bangle is rigid and closed or hinged. A rigid open-ended piece is a cuff bracelet. A maker's model is classed by this test, not by the maker's own word, and the maker's word is stored as an alias. A bangle sold as a "bracelet" is therefore a bangle.

**Regional names (C2(c), C11; CV-6, CV-26, CV-27).** Maang tikka, mangalsutra and jhumka are kinds (named in C2(c); Tanishq lists the last two as types). Where a generic kind already covers the form, the regional word is a non-maker named form or an alias with a region tag: nath under nose ring, bajubandh under armlet, payal under anklet, kamarbandh under waist chain, borla and mandoria under maang tikka, ucu under beaded necklace, and, applying the same rule, chandbali under drop earring and kada under bangle (a rigid piece, by the bangle test). A regional name is a kind only when no generic kind covers its form, and its kind label is then the generic description: haath phool, a ring-and-bracelet harness, is the kind "hand harness" with the regional word as its synonym. Two regions decide level only (C11): a named form becomes a kind when fetched sellers in two regions give it its own category and no generic kind covers its form; it is in either way, on one fetched page. Haath phool is provisional until attested; if the stage-3 pages do not show it sold apart, it becomes a non-maker named form under bracelet. A region never names a sub-family or a leaf, so the leaf formerly named "Bangles and kadas" is "Bangles". A "by tradition" view is generated from the region tags.

Full URLs are in section 1 and the sources file.

**Where Candidate A breaks (edge objects), with the rule applied:**
- *Charm.* A charm fits a bracelet, a chain or a pendant loop. It has one home, the leaf Pendants and charms (C14(b): the one leaf that holds all its uses); a generated view lists it under each carrier. By S3 a charm made for one kind of bracelet stays in A3 anyway. A charm hung on a bag is A4.
- *Jewellery set (parure, bridal set).* Decided (K-1): a set of objects of one kind is filed as that kind; a mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed and a unit-of-sale attribute. There is no "jewellery set" kind. A necklace set with earrings is filed under the necklace kind of its main piece and "bridal set" is a generated view. Tanishq lists "necklace set" under neckwear and Etsy has "jewelry sets" as a category (sources above); both are sales groupings, recorded as the unit-of-sale attribute.
- *Toe ring and thumb ring.* Both are rings by form but sit on foot and hand by site; the draft puts toe ring at Leg and foot and thumb ring at Finger rings (Wikipedia lists thumb ring under "special functions").
- *Nose pin sold by a jeweller versus a gauge-specified nose stud.* Same object, two vocabularies, one kind (nose stud, alias nose pin), sub-family 3, by the fitting test.
- *Pendant sold with its chain.* A pendant on its chain is a pendant; necklaces are pieces whose ornament is the strand itself.
- *Waist chain and belly chain.* One form, one kind; "belly chain" is an alias.
- *Fascinator.* Held on a band or headpiece it is A1 headwear (the head-ornament sentence, K-129, section 9); sold on a clip or comb the same sentence sends it to A3 as a pinned or clipped hair ornament.

### 3.2 Candidate B: form family > kind > named form (retail-flat)

This is Candidate A without its first level; the 22 leaf names of the worked tree are the same. It is the navigation of Etsy, CTF, CHRIST, Tanishq and Cartier. Example kinds are those in the tree above.

**Where it breaks:**
- The leaf axis is already mixed: "rings" and "earrings" are forms, "hair ornaments" is a site, "cufflinks" is tied to a garment, "body piercing jewellery" is a fitting system. Etsy's own category list shows the same mixture (rings, earrings, body jewelry, cufflinks, storage, cremation jewelry). It fails "one stated axis per level".
- Indian head-to-toe ornaments (haath phool, kamarbandh, maang tikka) and African regalia (Maasai collar, Zulu love letter, Yoruba beaded crown) do not match a Western form list and land in "other".
- About 14 sibling leaves is a long first level; Candidate A keeps each sibling list at 2 to 5.

### 3.3 Candidate C (rejected): tier or material first (fine > demi-fine > fashion, or gold > silver > pearl > costume)

Used by: 1stDibs' fine / fashion jewelry type filter (https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry), Borsheims' fine / demi-fine / fashion (https://www.borsheims.com/blog/fine-jewelry-vs-fashion-jewelry/), customs 7113 versus 7117, and oldthing's material-first tree (https://oldthing.ch/de/Silberschmuck/Ketten?ic=48).

**Where it breaks:**
- *Gold-plated sterling silver.* Borsheims puts sterling in both "fine" and "demi-fine"; customs treats metal clad with precious metal as 7113.20 but plated base metal as 7117.19.
- *Chanel, Haskell and Trifari costume pieces.* "Fashion" by material, but collected and priced by maker mark and period (CVB maker guide: signed Eisenberg and Haskell pieces sell for hundreds of dollars).
- *Laboratory-grown stones in solid gold:* the metal says fine, the stone's origin says "grown"; Appendix A already lists origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown) as a modifier, so it cannot also decide the tier level.
- *Duplication:* oldthing repeats Anhänger, Armbänder, Ketten, Ohrringe, Ringe under each of five materials.

The line is useful and well attested, but as a **tier entry on a maker, line or named form** (section 5), not a level and not a property of a kind.

### 3.4 Gem and metal: form axis or modifier? (argued)

**Modifier.** Reasons:
1. Appendix A names material and origin as modifiers; "unless material is the form" is the only exception.
2. Every retailer in the sample treats them as filters beside the form: Tanishq (jewellery type, karatage, metal, metal colour, clarity), CTF (metal), Borsheims (shop by gemstone, shop by metal), 1stDibs (stone, stone cut, carat weight), CHRIST (material, alloy, gemstone/pearl, gem colour), Rakuten (gold purity, metal-allergy-safe). None makes them the first division of a form tree except oldthing.
3. One piece has several metals and stones, so a material level would not give one home.
4. The same form list recurs under every material (oldthing), so a material level multiplies the tree by 5 without adding a kind.

**Cases where material is the form** (and so may be a kind): a *pearl necklace* (a strand of pearls; customs gives pearls their own heading 7116) and the bead-and-stone *beaded bracelet* (Rakuten sells power-stone bracelets as a type). These are kinds by form (strand, stretch bead bracelet), with stone as attribute and "power-stone" as a motive tag (section 7). A bare gem is not here: unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (est.):** 110 to 140. Basis: the Wikipedia list of jewellery types gives about 70 form names (hair, arms, hands, body, feet, special functions, components); piercing retailers add about 15 distinct fitting forms (BM25 chart); Indian and African sources add about 25 culturally named forms (maang tikka, nath, jhumka, kada, mangalsutra, haath phool, kamarbandh, payal; Maasai collar, Zulu love letter, ucu). Under C2(c) and C11 some of these culturally named forms are named forms or aliases, not kinds, so the true kind count will be nearer the lower end. The count overlaps, so the range is wide; it is an estimate, not a census. The worked tree names 54 kinds.
- **Named forms (est.):** 800 to 2,000. Cartier's navigation alone lists 16 jewellery collections, each repeated under up to four forms (https://www.cartier.com/en-sg/jewellery/collections); CTF lists about 30 collection facets (https://www.chowtaifook.com/sg/eshop/jewellery); costume-jewellery makers add signature designs (https://californiavintagebuyers.com/guides/costume-jewelry-makers-marks). Chain-link patterns (kihei, ball chain, station chain) and motifs (floral, geometric, heart, evil eye; Tanishq type facet) are **attributes**, not named forms (C2(b)). The 800 to 2,000 is an estimate, not a cap: named forms enter on one fetched page each (C2(f)) and grow as far as the attested evidence goes; large sets (for example a closed system's charm range) live in registers linked to their kind (C2(g)).
- **Where depth concentrates:** finger rings, earrings, necklaces with pendants and chains, and bracelets (the broad leaves); piercing-fit jewellery (many forms, each multiplied by gauge and length, but those are attributes); and maker-defined named forms (collections that cut across forms).
- **Leaf width:** *broad* (30 or more kinds expected): Finger rings, Earrings, Necklaces, Bracelets. *Medium* (8 to 20): Pendants and charms, Brooches and pins, Studs and labrets, Hair ornaments, Bangles. *Narrow* (fewer than 8): Armlets, Hand ornaments, Anklets and toe rings, Crowns and tiaras, Shirt and tie accessories, Waist and body chains, Navel jewellery, Forehead and parting ornaments, the parts sub-family.

## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the form that buyers name and compare, such as hoop earring, tennis bracelet, signet ring or labret stud. Below that, metal, stone, karat, motif, link pattern, length and size are attributes. A named form is a specific design below the kind, either maker-bound (a model or line piece) or not maker-bound (a generic or traditional design that many makers make). Specifications, motifs, sizes, colourways and editions are never named forms (C2(b)).**

Evidence:
- Retail "type" facets list forms, with motif and style mixed in: Tanishq's type list (hoop, stud, drops, jhumka, ear cuff, ear climber, tennis bracelet, kada, stackable rings, open rings, choker, layered chain, ball chain, station chain, nosepin, vati) and the same facet's motifs (floral, geometric, heart, evil eye) (https://www.tanishq.co.in/shop/jewellery?lang=en_IN). Form words are kinds; motif words and chain patterns are attributes.
- 1stDibs reaches form sub-types inside each category (the page's item links show band-rings and drop-necklaces) and then switches to stone, cut, carat, period and creator facets (https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry). Quality is compared on those later facets, not on more kinds.
- Body-jewellery buyers compare gauge, length or inner diameter and material **within** a form (barbell, labret, captive bead ring) (https://bm25.com/pages/size-conversion-chart ; https://www.body-jewelry.com/blog/piercing-size-guide.html ; https://safepiercing.org/jewelry-for-initial-piercings). Gauge is a measurement (16g = 1.2 mm), so each gauge is an attribute value, never a kind.
- Ring size is a standardised attribute (ISO 8653, https://www.iso.org/standard/65408.html); CHRIST offers it as a facet (https://www.christ.de/category/herrenschmuck/index.html). Size, length and gauge are attributes with their standard named (C6).
- The diamond's 4Cs attach to the loose stone (https://www.gia.edu/gem-lab-service/diamond-grading), so a solitaire ring is one kind with stone grade as an attribute. The scale is recorded as a scheme; the grade or certificate of one particular stone is not stored (C16).
- Makers are compared one level lower: Cartier's collection pages compare "Love" with "Juste un Clou" and "Trinity" (https://www.cartier.com/en-sg/jewellery/collections), and costume-jewellery collectors compare signed Trifari with Coro (CVB guide). These are maker-bound named forms; the collection itself is a line in the line table (section 6).

**Does the level differ between sub-families?** Slightly. In Body and facial piercing jewellery the kind is the fitting system plus form (flat-back labret stud; threadless or threaded are attributes). In Neck and chest, **chain** is one kind and the link pattern is the attribute `chain_pattern`. Japanese buyers treat the kihei pattern as a main comparison (Rakuten chain-type facet, https://www.rakuten.co.jp/category/216129), which is a reason to make the attribute searchable, not to make kihei a named form. This replaces the draft's section 4b recommendation of kihei as a named form, which contradicted section 4. Culturally named forms follow section 3.1 (C2(c), C11): jhumka and mangalsutra are kinds; kada, chandbali and the nosepin (an alias of nose stud) are named forms or aliases under the nearest generic kind, because a generic kind covers their form, each with a region or culture tag.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Decided classification: consensus-ranked, stored as tier entries on makers, lines and named forms (C4), with spec-ranked attributes beside them.** There is no single ladder, and **no tier sits on a kind** (C4(a)): a hoop earring exists at every tier.

- **Tier entries (C4(a), CV-10).** Rows of (`tier_entry_id`, `subject_id`, `subject_type`, `scheme_id`, `value`, `rank`, `direction`, `scope`, `stale_after`, `source_url`, `date`, and a computed `disputed` flag) where the subject is a maker, a line, a named form or, under C4(b), a variant. One scheme is registered: "US retail convention (Borsheims)", values fine / demi-fine / fashion, scale class (iii) editorial or community class, `kinds_served` the A3 kinds, source Borsheims (fetched). The scheme enters on that one fetched page (C4(e)). A named form does not inherit its maker's tier unless an entry says so. `disputed` marks subjects with entries that disagree (Borsheims counts sterling as both fine and demi-fine; section 1.6).
- **Status of the vocabulary.** One US retailer page is enough to enter the scheme (CV-10). A second source or a non-US source only adds weight (it feeds `disputed` and the scheme's weight in views); the fetched non-US pages divide by metal, purity or material, not by a three-way tier (section 1.1). The 1stDibs "fine jewelry / fashion jewelry" filter is a platform's own segment label: a dated `platform_tag` (scale class vi), not a tier entry. Schemes are registered once for the whole domain (`scheme_id`, `owner`, `scale_class`, `steps`, `direction`, `kinds_served`, `sources`), so the tier vocabulary is domain-wide, not per category; any further scheme found at the stage-3 harvest (a maker's own ladder, an auction house's class, a grader's) enters on one fetched source.
- **Derivation.** Where possible the tier is computed from attributes (`fineness`, plating, `stone`, `origin`) so that a reading of Borsheims cannot set it by hand. The mapping from attributes to the three values needs one fetched statement (**†**); Borsheims divides by materials and price.
- **Spec attributes, not tier entries (scale class i):**
  - Metal fineness, with the standard named: UK Hallmarking Act carat table (9k = 375, 14k = 585, 18k = 750, 22k = 916.6; platinum 950) (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1973/43/data.html); APP minimum 14k for fresh piercings. Other countries' fineness standards are added on one fetched page each (**†**); none is a condition of the UK table's entry.
  - Stone grade: the GIA 4Cs scale for loose natural diamonds is one entry in the grading-scale registry (C16, `scale_type` measured spec), served to stone-set kinds such as the solitaire ring; no grade of one stone is stored. Gem reports for other stones were not fetched.
  - Ring size (ISO 8653), chain length and gauge (C6). `standard_met` (C17): the APP materials standard (ASTM F138 / ISO 5832-1) on body-jewellery named forms whose maker page states it.
- **Price band.** The C4(d) USD band, from a dated, sourced list price, computed on maker, line and named-form rows; fine jewellery reaches the upper bands (up to over 5,000,000 USD). No A3 price source has been fetched yet (**†**).
- **Preference:** design, motif and fashion.
- **Collector value:** maker mark and period (CVB; 1stDibs), which is the maker ledger's job, not a tier.

How it appears: tier entries and `platform_tag`s in the ledgers; `fineness` (375 to 999), `stone`, `origin` (natural / cultured / laboratory-grown), `chain_pattern` and `post_type` as attributes; never a level. Customs headings 7113, 7116 and 7117 are evidence only (C17, CV-36); the UK hallmarking requirement is a row in the jurisdiction ledger.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought, in three ways:
- *Houses and designers* in fine jewellery (Cartier: named collections such as Love, Trinity, Juste un Clou, Clash, Panthère). 1stDibs gives every maker its own creator page.
- *Costume-jewellery makers* (Trifari, Coro, Miriam Haskell, Eisenberg, Weiss), identified by mark and dated by mark features (CVB guide).
- *Retail brands with series* (Tanishq, Chow Tai Fook): CTF's collection facet holds licensed-character and zodiac series (Disney, Chibi Maruko Chan, Year of the Horse, Palace Museum), which are line entries and collection tags.

**Maker ledger: yes (decided), one row per maker in the domain-wide ledger of C1.** Admission (C1, CV-5): one fetched page (a maker or shop page, a catalogue, an auction or marketplace listing, a museum or trade record) admits a maker; the ledger is seeded first from existing catalogues and registries (the 1stDibs creator pages, costume-jewellery maker-mark guides) and grows without a cap. Houses, artisans, ateliers and workshops are ledger rows, never kinds.
- *Core columns (C1):* `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class` (multi-valued); `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `licensor_id`; `authority_id`; `scene_tags` (via the C8 tables, core or adjacent strength); `price_band` (C4(d), computed); `sources`. `known_for_kinds` is not stored: it is derived from the named-form ledger.
- *`maker_class` for A3:* house or manufacture (Cartier, Pandora); costume or fashion brand (Trifari, Coro, Miriam Haskell, Eisenberg, Weiss); retail own-label (Tanishq, Chow Tai Fook lines); licensee or diffusion where a house licenses a line to another maker; artisan or atelier (artist-jewellers, goldsmith workshops); independent; supplier where a clasp or chain maker matters.
- *A3 extension column:* `marks` (maker's marks and hallmark notes). This is a note about the maker's mark features (CVB), not a record of one object's hallmark (C16).
- *Separate tables, not columns:* **lines and collections** (`line_id`, `maker_id`, `name`, `kinds` the line spans, `line_rank`, `built_by_maker_id`, `licensor_id`, `years`, `sources`; Cartier Love spans ring, bracelet, necklace and earrings, and the CTF licensed-character series carry a `licensor_id`); **tier entries** (section 5); **named forms** (section 3.1, with `maker_bound`); **variants** (below); the **maker-relations table** (OEM, supplier, licensed by, rebrand of); and the standing **school table** (traditions of makers, linked by `school_id`; candidates are the kundan, polki, meenakari and jadau workshop traditions named by the Indian sources, **†**). The known-for list is derived from the named-form table, not stored.
- *Variants (C3, CV-8).* The variant ledger (`variant_id`, `named_form_id`, `variant_code`, `variant_type`, `partner`, `year`, `sources`) is filled wherever a community compares variants (colourways, materials, sizes, references, collaborations, limited editions, retro or re-issues) and grows without a cap. A model or revision is a named form only when it is a distinct target; otherwise it is a variant. A tier entry sits on a variant only under C4(b).
- Collections link to the named forms they contain and never become a level, because they cut across forms.

**Compatibility ledger (C13).** The Pandora platform sub-case goes here, not in the tree: `platform_id` Pandora Moments; `owner` Pandora; `kinds_served` charm bracelet, charm, ring (sizes 48 to 60), with a stated maximum number of charms per bracelet type (Pandora sizing sheet); `standard_ref` none (proprietary), with `compatibility_relation` rows (fits, requires) to the charm and bracelet named forms. The piercing thread systems (threadless, internally threaded) are a second entry owned by makers, with APP as the standard reference for internally tapped posts irrespective of brand. Platforms are never levels.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds (C7):** the canonical form first (band ring before signet ring; stud before drop), then the secondary key the sub-family states, then culturally named kinds last. The secondary key is: in sub-families 1, 2 and 4 to 8, simplicity of the form (plain before elaborate, as the Wikipedia list and the Tanishq type list order them); in sub-family 3, the fitting system (barbell, ring, stud, navel, stretched-ear). The gauge attribute is ordered by spec (22g to 00g, https://www.bodycandy.com/blogs/news/body-piercing-gauge-size-chart).

**Named forms (C7, CV-21):** non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically (borla, mandoria, ucu, nath, kada, chandbali and the others, each with its region tag); then maker-bound named forms, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically. Maker ladders (`line_rank`), newest-first and typology orders are generated views, not stored order. Collectors date pieces by mark and period (CVB guide; 1stDibs period facet), so the years matter; they still need attested sources (**†**).

**Variants:** by `variant_code` or year.

## 9. Boundaries

All rules below are decided (section 12); none is open.

1. **A3 versus F6 and C4 (loose stones, beads, findings).** An unset gemstone or loose pearl, natural, cultured or laboratory-grown, is F6 when undrilled and loose; a pearl or gem bead drilled for stringing is C4; strung or mounted as a piece for wearing it is A3 (K-78). A gem simulant is not a gemstone: A3 or C4 by form (K-77). Loose beads, clasps, findings and jewellery-making tools, jewellery torches and soldering kits included, are C4 (step 28, C4-O2). A replacement part made for one jewellery piece (an earring back, a chain extender) is A3 under S3, in sub-family 9; one fetched page showing it sold on its own admits it, otherwise it is a C4 finding (**†**). Customs separates pearl or stone articles (7116) from jewellery (7113), as evidence only (C17).
2. **A3 versus A5 (watches and watch chains) — W1 (K-20, now in the key).** A watch is A5, and a watch strap follows the watch under S3. **A watch chain, Albert or fob chain made to attach a pocket watch is A5 under S3; a decorative fob, seal or charm worn on a chain without a watch is A3.** Smartwatches are D2 and smart rings are D2.
3. **A3 versus F4 (religious objects).** An amulet, cross or medal worn as an ornament is A3, and so is a cremation or memorial pendant, ring or other ash jewellery made to be worn; a keepsake urn that is not worn stays F4 (F4-O1). A devotional medal is F4 when loose and A3 when made with a chain or bail to be worn; a medal struck to commemorate or reward is F3 (K-107). A strand made for counting prayers (with counter, tassel or guru bead) is F4; a necklace of the same beads made as jewellery is A3 (K-111). A token or charm made to bring luck or protection and carried, not worn (omamori, a carried evil-eye charm), is F4; worn, A3; issued for money, F3 (K-117). Worn Masonic and fraternal regalia follow A1 and A3; their ritual implements are F4 (K-115).
4. **A3 versus F3, F5, H2 (badges, medals, tags, crowns, coins).** A badge or patch worn as ornament is A3; an identity or access badge is F5; a medal or decoration awarded is F3; a medical alert bracelet is H2. **A crown or tiara made to be worn as an ornament is A3; a crown that is regalia of an office is F3; a toy crown is E1 (K-123).** A coin or ingot made or mounted with a bail, loop or bezel to be worn is A3; one without a mount is F3 (K-122): a form test, not the motive. CTF lists "Gold Coin / Gold Bar" under jewellery; the bare coin or bar is F3 and the gold-ingot pendant is A3.
5. **A3 versus A1, H1 and A4 (dress, hair, bags).** Cufflinks, tie clips and tie pins are A3; ties and belts are A1. **Head ornaments (K-129):** worn on the head and held by its own band, crown, elastic or ties (hat, bonnet, headband, decorative hairband, headbow on a band, fascinator on a band or headpiece) is A1; fixed into the hair by a pin, comb, clip or slide (hairpin, kanzashi, jewelled clip, hair comb, bow on a clip) is A3; plain functional clips, ties and bands are H1. A stick-on bindi is A3; kumkum and other powder or paint applied to the skin is H1 (K-129). A stick-on temporary jewel is filed with the bindi by the same ornament test (applied by default). **Buckles (K-21):** a belt buckle, plain or decorative, is a part of a belt and is A1 under S3. Keyrings and bag charms are A4 under S3 (made for bags).
6. **A3 versus H3 and H2 (adult and medical).** Ornamental piercing jewellery, including intimate-site barbells, is A3; a collar made only as an ornament is A3, while a collar, cuff, armbinder or harness made with fixing points to restrain a person is H3 (K-81); objects made for sexual stimulation or restraint are H3; surgical implants and medical retainers are H2.
7. **A3 versus B1, B4 (storage and display).** Jewellery boxes, trays and stands are B1 (K-191; Etsy and CHRIST list "jewellery storage" beside jewellery); display ornaments and figurines in gold are B4 (CTF "Display Ornament / Gift").
8. **A3 versus E1 (play).** Toy crowns and other dress-up accessories made as toys are E1; a real ornament made in a child's size stays A3, with size and user as attributes (C6, C15). K-130 writes this into step 17, so step 17 decides play jewellery and the draft's reliance on key order is gone.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure.** Category A3 > **sub-family** (site of wear: head and hair; ear; neck and chest; arm and wrist; hand and finger; torso, waist and clothing; leg and foot; plus one fitting-defined class, body and facial piercing jewellery, a second stated exception to the level-1 axis; plus the closing parts sub-family, C5(d); 9 in all) > **leaf** (form family, about 25 est., 22 in the worked tree; stored as `leaf = null` in a sub-family of 12 kinds or fewer) > **kind** (generic type, about 110 to 140 est., 54 named in the worked tree) > **named form** (maker-bound or not, uncapped). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels.** Fineness, metal, stone, origin, chain pattern, era, region, culture, user, occasion (wedding and engagement included), motive, technique and scene are tags or attributes; maker, line, tier entry, variant, compatibility platform, school and legal class are ledger rows. Fine / demi-fine / fashion is a tier entry on makers, lines and named forms, never on a kind. Cartier-style collections are line rows linked to named forms across kinds.
