# A3 Jewellery — structure memo (research draft)

*Research, not a decision. Sources are in `verify/out/S2_A3_sources.tsv` (28 fetched pages; 10 searches, 28 fetches used). Every "source divides X as Y" claim below cites a fetched URL. Estimates of counts are the researcher's, marked "est.".*

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** A3 Jewellery (domain A, Worn & Carried).

**Membership test (CATEGORIES_v0_6, key step 17, third branch):** an ornament worn on the body, hair or clothing: rings, necklaces, bracelets, body jewellery, brooches, pins, badges and patches worn as ornament, hair ornaments, cufflinks, worn amulets.

**Notes that bind (S1b):** identity and access badges are F5; smart rings are D2; medical alert bracelets and tags are H2; tassels and cords worn on a cap or gown are A1. Step 14's note: anything worn as an ornament, including amulets worn as pendants, rings or bracelets, is A3 (so a cremation pendant or a cross pendant is A3), while prayer beads and rudraksha beads are F4. Step 7's note: unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6 whether natural, cultured or laboratory-grown. Step 28: loose beads and jewellery-making tools are C4. Step 15: medals and decorations are F3. Step 16: watches are A5; smartwatches D2. Step 17's own note: plain hair clips, bands and ties, and wigs, are H1.

**Appendix A modifiers that apply here and so may never be a level:** region and culture, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, user (gender, child, adult), occasion (wedding, festival), motive (heirloom, memorial), origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), material, condition, edition.

**Precedence sentences touching A3:** see section 9.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and the traditional-jewellery literature

- **Body jewellery:** the buyer's questions are placement, gauge, length or inner diameter, then material (https://www.body-jewelry.com/blog/piercing-size-guide.html). The Association of Professional Piercers lists style by placement ("rings for some areas, straight or curved bars for others, nostril screw or labret stud for specific placements") and sets a materials standard (ASTM F138 / ISO 5832-1 steel, implant titanium, 14k+ gold, platinum, niobium, lead-free glass; gold-plated, filled and vermeil not acceptable for fresh piercings) (https://safepiercing.org/jewelry-for-initial-piercings). Gauge is a thickness attribute from wire gauge, identical across materials (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_jewellery ; BM25 chart above).
- **Indian bridal jewellery** is divided **head to toe by body part**, not by material: bindi, maang tikka (Rajasthani borla, Maharashtrian mandoria), nath (nose ring), karn phool / earrings (jhumka, chandbali), haar and mangalsutra, bajubandh (upper-arm band), bangles (regional: shankha pola, chuda), haath phool or aarsi (rings joined by chains to a bracelet), kamarbandh (waist), payal (anklets, silver because gold on the feet is held inauspicious) (https://www.culturalindia.net/weddings/wedding-traditions/solah-shringar.html). A retailer guide groups Indian jewellery as hair and head, ear, neck, hand and arm, waist (https://www.mirraw.com/blogs/2025/12/25/the-ultimate-guide-to-indian-jewelry-history-types-regional-styles-bridal-traditions). Techniques (kundan, polki, meenakari, filigree) appear as embellishments, not categories.
- **African beadwork** is described by cultural group and by the **object worn**: Zulu "love letters", armbands, waistbands, anklets, bandoliers and the long white *ucu* neck strand (https://collections.museumsvictoria.com.au/articles/16600); Maasai collars and neck rings that change with age grade, Yoruba beaded crown (https://bowers.org/index.php/collection/collections-blog/the-social-beadwork-the-use-of-beads-in-sub-saharan-africa); Zulu love-letter belt, Maasai beaded necklace, Fang anklet and Yoruba bracelet currency (https://umaine.edu/hudsonmuseum/online-exhibits-2/body-adornment-ethnographic-jewelry-from-around-the-world/africa). Bead colour is a symbol vocabulary, not a form (https://www.kirtilals.com/blog/african-maasai-zulu).

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- **1stDibs:** facets are stone, period (centuries and decades), gender, stone cut, carat weight and a **jewelry type** filter splitting "fine jewelry" (321,324 listings) from "fashion jewelry" (32,874); each maker has its own creator page (https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry).
- **oldthing.ch (Switzerland):** the tree is **material-first** (Goldschmuck, Silberschmuck, Perlenschmuck, Modeschmuck und Luxus-Accessoires, Edelsteine) and repeats the same form list (Anhänger, Armbänder, Armreifen, Broschen und Nadeln, Ketten, Ohrringe, Ringe, Sets) under each material; antique jewellery is split by period (Barock, Rokoko, Art Deco) and folk jewellery by culture (https://oldthing.ch/de/Silberschmuck/Ketten?ic=48). This is the one source that puts material above form, and it shows the cost: five copies of one form list.
- **Costume jewellery collectors** identify by **maker mark** (Trifari, Coro, Miriam Haskell, Eisenberg, Weiss) and date by mark features (copyright symbol after the mid-1950s, wartime sterling 1942 to 1947, patent numbers) (https://californiavintagebuyers.com/guides/costume-jewelry-makers-marks).

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Customs (HS / EU Combined Nomenclature, chapter 71):** 7113 articles of jewellery of precious metal or metal clad with precious metal (7113.11 silver, 7113.19 other precious metal, 7113.20 clad); 7116 articles of pearls or stones; **7117 imitation jewellery** (7117.11 cufflinks and studs of base metal, 7117.19 other base metal, 7117.90 other). Note 9 defines "articles of jewellery" as small objects of personal adornment (rings, bracelets, necklaces, brooches, earrings, watch-chains, fobs, pendants, tiepins, cufflinks, dress-studs, religious or other medals and insignia) and also pocket articles such as snuff boxes and prayer beads; note 11 keeps dress-combs, hair-slides and hairpins out of imitation jewellery (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/eur/2019/1776/annex/I/chapter/71/adopted/data.xht?view=snippet&wrap=true). Trade law divides by **material**, because duty depends on it.
- **UK Hallmarking Act 1973:** fineness standards by parts per thousand; carat table 9 = 375, 12 = 500, 14 = 585, 15 = 625, 18 = 750, 22 = 916.6; platinum minimum 950 (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1973/43/data.html).
- **ISO 8653:2016** defines and measures ring sizes (sizes 41 to 76) and is the only ISO jewellery form standard found (https://www.iso.org/standard/65408.html).
- **GIA** grades loose natural diamonds on the 4Cs; the report attaches to the stone, not to a mounted piece (https://www.gia.edu/gem-lab-service/diamond-grading).
- **APP** (above) is the body-jewellery materials standard.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **Wikipedia, List of jewellery types** groups by **site on the body**: hair ornaments, arms, hands, body, feet, "special functions" (signet, thumb ring), components (cameo, findings, locket, medallion, pendant), plus separate lists of gem and non-gem materials (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_jewellery_types).
- **Hudson Museum, Bowers Museum and Museums Victoria** (above) group ethnographic jewellery by culture, then object.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

**Agree:** the core forms are ring, necklace/chain, bracelet, earring, pendant, brooch/pin; every retailer navigates by these first (Tanishq, CTF, Etsy, CHRIST, Cartier). Reference works and Indian sources group the same forms by body site. Metal, stone, karat, period, price and occasion are always **filters beside the form**, except at oldthing and in customs law.

**Split the same objects differently:**
- Pendant and charm: categories of their own (Tanishq pendants; CTF pendants and charms) or a component (Wikipedia: pendant, locket, medallion listed under components).
- Bangle versus bracelet: one group at Tanishq ("bracelets & bangles") but separate at CTF ("Bracelets / Anklets" and "Bangles"); anklets join bracelets at CTF.
- Watches sit inside "jewelry" at Etsy and 1stDibs ("Jewelry & Watches"); A5 owns them here.
- Gold coin and bar are listed under jewellery at CTF; F3 owns them here. "Display ornament/gift" (CTF) is B4 here. Jewellery storage (Etsy, CHRIST) is B1 here.
- Silver: "fine" at Borsheims and precious at customs (7113), but also the base of "demi-fine" (sterling, vermeil) at Borsheims. The fine / demi-fine / fashion line is a retail convention, not a standard.
- Body jewellery is its own category (Etsy, Wikipedia) although it includes ear jewellery (plugs, tunnels, studs).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Buyers choose by form first, then narrow by metal, stone, price and occasion.** Evidence:
- Every retailer's top-level navigation is a form list (Tanishq types, CTF categories, Etsy categories, CHRIST product type). Metal, occasion and collection come after (Tanishq, CTF pages above). Cartier's navigation is **form (rings, bracelets, necklaces, earrings) crossed with named collections** (Love, Trinity, Juste un Clou, Panthère) that repeat under every form (https://www.cartier.com/en-sg/jewellery/collections).
- Buyer questions in body jewellery start from **placement**, because placement fixes the form, gauge and length (APP; body-jewelry.com; BM25).
- Indian tradition and museum reference lists start from **body site** (head, ear, nose, neck, arm, hand, waist, foot).
- Resale markets start from **maker and period** (1stDibs creator pages; costume-jewellery maker marks) or **material** (oldthing).
- Spec facets that follow: ring size (ISO 8653; CHRIST ring-size facet), chain length, gauge, karat, carat weight, clarity (Tanishq).

So: form (what it is) is the first choice at retail; body site is the first choice in reference and in piercing; maker is the first choice in the resale and collector market. Gem and metal are filters in every retail source.

## 3. Candidate structures

Three candidates were tested. Candidate A is recommended. In every candidate, **metal, gem, fineness, price tier, era, region, maker, occasion and user are tags or ledgers, never levels** (Appendix A).

### 3.1 Candidate A (recommended): site of wear > form family > kind > named form

| Level | Name | One stated axis | Count (est.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | the part of the body or dress the piece is made to adorn (head and hair; ear; neck and chest; arm and wrist; hand and finger; torso, waist and clothing; leg and foot) **plus one fitting-defined class, piercing-fit body jewellery** (see the exception below) | 8 |
| 2 | Leaf | form family: the word a shop or museum uses as its category (earrings, necklaces, pendants and charms, bangles and kadas, brooches and pins) | about 25 |
| 3 | Kind | generic type of piece (hoop earring, tennis bracelet, signet ring, maang tikka) | about 110 to 140 |
| 4 | Named form | model, cut or iconic design (Cartier Love bangle, Pandora Moments charm bracelet, kihei chain, Zulu love letter) | many hundreds |

**Exception to state openly.** Sub-families 1 to 7 are sites. Piercing-fit body jewellery (barbell, labret, captive bead ring, nose stud, navel curve, plug, tunnel, taper) cannot be split by site, because one barbell is worn in the ear, brow, tongue and nipple. All sources divide it by **form and gauge**, not site (BM25, BodyCandy, body-jewelry.com, APP, Wikipedia), and Etsy and Wikipedia keep it apart from earrings. The proposed rule: a piece made and sold as gauge-specified piercing-studio jewellery goes to sub-family 3 whatever the site; a decorative earring, nose pin or ear cuff sold through jewellers goes to its site. The owner should decide whether that rule is acceptable (section 10, question 2).

**Where Candidate A breaks (edge objects):**
- *Charm.* A charm fits a bracelet, a chain or a pendant loop; it is filed once, under Pendants and charms, with the carrier recorded as a link. By S3 a charm made for one kind of bracelet stays in A3 anyway.
- *Jewellery set (parure, bridal set).* A set spans several sites. Tanishq lists "necklace set" under neckwear and Etsy has "jewelry sets" as a category (sources above). Proposed: a set is filed under its principal piece when sold as one unit (necklace set, with earrings recorded as contents), and "bridal set" is a generated view; flagged for the owner.
- *Toe ring and thumb ring.* Both are rings by form but sit on foot and hand by site; A puts toe ring at Leg and foot and thumb ring at Finger rings (Wikipedia lists thumb ring under "special functions").
- *Nose pin sold by a jeweller (Tanishq "nosepin") versus a gauge-specified nose stud.* Same object, two vocabularies; the rule above sends both to Piercing-fit body jewellery.

### 3.2 Candidate B: form family > kind > named form (retail-flat)

| Level | Axis |
|---|---|
| 1 Leaf | form family as shops name it (about 14: rings; earrings; necklaces and chains; pendants and charms; bracelets; bangles and kadas; anklets; brooches and pins; badges and patches; cufflinks and tie accessories; hair ornaments; crowns and tiaras; waist and body chains; body piercing jewellery) |
| 2 Kind | generic type |
| 3 Named form | model, cut or design |

This is Candidate A without its first level; the 25 leaf names are the same. It is the navigation of Etsy, CTF, CHRIST, Tanishq and Cartier. Example kinds are those in the tree above.

**Where it breaks:**
- The leaf axis is already mixed: "rings" and "earrings" are forms, "hair ornaments" is a site, "cufflinks" is tied to a garment, "body piercing jewellery" is a fitting system. Etsy's own category list shows the same mixture (rings, earrings, body jewelry, cufflinks, storage, cremation jewelry). It fails "one stated axis per level".
- Indian head-to-toe ornaments (haath phool, kamarbandh, maang tikka) and African regalia (Maasai collar, Zulu love letter, Yoruba beaded crown) do not match a Western form list and land in "other".
- About 14 sibling leaves is a long first level; Candidate A keeps each sibling list at 2 to 5.

### 3.3 Candidate C (rejected): tier or material first (fine > demi-fine > fashion, or gold > silver > pearl > costume)

Used by: 1stDibs' fine / fashion jewelry type filter (https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry), Borsheims' fine / demi-fine / fashion (https://www.borsheims.com/blog/fine-jewelry-vs-fashion-jewelry/), customs 7113 versus 7117, and oldthing's material-first tree (https://oldthing.ch/de/Silberschmuck/Ketten?ic=48).

**Where it breaks:**
- *Gold-plated sterling silver.* Borsheims puts sterling in both "fine" and "demi-fine"; customs treats metal clad with precious metal as 7113.20 but plated base metal as 7117.19.
- *Chanel, Haskell and Trifari costume pieces.* "Fashion" by material, but collected and priced by maker mark and period (CVB maker guide: signed Eisenberg and Haskell pieces sell for hundreds of dollars).
- *Laboratory-grown stones in solid gold:* the metal says fine, the stone's origin says "grown"; Appendix A already lists origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown) as a modifier, so it cannot also decide the tier level.
- *Duplication:* oldthing repeats Anhänger, Armbänder, Ketten, Ohrringe, Ringe under each of five materials.

The line is useful and well attested, but as a **tier modifier** (section 5), not a level.

### 3.4 Gem and metal: form axis or modifier? (argued)

**Modifier.** Reasons:
1. Appendix A names material and origin as modifiers; "unless material is the form" is the only exception.
2. Every retailer in the sample treats them as filters beside the form: Tanishq (jewellery type, karatage, metal, metal colour, clarity), CTF (metal), Borsheims (shop by gemstone, shop by metal), 1stDibs (stone, stone cut, carat weight), CHRIST (material, alloy, gemstone/pearl, gem colour), Rakuten (gold purity, metal-allergy-safe). None makes them the first division of a form tree except oldthing.
3. One piece has several metals and stones, so a material level would not give one home.
4. The same form list recurs under every material (oldthing), so a material level multiplies the tree by 5 without adding a kind.

**Cases where material is the form** (and so may be a kind): a *pearl necklace* (a strand of pearls; customs gives pearls their own heading 7116) and a bead-and-stone *power-stone bracelet* (Rakuten sells them as a type). These are kinds by form (strand, stretch bead bracelet), with stone as attribute. A bare gem is not here: unset gemstones and loose pearls are F6.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (est.):** 110 to 140. Basis: the Wikipedia list of jewellery types gives about 70 form names (hair, arms, hands, body, feet, special functions, components); piercing retailers add about 15 distinct fitting forms (BM25 chart); Indian and African sources add about 25 culturally named forms that retailers sell as categories (maang tikka, nath, jhumka, kada, mangalsutra, haath phool, kamarbandh, payal; Maasai collar, Zulu love letter, ucu). The count overlaps, so the range is wide; it is an estimate, not a census.
- **Named forms (est.):** 800 to 2,000. Cartier's navigation alone lists 16 jewellery collections, each repeated under up to four forms (https://www.cartier.com/en-sg/jewellery/collections); CTF lists about 30 collection facets (https://www.chowtaifook.com/sg/eshop/jewellery); costume-jewellery makers add signature designs (https://californiavintagebuyers.com/guides/costume-jewelry-makers-marks). Chain-link patterns (kihei, ball chain, station chain) and motifs (floral, geometric, heart, evil eye; Tanishq type facet) are **attributes**, not named forms.
- **Where depth concentrates:** finger rings, earrings, necklaces with pendants and chains, and bracelets (the broad leaves); piercing-fit jewellery (many forms, each multiplied by gauge and length, but those are attributes); and maker-defined named forms (collections that cut across forms).
- **Leaf width:** *broad* (30 or more kinds expected): Finger rings, Earrings, Necklaces, Bracelets. *Medium* (8 to 20): Pendants and charms, Brooches and pins, Studs and labrets, Hair ornaments, Bangles and kadas. *Narrow* (fewer than 8): Armlets, Hand ornaments, Anklets and toe rings, Crowns and tiaras, Shirt and tie accessories, Waist and body chains, Navel jewellery, Forehead and parting ornaments.

## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the form that buyers name and compare, such as hoop earring, tennis bracelet, signet ring or labret stud. Below that, metal, stone, karat, motif, length and size are attributes. Maker-designed items are named forms.**

Evidence:
- Retail "type" facets list forms, with motif and style mixed in: Tanishq's type list (hoop, stud, drops, jhumka, ear cuff, ear climber, tennis bracelet, kada, stackable rings, open rings, choker, layered chain, ball chain, station chain, nosepin, vati) and the same facet's motifs (floral, geometric, heart, evil eye) (https://www.tanishq.co.in/shop/jewellery?lang=en_IN). Form words are kinds; motif words are attributes.
- 1stDibs reaches form sub-types inside each category (the page's item links show band-rings and drop-necklaces) and then switches to stone, cut, carat, period and creator facets (https://www.1stdibs.com/jewelry). Quality is compared on those later facets, not on more kinds.
- Body-jewellery buyers compare gauge, length or inner diameter and material **within** a form (barbell, labret, captive bead ring) (https://bm25.com/pages/size-conversion-chart ; https://www.body-jewelry.com/blog/piercing-size-guide.html ; https://safepiercing.org/jewelry-for-initial-piercings). Gauge is a measurement (16g = 1.2 mm), so each gauge is an attribute value, never a kind.
- Ring size is a standardised attribute (ISO 8653, https://www.iso.org/standard/65408.html); CHRIST offers it as a facet (https://www.christ.de/category/herrenschmuck/index.html).
- The diamond's 4Cs attach to the loose stone (https://www.gia.edu/gem-lab-service/diamond-grading), so a solitaire ring is one kind with stone grade as an attribute.
- Makers are compared one level lower: Cartier's collection pages compare "Love" with "Juste un Clou" and "Trinity" (https://www.cartier.com/en-sg/jewellery/collections), and costume-jewellery collectors compare signed Trifari with Coro (CVB guide). These are named forms.

**Does the level differ between sub-families?** Slightly. In Piercing-fit body jewellery the kind is the fitting system plus form (flat-back labret stud, threadless or threaded are attributes). In Neck and chest, **chain** is one kind and the link pattern an attribute, but buyers in Japan treat the kihei pattern as a main comparison (Rakuten chain-type facet, https://www.rakuten.co.jp/category/216129); recommend kihei as a named form of chain. Cultural forms (mangalsutra, maang tikka) are kinds because shops there sell them as categories (Tanishq).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended classification: consensus-ranked, with two spec-ranked attributes attached.** There is no single ladder.
- **Consensus-ranked band:** fine, demi-fine, fashion/costume (Borsheims; 1stDibs "fine jewelry" and "fashion jewelry" filter). The line is not standardised and sources disagree on silver, gold-plate and vermeil (section 1.6). It should be a three-value **tier modifier** on the kind.
- **Spec-ranked attributes (measurable):**
  - Metal fineness: UK Hallmarking Act carat table (9k = 375, 14k = 585, 18k = 750, 22k = 916.6; platinum 950) (https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1973/43/data.html); APP minimum 14k for fresh piercings.
  - Stone grade: GIA 4Cs for natural diamonds (loose only); gem reports for other stones are outside what was fetched.
- **Preference:** design, motif and fashion.
- **Collector value:** maker mark and period (CVB; 1stDibs), which is the maker ledger's job, not a tier.

How it appears: a modifier set on each kind or named form (`tier`: fine / demi-fine / fashion; `fineness`: 375 to 999; `stone`; `origin`: natural / cultured / laboratory-grown), never a level. Customs headings 7113 and 7117 are a useful cross-check column, not a structure.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought, in three ways:
- *Houses and designers* in fine jewellery (Cartier: named collections such as Love, Trinity, Juste un Clou, Clash, Panthère). 1stDibs gives every maker its own creator page.
- *Costume-jewellery makers* (Trifari, Coro, Miriam Haskell, Eisenberg, Weiss), identified by mark and dated by mark features (CVB guide).
- *Retail brands with series* (Tanishq, Chow Tai Fook): CTF's collection facet holds licensed-character and zodiac series (Disney, Chibi Maruko Chan, Year of the Horse, Palace Museum), which are collection tags.

**Platform sub-case.** Pandora sets compatibility: ring sizes 48 to 60, bracelet lengths, and a stated maximum number of charms per bracelet type (Pandora sizing sheet). Threadless and internally threaded piercing systems are also maker-compatible, but APP asks for internally tapped posts irrespective of brand.

**Recommend a maker ledger: yes.** Fields: maker; maker class (house, costume maker, retail brand, artist-jeweller); known-for kinds and named forms; collection names and the forms each spans (Cartier Love: ring, bracelet, necklace, earrings); scene tags; tier; price band; country; era (founding and active dates); mark or hallmark notes; attestation URL. Collections link to the named forms they contain and never become a level, because they cut across forms.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then variants, then culturally specific forms, then named forms ordered by year of introduction.** Reason: the sources all start from the plain classic form (band ring before signet; stud before drop) and treat designs as variations (Wikipedia list, Tanishq type list). Named forms are best ordered chronologically by introduction year, because a maker's collections are launched in sequence and collectors date pieces by mark and period (CVB guide; 1stDibs period facet). The introduction years still need attested sources (S6). In piercing-fit jewellery the kinds are ordered by fitting system and the **gauge attribute is ordered by spec** (22g to 00g, https://www.bodycandy.com/blogs/news/body-piercing-gauge-size-chart).

## 9. Boundaries

1. **A3 versus F6 and C4 (loose stones, beads, findings).** An unset gemstone or loose pearl, natural, cultured or laboratory-grown, is F6; once mounted or strung as a piece for wearing it is A3. Loose beads, clasps, findings and jewellery-making tools are C4. Customs also separates pearl or stone articles (7116) from jewellery (7113).
2. **A3 versus A5 (watches).** A watch is A5, and a watch strap follows the watch under S3; a watch chain or fob worn as an ornament is A3. Smartwatches are D2 and smart rings are D2.
3. **A3 versus F4 (religious objects).** An amulet, cross or medal worn as an ornament is A3, and a cremation or memorial pendant is A3 under the F4 note. Prayer beads and rudraksha made for devotion are F4. The step-14 text lists "prayer beads" explicitly, so a mala worn as a necklace needs a rule (owner question 6); recommended: F4 if sold as prayer beads, A3 if sold as a necklace.
4. **A3 versus F3, F5, H2 (badges, medals, tags).** A badge or patch worn as ornament is A3; an identity or access badge is F5; a medal or decoration awarded is F3; a medical alert bracelet is H2. Gold coin, gold bar and a gold-ingot pendant sold in jewellery shops (CTF lists "Gold Coin / Gold Bar" under jewellery) are F3 when kept as bullion and A3 when made as a pendant.
5. **A3 versus A1, H1 and A4 (dress, hair, bags).** Cufflinks, tie clips and tie pins are A3; ties, belts and plain buckles are A1. Decorative hair combs, hairpins and jewelled clips are A3; plain clips, bands, ties and wigs are H1. Keyrings and bag charms are A4. A stick-on bindi or temporary jewel is an edge case (H1 cosmetic or A3 ornament) for the owner.
6. **A3 versus H3 and H2 (adult and medical).** Ornamental piercing jewellery, including intimate-site barbells, is A3; objects made for sexual stimulation or restraint are H3; surgical implants and medical retainers are H2.
7. **A3 versus B1, B4 (storage and display).** Jewellery boxes, trays and stands are B1 (Etsy and CHRIST list "jewellery storage" beside jewellery); display ornaments and figurines in gold are B4 (CTF "Display Ornament / Gift").
8. **A3 versus E1 (play).** Dress-up and toy jewellery made for play is filed by key order (step 17 before 25): A3 when worn as an ornament.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A. Category A3 > **sub-family** (site of wear, with piercing-fit body jewellery as its own class; 8) > **leaf** (form family, about 25) > **kind** (generic type, about 110 to 140) > **named form** (model, collection piece or iconic design). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels.** Tier, fineness, metal, stone, origin, era, region, culture, user, occasion, motive, technique and maker are tags or a maker ledger. Fine / demi-fine / fashion is a tier modifier. Cartier-style collections are ledger links across forms.

**Choice level:** kind = the form buyers name (hoop earring, tennis bracelet). **Tier type:** consensus-ranked (fine / demi-fine / fashion) with spec-ranked attributes (fineness, stone grade). **Maker centrality:** identity (houses and costume makers), with a platform sub-case (Pandora Moments).

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. Accept a two-step top (site, then form family), or the flat form-leaf Candidate B (same leaf names, one fewer level).
2. Piercing-fit rule: should all gauge-specified piercing jewellery sit in one sub-family whatever the site (recommended), and should fashion earrings stay in Ear?
3. Culturally named generic forms as kinds (mangalsutra, maang tikka, nath, kada, jhumka, haath phool): recommended yes, since shops there sell them as categories.
4. Wedding band and engagement ring: occasion tags on band and solitaire rings (Appendix A) or kinds (Wikipedia lists engagement, wedding, class and championship rings as ring forms)?
5. Jewellery sets (parure, bridal set): filed under the principal piece with contents recorded, or a separate kind?
6. Prayer-bead necklaces and malas: F4 or A3 rule (section 9, item 3).
7. Gold-ingot pendants and coin jewellery: where bullion ends and ornament begins (section 9, item 4).
8. Confirm the maker ledger fields and the `tier` / `fineness` / `origin` modifier schema.
9. Stick-on bindis, temporary jewels and decorative hairbands: A3, H1 or A1.
10. Sourcing still to do under S6: pin badge, patch, hat pin, kilt pin, lapel pin (kinds named in the framework or Wikipedia list but not yet attested by a fetched retail page), and a fuller piercing glossary (spiral, claw, surface bar).
