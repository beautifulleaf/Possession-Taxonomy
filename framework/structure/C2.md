## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C2 Garden & Grounds.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 29): *made for **growing plants or keeping gardens, grounds, paths, pools and drives**, powered or not: garden tools, mowers and ride-on mowers, trimmers, leaf blowers, pressure washers, snow clearers, wheelbarrows, planters and plant pots, plant care products, grow lights, irrigation, greenhouses and polytunnels, compost bins, artificial turf, garden stone, above-ground and inflatable swimming pools, pool cleaning equipment, test kits and pumps.* Notes on the step: garden furniture is B1; garden ornaments, statues and gnomes are B4; paddling pools made for play are E1. Category line: "tools, machines and supplies for growing plants and keeping grounds and pools."

**Notes and precedence sentences elsewhere that name C2:**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): "Never parts: ... anything for growing plants (C2)". A part, attachment or consumable made for one kind of C2 machine (a mower blade, a pool pump cartridge) follows that machine.
- Step 4 (G2): greenhouses and polytunnels are C2, not G2; in-ground pools, pergolas, gazebos, summerhouses, garden rooms and sheds are G2; pop-up gazebos are B1.
- Step 7 (F6): garden stone is C2 when a natural material is sold for that use.
- Step 11 (G1): wheelbarrows and garden carts are C2; sack trucks and dollies are C1. A machine that carries its operator while doing other work (ride-on mower) goes by that work, so it is C2.
- Step 12 (C7): equipment for a household garden or allotment is C2, "including garden and compact lawn tractors"; commercial and farm machines are C7.
- Step 10 (E4): machetes and axes made for cutting wood or vegetation are "C1 or C2" (the note does not say which; see section 10).
- Step 23 (C6): pool and soil test kits are C2; weather instruments are C6.
- Step 27 (B9) and step 6 (C3): seeds, bulbs, spores and living plants are C3, so "plants" and "seed" are not C2 even when sold in a grow kit.
- Step 30: garden furniture and plant stands are B1 (tried after C2 in the key, but named there); B3 lighting excludes grow lights; B7 holds hot tubs, water heaters, filters and softeners for bathing; B8 holds "poisons and repellents" for the home; B6 holds cleaning products.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object (robot mower) is filed by its task; connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 32: C1 holds tools made to cut, shape, join, measure or power; C8 holds fencing, timber, paving, fuels, firewood.

**Enthusiast and authority sources.**
- **RHS (UK)** [S12, S18]: mowers have "four main styles ... cylinder, rotary, hover and ride-on", with robotic and mulching mowers as variations and strimmers or scythes for long grass; the advice starts from the kind of lawn. For greenhouses the RHS divides the choice by frame (aluminium or wood) and glazing (glass 90 % of light, polycarbonate 83 %, acrylic 85 %); polytunnels and cold frames appear as alternatives (that alternatives passage was seen in a search excerpt of the fetched RHS URL, not in the fetched text).
- **English Garden buyers guide (UK)** [S13]: the first question is "what kind of lawn do you want" (cylinder for striped perfection), then lawn size; sections of "best" lists run per type (cylinder, rotary, hover, robotic, cordless), so type and power source are both used as list headings.
- **BBC Gardeners World secateurs test (UK)** [S14] and a garden designer's Felco-versus-Okatsune comparison [S30]: one comparison table covers bypass, anvil and ratchet secateurs together, with columns Type, Cutting capacity, Weight, Warranty; the quality signal is the maker line (Okatsune 103 and Felco models), not the type.
- **Japanese farming-site mower guide** [S35]: mowers are ordered by lawn area in tsubo (scissors and clippers to about 5; manual reel to about 100; self-propelled and ride-on from 100; robot to about 90), then by maker (Honda, Husqvarna Automower, Makita, Ryobi).
- **Grow-light guide** [S38]: lighting is sold and ranked by PPF, PPFD and PPE (µmol/J), with top white LED fixtures reaching 2.8 to 3.0 µmol/J and a certified-products list (DLC) of over 1,200 products.
- **Wikipedia** [S22, S19]: "Garden tools can be divided into hand tools and power tools"; greenhouse, glasshouse and hothouse are terms by material and heating, with the cold frame as the smallest example.

**Collectors and the resale market.** The only collector source found is Old Garden Tools [S23], a private "virtual museum" of several thousand vintage and antique tools (an early cucumber straightener, an 1850s elm wheelbarrow), mostly English with a French display, arranged as period tool types; it hires the tools out as props. No auction or price guide was fetched, so there is no evidence here of a maker-led collecting scene beyond Felco and Okatsune use-value talk [S30].

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **UNSPSC** [S11]: class 27112000 "Agriculture, forestry and garden handtools" (Segment 27 Tools and General Machinery, family Hand tools) holds machetes, spades, rakes, shovels, bulb planter, pruning saw, sickle, lawn aerator, border shear, hand cultivator, short-handled hoe, garden broom, watering can, garden sieve, hedge trimmer, garden shredder, brush cutter, garden chainsaw, lopper and sod cutter. It treats hand and powered garden tools as one list (power source is not a split).
- **HS/TARIC** [S20, S17]: heading 8201 divides agricultural and horticultural hand tools by tool family (spades and shovels; mattocks, picks, hoes and rakes; axes and billhooks; secateurs and one-handed pruners; hedge shears and two-handed shears; other); heading 8432 divides soil-preparation machinery (ploughs, harrows/scarifiers/cultivators/weeders/hoes, seeders and planters, manure spreaders and fertiliser distributors, lawn or sports-ground rollers).
- **EU Fertilising Products Regulation 2019/1009** [S21, S28]: fertilising products are "divided into different product function categories"; the fetched annex text names PFC 1(C) inorganic fertiliser and PFC 5 inhibitor, and the full list seen in the search result for the same legislation.gov.uk page is 1 fertiliser (organic, organo-mineral, inorganic), 2 liming material, 3 soil improver, 4 growing medium, 5 inhibitor, 6 plant biostimulant, 7 blend. This is a function-based tree for the consumables.
- **EU Directive 2000/14/EC** [S31]: Annex I defines outdoor machines (lawnmowers including the cylinder mower, lawn trimmers and edge trimmers, brush cutters, leaf blowers, and others) and sets sound limits for lawnmowers by cutting width, so a standards body treats "lawnmower" as one kind with width as a spec.
- **ANSI/APSP/ICC-5 and the APSP family** [S29]: separate standards for residential inground pools (ICC-5), aboveground/onground residential pools (APSP-4), permanently installed residential spas (APSP-3) and public pools (APSP-1): pools are divided by installation and use, which matches the key's G2/C2 line.

**Museums and reference works.** Wikipedia's Garden tool article states the hand-tool / power-tool division and its navbox has a middle group of manual-or-powered tools (lawn aerator, lawn mower, lawn sweeper, plough) and a power-tool group (brushcutter, chainsaw...) **Where the sources agree.** (1) Every large retailer and the UNSPSC class separate a tool/machine group, a container group, a water group, a consumables group (soil, feed, chemicals), a protected-growing group and a pool group. (2) Hand-cutting tools are divided by task (pruning, digging, raking, weeding) in B&Q, Hornbach, Engei Net and HS 8201. (3) Mower type is the first decision in UK and Japanese guides, ahead of power source [S12, S13, S35].

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- Hand versus powered is the top split in B&Q, Bunnings, Leroy Merlin and Wikipedia [S3, S2, S24, S22], but UNSPSC and the HS tree put hedge trimmers, brush cutters and shredders beside hand tools, and Hornbach files Gartenmaschinen apart from Gartenwerkzeug [S11, S6, S7].
- Mowers: under Outdoor Power Equipment (Home Depot), Garden Power Tools (B&Q), Gartenmaschinen (Hornbach), 園芸機械 (Cainz) [S1, S3, S7, S5].
- Pots and stands: Home Depot puts Plant Stands and Planters under Outdoor Living; Cainz puts pots, stands and greenhouses together; Hornbach keeps Blumentopf-Untersetzer, Gießkannen and Pflanzensprüher inside Blumentöpfe [S1, S5, S9].
- Greenhouses: with pots (Cainz), with Garden Centre (Home Depot), with Gartenhäuser & Carports (Hornbach's related-category list [S6]).
- Pest and weed control: a separate Pest Control branch (Bunnings), Pflanzenschutz (Hornbach), 駆除剤/除草剤 (Cainz), Traitement et protection plus Anti-nuisibles (Leroy Merlin) [S2, S6, S5, S24].
- Pools: Hornbach includes Outdoor Whirlpools, Poolroboter and Planschbecken in one Pools branch [S10]; Leslie's has Spa/Hot Tub as a Pool Type facet [S15]; the key splits them across C2, B7, E1 and G2.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence shows no single axis; the first choice depends on the leaf.

- **Job to be done** is the axis of the broad navigation and the how-to content. B&Q offers "Autumn garden jobs" [S3]; Engei Net divides tools into cutting, digging, carrying, measuring [S34]; HS 8201 divides by tool family tied to a task [S20]; Gardena's watering groups are situations (balcony, planted rows, hedge) [S25]. For hand tools, buyers decide "what am I doing" (prune, dig, rake, weed) before maker.
- **Form or mechanism** is the first choice for lawn machines: cylinder, rotary, hover, robotic, ride-on [S12, S13]. The Directive defines the cylinder mower by its shearing action [S31]. A Japanese guide instead orders mowers by lawn area [S35]; UK guides put lawn size second [S13]. Cutting mechanism and operator relation (walk-behind, ride-on, robot) are therefore the first axes, and power source follows (English Garden has a "cordless" list, B&Q and Home Depot filter by power).
- **Fit or size** matters second: cutting width (30 to 35 cm for a small lawn [S13]), lawn area [S35], cutting capacity in secateurs [S14], pot inside dimensions [S27], canopy area for grow lights [S36].
- **Material** is the first visible facet for pots (Bunnings sub-categories terracotta, glazed, metal, lightweight; Hornbach popular filters Keramik, Holz, Beton, Ton) [S4, S9], and for greenhouses (frame and glazing) [S18]. Appendix A makes material a modifier unless it is the form; here it works as an attribute, with bonsai pots (unglazed, glazed, slabs, kusamono) the main case where the container form differs [S27].
- **Performance spec** leads in grow lights (PPFD at canopy, PPE) [S36, S38], pool filters (filter area, cartridge versus DE versus sand) [S15] and cordless machines (battery size) [S33].
- **Maker or system** leads once the owner owns a platform: Gardena combisystem heads fit Gardena handles [S32]; Stihl AK tools take AK batteries [S33]; Gardena Micro-Drip parts connect to Gardena master units [S25]. Platform choice precedes the kind in these cases.
- **Scene** (balcony, allotment, indoor hydroponics, bonsai) is a store-level view, not a first filter: Gardena sets are labelled balcony set and terrace set but sit in one product tree [S25].
