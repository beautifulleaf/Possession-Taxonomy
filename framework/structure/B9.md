## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B9 Smoking & Vaping, in domain B Home.

**Membership test (placement key step 27, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md):** tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products in any form, and other plant products taken for a stimulant or psychoactive effect rather than as food or drink: cigarettes, cigars, loose, pipe and chewing tobacco, snuff, snus and nicotine pouches, heated-tobacco sticks, e-liquids and pods, cannabis flower, resin, concentrates, edibles, drinks, tinctures and CBD products, herbal smoking blends, betel quid and areca nut sold for chewing, khat, kratom; **or implements made for smoking, vaping or preparing these** (pipes, bongs, hookahs and hookah coals, vapes, pocket lighters, ashtrays, cigar cutters, cigarette cases, rolling papers and machines, herb grinders).

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch B9:**
- Step 1 note: tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products are step 27 in any form, including edibles, drinks and supplements, so none of them is "OUT" as a prepared food or drink. Medicines, vitamins and supplements are step 2.
- Step 2 note and step 27 note: CBD products are B9, even when sold as supplements. Medicinal nicotine and cannabis medicines are H2 only under step 2's H1/H2 sentence (a labelled main purpose to treat, relieve or prevent a named condition, or an active ingredient sold only as a medicine in every sourced jurisdiction); prescription or licence status alone goes to the jurisdiction ledger (C17) and does not move the object.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Retail navigation (S7, S8, S9, S15, S22, S23):** the first click is **what kind of thing**: a cigar, a pipe, pipe tobacco, a vape kit, e-liquid, coils, pouches, heated tobacco, a hookah. The second click differs by sub-domain:
- **Cigars:** origin, strength, size and wrapper are all one-level filters beside each other (S1, S22). Guides tell buyers the shape and size set intensity and burn time, and that a "benchmark" Corona is about 5.5 to 6 inches by ring gauge 42 to 44 (S3, S2). Habanos notes that bigger cigars give richer, cooler smoke (S24).
- **Pipes:** shape or bend, then maker (S30, S22, S15). Pipeonline's buying table maps shape to smoker preference (first pipe: Billiard, Apple, Canadian; cooler smoke: Canadian, Churchwarden) (S30).
- **Pipe tobacco:** blend family (aromatic versus English/natural) first, then cut and flavour (S5, S25, S22, S23).
- **Vapes:** device architecture (disposable/big puff, pod, pen, box mod) first, then experience level (S8, S9), and for e-liquid the nicotine form (freebase, salt, nicotine free) then size (S7, S8). The EU rule that fixes refill containers at 10 ml and nicotine at 20 mg/ml (Article 20, search excerpt only) makes bottle size and strength compliance specs; S7 shows the market sorting 10 ml freebase from 50 ml and 100 ml shortfills.
- **Cannabis:** form (flower, pre-roll, concentrate, edible, vape, topical) first for buyers and regulators (S10, S11, S12), and route to the body second (S13).
- **Hookah:** the pipe, then bowl and heat, then tobacco flavour family (S17, S18).

**Conclusion:** the first axis is **form (what the object is), with the consumable-versus-hardware split as the very first fork**. Maker is the second click for pipes, cigars, lighters and vape hardware. Fit or size matters for cigars (ring gauge), papers (paper size, S31) and pouches (portion size, S21). Mechanism matters for vape hardware (disposable, pod, mod) and lighters.


## 3. Candidate structures

The category holds two kinds of object that every source keeps apart: **things taken** (the tobacco, nicotine, cannabis or plant product itself, including its package) and **things used** (pipes, vapes, lighters, papers). Because the framework says "one home per object" and files the implements here too, the first design choice is how to hold the two together. Three candidates follow. The maximum depth under the category is four levels: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (C5).

**Codes.** Sub-families are **B9.P** (Products), **B9.H** (Implements & hardware) and the closing **B9.X** (Parts and accessories made for one B9 object, CV-17). Leaves are **B9.P1 to B9.P11** and **B9.H1 to B9.H6**; B9.X holds six kinds and stores `leaf = null` (C5(b)). The earlier internal codes A1 to A14 and B1 to B7 collided with category codes and are gone (review B9-2); the decision pass (B-10, CV-17) reduced the product leaves from 14 to 11, moved the lighter, rolling and carry leaves up to H4 to H6 and put the parts in B9.X. The letters A, B and C in the headings below name the three candidate designs, not codes.

### Candidate A — Role, then form (recommended; decided, B-10)
- **Sub-family (axis: what the object is to its owner).** B9.P Products (the object contains the substance: it is used up or taken). B9.H Implements & hardware (an empty object made to hold, burn, heat, light, roll, cut or carry the substance). B9.X Parts and accessories made for one B9 object (CV-17; the one sub-family exempt from the role axis; its stated axis is the object served: vapes, then water pipes).

### Candidate B — Substance first
- **Sub-family (axis: the active substance).** Tobacco; Non-tobacco nicotine; Cannabis and hemp; Other plant products; Shared implements.
- **Leaf (axis: product form or implement job).** Tobacco → cigarettes, cigars, pipe tobacco, roll-your-own, hookah tobacco, heated sticks, smokeless; Non-tobacco nicotine → pouches, e-liquid; Cannabis → flower, concentrates, infused; Shared implements → pipes, lighters, papers, grinders, vape hardware, water pipes.
- **Kind and named form:** as in Candidate A.

- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **Lighters, ashtrays, grinders, rolling papers and vape hardware** serve several substances (S10, S11, S34 put papers and grinders beside cannabis; S15 puts them beside tobacco). "Shared implements" becomes a large leaf-less pile.
  2. A **vape device** takes nicotine liquid or cannabis oil; an ordinary shop sells it either way (S7, S10).
  3. **Hookah** takes tobacco shisha or herbal shisha (S15, S17).
  4. **Blunts and spliffs** mix tobacco and cannabis. Substance is also the key legal-status variable, which the owner treats as a modifier, so a top split by substance would turn a modifier into a level.

### Candidate C — Route into the body, then product or implement
- **Sub-family (axis: how the substance reaches the body).** Smoked (burned); Heated or vaporised; Smokeless oral and nasal; Ingested or applied.
- **Leaf (axis: product form or implement job).** Smoked → cigarettes, cigars, pipe tobacco, hookah tobacco, cannabis flower, plus pipes, water pipes, papers, lighters, cutters; Heated or vaporised → heated sticks, e-liquid, concentrates, plus vape hardware and parts; Smokeless → snus, snuff, chew, pouches, kratom; Ingested or applied → edibles, tinctures, topicals.
- **Example (11 kinds):** Smoked → Robusto, Billiard pipe, Rolling paper; Heated or vaporised → Nic-salt e-liquid, Box mod kit; Smokeless → Snus, Chewing tobacco; Ingested → Edible, Tincture.
- **Evidence for it:** regulators and health bodies use route (S13, S16, S19, S35: "tobacco products for smoking" versus smokeless).
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **Lighters, ashtrays, grinders, trays and scales** have no route. They need a fourth, route-free sub-family, which defeats the axis.
  2. **Heated tobacco** is vaporised in practice, yet MHLW classes it as pipe tobacco in the Tobacco Business Act but taxes it separately (S16), JT sells it as its own class (S14), and the EU calls it a "novel tobacco product" (S35).
  3. **Cannabis topicals and tinctures** are not smoked and not "oral tobacco"; a fourth "ingested or applied" sub-family is for cannabis alone.
  4. **A cigar cutter** belongs to Smoked only because cigars are smoked; a cutter is also bought by buyers who never see the route as a category (S29 groups cutters with lighters, cases, ashtrays, humidors).

Candidates B and C were not chosen (B-10, role first). Substance is a required tag and route a tag, each with a generated view over the Candidate A tree.

**Attributes with a named standard (C4(b), C6).** Ring gauge and length of a cigar (64ths of an inch, S2); nicotine strength in mg/ml for e-liquid (the EU cap of 20 mg/ml is in Article 20, seen in search excerpts only †, S35) and mg per pouch (S8); snus portion size (S21); paper size (S31); Dunhill size group 1 to 6, a maker's own size ladder (S27); pipe stem length (churchwarden) and bowl material and finish (B-29). None of these is a tier.

**Variants (C3, CV-8).** The variant ledger is filled wherever a community compares variants and grows without a cap; it is not limited to owner-emphasis scenes. Candidates for stage 3: Zippo date-code years (S28) as `variant_type` year or reference; Dunhill four-digit codes (size group, mouthpiece and shape, S27) as `variant_code`; limited-edition pipes (S30) as `variant_type` limited edition; pipe finishes (Root, Bruyere, Shell, Tanshell, Red Bark, Cumberland, S27) as `variant_type` material. Wrapper shade and cigar vitola are not variants.

**Multi-use kinds (C14).** A vape device sold for nicotine liquid or cannabis oil, a hand pipe, a grinder, a lighter and a rolling paper each serve more than one substance. Each is one kind in one hardware leaf (rule (a): the leaf whose job defines it); the generated substance view lists it under each use.

**No individual-item facts (C16, CV-33).** The data holds no serial number, box date, age, condition or provenance of one cigar, pipe or lighter. A Zippo date code is recorded as a scheme on the named form (a stamp family), never as the date of one lighter.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: consensus-ranked for cigars; preference (maker-defined) for pipes and most other leaves; spec attributes for vape and cannabis.** There is no single ordering across the category.

- **Cigars — consensus-ranked.** Cigar Aficionado's blind panel scores each cigar on a 100-point scale in bands: 95-100 Classic, 90-94 Outstanding, 80-89 Very Good to Good/Excellent, 70-79 Average to Good Commercial Quality, below 70 (S36, a third-party explainer; the magazine's own page was not fetched †). The score covers appearance and construction, smoking performance, flavour and overall impression, and does not include price. A second axis is strength (mild, medium, full: S1), which is a retailer's label and not a measured ladder (C4(b)), so it is an attribute. Habanos states 27 brands and more than 250 cigars (S24), but no fetched page gives a Habanos grade ladder, so brand and vitola identify the cigar and the rating band orders it. The Cigar Aficionado scheme is an editorial class (iii) scale of the C4(c) registry, registered once with its band steps.
- **Pipes — preference, with a maker-defined grade.** Tobacconist University says there are "no comprehensive industry standards": first and second grade are defined by each manufacturer, and "high grade" is the common term (S29). Grain pattern (straight, flame, bird's eye, random, cross-cut) is a value cue but, per the same page, "will not affect the way the pipe smokes" (S29). Dunhill's one to six groups are a **size** ladder, and Dunhill states one unwavering quality standard across all sizes (S27).
- **Vape hardware and e-liquid — spec attributes.** EU law caps refill containers at 10 ml, tanks at 2 ml and nicotine at 20 mg/ml (S35, Article 20, seen only in search excerpts of the Directive †). Shops sort by nicotine type and bottle size (S7, S8). No consensus ranking was found on a fetched page.
- **Cannabis — spec attributes.** Regulators sort by form and route, not by quality (S12, S13). Retail "grades" of flower are size grades (Big Buds, Bud, Smalls, Ground: S10).
- **Cigarettes, snus, tobacco blends, lighters — preference.** Shops give strength words and brand families, not a ladder (S14, S21, S5).

**How a tier appears (C4).** Never on a kind and never as a level. A tier is a **tier entry** `(subject_id, scheme, value, source_url, date)` on a maker, a line or a named form; it does not pass from a maker to its named forms unless an entry says so.
- Cigar rating: a tier entry on the marca-and-vitola named form, scheme "Cigar Aficionado 100-point", value the band, with the source and year. Several entries from different sources are allowed, with a computed `disputed` flag. This replaces the earlier `rating_band` field.
- Pipe grade: a tier entry on a maker's line (for example the "Dunhill Shell Briar" line, S27), scheme "maker's own grade", registered as a maker's-own-ladder scale, class (iv) of C4(c). It is never compared across makers (S29). This replaces the earlier `maker_grade` text field.
- A retailer's own segment labels (S1 "Boutique", "Famous Exclusives") are a dated `platform_tag`, not a tier (C4(c)).
- A measured specification (nicotine mg/ml, ring gauge, portion size, the EU container limits) is an attribute with its standard named, not a tier entry (C4(b)).
- `price_band` is the domain-wide USD scheme of C4(d) and CV-13/CV-14 (0 free, under 50, 50 to 150, 150 to 500, 500 to 1,500, 1,500 to 5,000, 5,000 to 15,000, and the upper bands to over 5,000,000, most of which B9 leaves unused), derived from a dated, sourced list price. The earlier memo's separate price tag is replaced by it.
- A scheme enters the data on one fetched source (CV-10, C4(e)). A second source or a second region adds weight only (it feeds the computed `disputed` flag and the scheme's weight in views) and is never a condition of entry. The cigar scheme enters on S36 (third-party; a first-party Cigar Aficionado page would add weight †); pipe grades enter on S29.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity, with platform in heated tobacco and pod vapes.**

- **Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought for cigars (27 Habanos marcas and more than 250 cigars: S24; US shops browse by brand group: S1; German shops list "Top Zigarren-Marken": S22), pipes (Japanese and German shops list pipes by maker first: S15, S22, S23; Pipedia is organised around Dunhill's own numbers: S27), lighters (Zippo is a collecting class: S28), rolling papers (brand filter: S31), shisha (S17, S18) and e-liquid (UK shops list "hardware brands" and "e-liquid brands" separately: S7).
- **Platform.** Heated tobacco is sold under the device brand: IQOS, Ploom and NEAFS each have devices and their own stick lines (S8, S14, S15). Vape shops sell coils and pods beside hardware (S7, S8), and the coil is usable only with its platform. The sources do not state compatibility lists on the fetched pages, so each compatibility row would need a separate source †.
- **Incidental.** Generic grinders, ashtrays, cigar cutters and rolling machines are bought on design and material more than maker (S32 compares grinders by pieces, material and teeth).

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger, in the shared schema of C1.**
- *Core columns:* `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class` (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; retail own-label; licensee or diffusion; artisan or atelier; supplier); `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `known_for_kinds`; `scene_tags` (with `core` or `adjacent` strength); `price_band` (C4(d)); `sources` (at least one fetched URL). ## 8. Order inside a leaf

The orders below are adopted as written (R-0); where a fetched source itself orders the items, it is cited. Kinds go canonical form first, then the secondary key each leaf states; non-maker named forms go first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; maker-bound named forms follow, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically (C7, CV-21); variants go by `variant_code` or year. Ladder views (a maker's `line_rank`) are generated, not stored. Where a leaf has no canonical form, it starts with its secondary key.

- **Cigars: canonical form first, then spec order.** Corona first, the benchmark vitola that both guides use as the yardstick (S3 "benchmark Corona" at about 5.5 to 6 inches by ring gauge 42 to 44; S2). Then parejos from small to large by length and ring gauge, then figurados (S2, S3). Reason: length and ring gauge are the stable, generic yardstick (S24).
- **Pipes: canonical form first, then by degree of bend, then odd forms.** Billiard first because every source calls it the baseline (S4 "possibly the most ubiquitous"), then Bulldog, Canadian and the other shape families (S27, S30); straight before half-bent before bent (S30); Freehand, the carver's free form, after the other shape families (R-33); Kiseru and other non-Western forms last.
- **Pipe tobacco: tradition order.** English, American, Aromatic, then single-leaf styles (S5, S4).
- **Vape hardware: by mechanical complexity, simplest first.** Disposable, prefilled pod kit, refillable pod kit, pen, box mod (the order UK shops use: S8, S9).
- **E-liquid: by nicotine form.** Freebase, nic salt, zero-nicotine (S7, S8).
- **Cannabis concentrates: by process family then texture.** Solventless (kief, ice water hash, rosin) before solvent (shatter, badder, live resin, distillate); Weedmaps lists its L2 alphabetically (S10), so this order is the memo's own, adopted (R-0). Flavours and strains never order a leaf.
- **Oral & nasal products: by degree of processing.** Chewing tobacco, dry snuff, moist snuff, loose snus, portion snus (S19, S21), then the tobacco-free nicotine pouch, pearl and toothpick.
- **Leaf order inside Products** follows the regulators' and shops' common order: cigarettes and pre-rolls, cigars, cigarillos, pipe tobacco, loose plant material, hookah shisha, heated tobacco, oral and nasal products, e-liquids and prefilled cartridges, concentrates, then infused and dosed products (S14, S19, S35, S10).
- **Named forms:** non-maker forms first by first-attested year, then maker-bound forms by maker, then chronology within a maker (Dunhill's shape numbers carry the order: S27), otherwise alphabetical (CV-21).

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure.** B9 is divided role first (B-10): **B9.P Products** (the object contains the substance), **B9.H Implements & hardware** (the empty object made to hold, burn, heat, light, roll, cut or carry it) and the closing **B9.X Parts and accessories made for one B9 object** (CV-17). Products have 11 leaves by the form of the product, with `substance` (tobacco, non-tobacco nicotine, cannabinoid, other plant) a required tag and a generated view, never part of a leaf: Cigarettes & pre-rolls; Cigars; Cigarillos & little cigars; Pipe tobacco; Loose plant material; Hookah shisha; Heated tobacco; Oral & nasal products; E-liquids & prefilled cartridges; Concentrates; Infused & dosed products. Implements have 6 leaves by the job done in use: Pipes; Water pipes; Vape & heating hardware; Lighters; Rolling & grinding; Cutters, ashtrays & carry. B9.X (vape parts, then water-pipe parts and accessories) stores `leaf = null`. The maximum depth below the category is four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; CV-16). The worked example lists 84 attested kinds and 9 named gaps. Kinds stop where buyers compare: the generic vitola for cigars, the shape family for pipes (material, finish and stem length are attributes; Freehand is a shape-family kind with maker-bound named forms under it, R-33), nicotine chemistry for e-liquid, process type for concentrates, device architecture for vape hardware. A disposable vape is filed by the device (B9.H3), an empty pod is a part (B9.X) and a prefilled pod or cartridge a product (B9.P9). Named forms, makers, lines and variants have no cap and one fetched page admits each; cigar marca-and-vitola forms live in a register; cigarette and e-liquid brand lines are lines rows; cannabis cultivars are rows of the shared variety table; makers, carvers, artisans and schools are in ledgers, not kinds. Tiers are tier entries on a maker, line or named form (cigar rating bands, maker pipe grades), never on a kind; legal classes go to the jurisdiction ledger. The structure follows the way every fetched retailer separates the thing taken from the thing used (S7, S8, S10, S11, S15, S17, S18, S23) and keeps one home for each object (section 3, twenty-object check).
