## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B9 Smoking & Vaping, in domain B Home.

**Membership test (placement key step 27, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products in any form, and other plant products taken for a stimulant or psychoactive effect rather than as food or drink: cigarettes, cigars, loose, pipe and chewing tobacco, snuff, snus and nicotine pouches, heated-tobacco sticks, e-liquids and pods, cannabis flower, resin, concentrates, edibles, drinks, tinctures and CBD products, herbal smoking blends, betel quid and areca nut sold for chewing, khat, kratom; **or implements made for smoking, vaping or preparing these** (pipes, bongs, hookahs and hookah coals, vapes, pocket lighters, ashtrays, cigar cutters, cigarette cases, rolling papers and machines, herb grinders).

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch B9:**
- Step 1 note: tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products are step 27 in any form, including edibles, drinks and supplements, so none of them is "OUT" as a prepared food or drink. Medicines, vitamins and supplements are step 2.
- Step 2 note: CBD products are B9. Medicinal nicotine and cannabis prescribed or licensed as a medicine are H2 (step 27 note).
- Step 3 note: areca nut, khat, kratom and the other products step 27 names are B9, not groceries.
- Step 6: a seed, bulb, spore or living plant is C3, so cannabis seeds and clones and tobacco seedlings are C3.
- Step 27 note: hemp seeds and hemp oil sold as food are J5; coffee, tea and kava are J6; alcoholic drinks are J7; **humidors are B1**; long utility and candle lighters, matches and firelighters are C8 (step 32), pocket lighters are B9.
- Step 32, C8 branch: fuels and fuel cans, household batteries, matches and firelighters are C8. S3 says fuels are C8 whatever they are used in.
- Step 27 names the implements (pipes, bongs, hookahs, vapes, lighters, ashtrays, cutters, cigarette cases, papers, machines, grinders) as B9, so they are filed here and not by S3 (parts follow the whole). Parts of those implements that step 27 does not name (hookah bowls, hoses, coils, tanks, dab nails) have no other natural home and follow the implement into B9; generic batteries and cables are the exception (section 9).
- Step 14: an object made for use in a religious rite is F4 (a ritual pipe), and home-fragrance incense is B4.
- Step 24 and step 30 notes: cables and chargers are D1; a connected household object is filed by its task; connectivity is a modifier.
- Sister taxonomy boundary only (`/home/user/culinary-taxonomy/culinary/FRAMEWORK_v1.md`, line 19 and SW-H3-1): Culinary holds "chews eaten as confectionery" (chewing gum, jelly chews) and states that "non-food stimulants are out".

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK, Germany, Japan, Italy, Australia, Sweden/EU)
- **Cigar and pipe shops, Germany (S22 Pipe House, S23 Cigarworld).** The top level is the product type, with hardware separate from consumables: Cigarworld has Cigars, Cigarillos, Pipes, Tobaccos, Accessories, Spirits; Pipe House has Pfeifen, Pfeifentabak, Zigarren, Zigarillos, Spirituosen, Weine, Accessoires. Pipes filter by country, brand and shape (Pfeifenformen). Pipe tobacco filters by brand, flavour, tobacco type and cut (loose cut, flake, curly, mixture, ready rubbed, rollcake, cube cut). Cigars filter by origin (Cuba, Dominican Republic, Nicaragua, Honduras, Brazil, Peru) and format (Robusto and so on). Cigarworld's pipe-tobacco filters are type, flavouring, cut, strength, flavour profile, raw tobacco, room smell, brand, price.
- **Cigar shop, US (S1 Famous Smoke).** One cigar page carries these filters: Category (Handmade, Machine Made, Boutique, Small Cigars, Cigarillos, Filtered Cigars, Flavored), Country, Strength (Mild, Medium, Full), Package (box, bundle, single, tin, jar, carton), Wrapper Leaf (Habano, Connecticut Shade, Sumatra, Broadleaf, San Andres), Wrapper Origin and Ring Gauge range (under 38 up to 60 and over). Humidors sit in the same store as a separate branch (size, type, humidification).
- **Tobacconist, Japan (S15 Cigar Shop Kitahara).** Top level is "Japanese tobacco", "World tobacco", "Rare tobacco" (snuff and similar), "Cigars" (with "dry/little cigars"), "Pipes", "Roll-your-own tobacco", "Water-pipe tobacco", heated tobacco (Ploom Tech, IQOS, glo, each split into device and sticks), and kiseru (traditional Japanese pipe and its leaf). ### 1.3 Enthusiast communities, trade education and wikis
- **Cigars (S2 Cigar Aficionado, S3 Holt's).** Both split cigars first into Parejos (straight) and Figurados (tapered or irregular), then name vitolas: Corona, Corona Gorda, Robusto, Toro, Churchill, Double Corona, Lancero, Panatela, Grande; Pyramid, Belicoso, Torpedo, Perfecto, Culebra, Diadema/Salomon. Size is length in inches by ring gauge in 64ths. Wrapper colour has seven classes from Double Claro (Candela) through Claro, Colorado Claro, Colorado, Colorado Maduro, Maduro to Oscuro (S2).
- **Pipes (S4 Smokingpipes glossary, S6 Wikipedia, S27 Pipedia, S29 Tobacconist University).** Wikipedia gives bowl materials (briar, meerschaum, corn cob, clay, gourd, porcelain, synthetics and others), stem materials, and twelve shape families with subtypes: Apple, Billiard, Bulldog, Calabash, Canadian, Cavalier, Churchwarden, Dublin, Freehand, Sitter, Tyrolean, Vest Pocket. Pipedia records the Dunhill system: 35 standard shapes with two-digit codes, a four-digit code of size group (1 to 6), mouthpiece and shape, and finishes (Root, Bruyere, Shell, Tanshell, Red Bark, Cumberland). Non-standard Dunhill shapes are "Quaint". Tobacconist University (S29) adds grain patterns (straight, flame, bird's eye, random, cross-cut) and finishes (smooth, sandblast, carved).
- **Pipe tobacco (S5 Tobacconist University, S4, S25 Pfeifenstube).** Two major blend categories, English (Balkan: Oriental, Virginia, Latakia, Perique) and Aromatic (Burley and Virginia with casing and topping, often Cavendish), plus personal blends (S5). Smokingpipes adds the American blend (Virginia, Burley, Oriental) (S4). Pfeifenstube splits aromatisiert versus naturbelassen, and filters by cut (loose cut, flake, curly, mixture), leaf (Virginia, Burley, Black Cavendish, Orient, Perique, Latakia, Dark Fired Kentucky) and aroma.
- **Cigar quality (S36, a third-party explainer of Cigar Aficionado's method).** The magazine's blind-tasting panel scores each cigar on a 100-point scale with bands: 95-100 Classic, 90-94 Outstanding, 80-89 Very Good to Good/Excellent, 70-79 Average, below 70.
- **Grinders (S32).** Compared by number of pieces (2, 3, 4), material (metal, wood, mixed), tooth design, magnets, capacity, screens.
- **Kratom (S33).** Forms are ranked by strength: dried leaf, powder, capsules, tea, extracts, shots, tablets and gummies; botanical kratom is kept apart from concentrated "7-OH" products because their legal status differs.

### 1.4 Collectors and the resale market
- **Collectors Weekly (S28).** Tobacciana tree: Tobacco (cards, jars, tins, signs), Lighters and Matches (lighters, table lighters, matchbooks, matchbox labels, match holders, Zippo), Cigars (boxes, box labels, cutters, humidors), Pipes (pipes, Native American pipes, meerschaum pipes), Other (ashtrays, cigarette cases, snuff boxes, snuff bottles). Zippo is a collecting class of its own, dated by stamp since 1955.

### 1.5 Standards bodies, trade classifications and law
- **EU Directive 2014/40/EU (S35).** Definitions: cigarettes, roll-your-own tobacco, pipe tobacco, waterpipe tobacco, cigars, cigarillos, chewing tobacco, nasal tobacco, tobacco for oral use; "tobacco products for smoking" versus smokeless; "novel tobacco product" (heated tobacco falls here); "herbal product for smoking"; "electronic cigarette" (device, cartridge, tank) and "refill container". The 10 ml refill-container limit, 2 ml tank limit and 20 mg/ml cap are in Article 20, which appeared only in search excerpts of the Directive and not in the fetched extract. Where a product fits two categories (pipe, roll-your-own) the stricter rule applies.
- **HS/CN (S20).** Heading 2403 splits water-pipe tobacco (240311), other smoking tobacco (240319), homogenised or reconstituted tobacco (240391) and chewing tobacco and snuff (240399). Heading 2402 (cigars, cheroots, cigarillos; cigarettes) was seen in search excerpts only and not fetched.
- **FDA (S19).** Product pages are Cigarettes; Cigars, cigarillos, little filtered cigars; E-cigarettes and ENDS; Hookah tobacco; Other tobacco products; Non-tobacco nicotine products; Roll-your-own; Pipe tobacco; Smokeless (chewing tobacco in loose leaf, plug or twist; dry snuff; moist snuff and snus loose or pouched; dissolvables).
- **Japan MHLW (S16).** Forms of tobacco: cigarettes (filter or plain), cigars, water-pipe tobacco, pipe and kiseru, roll-your-own, smokeless (snus, snuff, chewing), heated tobacco (classed as pipe tobacco in the Tobacco Business Act, a separate tax class since 2018).

### 1.6 Museums and reference works

### 1.7 Manufacturers and trade bodies (outside the template list)
- **Habanos S.A. (S24).** 27 marcas and more than 250 cigars. Cuban sizes are "vitolas": the **vitola de galera** is the factory size name, the **vitola de salida** is the market name on the box. The same size carries different market names in different brands, and the same market name can denote different sizes. Generic popular names (Petit Corona, Robusto, Churchill, Torpedo, Double Figurado) sit over the factory names (Mareva, Cañonazo, Julieta No. 2, Pirámide, Exquisito).
- **Indonesian kretek (S26).** Clove-tobacco cigarettes are split by hand-rolled unfiltered (SKT) versus machine-made filtered (SKM) and by brand (Djarum, Sampoerna, Gudang Garam).

### 1.8 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects differently
- **Agree:** the top split is by **product form** (cigar, cigarette, pipe tobacco, roll-your-own, smokeless, vape, heated, flower, concentrate, edible), and **hardware is held apart from the consumable** in every retail tree (S7, S8, S9, S10, S11, S15, S17, S18, S23). Regulators also split by product form (S12, S16, S19, S35).
- **Split 1 — route versus form.** Regulators and health sources group by route (smoked, smokeless, inhaled, ingestible, topical: S13, S16, S35); shops group by form and hardware. The same snus pouch is "smokeless tobacco" to the regulator and a "nicotine pouch" neighbour at the shop (S19, S21, S8).
- **Split 2 — legal regime as a category.** Leafly makes Delta-8 and Hemp CBD top-level branches (S11); Weedmaps and the regulators do not (S10, S12). The framework treats legal status as a modifier.
- **Split 3 — hardware versus liquid for pods.** Weedmaps puts "Vape Pens > Pods" and cartridges in the product tree (S10); UK vape shops put empty pods and coils beside hardware (S7); prefilled pods sit with e-liquid (S7).
- **Split 4 — material versus shape for pipes.** Wikipedia and museums give material first (S6, S37); pipe shops give shape or bend first (S22, S30); Japanese shops give maker first (S15).
- **Split 5 — heated tobacco.** Japan taxes and sells it as its own class (S16, S14, S15); EU calls it a "novel tobacco product" (S35); FDA lists it under Other tobacco products (S19); UK shops put it beside vapes (S7, S8).
- **Split 6 — cigars.** Shops split on construction and size class (handmade, machine-made, small cigars, cigarillos, filtered, flavored: S1); enthusiast guides split on shape (parejo versus figurado) and vitola name (S2, S3); Habanos adds the factory-versus-market name problem (S24).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Retail navigation (S7, S8, S9, S15, S22, S23):** the first click is **what kind of thing**: a cigar, a pipe, pipe tobacco, a vape kit, e-liquid, coils, pouches, heated tobacco, a hookah. The second click differs by sub-domain:
- **Cigars:** origin, strength, size and wrapper are all one-level filters beside each other (S1, S22). Guides tell buyers the shape and size set intensity and burn time, and that a "benchmark" Corona is about 5.5 to 6 inches by ring gauge 42 to 44 (S3, S2). Habanos notes that bigger cigars give richer, cooler smoke (S24).
- **Pipes:** shape or bend, then maker (S30, S22, S15). Pipeonline's buying table maps shape to smoker preference (first pipe: Billiard, Apple, Canadian; cooler smoke: Canadian, Churchwarden) (S30).
- **Pipe tobacco:** blend family (aromatic versus English/natural) first, then cut and flavour (S5, S25, S22, S23).
- **Vapes:** device architecture (disposable/big puff, pod, pen, box mod) first, then experience level (S8, S9), and for e-liquid the nicotine form (freebase, salt, nicotine free) then size (S7, S8). The EU rule that fixes refill containers at 10 ml and nicotine at 20 mg/ml (Article 20, search excerpt only) makes bottle size and strength compliance specs; S7 shows the market sorting 10 ml freebase from 50 ml and 100 ml shortfills.
- **Cannabis:** form (flower, pre-roll, concentrate, edible, vape, topical) first for buyers and regulators (S10, S11, S12), and route to the body second (S13).
- **Hookah:** the pipe, then bowl and heat, then tobacco flavour family (S17, S18).

**Conclusion:** the first axis is **form (what the object is), with the consumable-versus-hardware split as the very first fork**. Maker is the second click for pipes, cigars, lighters and vape hardware. Fit or size matters for cigars (ring gauge), papers (paper size, S31) and pouches (portion size, S21). Mechanism matters for vape hardware (disposable, pod, mod) and lighters.
