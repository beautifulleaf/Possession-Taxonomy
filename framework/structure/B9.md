## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B9 Smoking & Vaping, in domain B Home.

**Membership test (placement key step 27, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products in any form, and other plant products taken for a stimulant or psychoactive effect rather than as food or drink: cigarettes, cigars, loose, pipe and chewing tobacco, snuff, snus and nicotine pouches, heated-tobacco sticks, e-liquids and pods, cannabis flower, resin, concentrates, edibles, drinks, tinctures and CBD products, herbal smoking blends, betel quid and areca nut sold for chewing, khat, kratom; **or implements made for smoking, vaping or preparing these** (pipes, bongs, hookahs and hookah coals, vapes, pocket lighters, ashtrays, cigar cutters, cigarette cases, rolling papers and machines, herb grinders).

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch B9:**
- Step 1 note: tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products are step 27 in any form, including edibles, drinks and supplements, so none of them is "OUT" as a prepared food or drink. Medicines, vitamins and supplements are step 2.
- Step 2 note: CBD products are B9. Medicinal nicotine and cannabis prescribed or licensed as a medicine are H2 (step 27 note).
- Step 3 note: areca nut, khat, kratom and the other products step 27 names are B9, not groceries.
- Step 6: a seed, bulb, spore or living plant is C3, so cannabis seeds and clones and tobacco seedlings are C3.
- Step 27 note: hemp seeds and hemp oil sold as food are J5; coffee, tea and kava are J6; alcoholic drinks are J7; **humidors are B1**; long utility and candle lighters, matches and firelighters are C8 (step 32), pocket lighters are B9.
- Step 32, C8 branch: fuels and fuel cans, household batteries, matches and firelighters are C8. S3 says fuels are C8 whatever they are used in.
- Step 27 names the implements (pipes, bongs, hookahs, vapes, lighters, ashtrays, cutters, cigarette cases, papers, machines, grinders) as B9, so they are filed here and not by S3 (parts follow the whole). Parts of those implements that step 27 does not name (hookah bowls, hoses, coils, tanks, dab nails) have no other natural home and follow the implement into B9; generic batteries and cables are the exception (section 9).
- Step 14: an object made for use in a religious rite is F4 (a ritual pipe), and home-fragrance incense is B4.
- Step 24 and step 30 notes: cables and chargers are D1; a connected household object is filed by its task; connectivity is a modifier.
- Sister taxonomy boundary only (`/home/user/culinary-taxonomy/culinary/FRAMEWORK_v1.md`, line 19 and SW-H3-1): Culinary holds "chews eaten as confectionery" (chewing gum, jelly chews) and states that "non-food stimulants are out".

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Retail navigation (S7, S8, S9, S15, S22, S23):** the first click is **what kind of thing**: a cigar, a pipe, pipe tobacco, a vape kit, e-liquid, coils, pouches, heated tobacco, a hookah. The second click differs by sub-domain:
- **Cigars:** origin, strength, size and wrapper are all one-level filters beside each other (S1, S22). Guides tell buyers the shape and size set intensity and burn time, and that a "benchmark" Corona is about 5.5 to 6 inches by ring gauge 42 to 44 (S3, S2). Habanos notes that bigger cigars give richer, cooler smoke (S24).
- **Pipes:** shape or bend, then maker (S30, S22, S15). Pipeonline's buying table maps shape to smoker preference (first pipe: Billiard, Apple, Canadian; cooler smoke: Canadian, Churchwarden) (S30).
- **Pipe tobacco:** blend family (aromatic versus English/natural) first, then cut and flavour (S5, S25, S22, S23).
- **Vapes:** device architecture (disposable/big puff, pod, pen, box mod) first, then experience level (S8, S9), and for e-liquid the nicotine form (freebase, salt, nicotine free) then size (S7, S8). The EU rule that fixes refill containers at 10 ml and nicotine at 20 mg/ml (Article 20, search excerpt only) makes bottle size and strength compliance specs; S7 shows the market sorting 10 ml freebase from 50 ml and 100 ml shortfills.
- **Cannabis:** form (flower, pre-roll, concentrate, edible, vape, topical) first for buyers and regulators (S10, S11, S12), and route to the body second (S13).
- **Hookah:** the pipe, then bowl and heat, then tobacco flavour family (S17, S18).

**Conclusion:** the first axis is **form (what the object is), with the consumable-versus-hardware split as the very first fork**. Maker is the second click for pipes, cigars, lighters and vape hardware. Fit or size matters for cigars (ring gauge), papers (paper size, S31) and pouches (portion size, S21). Mechanism matters for vape hardware (disposable, pod, mod) and lighters.

## 3. Candidate structures

The category holds two kinds of object that every source keeps apart: **things taken** (the tobacco, nicotine, cannabis or plant product itself, including its package) and **things used** (pipes, vapes, lighters, papers). Because the framework says "one home per object" and files the implements here too, the first design choice is how to hold the two together. Three candidates follow. The maximum depth under the category is four levels: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form.

### Candidate A — Role, then form (recommended)
- **Sub-family (axis: what the object is to its owner).** A. Products (the object contains the substance: it is used up or taken). B. Implements & hardware (an empty object made to hold, burn, heat, light, roll, cut or carry the substance).
- **Leaf, inside Products (axis: form and route of the substance).** 14 leaves: Cigarettes; Cigars; Cigarillos & little cigars; Pipe tobacco; Roll-your-own & cut tobacco; Hookah tobacco; Heated tobacco; Smokeless tobacco; Nicotine pouches & other non-tobacco nicotine; E-liquids & prefilled vape consumables; Cannabis flower & pre-rolls; Cannabis concentrates & cartridges; Cannabis infused products; Other plant products (kratom, betel, khat, herbal smoking blends).
- **Leaf, inside Implements (axis: the job it does in use).** 7 leaves: Pipes; Water pipes; Vape & heating hardware; Water-pipe, vape & dab parts; Lighters; Rolling & grinding; Cutters, ashtrays & carry.
- **Kind (axis, stated per leaf):** vitola for cigars; blend family for pipe tobacco; construction type for cigarettes; delivery chemistry for e-liquid; process type for concentrates; shape family for pipes; device architecture for vape hardware.
- **Named form:** a model, series, cut or iconic design (Montecristo No. 2, Dunhill Billiard 03, IQOS ILUMA i, Zippo windproof).
- **Example leaves and kinds (13):** Cigars → Robusto, Toro, Churchill, Torpedo, Perfecto (S2, S3, S24). Pipes → Billiard, Bulldog, Freehand, Churchwarden (S6). Smokeless tobacco → Original portion snus, White portion snus, Moist snuff, Chewing tobacco (S19, S21).
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. A **disposable vape** is hardware and liquid in one object (S8 "big puff kits", S7). Rule needed: file by the hardware (it is sold and bought as a device).
  2. A **prefilled pod or cannabis cartridge** carries liquid but is also a part. Rule: if it contains the substance it is a product (A10, A12); an empty pod is hardware (B4).
  3. A **blunt** (a cigar wrapper filled with cannabis) fits both Cigars and Cannabis pre-rolls (S10 lists "Blunts" under Pre Roll). Rule: filed by what is inside as sold (a pre-rolled blunt is a cannabis pre-roll; an empty blunt wrap is a rolling item).
  4. A **tobacco-free shisha** or **herbal smoking blend** has no tobacco and is still smoked (S35 defines "herbal product for smoking"; S15 lists nicotine-free shisha). Rule: step 27 already files it here; it sits with the form it imitates.

### Candidate B — Substance first
- **Sub-family (axis: the active substance).** Tobacco; Non-tobacco nicotine; Cannabis and hemp; Other plant products; Shared implements.
- **Leaf (axis: product form or implement job).** Tobacco → cigarettes, cigars, pipe tobacco, roll-your-own, hookah tobacco, heated sticks, smokeless; Non-tobacco nicotine → pouches, e-liquid; Cannabis → flower, concentrates, infused; Shared implements → pipes, lighters, papers, grinders, vape hardware, water pipes.
- **Kind and named form:** as in Candidate A.
- **Example (11 kinds):** Tobacco → Robusto, English blend, White portion snus; Non-tobacco nicotine → Nicotine pouch, Nic-salt e-liquid; Cannabis → Flower, Shatter, Rosin, Edible; Shared implements → Grinder, Rolling paper.
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **Lighters, ashtrays, grinders, rolling papers and vape hardware** serve several substances (S10, S11, S34 put papers and grinders beside cannabis; S15 puts them beside tobacco). "Shared implements" becomes a large leaf-less pile.
  2. A **vape device** takes nicotine liquid or cannabis oil; an ordinary shop sells it either way (S7, S10).
  3. **Hookah** takes tobacco shisha or herbal shisha (S15, S17).
  4. **Blunts and spliffs** mix tobacco and cannabis. Substance is also the key legal-status variable, which the owner treats as a modifier, so a top split by substance would turn a modifier into a level.

### Candidate C — Route into the body, then product or implement
- **Sub-family (axis: how the substance reaches the body).** Smoked (burned); Heated or vaporised; Smokeless oral and nasal; Ingested or applied.
- **Leaf (axis: product form or implement job).** Smoked → cigarettes, cigars, pipe tobacco, hookah tobacco, cannabis flower, plus pipes, water pipes, papers, lighters, cutters; Heated or vaporised → heated sticks, e-liquid, concentrates, plus vape hardware and parts; Smokeless → snus, snuff, chew, pouches, kratom; Ingested or applied → edibles, tinctures, topicals.
- **Example (11 kinds):** Smoked → Robusto, Billiard pipe, Rolling paper; Heated or vaporised → Nic-salt e-liquid, Box mod kit; Smokeless → Snus, Chewing tobacco; Ingested → Edible, Tincture.
- **Evidence for it:** regulators and health bodies use route (S13, S16, S19, S35: "tobacco products for smoking" versus smokeless).
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **Lighters, ashtrays, grinders, trays and scales** have no route. They need a fourth, route-free sub-family, which defeats the axis.
  2. **Heated tobacco** is vaporised in practice, yet MHLW classes it as pipe tobacco in the Tobacco Business Act but taxes it separately (S16), JT sells it as its own class (S14), and the EU calls it a "novel tobacco product" (S35).
  3. **Cannabis topicals and tinctures** are not smoked and not "oral tobacco"; a fourth "ingested or applied" sub-family is for cannabis alone.
  4. **A cigar cutter** belongs to Smoked only because cigars are smoked; a cutter is also bought by buyers who never see the route as a category (S29 groups cutters with lighters, cases, ashtrays, humidors).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers; finer distinctions are named forms or attributes.

**The level differs by sub-family:** cigars stop above the marca-line (a vitola is generic), while pipes stop at shape family although buyers compare makers one level below, because the maker is the identity (section 6). Cannabis concentrates go deeper than the regulators because buyers shop by texture and extraction.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: consensus-ranked for cigars; preference (maker-defined) for pipes and most other leaves; spec attributes for vape and cannabis.** There is no single ordering across the category.

- **Cigars — consensus-ranked.** Cigar Aficionado's blind panel scores each cigar on a 100-point scale in bands: 95-100 Classic, 90-94 Outstanding, 80-89 Very Good to Good/Excellent, 70-79 Average to Good Commercial Quality, below 70 (S36, a third-party explainer). The score covers appearance and construction, smoking performance, flavour and overall impression, and does not include price. A second, spec-ranked axis is strength (mild, medium, full: S1). Habanos states 27 brands and more than 250 cigars (S24), but no fetched page gives a Habanos grade ladder, so brand and vitola identify the cigar and the rating band orders it.
- **Pipes — preference, with a maker-defined grade.** Tobacconist University says there are "no comprehensive industry standards": first and second grade are defined by each manufacturer, and "high grade" is the common term (S29). Grain pattern (straight, flame, bird's eye, random, cross-cut) is a value cue but, per the same page, "will not affect the way the pipe smokes" (S29). Dunhill's one to six groups are a **size** ladder, and Dunhill states one unwavering quality standard across all sizes (S27).
- **Vape hardware and e-liquid — spec attributes.** EU law caps refill containers at 10 ml, tanks at 2 ml and nicotine at 20 mg/ml (S35, Article 20, seen only in search excerpts of the Directive). Shops sort by nicotine type and bottle size (S7, S8). No consensus ranking was found on a fetched page.
- **Cannabis — spec attributes.** Regulators sort by form and route, not by quality (S12, S13). Retail "grades" of flower are size grades (Big Buds, Bud, Smalls, Ground: S10).
- **Cigarettes, snus, tobacco blends, lighters — preference.** Shops give strength words and brand families, not a ladder (S14, S21, S5).

**How a tier appears.** Never as a level. Three attribute fields on the item or named form: `rating_band` (cigars only, source and year recorded), `strength` (mild, medium, full, or mg/ml for liquid and mg per pouch), and `maker_grade` (a text field for pipe grades and series names such as "Dunhill Shell Briar", S27). A separate `price_band` tag carries the price. A rating belongs to the marca-and-vitola named form, not to the kind.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity, with platform in heated tobacco and pod vapes.**

- **Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought for cigars (27 Habanos marcas and more than 250 cigars: S24; US shops browse by brand group: S1; German shops list "Top Zigarren-Marken": S22), pipes (Japanese and German shops list pipes by maker first: S15, S22, S23; Pipedia is organised around Dunhill's own numbers: S27), lighters (Zippo is a collecting class: S28), rolling papers (brand filter: S31), shisha (S17, S18) and e-liquid (UK shops list "hardware brands" and "e-liquid brands" separately: S7).
- **Platform.** Heated tobacco is sold under the device brand: IQOS, Ploom and NEAFS each have devices and their own stick lines (S8, S14, S15). Vape shops sell coils and pods beside hardware (S7, S8), and the coil is usable only with its platform. The sources do not state compatibility lists on the fetched pages, so the `platform` field would need a separate source per item.
- **Incidental.** Generic grinders, ashtrays, cigar cutters and rolling machines are bought on design and material more than maker (S32 compares grinders by pieces, material and teeth).

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger.** Rows: maker name; `maker_type` (cigar marca, tobacco manufacturer, pipe factory, pipe artisan, hardware brand, e-liquid brand, lighter maker, paper maker); known-for kinds (for example Habanos → Robusto, Torpedo); scene tags; tier (S1 separates "Boutique" and "Famous Exclusives" from the mass lines); price band; country; era; `platform` (device family for heated and pod products). Each row attested. The ledger needs two maker roles for cigars, because Habanos separates the **marca** from the factory and the **vitola de galera** from the **vitola de salida** (S24); a brand-owner field and a factory field cover it. Named forms point to a ledger row.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

The orders below are proposals; where a fetched source itself orders the items, it is cited.

- **Cigars: spec order, parejos first, small to large.** Both enthusiast guides split Parejos then Figurados (S2, S3) and describe each vitola by length and ring gauge. Reason: length and ring gauge are the stable, generic yardstick (S24).
- **Pipes: canonical form first, then by degree of bend, then odd forms.** Billiard first because every source calls it the baseline (S4 "possibly the most ubiquitous"), then Bulldog, Dublin, Apple, Canadian, Pot; straight before half-bent before bent (S30); Freehand, Churchwarden and non-Western forms last.
- **Pipe tobacco: tradition order.** English, American, Aromatic, then single-leaf styles (S5, S4).
- **Vape hardware: by mechanical complexity, simplest first.** Disposable, prefilled pod kit, refillable pod kit, pen, box mod (the order UK shops use: S8, S9).
- **E-liquid: by nicotine form.** Freebase, nic salt, zero-nicotine/shortfill (S7, S8).
- **Cannabis concentrates: by process family then texture.** Solventless (kief, hash, rosin) before solvent (shatter, wax, live resin, distillate); Weedmaps lists its L2 alphabetically (S10), so this is a proposal. Flavours and strains never order a leaf.
- **Smokeless: by degree of processing.** Chewing tobacco, dry snuff, moist snuff, loose snus, portion snus (S19, S21).
- **Leaf order inside Products** follows the regulators' and shops' common order: cigarettes, cigars, cigarillos, pipe tobacco, roll-your-own, hookah tobacco, heated tobacco, smokeless, pouches, e-liquid, then cannabis forms, then other plants (S14, S19, S35, S10).
- **Named forms:** chronology within a maker (Dunhill's shape numbers carry the order: S27), otherwise alphabetical.

## 9. Boundaries

The five closest neighbours are B1, C8, H2/H1, J (J5, J6, J7 and Culinary) and C3/C2. F4, D1 and H3 are smaller edges.

- **B1 Furniture & Storage — humidors and storage.**
  *Precedence: a humidor, tobacco jar, tin or cigar box that holds several cigars and stands in a room is B1 (step 27 note: "humidors are B1"); a case carried on the person to hold cigars or cigarettes, a cigar cutter and an ashtray are B9.*
  Closest objects: travel humidor (S1 sells it in the humidor branch), cigar case (S29), cigarette case (S15, named in step 27), tobacco tin (S28). **The framework is silent on a cigar case**: S3 says cases that hold several objects are B1, yet step 27 names cigarette cases as B9. See section 10.
- **C8 Building Materials & Hardware — fire and fuel.**
  *Precedence: matches, firelighters, lighter fuel and gas refills, flints and long utility or candle lighters are C8; a pocket, pipe, cigar or table lighter is B9.*
  Closest objects: butane gas cylinder (Kitahara files it with pipe goods, S15, but S3 and step 32 file fuels in C8 whatever the use), matchbooks (S28 lists them under Lighters and Matches), hookah charcoal (S17; step 27 names "hookah coals" as B9, so it is B9 despite being charcoal).
- **H2 Health, Medical & Mobility / H1 Personal Care — nicotine and cannabinoid as medicine or cosmetic.**
  *Precedence: nicotine or cannabis sold and licensed as a medicine is H2; a nicotine pouch, toothpick or pearl sold as a consumer product, and any cannabis or CBD product sold as a consumer product including lotions and balms, is B9; a skin-care product without a cannabinoid is H1.*
  Closest objects: CBD balm (Leafly Hemp CBD "bath and body", S11; Illinois "Topical", S12), nicotine toothpicks (S8), nicotine pearls (S7), prescription cannabis (step 27 note: H2).
- **J (Groceries) and the Culinary taxonomy — food form versus stimulant.**
  *Precedence: coffee, tea and kava are J6, hemp seeds and hemp oil sold as food are J5, alcoholic drinks are J7; a cannabis-, kratom- or areca-bearing edible, drink or tincture is B9 whatever its food form; a chew eaten as confectionery with none of these is a Culinary food.*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A, role then form.** Two sub-families (Products; Implements & hardware), 14 and 7 leaves, kinds by a stated axis per leaf (section 3), and named forms as models, series, cuts and iconic designs. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). It follows the way every fetched retailer separates the thing taken from the thing used (S7, S8, S10, S11, S15, S17, S18, S23), and it keeps one home for each object.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. **First fork.** Role (Products versus Implements & hardware, recommended), route (Candidate C) or substance (Candidate B). Route and substance can still be generated views over the recommended tree.
2. **Pipes: shape or material as the kind axis.** The recommendation uses shape family (S6, S4, S27, S30, S22) with material as an attribute.
