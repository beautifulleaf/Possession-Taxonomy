## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B2 Soft Furnishings.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, branch 6): *made to cover, soften or dress beds, floors, windows, seats and tables, or to wrap or dry the body: bedding, mattress toppers, rugs and carpets (loose or fitted), chair and floor mats, blinds and curtains, cushions, throws, electric blankets, table linen, towels, swaddling cloths, travel pillows.* Category list line: "bedding, rugs and carpets, blinds and curtains, cushions, throws, table linen, towels."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name B2:**
- Step 30 note on B7: electric blankets and heated throws are B2.
- Step 4 (G2) note: carpets, blinds and curtains are B2 (they are not building products, even when fitted).
- Step 18 (H1) note: towels of every kind are B2.
- Step 7 (F6) note: tanned hides and leather are C4 as material and B2 as rugs.
- Step 14 (F4) note: meditation cushions and mats are B2 (they are not religious objects).
- Step 17 note (A1): wearable baby sleeping bags and bathrobes are garments, not B2.
- Step 30 branch order: B5, B6, B7, B8, B3, B1 (beds and mattresses, bean bags) are tried before B2; B4 (decor) after it.
- Step 20 sends tapestries and textile hangings to F2 Art, before B2 is reached.
- Step 8 (S3) is tried before step 30: a part or cover made for one kind of object follows that object (relevant to sofa covers and curtain fittings, section 9).
- Step 13 (B10) sends beds, blankets and cushions made for animals to B10.
- S3: parts follow the whole (matters for curtain poles, rug pads, cushion covers, see section 9).
- Appendix A: installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted), setting, user, material, region, era, price and tier, brand are modifiers.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, rug dealers' encyclopedias and wikis

- Rug dealers and enthusiasts divide rugs first by **construction**: knotted pile vs flatweave, then hand-knotted vs hand-tufted vs machine-made (Harcourt [S28]; Kilim.com [S8]). Dealer navigation (Kilim.com) offers Type (vintage, overdyed, patchwork, runner, new kilims, hand-knotted), Style, Size, Age, Color [S8].
- The same dealers then divide **Persian rugs** by social context of production (city or workshop, village or trade, tribal or nomadic) and by place names: Catalina Rug lists Traditional (Tabriz, Kashan, Isfahan, Kerman...), Village (Heriz, Qashqai...), Nomadic (Bakhtiari, Shiraz...) and "Persian-style" Pakistani and Indian types [S7]; Wikipedia's Classification section runs through nomadic/tribal, village and town carpets [S35].
- Both enthusiasts and dealers warn that these names are not construction types. WOVEN: "Names such as Tabriz, Kashan, Heriz... may point to a city, region, workshop network, community, or trade convention"; "pile and flatwoven structures... construction may cut across geographic labels" [S5]. Catalina: "Hamadan" is "a collective name for all rugs woven in that region" [S7]. The Turkotek collector forum debates Afshar attribution by knot type, design source and ethnicity and calls it "a broad rubric" [S37]. Wikipedia: a village name may be used "as a label for a special quality", as with Serapi [S35].
- Flatweave names overlap the same way: "Kilim commonly describes weft-faced tapestry; dhurrie is a South Asian rug category...; soumak is distinguished by weft wrapping... The names therefore overlap" [S6]; the trade uses kilim and flatweave interchangeably [S8].
- Sleep and sheet guides (Consumer Reports [S23], Wirecutter [S25], Sleep Foundation [S24]) divide bedding by **construction and weave**: duvet (insert plus separate cover), comforter (cover built in), quilt (stitched layers); sheets by weave (percale, sateen), fibre staple (short, long, extra-long; Pima, Egyptian, Supima) and thread count.
- Towel guides (allcottonandlinen [S29]) list seven types that mix **roles** (bath towel, bath sheet, hand towel, face towel) with **weaves** (Turkish/peshtemal, waffle, terry) and give weight bands in GSM.
- Window-covering guides disagree on the first axis: for blinds the **mechanism** comes first ("Bei Rollos steht der Mechanismus an erster Stelle", [S21]); for curtains the fabric and heading ([S21], [S19]); Wikipedia uses "blind" for slatted types and "shade" for single soft pieces, and notes the loose use of "window blinds" for all coverings [S20].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- 1stDibs "Rugs and Carpets" [S16]: facets item type (new/made to order, antique and vintage, pre-owned), style, period, material, place of origin. Its listing categories read as period plus origin plus a name (for example "Mid-20th Century French Aubusson", "Early 20th Century Heriz Serapi", "Turkish Kashan", "Indian Sultanabad"), so the **same name floats free of place** (a Heriz-design rug listed as Afghan, a Kashan as Turkish).
- Chairish [S17]: Rugs, shop by style and brand; named styles (Beni Ourain, jute, hide, rag rugs, Turkish kilims, Moroccan Berber) next to material and colour.
- Christie's [S18]: "Rugs and Carpets" is a collector department of "antique hand-woven rugs and carpets dating from the 15th to the early 20th century", sold inside Islamic and Indian Worlds sales.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **WCO Harmonized System 2022 chapter 57** [S31]: 5701 knotted; 5702 woven, not tufted or flocked, "including Kelem, Schumacks, Karamanie and similar hand-woven rugs", with coir and pile/non-pile; 5703 tufted (including turf); 5704 felt; 5705 other. Division is by **construction first**, then fibre.
- **HTS chapter 63** [S32]: 6301 blankets and travelling rugs (including electric blankets); 6304 furnishing articles (wall hangings appear at 6304.99); Note 3 groups bed linen, table linen, toilet linen and kitchen linen. (Heading 6303, curtains and interior blinds, was seen only in a search excerpt of a different PDF and is not cited as fetched.)
- **UNSPSC** family 5212 "Bedclothes and table and kitchen linen and towels" [S33]; class 52121500 Bedclothes lists quilts, comforters, comforter covers, mattress covers, pillows, mattress pads, featherbeds, blankets, sheets, dust ruffles, duvets, pillow cases, bed spreads, duvet cover.
- **ISO 2424:2024** defines textile floor coverings and their categories [S9]; **EN 1307** classifies textile floor coverings and carpet tiles "excluding rugs and runners" into use classes for wear, appearance retention, additional performance and luxury rating, and refers to EN ISO 10874 room-use classes [S10]. So the standards world itself treats **fitted carpet and made-up rugs as different regimes**.
- **ASTM** [S11] keeps Home Furnishings standards for bedspreads, comforters, curtain and drapery fabrics, soft window coverings, towel products, shower curtains, sheeting products; a separate Pile Floor Coverings family; D3775 end and pick count; D4522 feather and down fillings.
- Measures: fill power (cubic inches per ounce, IDFB cylinder test, about 300 for feathers to about 900 for the best goose down) [S13]; thread count (a North American marketing term of art, with the FTC acting on inflated counts) [S12]; knot density (kpsi) [S27]; GSM [S29].

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- The Metropolitan Museum describes Islamic carpets by court or region (Ushak, Anhalt Medallion, Vase, Polonaise), knot type (symmetric or asymmetric knotted pile) and tapestry-woven pieces; kilims "(or flat-weaves)" are a separate family [S26].
- Wikipedia on knotted-pile carpets and Persian carpets [S27][S35] uses the same layers: construction, then social context of weavers, then place labels. The Persian carpet article also gives format words (ghali, dozar, kelleghi, kenareh) that are size classes, not kinds [S35].

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

- **Agree:** the first split is by what is dressed (bed, floor, window, table, bath); rugs split by construction (knotted, woven, tufted, felt); quilts, comforters and duvets differ by construction; quality is quoted as numbers (tog, fill power, thread count, kpsi, GSM) alongside fibre grade.
- **Split:** pillows and cushions (bed or sofa); blankets and throws; whether fitted carpet is "rugs" (retail: no; HS: yes; EN 1307: it excludes rugs); whether place names such as Tabriz are types (dealers) or provenance (museums and the more careful dealers).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence for what owners decide first, from navigation order and buyer guides:
- **What is being dressed:** top-level navigation at IKEA, El Corte Inglés, Otto and John Lewis begins with bed, floor, window, table and bath groupings [S1][S3][S4][S34].
- **Then mechanism or construction, not material:** John Lewis duvet filters put Category and Tog first, then Filling and Size [S2]; the John Lewis guide says "Where to begin picking a duvet: Tog... Filling type" [S38]; Argos begins curtains with lining and heading, blinds with type [S19]; curtainspace says the mechanism comes first for blinds [S21]; Home Blinds Australia says "Start with the job, not the look" [S22]; rug dealers start with hand-knotted vs flatweave vs tufted [S8][S28]; IKEA Singapore's recommended rug filters start with pile type and suitable-for room before pattern [S39].
- **Performance tier second or third, as a number:** tog, fill power, thread count, GSM, kpsi appear as the second decision after the type (sections 4b and 5).
- **Fit or size is an attribute, not an axis (C6):** bed sizes (single to super king), rug sizes in tatami units (Nitori [S14]), towel sizes (bath sheet to washcloth [S30]). Where size or role is the whole difference (bath towel vs hand towel) it is a kind distinction; where it is a dimension (rug 160x230) it is an attribute.

So the owner-facing axis is **what the textile dresses, covers, screens or wraps**, and below it **construction or role**; material, tier, maker and region are filters or attributes.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (decided): dressed object, then product type, then construction or role

Levels (fixed names, C5(a)) and the single axis of each. At most four levels below the category; the named form is the fourth.
1. **Sub-family** — axis: *what the textile dresses, covers, screens or wraps* (the wording of the B2 test itself): bed (or heated table); floor; window, door or shower opening; seat; table and kitchen; body; then the closing sub-family "Parts and accessories made for one object", the only one exempt from this axis (C5(d)). The wording "bed or heated table" and "window, door or shower opening" is decided (B-3): the kotatsu cover and the shower curtain have a home, and there is no Japan leaf.
2. **Leaf** — axis stated per sub-family (C5(c)): for bed, floor and window textiles, *product type sold as a shopping category* (the leaf names in the retailer trees: bed linen, duvets and bed covers, pillows, mattress covers, rugs and carpets, mats, curtains, blinds and shades). **Seat, table and kitchen, body textiles, and parts and accessories hold 12 kinds or fewer each, so they skip the leaf level** (C5(b)); the data stores their kinds with `leaf = null`. The parts sub-family takes leaves by object served (curtains and blinds; rugs) once it outgrows about 12 kinds.
3. **Kind** — axis stated per leaf (or per sub-family where the leaf is skipped): *piece* for bed linen; *construction* for rugs and carpets (HS 5701–5705 order) and for bed insulating covers (duvet, comforter, quilt); *job* for mats; *fabric class* for curtains; *mechanism* for blinds; *object served, then fitting type* for parts and accessories; *form* (stuffed piece before cover) for seat textiles; *role and size* for towels.
4. **Named form** — models, cuts and iconic designs (Kilim, Soumak, Aubusson, Gabbeh, Wilton...), only when the name denotes a design or construction that travels independent of origin. Every named form carries `maker_bound` (C2(a)); all those below are `maker_bound: no`. Region or culture is a tag, never a level (C11).

### Candidate B: trade-classification split (floor coverings vs made-up articles)

## 5. Quality and tier

Verdict: **mixed, kind by kind.** There is no single ordering for the category. Under C4, no tier sits on a kind. A spec ladder measured on the object is an **attribute with its standard named** (C4(b)); a tier entry sits on a maker, line or named form (C4(a)). A scheme enters on one fetched source (CV-10, CV-12); a second source only adds weight and feeds `disputed`. B2 has no tier entry yet. The first candidates are reviewer results, such as Consumer Reports' test results for sheet brands [S23], as class (ii) or (iii) entries on makers, lines or named forms, read at the stage 3 harvest.
- **Spec-ranked (attributes):**
  - *Down fill power* (cubic inches per ounce, IDFB cylinder test; roughly 300 for feathers to about 900 for the best goose down) [S13]; *tog* (thermal overall grade, "relates to its warmth (but not its weight)") [S38]; the test standard behind tog is not named in a fetched page †. Both are measured numbers; fill power orders down quality, tog orders warmth. Tog rests on one UK source, which is enough to admit it (one-source rule; a second region would only add weight).
  - *Knot density* (kpsi, or raj for Persian rugs) for knotted-pile rugs only: as a rough scale, 80 or fewer poor, 120 to 330 medium to good, 330 or more very good, with the caveat that "KPSI is only one measurement of quality and value" [S27]. No test standard is named in the fetched page †.
  - *Fitted carpet use class* under EN 1307 and EN ISO 10874 (wear, appearance retention, luxury rating) [S10]; rugs and runners are outside that standard.
  - *Towel weight* (GSM), quoted in bands (300 to 400 travel and gym, 400 to 500 everyday, 500 to 600 quality, 600 and above luxury) [S29]. Weight is a spec but not strictly a quality rank; the fabric-mass standard is not named in a fetched page †. The band figures come from S29, whose fetch failed, so they are held until a fetched page shows them †.
- **Consensus-ranked or marketing-ranked (attributes with a caveat):** *thread count*, the most quoted number in sheets, is a North American "term of art"; counting plies inflates it, the FTC acted on inflated counts, linen experts quoted in the reference article say there is no quality difference beyond about 400 [S12], and Consumer Reports says count "isn't necessarily an indication of high quality" [S23]. Decided (B-16): it is stored, with its counting rule (ASTM D3775) and a ply-counting flag, as a marketing-ranked attribute. *Cotton staple class* (short, long, extra-long; Pima and Egyptian as extra-long; Supima a trademark) is a consensus grade [S23].
- **Preference (price band only):** curtains, blind fabrics, cushions, table linen, throws, decorative rugs. The only functional grades are lining and opacity (blackout, thermal, sunscreen) [S19][S22].
- **Where the data holds them:** as attributes on the kind, never as levels: `tog`, `fill_power` (with test method), `fill_type`, `thread_count` (with the ASTM D3775 counting rule [S11] and a flag for ply counting), `staple_class`, `gsm`, `knot_density_kpsi`, `standard_met` (EN 1307 and its class, EN ISO 10874, ASTM D3775; C17), `lining`, `opacity`, `process` (hand or machine, rule T), `size` (with the national bed-size table that B1 also uses; the standard is not named in a fetched page †, C6). Tier names ("luxury", "hotel quality") are not stored.
- **Price band (C4(d)):** one domain-wide USD scheme (0 (free), under 50, 50–150, 150–500, 500–1,500, 1,500–5,000, 5,000–15,000, 15,000–50,000 and the higher bands to over 5,000,000; B2 uses mostly the lower ones and leaves the unused bands empty; CV-13, CV-14), derived from a dated, sourced list price and held on a maker, line or named form. It is not a free field on the kind. The review's four-word vocabulary (budget, mid, premium, luxury) is not used.
- **Scale registry (C4(c)):** fill power, tog, thread count, GSM, knot density and the EN 1307 use class are each registered once as a class (i) scheme (an attribute with its standard named, never a tier entry). A reviewer's test score would be class (ii), a reviewer's grade class (iii); a shop's own label would be class (vi), a dated `platform_tag`, never a tier.
- **Platform tags and certifications:** none stored as tiers. A pass/fail mark is a certification field, not a tier.
- **No individual-item facts (C16):** the age, condition, appraisal or knot count of one particular rug is not stored. Grading scales are recorded only as the scheme a kind uses.
- **Jurisdiction ledger (C17):** no row proposed. The FTC action on thread count [S12] is a regulatory note on an attribute, not a legal class of a kind.

## 6. Makers and brands

- **Verdict: incidental for mass goods, identity for hand-woven rugs; the ledgers have no cap.**
  - *Incidental:* mass bedding, towels, curtains, cushions. Consumer Reports names brands as examples and rates them by test results (Amazon Basics, L.L.Bean, Matouk, Brooklinen, Parachute, Kirkland, Lands' End) [S23]; Nitori and IKEA are own-brand retailers whose product names are the models [S14][S3]. Such product names are maker-bound named forms or variants (C2, C3). They are recorded at the stage 3 harvest from maker and retailer pages, one fetched page each and with no cap (depth-and-breadth rule); they are not left out for want of an owner-emphasis scene.
  - *Identity:* hand-woven rugs, where the weaving centre or workshop is part of what is sought (Hereke workshops appear in the knot-density article [S27]; Aubusson appears as a type name and Missoni for T&J Vestor as a designer rug in 1stDibs listings [S16]; designer rug lines such as Schumacher and Stark Studio appear on Chairish [S17]).
  - *Platform:* none for the category as a whole. Two small compatibility cases go in the compatibility ledger (C13): motorised blinds (Somfy and Acmeda motors named by Home Blinds Australia [S22]) and curtain pole, track and ring systems [S19]. Sofa and chair covers record the sofa or chair model they fit in `fits` (B2-O1).
- **Maker ledger (C1), in the one domain-wide ledger:** `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class` (here: artisan or atelier for rug workshops; house or manufacture for linen and towel houses; retail own-label for IKEA and Nitori; independent; supplier; tannery or mill; licensee or diffusion where a licence applies); `country`; `place` (weaving centre); `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `licensor_id`; `authority_id`; `scene_tags`; `price_band` (C4(d)); `sources`. No B2 extension columns are proposed. `known_for_kinds` is not stored: it is derived from the named-form ledger.
- **Scope (decided, CV-2, CV-5):** every maker, atelier, workshop, mill and house on one fetched page each, with no cap and no restriction to rug workshops and linen houses. Makers, artisans and workshops are recorded in the ledger, never as kinds.
- **Separate tables, not columns (C1):**
  - *Lines and collections:* a designer rug line spans kinds and carries `built_by_maker_id` and `licensor_id` (the Missoni and Schumacher examples [S16][S17]); towel lines such as Lexi, Essence and Kind Earth at one specialist [S30]; `line_rank` for the maker's own order.
  - *Maker relations:* OEM, rebrand of, licensed by, with scope and years.
  - *School table (standing):* weaving traditions, linked to makers by `school_id`. Candidate rows seen in the sources are the Hereke workshops [S27] and Aubusson [S16]; the stage 3 census extends the list.
  - *Tier entries* (C4, per source, none yet); *named forms* (C2); *variants* (C3: colourway, print, size, collaboration, limited edition, bundle; filled wherever a community compares them, with no cap); *registers* for large named-form sets (C2(g)).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Following C7:
- **Sub-families** in the order of the B2 test wording: bed, floor, window, seat, table, body; the parts and accessories sub-family closes the list (C5(d)).
- **Kinds within a leaf** (or a leafless sub-family): *canonical form first*, then the secondary key the leaf states. Rugs follow HS order (the construction key): knotted, woven (flatweave, then woven pile), tufted, then the rest [S31]; towels run from the largest role to the smallest (bath sheet, bath towel, hand towel, washcloth) as the specialist's size filter does [S30]; blinds start with the roller blind, the most common type in the guides [S21][S22], then the others.
- **Named forms** (C7, CV-21): non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms by maker (ledger `name` order), first-introduction year, then alphabetically. Every B2 named form so far is `maker_bound: no` and no year is attested, so the order is alphabetical in practice; chronology is unreliable for names that are traditions. Ladder and ranking orders (a maker's `line_rank`, fill power, tog, knot density) are generated views, not stored order.
- **Variants:** by `variant_code` or year (none yet).
- Reason: buyers look up a kind by its canonical name first; specs order attributes, not kinds.

## 9. Boundaries

1. **Mattresses, futon bases and bean bags vs bedding.** A mattress, a shiki-futon used as the sleeping base and a bean bag are B1 (step 30, furniture and storage, which is tried before B2); a mattress topper, protector, pad and a kake-futon quilt are B2. Proposed sentence for B1 (review B1-3): "A mattress made to lie on the floor and be folded away (shiki-futon) is B1; the quilt laid over it (kake-futon) is B2." "Futon" is a homonym: US futon sofa (B1), shiki-futon (B1), kake-futon (B2 named form); see the alias table below.
2. **Tapestries, wall hangings and rugs.** An object made to be walked on, whether knotted, flatwoven or tufted, is a B2 rug even if it is later hung; an object made to be looked at (tapestry, textile hanging, Aubusson panel) is F2 Art under step 20. 1stDibs files Aubusson tapestries both as rugs and as wall decoration [S16], so the made-for test, not the listing, decides. A rug made as a prayer rug is F4 (K-113); the made-for test decides there too.
3. **Sleeping bags, picnic or camping rugs and sleeping mats vs bedding.** Sleeping bags and sleeping mats are E2 (sport and outdoor activity); a down duvet is B2 even though fill power is the same measure [S13]. Wearable baby sleeping bags and bathrobes are garments (A1); a hooded or ordinary towel is B2.
4. **Fitted carpet vs flooring and artificial turf.** Carpets and carpet tiles, loose or fitted, are B2; other flooring and tiles are C8; artificial turf is C2 (the framework's garden step), even though HS 5703 counts turf with tufted carpets [S31]. Fitted or loose is an installation attribute (rule M, C12).
5. **Curtain poles, tracks, rings and blind motors vs hardware.** Under S3 a part made for one kind of object goes with it, so poles, tracks, rings and blind cords are B2, not C8 (K-189 names curtain poles, rails and rings and rug underlay as B2); they are filed in the closing parts sub-family B2.7 (CV-17); fixed window joinery such as plantation shutters is a building product (C8) [S22 calls them "part of the joinery"]; mosquito nets are B8.
6. **Table linen vs tableware; tea towels.** Tablecloths, table runners, textile placemats and cloth napkins are B2 † (B5-O1); hard placemats, coasters, trivets and boards are B5; oven gloves and aprons are B5 (framework text); tea towels are B2 because "towels of every kind are B2". B5 relies on the same split.
7. **Pet bedding and baby bedding.** A bed, blanket or cushion made for an animal is B10 (animal equipment); baby and children's bedding is B2 with user as a modifier (BRIEF); a cot is B1.
8. **Cushion covers, sofa covers and chair covers vs vehicle seat covers.** Cushion covers, sofa covers and chair covers are B2 kinds, also when made to fit one sofa or chair model (the model goes in `fits`, C13); a textile cover for furniture is never an S3 part (B2-O1). A cover made for a car seat follows S3 to G1.
9. **Weighted blankets, orthopaedic pillows, positioning cushions and heated throws.** Heated throws and electric blankets are B2 (framework note on B7). Weighted blankets and orthopaedic pillows are B2 unless sold to treat a stated condition, when they are H2 (K-40); weight and orthopaedic shaping are attributes of Blanket or throw and Bed pillow. Positioning cushions, inflatable ones included, are B2 (K-82, H-14); furniture made to restrain or suspend is H3.
10. **Floor cushions, meditation cushions and mats vs furniture.** The F4 note puts meditation cushions and mats in B2 (K-113). A cushion with no frame made to sit on at floor level (zabuton, meditation cushion) is B2 †; a seat with a frame or rigid body (zaisu, stool, pouf, ottoman) and a bean bag are B1.
11. **Kotatsu and its cover.** The heated low table is B1 (framework note on B7); the quilt that covers it is B2 (review C-7 row 19). A towel radiator is a heater, B7 (row 18); a heated towel rail is B7 and a heated airer B6.
12. **Sets.** Bedding sets [S3], 6-piece towel sets [S30] and kake-shiki futon sets [S15] are a unit-of-sale attribute, with each piece filed as its own kind and no suite kinds (K-1).
13. **Studio backdrops.** A backdrop is B2 (K-17), kind Studio backdrop † in B2.3.1; studio furniture is B1, and studio lights, stands and flashes are D4.
14. **Carry cloths.** Furoshiki and other carry cloths are B2 (K-134), kind Carry cloth † in B2.6, with a cross-reference from A4.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure (Candidate A; B-3, B-15, B-16, B2-O1, B2-O2).** Category B2 > sub-family by what the textile dresses, covers, screens or wraps (bed or heated table; floor; window, door or shower opening; seat; table and kitchen; body), closed by the sub-family "Parts and accessories made for one object", which is exempt from that axis > leaf by product type where a sub-family holds more than about 12 kinds (bed, floor and window textiles; the others store `leaf = null`) > kind by piece, construction, use, job, mechanism, form, role and size, or object served > named form (non-maker designs and constructions that travel, such as Kilim, Soumak, Dhurrie, Aubusson, Gabbeh, Beni Ourain, Oushak, Bokhara, Savonnerie, Wilton, Axminster and kake-futon †, plus maker-bound product names from the ledger). Maximum depth below the category: four levels. The tree has 66 kinds today, 15 of them †, and no cap. Rug construction is the kind, and city, village and tribal names are provenance tags; fitted carpet and loose rugs are one leaf with `installation` an attribute; blanket and throw is one kind. Beside the tree sit attributes (tog, fill power, thread count with its counting rule and ply flag, GSM, knot density, `standard_met`, lining, opacity, `process`, weight, size, `fits`), tags (region, culture, era, setting, scene), the USD price band, the one domain-wide maker ledger with lines, relations, schools, tier entries, variants and registers, and the compatibility ledger.

**Reasons:** it uses the axis the B2 test and the retailers already use; it avoids Appendix A modifiers (room, material, user, installation, power source); it keeps loose rugs and fitted carpet together as the HS does, with installation as an attribute; and it puts tier numbers where buyers actually compare them, as attributes.

**Remaining risks.**
- Some of the 15 † kinds may not be found on a fetched page; those stay out of the data.
- Level calls that rest on regions: peshtemal as a kind (CV-27) and the kotatsu cover (sold in Japan); each is provisional until attested.
