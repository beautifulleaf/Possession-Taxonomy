## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B2 Soft Furnishings.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, branch 6): *made to cover, soften or dress beds, floors, windows, seats and tables, or to wrap or dry the body: bedding, mattress toppers, rugs and carpets (loose or fitted), chair and floor mats, blinds and curtains, cushions, throws, electric blankets, table linen, towels, swaddling cloths, travel pillows.* Category list line: "bedding, rugs and carpets, blinds and curtains, cushions, throws, table linen, towels."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name B2:**
- Step 30 note on B7: electric blankets and heated throws are B2.
- Step 5/G2 note: carpets, blinds and curtains are B2 (they are not building products, even when fitted).
- Step 18 (H1) note: towels of every kind are B2.
- Step 7 (F6) note: tanned hides and leather are C4 as material and B2 as rugs.
- Step 30 branch order: B5, B6, B7, B8, B3, B1 (beds and mattresses, bean bags) are tried before B2; B4 (decor) after it.
- Step 20 sends tapestries and textile hangings to F2 Art, before B2 is reached.
- S3: parts follow the whole (matters for curtain poles, rug pads, cushion covers, see section 9).
- Appendix A: installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted), setting, user, material, region, era, price and tier, brand are modifiers.

Where retailers agree: the first split is by the thing dressed or wrapped (bed, floor, window, table, bath), not by fibre. IKEA, El Corte Inglés and the Spanish Conforama tree all use bed, bath, decoration (rugs, cushions, curtains) and table/kitchen blocks. Where they split the same objects differently: **pillows** (bedding at John Lewis, "Descanso" with mattresses at El Corte Inglés); **cushions** (with rugs at IKEA, a separate department at John Lewis); **blankets and throws** (inside bedding at John Lewis and El Corte Inglés, their own leaf at IKEA, "Wohndecken" beside beds at Otto); **mattresses** (inside home textiles at Otto, outside at the others; the framework files them in B1).

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, rug dealers' encyclopedias and wikis

- Rug dealers and enthusiasts divide rugs first by **construction**: knotted pile vs flatweave, then hand-knotted vs hand-tufted vs machine-made (Harcourt [S28]; Kilim.com [S8]). Dealer navigation (Kilim.com) offers Type (vintage, overdyed, patchwork, runner, new kilims, hand-knotted), Style, Size, Age, Color [S8].
- The same dealers then divide **Persian rugs** by social context of production (city or workshop, village or trade, tribal or nomadic) and by place names: Catalina Rug lists Traditional (Tabriz, Kashan, Isfahan, Kerman...), Village (Heriz, Qashqai...), Nomadic (Bakhtiari, Shiraz...) and "Persian-style" Pakistani and Indian types [S7]; Wikipedia says Persian carpets "are best classified by the social context of their weavers" [S35].
- Both enthusiasts and dealers warn that these names are not construction types. WOVEN: "Names such as Tabriz, Kashan, Heriz... may point to a city, region, workshop network, community, or trade convention"; "pile and flatwoven structures... construction may cut across geographic labels" [S5]. Catalina: "Hamadan" is "a collective name for all rugs woven in that region" [S7]. The Turkotek collector forum debates Afshar attribution by knot type, design source and ethnicity and calls it "a broad rubric" [S37]. Wikipedia: a village name may be used "as a label for a special quality", as with Serapi [S35].
- Flatweave names overlap the same way: "Kilim commonly describes weft-faced tapestry; dhurrie is a South Asian rug category...; soumak is distinguished by weft wrapping... The names therefore overlap" [S6]; the trade uses kilim and flatweave interchangeably [S8].
- Sleep and sheet guides (Consumer Reports [S23], Wirecutter [S25], Sleep Foundation [S24]) divide bedding by **construction and weave**: duvet (insert plus separate cover), comforter (cover built in), quilt (stitched layers); sheets by weave (percale, sateen), fibre staple (short, long, extra-long; Pima, Egyptian, Supima) and thread count.
- Towel guides (allcottonandlinen [S29]) list seven types that mix **roles** (bath towel, bath sheet, hand towel, face towel) with **weaves** (Turkish/peshtemal, waffle, terry) and give weight bands in GSM.
- Window-covering guides disagree on the first axis: for blinds the **mechanism** comes first ("Bei Rollos steht der Mechanismus an erster Stelle", [S21]); for curtains the fabric and heading ([S21], [S19]); Wikipedia uses "blind" for slatted types and "shade" for single soft pieces, and notes the loose use of "window blinds" for all coverings [S20].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- 1stDibs "Rugs and Carpets" [S16]: facets item type (new/made to order, antique and vintage, pre-owned), style, period, material, place of origin. Its listing categories read as period plus origin plus a name (for example "Mid-20th Century French Aubusson", "Early 20th Century Heriz Serapi", "Turkish Kashan", "Indian Sultanabad"), so the **same name floats free of place** (a Heriz-design rug listed as Afghan, a Kashan as Turkish).
- Chairish [S17]: Rugs, shop by style and brand; named styles (Beni Ourain, jute, hide, rag rugs, Turkish kilims, Moroccan Berber) next to material and colour.
- Christie's [S18]: "Rugs and Carpets" is a collector department of "antique hand-woven rugs and carpets dating from the 15th to the early 20th century", sold inside Islamic and Indian Worlds sales.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **WCO Harmonized System 2022 chapter 57** [S31]: 5701 knotted; 5702 woven, not tufted or flocked, "including Kelem, Schumacks, Karamanie and similar hand-woven rugs", with coir and pile/non-pile; 5703 tufted (including turf); 5704 felt; 5705 other. Division is by **construction first**, then fibre.
- **HTS chapter 63** [S32]: 6301 blankets and travelling rugs (including electric blankets); 6304 other furnishing articles (including wall hangings); Note 3 groups bed linen, table linen, toilet linen and kitchen linen. (Heading 6303, curtains and interior blinds, was seen only in a search excerpt of a different PDF and is not cited as fetched.)
- **UNSPSC** family 5212 "Bedclothes and table and kitchen linen and towels" [S33]; class 52121500 Bedclothes lists quilts, comforters, comforter covers, mattress covers, pillows, mattress pads, featherbeds, blankets, sheets, dust ruffles, duvets, pillow cases, bed spreads, duvet cover.
- **ISO 2424:2024** defines textile floor coverings and their categories [S9]; **EN 1307** classifies textile floor coverings and carpet tiles "excluding rugs and runners" into use classes for wear, appearance retention, additional performance and luxury rating, and refers to EN ISO 10874 room-use classes [S10]. So the standards world itself treats **fitted carpet and made-up rugs as different regimes**.
- **ASTM** [S11] keeps Home Furnishings standards for bedspreads, comforters, curtain and drapery fabrics, soft window coverings, towel products, shower curtains, sheeting products; a separate Pile Floor Coverings family; D3775 end and pick count; D4522 feather and down fillings.
- Measures: fill power (cubic inches per ounce, IDFB cylinder test, about 300 for feathers to about 900 for the best goose down) [S13]; thread count (a North American marketing term of art, with the FTC acting on inflated counts) [S12]; knot density (kpsi) [S27]; GSM [S29].

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- The Metropolitan Museum describes Islamic carpets by court or region (Ushak, Anhalt Medallion, Vase, Polonaise), knot type (symmetric or asymmetric knotted pile) and tapestry-woven pieces; kilims "(or flat-weaves)" are a separate family [S26].
- Wikipedia on knotted-pile carpets and Persian carpets [S27][S35] uses the same layers: construction, then social context of weavers, then place labels. The Persian carpet article also gives format words (ghali, dozar, kelleghi, kenareh) that are size classes, not kinds [S35].

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

- **Agree:** the first split is by what is dressed (bed, floor, window, table, bath); rugs split by construction (knotted, woven, tufted, felt); quilts, comforters and duvets differ by construction; quality is quoted as numbers (tog, fill power, thread count, kpsi, GSM) alongside fibre grade.
- **Split:** pillows and cushions (bed or sofa); blankets and throws; mattress protectors and toppers (bedding at John Lewis, "protectors/toppers" under Descanso at El Corte Inglés); whether fitted carpet is "rugs" (retail: no; HS: yes; EN 1307: it excludes rugs); whether place names such as Tabriz are types (dealers) or provenance (museums and the more careful dealers).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence for what owners decide first, from navigation order and buyer guides:
- **What is being dressed:** top-level navigation at IKEA, El Corte Inglés, Conforama, Otto and John Lewis all begins with bed, floor, window, table and bath groupings [S1][S3][S4][S34].
- **Then mechanism or construction, not material:** John Lewis duvet filters put Category and Tog first, then Filling and Size [S2]; the John Lewis guide says "Where to begin picking a duvet: Tog... Filling type" [S38]; Argos begins curtains with lining and heading, blinds with type [S19]; curtainspace says the mechanism comes first for blinds [S21]; Home Blinds Australia says "Start with the job, not the look" [S22]; rug dealers start with hand-knotted vs flatweave vs tufted [S8][S28]; IKEA Singapore filters rugs by pile type before pattern [S39].
- **Performance tier second or third, as a number:** tog, fill power, thread count, GSM, kpsi appear as the second decision after the type (sections 4b and 5).
- **Fit or size is a filter, not an axis:** bed sizes (single to super king), rug sizes in tatami units (Nitori [S14]), towel sizes (bath sheet to washcloth [S30]). Where size or role is the whole difference (bath towel vs hand towel) it is a kind distinction; where it is a dimension (rug 160x230) it is an attribute.

So the owner-facing axis is **what the textile dresses**, and below it **construction or role**; material, tier, maker and region are filters.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): dressed object, then product type, then construction or role

Levels and the single axis of each:
1. **Sub-family** — axis: *what the textile dresses or wraps* (the wording of the B2 test itself): bed; floor; window; seat; table; body.
2. **Leaf** — axis: *product type sold as a shopping category* (the leaf names in the retailer trees: bed linen, duvets and bed covers, pillows, rugs and carpets, mats, curtains, blinds and shades, cushions, towels...).
3. **Kind** — axis stated per leaf: *construction* for rugs and carpets (HS 5701–5705 order) and for bed insulating covers (duvet, comforter, quilt); *mechanism* for blinds; *role and size* for towels; *piece* for bed linen.
4. **Named form** — models, cuts and iconic designs (Kilim, Soumak, Aubusson, Gabbeh, Imabari towel...), only when the name denotes a design or construction that travels independent of origin.

Where it breaks (edge objects):
1. **Throw/blanket**: the object dresses a bed or a sofa. Resolved by S5 (test the object): one kind "Blanket/Throw" in B2.1, with setting (sofa) as a tag. Retail splits (IKEA own leaf, John Lewis in bedding) show a sub-family-neutral kind would also be valid.
2. **Cushion vs pillow**: a bed pillow and a scatter cushion differ by use, yet both are stuffed rectangles; sub-family places them (bed vs seat).
3. **Kilim used as a wall hanging or a cushion cover** (1stDibs lists kilim cushion covers and Aubusson tapestries inside rugs [S16]): the sub-family says floor, step 20 says F2 if made to hang.
4. **Curtain pole/track**: part of a curtain under S3, but sold as hardware (Argos "poles and tracks" [S19]).

### Candidate B: trade-classification split (floor coverings vs made-up articles)

- Level 1 — axis *construction of the exposed surface*: Carpets and floor coverings (HS 57) vs made-up textile articles (HS 63) [S31][S32].
- Level 2 — axis *article*: knotted, woven, tufted, felt | blankets, bed linen, table linen, toilet and kitchen linen, curtains and blinds, other furnishing (cushions) [S31][S32][S33].
- Example kinds (12): knotted carpet, woven rug, tufted carpet, felt carpet, blanket, electric blanket, bed sheet, pillowcase, tablecloth, towel, curtain, cushion cover.
- Breaks: towels and kilims are both woven but sit in different chapters; cushions, mattress covers and wall hangings fall into "other furnishing"; trade classes ignore buyer-facing differences such as duvet vs comforter; blinds and curtains share one heading although mechanisms differ [S21].

### Candidate C: room or setting split (retailer "textile rooms")

- Level 1 — axis *room of use*: bedroom, bathroom, kitchen, living room, outdoor, kids (IKEA bathroom textiles, kitchen towels, outdoor cushions, textiles for kids appear in its tree [S3]; El Corte Inglés "Textil baño" and "Mini Home" [S34]).
- Level 2 — *product type*.
- Example kinds (10): sheet, duvet, bath towel, bath mat, kitchen towel, tablecloth, scatter cushion, outdoor cushion, children's bedding, nursery rug.
- Breaks: setting and user are Appendix A modifiers; a bath mat is also a rug; a towel is used in bath, kitchen, beach and gym; the same duvet appears in "adult" and "kids" lists.
