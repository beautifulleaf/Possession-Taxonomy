## 0. Category

**Code and name:** F5 Documents & Personal Records. Category list line: "issued documents, access and payment cards, and personal records and keepsake papers."

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 19): *a **document issued to prove** identity, status, ownership or achievement (passport, licence, certificate, deed, diploma, share certificate, identity and access badges); a **card, pass or token issued to give access to an account, service, place or stored value** (payment, gift, transit, membership and key cards); or a **personal record or keepsake paper**: letters, written diaries, photo albums, photographic prints and slides of the owner's own life, family and places, home recordings, scrapbooks, picture postcards used or not, autographs, kept tickets and programmes, house plans?*

**Notes and precedence sentences that name F5:**
- Step 19 note: published works are F1; photographs made as art, and posters, are F2; blank notebooks, diaries, greeting cards and calendars are C5; blank scrapbook albums are C4.
- Step 15 note (F3): paper certificates and diplomas are F5. Casino and poker chips are E1.
- Step 17 note (A3): identity and access badges are F5; a badge or patch worn as ornament is A3; medical alert bracelets and tags are H2.
- Step 21 note (F1): home recordings of the owner's own life are F5; blank recording media, and carriers recorded with published music or film such as mixtapes, are F1.
- Step 5 (I2): the owner's own photographs, home videos and scanned records held as files are I2, "the carrier decides: prints are F5". Step 5 (I3) lists "season tickets and passes for venues, events or travel valid for a period or a number of visits" and membership as subscriptions, and step 5 comes before step 19.
- Step 24 / D1: hardware keys and security keys are D1 (D1 memo: payment, access and transit cards are F5, a hardware wallet is D1). A4 memo: the wallet, card holder and passport cover are A4, the cards inside are F5 or I3.
- S5 (test the object, not the motive) and Appendix A: being written, drawn or recorded on is not condition; used paper goods become records (step 19). Edition (limited, signed), era, price and tier, brand and maker, region, occasion and motive are modifiers.
- Key position: step 19 comes after software and subscriptions (5), money and awards (15), timepieces (16) and worn items (17), and before art (20) and media (21).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** (1) paper and card records are one family whose members differ by function (certificate, letter, ticket, postcard); (2) photographs, film and tape are separate because their carrier decides how they are kept (archives, ISO 18902, albums); (3) an album, scrapbook or box is a record-holding object sold by mechanism and format.
- **Split:** (a) scripophily is filed with money by Wikipedia and HTS but with ephemera by dealers [S23][S29][S11]; (b) the Ephemera Society counts passports, deeds and birth certificates as ephemera, while the framework files them as issued documents; (c) HS 4909 puts postcards and greeting cards together, the framework puts used greeting cards and picture postcards in F5 and blank ones in C5; (d) the card world splits by technology and format (ISO 7810, optical against chip) while the framework splits cards by what they give access to; (e) archives split by carrier, collectors by topic, standards by size.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Three audiences choose differently, and F5 has no single axis.

1. **Holders of issued documents and cards choose by function:** which paper proves who I am, who I married, what I own, what I achieved; which card pays, stores value, rides the train, opens the door. Evidence: the Ephemera Society, NAA and Wikipedia identity-document pages all name documents by what they certify [S12][S34][S21]; the payment and stored-value pages divide cards by what the card does (credit, debit, gift, phone, transit) [S20][S18]; the standards divide by size, but only after function (ID-1 holds licences, payment and transit cards alike) [S27].
2. **Buyers of albums choose by mechanism, then photo size, then capacity.** Hakuba's four sub-categories [S1], Gaylord's Album Type facet [S5], and the German slip-in against stick-in contrast with format and capacity filters [S2][S3] all lead with how the pages hold prints.
3. **Collectors choose by kind of paper, then by topic.** The Ephemera Society says ephemera is organised by topic, not by item [S12]; phonecard collectors choose country, company, theme and chip type [S15]; scripophilists choose industry [S13].
4. **Conservators choose by carrier:** paper, bound, photographic, film, magnetic [S32][S37].

**Result.** For kinds, the first axis is **function (what the object proves, gives access to, or remembers)**; for photographic and recorded records, the **carrier** is the function (print, slide, negative, film, tape), as in F1; for the albums that hold records, the **page mechanism**. Maker, tier and price do not lead anywhere in this category.

## 3. Candidate structures

### 3.1 Candidate A — function first (recommended)

**Levels and axes.**
- Sub-family: *what the object is for* (proves a fact; gives access to something; keeps a record). This is the three-part membership test of step 19, so the placement key and the tree agree.
- Leaf: *what it certifies, what it gives access to, or how the record was made* (one stated axis for each sub-family, named in the tree below).
- Kind: the generic type a person can own (passport, gift card, photographic print).
- Named form: a documented variant or iconic design (ICAO passport code PD, Suica, cabinet card, Walther Monza album).

**Example sub-families and leaves with 10 to 15 kinds.** Issued documents: identity and travel (passport, national identity card, driving licence, residence permit, visa); civil status (birth certificate, marriage certificate); title and legal instruments (deed, will, share certificate); achievement (diploma, certificate). Cards: payment (credit card, debit card); stored value (gift card, telephone card); transit (transit farecard). Records: written (letter, written diary); photographic (print, slide, negative); compiled (slip-in album, scrapbook). The full tree is in 3.4.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. A gym or club **membership card**: the entitlement is a subscription (I3) while the plastic is a card (F5). Needs the precedence sentence P4 in section 9.
2. A **photo ID badge that also opens doors**: identity badge (F5.1e) or key card (F5.2e)? Rule: if the face carries the holder's name and photograph it is an identity badge; the access function is an attribute.
3. A **signed photograph or autograph letter**: the signature is an edition modifier (Appendix A), so it is a photographic print or a letter with a "signed" tag; only a signature collected on a blank card or page (an autograph) is its own kind.
4. A **gift certificate on paper**: sits beside gift cards (stored value) in A, but company scrip is F3 (tokens and scrip). Needs P5.

### 3.2 Candidate B — carrier first (archival practice)

**Levels and axes.**
- Sub-family: *carrier* (single paper sheet; bound booklet or book; plastic card; photographic print or slide; film and tape).
- Leaf: function (certificate, letter, ticket, card of a given use).
- Kind and named form as in A.

**Example kinds:** passport (bound), diary (bound), photo album (bound), scrapbook (bound); birth certificate, diploma, deed, letter, ticket stub, postcard (single sheet); credit card, gift card, transit farecard, key card, identity badge (plastic card); photographic print, slide, negative (photographic); home film, home videotape (film and tape). This follows the National Archives holdings menu [S32] and ISO 7810 [S27].

**Where it breaks.**
1. **Gift card (plastic) and gift certificate (paper)** are the same function but fall in different sub-families.
2. A **scrapbook** is bound but is a bundle of prints, tickets, postcards and letters of every carrier.
3. A **passport book** and a **passport card** (or a national ID booklet and ID card) land in different sub-families although both are the same kind of proof.
4. A **diploma** printed on card stock and one on paper split with no difference in function.

### 3.3 Candidate C — life domain (what the paper is about)

**Levels and axes.**
- Sub-family: *area of life* (identity and travel, money, property, education and work, family, memory).
- Leaf: kind of document or record within the area.

**Example kinds:** passport, visa, driving licence (identity and travel); credit card, debit card, gift card, share certificate (money); deed, will (property); diploma, certificate (education); birth certificate, marriage certificate, letter (family); photographic print, scrapbook, ticket stub (memory).

**Where it breaks.** It fails rule S1 (one home) too easily, and the areas are motives rather than properties of the object (S5).
1. A **marriage certificate** is family, identity and law at once.
2. A **transit pass** is travel and money.
3. A **student card** is education, identity and access.
4. A **postcard from a holiday** is travel and memory.
It also cannot be used as a tree level without leaning on occasion and motive, which Appendix A forbids as splits. Kept as a **generated view** (see section 7), not as a structure.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds.** About 53 in the tree above. With the ephemera that collectors list [S11][S12][S24] and further document types (licences and permits of other kinds, vaccination records, school reports, tax and insurance papers), the full count is probably 80 to 120. The count is capped by how far the owner wants keepsake paper to extend (O2).
- **Named forms.** The fetched sources attest about 40 (9 ICAO passport codes, 4 national ID designs, 1 residence-permit design, 2 gift-card systems, 2 phonecard technologies, 7 transit systems, 4 photographic formats, 8 album and binder lines). At full depth the count could reach 150 to 300 if national ID and licence designs are kept as forms. Because those are region-bound, the recommendation is to hold them as region tags on the kind, and keep as named forms only those the sources name as a distinct form (ICAO codes, photographic formats, card systems, album lines).
- **Where depth concentrates.** (1) Compiled records, where retailers split by mechanism; (2) photographic and recorded carriers, where archives and collectors split by format; (3) transit and phone cards, where each scheme is a named form; (4) ephemera, which is open-ended (the Encyclopedia of Ephemera lists over 500 categories [S24]).
- **Breadth of leaves.** Broad: F5.3e kept souvenirs, F5.3d compiled records, F5.2b stored value. Medium: F5.1a identity and travel, F5.1c title and legal, F5.2a payment, F5.3a written, F5.3b photographic. Narrow: F5.1b civil status, F5.1d achievement, F5.1e badges, F5.2e key cards, F5.3c home recordings.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers or collectors actually compare quality or makers. For F5 almost no one compares issued documents or cards by quality; the comparisons happen (a) between album and storage products, (b) between collectable formats, and (c) between holders of cards by type of card. The choice level therefore differs by sub-family.

| Sub-family | Kinds stop at | Finer distinctions are | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| F5.1 Issued documents | the document type (passport, driving licence, birth certificate, deed, diploma) | named forms (ICAO passport code, a national design) and tags (issuing country, era, material) | No retailer compares them; the standards split a passport by holder class [S28] and by size [S27], the Ephemera Society names them by type [S12]. |
| F5.2 Cards | the function type (credit, debit, gift, telephone, transit, membership, key) | card systems and technologies are named forms; issuer, country and design are tags | Wikipedia's payment and stored-value pages split by function [S20][S18]; collectors add chip type and country but treat them as sub-specialties [S15][S16]. |
| F5.3b/c Photographic and recorded | the carrier (print, slide, negative, film reel, videotape) | print formats (carte de visite, cabinet card) are named forms | Archives and conservators split by carrier [S32][S35][S37]; the Ephemera Society names the formats collectors seek [S12]. |
| F5.3d Compiled records (albums) | the page-holding mechanism (slip-in, ring-binder, post-bound, stick-in, accordion, box) | product lines and sizes are named forms and attributes (capacity, print size, cover material) | Hakuba, Gaylord, fotoalben-discount and kasuwa all lead with mechanism; size and capacity are filters [S1][S2][S4][S5]. Not kinds: the individual passport or letter, a given person's autograph, the card issuer's tier (see section 5).
