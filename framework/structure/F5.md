## 0. Category

**Code and name:** F5 Documents & Personal Records. Category list line: "issued documents, access and payment cards, and personal records and keepsake papers."

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 19): *a **document issued to prove** identity, status, ownership or achievement (passport, licence, certificate, deed, diploma, share certificate, identity and access badges); a **card, pass or token issued to give access to an account, service, place or stored value** (payment, gift, transit, membership and key cards); or a **personal record or keepsake paper**: letters, written diaries, photo albums, photographic prints, developed negatives and slides, home recordings, scrapbooks, picture postcards used or not, autographs, kept tickets and programmes, house plans, and other kept printed ephemera (menus, invitations, trade cards, receipts, labels, bookplates)?*

**Notes and precedence sentences that name F5:**
- Step 19 note (v0.7): published works are F1; a photographic print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2, and every other photographic print, slide or negative, whoever is shown, is F5 (K-139); posters are F2; types of kept printed ephemera become kinds only by the choice-level test (C2), otherwise they are named forms (K-140); blank notebooks, diaries, greeting cards and calendars are C5; blank scrapbook albums are C4.
- Step 15 note (F3): paper certificates and diplomas are F5. Casino and poker chips are E1. Payment, gift, telephone, transit, membership and key cards are F5 whether live, used or expired; a token issued to stand for money or a service is F3; paper issued to circulate as money (scrip, emergency money, Notgeld) is F3; a voucher or gift certificate redeemable only with its issuer is F5; postal stationery with a printed stamp, and a cover issued by a postal administration or prepared as a first-day cover, are F3; any other used envelope, letter or picture postcard is F5, a cover that passed through the post included (K-118). Game money is E1; prop and souvenir notes are F3.
- Step 17 note (A3): identity and access badges are F5; a badge or patch worn as ornament is A3; medical alert bracelets and tags are H2.
- Step 21 note (F1): home recordings on a physical carrier are F5 (K-141); blank recording media, and carriers recorded with published music or film such as mixtapes, are F1.
- Step 5 (I2): photographs, home videos and scanned records held as files are I2, "the carrier decides: prints are F5". Step 5 (I3) lists "season tickets and passes for venues, events or travel valid for a period or a number of visits" and membership as subscriptions, and step 5 comes before step 19.
- Step 24 / D1: hardware keys and security keys are D1 (D1 memo: payment, access and transit cards are F5, a hardware wallet is D1). A4 memo: the wallet, card holder and passport cover are A4, the cards inside are F5 or I3.
- Step 1 notes: physical tokens are objects (printed certificates, kept tickets and access, membership or payment cards are step 19); the stored value, balance or entitlement behind a card (gift-card and wallet value, a membership that gives only discounts or points) is OUT. Step 5 note: a pass, membership or season ticket is I3 whatever its form (card, app or paper), and "a card kept after it has expired or been used up is F5".
- S3 and S3b (K-9): a holder, case, sleeve or stand made for one object follows it; an album, binder, stock book, box or tray made to hold several objects is B1 when empty; a filled photo album or scrapbook is F5; a blank scrapbook album is C4 (step 19 note).
- Step 1 notes (K-27, K-28): a printed share or bond certificate, cheque, traveller's cheque or postal order kept as an object is F5; a physical SIM card is F5 and an eSIM profile is part of its I3 plan.
- S5 (test the object, not the motive) and Appendix A: being written, drawn or recorded on is not condition; used paper goods become records (step 19). Edition (limited, signed), era, price and tier, brand and maker, region, occasion and motive are modifiers.
- Key position: step 19 comes after software and subscriptions (5), money and awards (15), timepieces (16) and worn items (17), and before art (20) and media (21).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** (1) paper and card records are one family whose members differ by function (certificate, letter, ticket, postcard); (2) photographs, film and tape are separate because their carrier decides how they are kept (archives, ISO 18902, albums); (3) an album, scrapbook or box is a record-holding object sold by mechanism and format.
- **Split:** (a) scripophily is filed with money by Wikipedia and HTS but with ephemera by dealers [S23][S29][S11]; (b) the Ephemera Society counts passports, deeds and birth certificates as ephemera, while the framework files them as issued documents; (c) HS 4909 puts postcards and greeting cards together, the framework puts used greeting cards and picture postcards in F5 and blank ones in C5; (d) the card world splits by technology and format (ISO 7810, optical against chip) while the framework splits cards by what they give access to; (e) archives split by carrier, collectors by topic, standards by size.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Three audiences choose differently, and F5 has no single axis.

1. **Holders of issued documents and cards choose by function:** which paper proves who I am, who I married, what I own, what I achieved; which card pays, stores value, rides the train, opens the door. Evidence: the Ephemera Society, NAA and Wikipedia identity-document pages all name documents by what they certify [S12][S34][S21]; the payment and stored-value pages divide cards by what the card does (credit, debit, gift, phone, transit) [S20][S18]; the standards divide by size, but only after function (ID-1 holds licences, payment and transit cards alike) [S27].
2. **Buyers of albums choose by mechanism, then photo size, then capacity.** Hakuba's four sub-categories [S1], Gaylord's Album Type facet [S5], and the German slip-in against stick-in contrast with format and capacity filters [S2][S3] all lead with how the pages hold prints.
3. **Collectors choose by kind of paper, then by topic.** The Ephemera Society says ephemera is organised by topic, not by item [S12]; phonecard collectors choose country, company, theme and chip type [S15]; scripophilists choose industry [S13].
4. **Conservators choose by carrier:** paper, bound, photographic, film, magnetic [S32][S37].

**Result.** For kinds, the first axis is **function (what the object proves, gives access to, or remembers)**; for photographic and recorded records, the **carrier** is the function (print, slide, negative, film, tape), as in F1; for the albums that hold records, the **page mechanism**. Maker, tier and price do not lead anywhere in this category.

## 3. Candidate structures

### 3.1 Candidate A — function first (recommended)

**Levels and axes.**
- Sub-family: *what the object is for* (proves a fact; gives access to something; keeps a record). This is the three-part membership test of step 19, so the placement key and the tree agree.
- Leaf: *what it certifies, what it gives access to, or how the record was made* (one stated axis for each sub-family, named in the tree below).
- Kind: the generic type a person can own (passport, gift card, photographic print).
- Named form: a documented design below a kind (ICAO passport code PD, Suica, cabinet card, Personalausweis), recorded with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2a). Technologies (optical, chip, magnetic), acceptance classes (closed, semi-closed), sizes and formats under ISO 7810 are attributes, never named forms (C2b, C10). The designs an issuing authority gives its own passport, identity card, licence or residence permit are non-maker named forms linked to the issuer table (F5-O1); the issuing state is also a tag.

**Example sub-families and leaves with 10 to 15 kinds.** Issued documents: identity and travel (passport, national identity card, driving licence, residence permit, visa); civil status (birth certificate, marriage certificate); title and legal instruments (deed, will, share certificate, cheque); achievement (diploma, certificate). Cards: payment (credit card, debit card); stored value (gift card, telephone card); transit (transit farecard); key and network access (key card, SIM card). Records: written (letter, written diary); photographic (print, slide, negative); compiled (photo album, scrapbook); kept printed ephemera (menu, ticket stub, advertising trade card). The full tree is in 3.4.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. A gym or club **membership card**: the entitlement is a subscription (I3) while the plastic is a card (F5). Needs the precedence sentence P4 in section 9.
2. A **photo ID badge that also opens doors**: identity badge, access badge (both F5.1e) or key card (F5.2e)? Rule (P5): if the face carries the holder's name or photograph and the card is carried to show who the holder is or what site they may enter, it is an identity or access badge; a card that only opens a lock and names no holder (a hotel key card) is a key card. The other function is an attribute.
3. A **signed photograph or autograph letter**: the signature is an edition modifier (Appendix A), so it is a photographic print or a letter with a "signed" tag; only a signature collected on a blank card or page (an autograph) is its own kind. A print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2 (K-139, P2).
4. A **gift certificate on paper**: sits beside gift cards (stored value) in A, but company scrip is F3 (tokens and scrip). Needs P3.
5. A **real photo postcard**: a photographic print on postcard stock and a picture postcard. One home, F5.3b (the carrier decides, as the Ephemera Society lists it under photographs [S12]); the picture-postcard view lists it too (C14).

### 3.2 Candidate B — carrier first (archival practice)

**Levels and axes.**
- Sub-family: *carrier* (single paper sheet; bound booklet or book; plastic card; photographic print or slide; film and tape).
- Leaf: function (certificate, letter, ticket, card of a given use).
- Kind and named form as in A.

**Example kinds:** passport (bound), diary (bound), photo album (bound), scrapbook (bound); birth certificate, diploma, deed, letter, ticket stub, postcard (single sheet); credit card, gift card, transit farecard, key card, identity badge (plastic card); photographic print, slide, negative (photographic); home film, home videotape (film and tape). This follows the National Archives holdings menu [S32] and ISO 7810 [S27].

**Where it breaks.**
1. **Gift card (plastic) and gift certificate (paper)** are the same function but fall in different sub-families.
2. A **scrapbook** is bound but is a bundle of prints, tickets, postcards and letters of every carrier.
3. A **passport book** and a **passport card** (or a national ID booklet and ID card) land in different sub-families although both are the same kind of proof.
4. A **diploma** printed on card stock and one on paper split with no difference in function.

### 3.3 Candidate C — life domain (what the paper is about)

**Levels and axes.**
- Sub-family: *area of life* (identity and travel, money, property, education and work, family, memory).
- Leaf: kind of document or record within the area.

**Example kinds:** passport, visa, driving licence (identity and travel); credit card, debit card, gift card, share certificate (money); deed, will (property); diploma, certificate (education); birth certificate, marriage certificate, letter (family); photographic print, scrapbook, ticket stub (memory).

**Where it breaks.** It fails rule S1 (one home) too easily, and the areas are motives rather than properties of the object (S5).
1. A **marriage certificate** is family, identity and law at once.
2. A **transit pass** is travel and money.
3. A **student card** is education, identity and access.
4. A **postcard from a holiday** is travel and memory.
It also cannot be used as a tree level without leaning on occasion and motive, which Appendix A forbids as splits. Kept as a **generated view** (see section 7), not as a structure.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds.** 66 in the tree above, as a seed with no cap (CV-5, CV-7). With the ephemera that collectors list [S11][S12][S24] (the Encyclopedia of Ephemera names over 500 categories) and further document types (licences and permits of other kinds, insurance papers), the full count is probably well over 120; the decision K-140 expected about 120 at the first extension. Each type enters on one fetched page and is a kind or a named form by the choice-level test.
- **Named forms.** 28 in the tree (9 ICAO passport codes, 6 identity-card designs, 1 residence-permit design, 1 ketubah, 7 transit card systems, 4 photographic formats), uncapped. Per-issuer passport, identity card, licence and residence-permit designs are non-maker named forms linked to the issuer table (F5-O1); with every issuer's designs the count could reach 150 to 300 and more, so they are kept in an issuer-design register linked to their kind (C2g, CV-45), and the issuing state stays a tag as well. Two gift-card acceptance classes and two phonecard technologies stay attributes; the seven album lines moved to B1 (K-9); a document binder is not an album.
- **Where depth concentrates.** (1) Issuer designs of passports, identity cards and licences, where each issuing authority has its own (register); (2) photographic and recorded carriers, where archives and collectors split by format; (3) transit cards, where each system is a named form (and a C13 row), and phone cards, where technology is an attribute; (4) ephemera, which is open-ended (the Encyclopedia of Ephemera lists over 500 categories [S24]); (5) the maker and issuer rows for card, certificate and postcard printers and publishers (section 6).
- **Breadth of leaves.** Broad: F5.3e kept printed ephemera, F5.1a identity and travel (issuer designs), F5.2b stored value. Medium: F5.1c title and legal, F5.2a payment, F5.3a written, F5.3b photographic. Narrow: F5.1b civil status, F5.1d achievement, F5.1e badges, F5.2e key and network cards, F5.3c home recordings, F5.3d compiled records.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type: preference.** This study fetched no source that orders issued documents, cards, or personal records by quality. People do not buy a "better" passport or a "better" letter; cards come in issuer product tiers, but no fetched page ranks them, so nothing here supports a tier ladder. No `tier` sits on any kind (C4a), and no tier entry is recorded for a maker, line or named form; a tier scheme enters on one fetched source (C4e; a second source or region only adds weight). No price band (C4d) is recorded for F5: no dated, sourced list price was fetched.

Two things do behave like specifications, and neither is a tier. Each is an **attribute with its standard named** (C4b), never a level and never a ranking:
- **Archival conformity (pass or fail).** ISO 18902:2013 sets requirements for albums, storage and framing materials, and the Photographic Activity Test (ISO 18916) is the test that LOC, NARA and suppliers quote [S26][S35][S32]; Gaylord's product lines carry "buffered; metal edges; passed P.A.T." [S5][S6]. It is a yes or no attribute (`standard_met`, C17) on albums and on the storage products that K-9 sends to B1 when empty ("photo-safe, ISO 18902"); on a filled F5 photo album it is the same attribute.
- **Card and booklet format.** ISO/IEC 7810 ID-1, ID-2, ID-3, ID-000 [S27] and the ICAO TD1, TD2, TD3 [S28] are size classes. They are attributes of a card or booklet (C6).

Other attributes that are not tiers and not levels: `technology` of a card (magnetic stripe, chip, optical, contactless; under C10 technology defines a kind only if it changes the object's form and a fetched source ranks the types separately, and no fetched page does so for an F5 kind), `acceptance` of a stored-value card (closed-system, semi-closed [S18]), and album `page_mechanism` (slip-in, ring-binder, post-bound, stick-in, accordion, box), which K-9 makes an attribute because the empty album is B1 and the filled photo album one kind.

**Condition and grading.** Condition matters to collectors (scripophily value factors include condition, issued or unissued [S14]; phonecard collectors prefer mint unused cards [S15]; gift-card collectors want an intact PIN [S19]). Appendix A treats condition as a modifier and C16 forbids storing the condition, grade, balance, PIN, serial number or provenance of one particular object (BRIEF: individual items are out). So no condition value ("used", "unused", "mint") sits on a kind or named form. Collectors' grading vocabularies enter the shared grading-scale registry (CV-34) as schemes only (`scale_id`, name, owner, `scale_type` = condition grade, `steps`, `kinds_served`, `sources`), each on one fetched source, never as values; no scale is sourced for F5 yet †. An important caution from Appendix A: a used card or a written-on paper is not a worse version of the unused one; being written on is what makes a paper a record (step 19).

**Legal classes (C17).** Classes that a jurisdiction assigns (driving-licence categories, visa types, residence-permit types) go to the C17 jurisdiction ledger, never to a kind or leaf †. The ICAO passport type codes stay named forms (`maker_bound: no`) because the standard itself names them [S28]. Customs headings (HS 4906 to 4911 [S29][S30]) are evidence only.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended type: incidental**, with issuers held apart from makers.
- **Albums and storage.** Brands are examples: Hakuba, Chululu [S1]; Walther, Goldbuch, Henzo, Deknudt, ZEP [S2][S3]; Gaylord Archival, Pioneer [S5]; Archival Methods [S7]. Retailers expose brand as a facet, never as the first split.
- **Issued documents and cards.** The "maker" is the issuer: a state, a bank, a transit authority, a telecom, a retailer, a university. For collectors the issuer is the main specialism (a country, a telecom company, a given company's certificates) [S15][S13], but they treat it as a sort key, not as a kind.
- **Platform.** The maker does not set compatibility, except through standards. ISO 7810 and ICAO 9303 are open standards, not makers.

**Maker ledger (C1, shared across domain F).** Album makers are rows in the shared ledger, not a F5 ledger of their own: the C1 core columns (`maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, multi-valued `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`), with `maker_class` = house or manufacture. A maker-bound named form (Walther Fun, Goldbuch Florentine, Hakuba Pポケットアルバム CP) links to its maker row. `known_for_kinds` is derived from the named-form ledger, not typed. `scene_tags` stay empty and `price_band` is unused (section 5). Country, place and dates come only from fetched pages †. A maker's line that spans several kinds would be a C1 line row; none is attested for F5 (the album lines above sit inside one kind, so they are named forms). The F5 memo adds no extension column.

**Issuer table (shared with F3; proposed domain F addition).** Issuing authorities and companies (states, central banks, transit authorities, telecoms, retailers, universities) are rows in one issuer table (`issuer_id`, name, country, type, years, sources). They are not makers. A kind, an ICAO named form or a transit-system named form carries links to issuers (many to one kind), filled only where a source attests the issuer [S15][S18][S21]. The issuer link is a sort key and a generated view, never a level. This replaces the earlier `issuer` field on named forms. OWNER: approval of the issuer table (review question 2).

**Card systems (C13 compatibility ledger).** Suica, Octopus, EZ-Link, China T-Union, SUBE, Beep and the Washington Metro farecard [S18] are compatibility rows: `platform_id`, `name`, `owner` (the operator or standards body; not named by any fetched page yet †), `kinds_served` (transit farecard), `standard_ref` (only where a fetched page gives it †), `sources` [S18]. The named form under "transit farecard" points to the row (`maker_bound: yes`). Platforms are never levels. The rows are filled only where the owner wants collector depth.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommended: canonical form first for kinds; for named forms, maker-bound forms by maker, then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically (C7); chronology where the form is defined by an era of technology.** Reasons:
- Kinds inside a leaf have no spec ladder, so the most universal kind goes first (passport before residence permit; credit card before ATM card; letter before house plans), with other kinds following by frequency of ownership.
- Non-maker named forms that are technology steps (photographic formats from carte de visite onward; years † to be attested) follow the order in which they appeared, which is how both the collector and conservator literatures list them [S12][S35]. Transit card systems (maker-bound) go by operator, then start year †.
- Album lines have no canonical order: list alphabetically by maker within a mechanism (no year is attested).
- ICAO passport codes keep the standard's own table order [S28].

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A, function first.** F5 → 3 sub-families (issued documents; cards, passes and tokens; personal records and keepsake papers) → 15 leaves, each with one stated axis (section 3.4) → 52 kinds in the worked example and 80 to 120 at full coverage → named forms for ICAO passport codes, transit card systems, photographic formats and album lines (27 in the tree). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form).** The leaf level can be dropped by the owner if a three-level tree is preferred (C5b would allow it in F5.2 only); it carries the distinction between what a paper proves and what a card gives access to, so it is recommended.
