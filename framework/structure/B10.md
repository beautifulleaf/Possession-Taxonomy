## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B10 Animal Equipment.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 13): *made for keeping, feeding, housing, carrying, training or caring for animals, kept or wild: hutches, coops and kennels, tack, carriers, beds, feeders, nest boxes, aquariums, beekeeping equipment, pet toys, pet food and animal feed of every kind (including bird seed, hay and frozen feeder animals; live feeders are OUT), grooming and care equipment, veterinary medicines and treatments, animal identification microchips.* Category line: "housing, tack, carriers, feeders and feed, care, veterinary and toys for animals, kept or wild."

**Notes and precedence sentences that touch B10:**
- Step 13 note: riders' clothing and helmets are step 17; pet cremation urns and memorials are F4.
- Step 17 note: equestrian riding hats and sport protective guards go to step 26 (E2); the A1 category line also lists helmets (see section 10, conflicts).
- Step 1 (OUT) note: food made for animals, kept or wild, is step 13; a live animal, live bait, live feeder insects and eggs for hatching are OUT.
- Step 2 (H2) note: veterinary goods are step 13; vitamins and supplements for people are H2.
- Step 4 (G2) note: animal housing that is not a building a person can stand in (hutches, coops, kennels, stalls inside a building) is B10; stable blocks, barns and sheds are G2.
- Step 11 (G1): trailers and horseboxes, horse-drawn vehicles, prams and strollers are G1; the note says every boat, bicycle and so on is G1.
- Step 12 (C7) note: equipment for pets, horses, or a few backyard hens or hives is B10 (or C2); animal feed in any quantity is B10; commercial poultry, dairy and herd equipment is C7.
- Step 7 (F6) note: animal chews are B10 (a natural material sold for a use is filed by its use).
- S3: parts follow the whole (aquarium filters, hive frames, saddle girths are parts of B10 wholes and stay in B10); cleaning products are B6 and fuels C8.
- Appendix A: region, era, price and tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, market, installation, power source, occasion, motive, material, condition, edition are modifiers.

### 1.2 Enthusiast guides and communities

- Hives: the guide at Beekeeper's Realm names three main hive types (Langstroth, top-bar, Warre) and treats the rest as variations (long Langstroth, Layens, Flow, British National, Dadant, AZ); it says hives differ in direction of growth (vertical box on top, Warre box underneath, horizontal follower board) and in frames vs bars, and it tabulates frame and box sizes by standard [S8]. Note this is a function/mechanism division, and regional standards (National, Langstroth, Dadant, AZ, Zander) are compatibility systems.
- Aquarium: filters come in five common types (sump, canister, hang-on-back, internal, under-gravel) and "the right filter depends on tank size and how fast it runs", with planted and CO2 tanks favouring canisters [S16]. Wikipedia classifies aquaria by salinity (freshwater, marine) and lists components (filtration, lighting, air pump, heater or chiller) [S35].
- Vivarium: a retailer guide lists the kit by function (enclosure, substrate, heating, thermostat, UVB, daylight lighting, thermometer, hygrometer, hides, dishes, decor), enclosures by build (glass terrarium, wooden vivarium, PVC), heat sources (basking lamp, ceramic heat emitter, deep heat projector, heat mat) and substrates by species habitat (desert, tropical, snake) [S17].
- Wild-bird feeding: Audubon pairs five feeder designs (tube, hopper, suet, nyjer, ground) with seed types and the birds each attracts [S18]; the RSPB buying guide divides feeders into suet, suet pellet and peanut (steel mesh only), seed and nyjer, and says the first consideration is bird safety, then food and cleaning [S19].
- Saddles: a fitter's guide divides saddles by discipline (dressage, general purpose, jumping, endurance, western, treeless) [S26].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

eBay's antique horse tack listing is searched by tack Type (headstall, one-ear headstall, bosal, halter bridle, bosal hackamore), Brand (mostly unbranded; Showman, Circle Y, Weaver) and Material (rawhide, nylon); individual loose parts (conchos, harness buckles, brass shields) are sold as lots [S32]. eBay's general pet-supplies page returned an error and its sitemap returned no category list (see BLOCKED_HOSTS), so the marketplace's own pet tree is not documented here.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- AAFCO (US) tells owners to select food by species, then life stage and condition (gestation/lactation, growth, maintenance, all life stages) and separates complete foods from treats and supplements, which carry no nutritional adequacy statement [S9].
- WSAVA says to judge a brand by quality control and nutritional adequacy (AAFCO or FEDIAF profile), distinguishes complete diets from "intermittent or complementary" feeding, and notes that unregulated terms such as "premium" or "holistic" tell the buyer little [S10].
- Customs (HS 4201, shown on the EU tariff site): one heading covers "saddlery and harness for any animal" including traces, leads, knee pads, muzzles, saddle cloths, saddlebags and dog coats [S11]. Customs therefore groups animal tack by function across all animal kinds.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- Wikipedia "Horse tack" lists saddles, stirrups, bridles, halters, reins, bits and harnesses; saddles fall into two major categories, English and Western, by riding discipline, with racing, Australian, sidesaddle and endurance saddles not fitting neatly; bits fall into two broad categories, direct-pressure (snaffle) and leverage (curb), despite hundreds of mouthpieces [S25].
- Wikipedia "Beehive": traditional hives (mud, clay/tile, skeps, bee gums) vs modern hives, which are horizontal (long box, top-bar, Dartington long deep) or vertical [S34]. The Museum of English Rural Life treats the skep as one hive type among logs, boxes and frame hives [S33].
- Wikipedia "Aquarium": components and salinity classification as above [S35].

### 1.6 Review sites

Dog Food Advisor rates each dog food only against products of its own form (dry vs dry, wet vs wet, raw vs raw), and its "best of" lists run by form, life stage, grain-free, raw, fresh, freeze-dried, breed size and budget [S24].

### Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** every retailer tree fetched in six regions (US, UK, Austria/Germany, France, Japan, Australia) opens with the animal (Pets at Home, Petco, Pet Circle, Fressnapf, zooplus, Maxi Zoo, Peppy, Tractor Supply). Inside the animal, the second cut is function: food, housing/habitat, care, tack/wear, toys.
- **Split on the same objects:** (a) poultry and horses: Petco files them under Farm & Feed, Pet Circle under "Other pets", Tractor Supply as separate Horse, Livestock and Poultry departments [S21][S13][S30]; (b) ponds: Pets at Home puts pond food and equipment inside Fish [S2]; (c) wild birds: Pets at Home joins wild birds with wildlife [S3], RSPB sells feeders, food, nest boxes and baths as one wild-bird shop [S20]; (d) feed form vs life stage vs health need, which retailers treat sometimes as sub-categories (Pet Circle, Petco) and sometimes as filters (Chewy product fields, Fressnapf hubs); (e) customs and encyclopaedias group by function across animals [S11][S25], retailers by animal.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence for animal first.** All eight retailer families fetched (Pets at Home, Petco, Pet Circle, Fressnapf, zooplus, Maxi Zoo, Peppy, Tractor Supply) put the animal first [S1][S21][S13][S12][S23][S31][S14][S30]. AAFCO's first instruction to buyers is "select a food labeled for the pet's species" [S9]. Beekeeping, aquarium and vivarium catalogues are in effect animal-first, because the whole catalogue is for one animal [S27][S28][S2][S4].

**Why: most kinds are species-bound by design.** Saddles, bits and girths exist only for equines; filters, heaters and test kits only for aquaria; UVB lamps and thermostats for vivaria; supers and smokers for bees; tube and nyjer feeders for wild birds [S5][S16][S17][S27][S18]. My estimate is that about three-quarters of B10 kinds are bound to one animal group by their design, and a quarter are cross-species (bowls, beds, carriers, brushes, flea treatments, microchips).

**Second choice: function or need.** Inside an animal the retailer trees split by what the object does (food, housing, tack, care, toys) [S1][S21][S12][S14]. Where the object is bought for a fit, the third choice is a size or compatibility spec: horse size scale pony/cob/full [S6], dog breed size [S21], hive system and box depth [S27][S29][S7], tank volume [S16].

**The questions buyers ask** in the guides: which saddle for which discipline [S26]; which filter for my tank size and plants [S16]; which feeder for which birds and which seed [S18][S19]; which hive type and frame standard [S8]; whether a food is "complete" for the animal's life stage [S9][S10].

**Where function is first.** Customs and encyclopaedias put the function first and the animal second (HS 4201 "for any animal"; Wikipedia "tack") [S11][S25]. That view is real but it serves a cross-animal reader, not an owner. It can be had as a generated view (all beds, all feeders).

**Is animal kind a modifier?** Appendix A lists "user (baby, child, adult, gender, size, ability: adapted forms)" as a modifier. The user there is the person. The framework already uses a kind-of-activity as the first axis in E2 ("by sport or activity first, then equipment type"). Whether "animal kept" is a permitted first axis is an owner decision (section 10, question 1).

## 3. Candidate structures

Common naming for the levels below B10: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels, the framework maximum).

### Candidate A — animal group first, then function (recommended)
- **Sub-family axis:** the animal group kept (husbandry group): dogs; cats; small mammals; horses and other equines; fish and aquaria; reptiles and amphibians (terrarium); cage and aviary birds; wild birds and wildlife; bees; poultry and other livestock (backyard); and a small "several animals" group.
- **Leaf axis:** function for that animal: feed and treats, housing and habitat, habitat life-support (aquarium and vivarium technology), tack and control, worn by the animal (rugs, coats, boots), feeding equipment, beds and furniture, carrying and travel, grooming and hygiene, health and veterinary, training and toys, identification.
- **Kind axis:** generic type of object (see worked tree below). **Named-form axis:** model, design or product line.
- **Example kinds (15):** dry dog food, wet dog food, dog bed, dog harness, English saddle, bridle, snaffle bit, turnout rug, aquarium (tank), canister filter, aquarium heater, vivarium, basking lamp, tube feeder (wild bird), top-bar hive.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) a food bowl, bed or brush for dog and cat has no single animal home; (2) a pet carrier for cats and small dogs; (3) a microchip or a flea treatment sold for dog and cat; (4) honey extractor: bee equipment or food processing. Mitigation: kinds made for three or more groups sit in the "several animals" group; every kind carries an "applies to" tag so a generated view per animal ("everything for a dog") exists.

### Candidate B — function first, animal as an attribute
- **Level 1 axis:** function (feed, housing, habitat technology, tack and control, wear, carrying, grooming, health, toys, identification). **Level 2:** kind of object. **Animal:** "made for" tag.
- **Example kinds (12):** pet food (dry), treat, hutch/cage/coop, aquarium, vivarium, hive, saddle, bridle, bed, collar, brush, toy.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) "tack" and "habitat technology" are heterogeneous parents (a saddle girth and a fish-tank filter share no buyer or maker); (2) a hive is housing for bees but its parts (frames, supers, smokers) form a sub-system that must stay with the hive system [S27][S7]; (3) the owner-facing retailers all open with the animal, so every generated page would reinvent the animal split; (4) aquarium filter beside rabbit hutch under "housing and habitat" tells no one anything.

### Candidate C — hybrid: function first for cross-animal consumables and health, animal first for species-bound equipment
- **Level 1 axis:** what the object is made for, either "a species' equipment system" (horse, aquarium, vivarium, bees, wild birds, poultry) or "a cross-species function" (companion-animal feed, companion-animal care and health, identification).
- **Example kinds (12):** saddle, bit, girth, hive body, frame, smoker, canister filter, heater, dry pet food, flea treatment, microchip, carrier.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) the level-1 list mixes two kinds of parent, which breaks the one-stated-axis rule; (2) dog and cat then lack a home of their own, though Peppy and Pet Circle give each its own tree [S14][S13]; (3) pet food: companion-animal feed holds dog and cat, but AAFCO and Dog Food Advisor treat dog and cat feed separately [S9][S24]; (4) edge: hay is bought for horses, rabbits and guinea pigs alike [S21].

## 4. Depth and size

- **Rough count of generic kinds:** 700 to 1,000 (estimate). By group: horses 150 to 200, dogs 100 to 130, aquaria 80 to 100, bees 80 to 100, cats 60 to 80, small mammals 50 to 70, reptiles 40 to 60, poultry and livestock 40 to 60, wild birds 30 to 40, cage birds 30 to 40, several-animals 20 to 30. The anchors are the product-type lists of Shires (about 50 types) [S6], SmartPak [S5], Thorne's roughly 40 catalogue sections [S29], and Maxi Zoo's roughly 30 aquarium categories [S31].
- **Rough count of named forms:** 1,500 to 2,500 (estimate), if only attested designs, standards and types are counted: bit mouthpieces and bridle styles [S5][S6], saddle disciplines [S26], hive systems and sizes [S8][S7][S27], filter types [S16], feeder designs [S18]. Product lines of feed (a brand's recipe line) would multiply this by ten or more; these belong in a maker ledger, not in the tree (section 6).
- **Where depth concentrates:** horse tack and wear (many parts, many styles per part), beekeeping parts (system × depth), aquarium technology (type × water type), and feed (form × life stage × diet, by animal).
- **Broad leaves** (many kinds, many makers): dog and cat food and treats; horse headgear; hive parts. **Medium:** aquarium technology; vivarium life support; dog and cat accessories. **Narrow:** wild-bird feeders; observation or top-bar hives; poultry housing.
- **Depth:** four levels below the category are enough: animal group, function, kind, named form. No fifth level is needed.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended tier type: preference for equipment, with two exceptions.** No source fetched sets a tier ordering for saddles, filters, hives, beds or toys; the sources show brand and price facets only [S5][S6][S13]. The exceptions:

- **Feed: consensus-ranked, with a spec gate.** The gate is the nutritional adequacy statement: "complete" vs complementary (AAFCO life stages and intended use; AAFCO or FEDIAF nutrient profiles per WSAVA) [S9][S10]. Above the gate, ordering is by rating sites: Dog Food Advisor's star ratings, applied within form only [S24]. WSAVA warns that "premium" and "holistic" are unregulated [S10], while Petco has a "Premium Food" list for cats [S21], so retailer words are not a tier.
- **Fit specs (not tiers):** filter turnover of 6 to 10 times per hour against bio-load [S16], hive frame depth [S27], saddle fit (seat size, tree width; SmartPak's saddle page mentions seat sizes and gullet widths in a search excerpt only) [S26], rug and tack size (pony, cob, full) [S6]. These set compatibility, not quality, and should be attributes.

**How tier appears as a modifier, never a level:** a `tier` field on the named form or product line (for feed: adequacy statement type, life-stage claim, third-party rating with source and date); a price band; a fit-spec attribute set per leaf. A "complete vs complementary" flag should be a required attribute on every feed kind.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended: identity, with a platform pocket.**

- **Identity:** saddlery (SmartPak's brand facet lists many third-party makers beside the retailer's own brands SmartPak, Harwich, Plymouth and Nantucket [S5]; specialist saddle houses such as Stubben, Passier, Wintec and Veredus appear in a search excerpt of the same listing but were not on the fetched extract); feed (Royal Canin, Hill's, Purina and so on, with Rakuten organising foods by manufacturer [S15] and Dog Food Advisor reviewing by brand [S24]); aquarium technology (Fluval, Tetra, Hikari, Seachem are the brands Pet Circle highlights [S13]); beekeeping suppliers that are also makers (Dadant, Mann Lake, Thorne) [S27][S28][S29]; collectors search antique tack by brand and by maker marks [S32].
- **Platform:** hive systems (Langstroth, National, Dadant, Zander, AZ, Warre) set which frames and boxes fit [S8][S7][S27][S29]; the dimensions are a standard, not a single maker, so the system is a compatibility tag. Aquarium cabinets and filter media have weaker platform links.
- **Incidental:** dog and cat accessories, wild-bird feeders (the RSPB sells its own range but buyers choose by design) [S20].

**Maker ledger: yes, light.** Fields: maker, known-for kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country, era, attestation. Priority makers: feed brands (dry/wet/raw/vet diets), saddle houses, aquarium and vivarium technology brands, beekeeping suppliers. Feed product lines are named forms linked to a ledger row; lines are not tree levels.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommended: canonical form first, then ascending specialisation.** Within a leaf, the most generic kind comes first (for example, dry dog food before wet before fresh before freeze-dried before veterinary diet; snaffle bit before curb before bitless), then by retailer prominence. Named forms follow chronology or standard size order where a standard exists (hive depth: deep, medium, shallow [S27]; sizes pony, cob, full [S6]). Reason: retailers present forms in this order [S21][S13] and AAFCO separates complete foods first from treats and supplements [S9].

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A. B10 → sub-family = animal group kept (about 11) → leaf = function for that animal (about 8 to 12 per group, drawn from a shared function vocabulary so cross-animal views can be generated) → kind = generic type of object (feed classes by form; tack and technology by type) → named form = model, design, standard or product line (with a maker ledger). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category.** The animal-group level should be stated as "the kind of animal the object is made for"; objects made for three or more groups go to "several animals".

**Choice level:** deepest for feed (class and form per animal), saddlery and bits (kind or discipline, see question 5), and aquarium and vivarium technology (type); shallowest for dog and cat accessories. **Tier type:** preference, with consensus-ranked rating plus a spec gate for feed only. **Maker centrality:** identity (feed, saddlery, aquarium technology, beekeeping suppliers) with a platform pocket in hive systems; light maker ledger.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. May "animal group" be the first axis below B10, given Appendix A lists "user" as a modifier and E2 already puts activity first? If not, Candidate B or C is required.
2. Where do objects made for several animals live: the "several animals" group (recommended) or duplicates under each animal?
3. Cat litter and animal-waste products: B10 or B6?
4. Fish ponds and their pumps and liners, GPS pet trackers, pet cameras: B10, C2, D2 or B8?
5. Saddles: kinds at English/Western level with discipline as named form (recommended), or discipline as kind?
6. Feed: do life stage and purpose stay attributes (recommended for dogs and cats) or do poultry and livestock feeds split into starter, grower and layer kinds?
7. Honey extraction, bottling and wax processing gear: B10 or elsewhere?
8. Pet strollers and dog bike trailers: G1 (key order) or B10?
9. Should the same product line be recorded in a maker ledger only (recommended), or as named forms in the tree?

**Gaps in this research:** no forum thread or community wiki was fetched (guides stand in for community voice); no Chinese, Korean or Spanish-language market was documented (Taobao-type hosts were blocked in earlier batches); the Chewy home page and eBay pet tree could not be fetched; bird cage, small-animal housing, dog training and veterinary-medicine kinds are documented only by retailer tree lines, not by buying guides.
