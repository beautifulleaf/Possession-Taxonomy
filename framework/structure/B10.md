## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B10 Animal Equipment.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 13): *made for keeping, feeding, housing, carrying, training or caring for animals, kept or wild: hutches, coops and kennels, tack, carriers, beds, feeders, nest boxes, aquariums, beekeeping equipment, pet toys, pet food and animal feed of every kind (including bird seed, hay and frozen feeder animals; live feeders are OUT), grooming and care equipment, veterinary medicines and treatments, animal identification microchips.* Category line: "housing, tack, carriers, feeders and feed, care, veterinary and toys for animals, kept or wild."

**Notes and precedence sentences that touch B10** (CATEGORIES_v0_7):
- Step 13 notes: riders' clothing and helmets are step 17; pet cremation urns and memorials are F4. The kind of animal served is this category's first axis (K-102; a written exception to Appendix A), with "several animals" for objects made for two or more groups. A gate sold for stairs and doorways for children, or children and pets, is B8; a gate, pen or camera sold only for pets is B10; a GPS pet tracker is B10 (K-103). Garden fish ponds, pond liners and pond pumps are C2 (K-104). Bee suits, veils and gloves are A1 personal protective equipment (K-105). Honey extraction and bottling gear is B10 with beekeeping equipment (K-106).
- Step 17 note (K-125): an equestrian riding hat is E2; riding boots are A2; weapon-threat helmets are E4; other hazard helmets are A1.
- Step 1 (OUT) note: food made for animals, kept or wild, is step 13; a live animal, live bait, live feeder insects and eggs for hatching are OUT.
- Step 2 (H2) note: veterinary goods are step 13; vitamins and supplements for people are H2.
- Step 4 (G2) note: animal housing that is not a building a person can stand in (hutches, coops, kennels, stalls inside a building) is B10; stable blocks, barns and sheds are G2.
- Step 11 (G1): trailers and horseboxes, horse-drawn vehicles, prams and strollers are G1; pet strollers and dog bike trailers are G1, in a hand-pushed and towed carriers leaf (K-95).
- Step 12 (C7) note: equipment for pets, horses, or a few backyard hens or hives is B10 (or C2); animal feed in any quantity is B10; commercial poultry, dairy and herd equipment is C7. Equipment made for herds or flocks (handling systems, milking, feed mixers, commercial hatchers, livestock EID stick readers) is C7; pet microchip scanners and small incubators are B10 (K-101).
- Step 7 (F6) note: animal chews are B10 (a natural material sold for a use is filed by its use); a mount of one animal prepared as in life is F6 (K-76).
- S3: parts follow the whole (hive bodies and frames, saddle girths and aquarium filter media are made for one B10 object and stay in B10, in the closing parts sub-family B10.12); cleaning products are B6 and fuels C8. S3a: a set of one kind is that kind; a mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed; a kit is a kind only when sold, standardised and compared as one object.
- Appendix A: region, era, price and tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, market, installation, power source, occasion, motive, material, condition, edition are modifiers. Written exception: the kind of animal served is the first axis of B10.

### 1.2 Enthusiast guides and communities

- Hives: the guide at Beekeeper's Realm names three main hive types (Langstroth, top-bar, Warre) and treats the rest as variations (long Langstroth, Layens, Flow, British National, Dadant, AZ); it says hives differ in direction of growth (vertical box on top, Warre box underneath, horizontal follower board) and in frames vs bars, and it tabulates frame and box sizes by standard [S8]. Note this is a function/mechanism division, and regional standards (National, Langstroth, Dadant, AZ, Zander) are compatibility systems.
- Aquarium: filters come in five common types (sump, canister, hang-on-back, internal, under-gravel) and "the right filter depends on tank size and how fast it runs", with planted and CO2 tanks favouring canisters [S16]. Wikipedia classifies aquaria by salinity (freshwater, marine) and lists components (filtration, lighting, air pump, heater or chiller) [S35].
- Vivarium: a retailer guide lists the kit by function (enclosure, substrate, heating, thermostat, UVB, daylight lighting, thermometer, hygrometer, hides, dishes, decor), enclosures by build (glass terrarium, wooden vivarium, PVC), heat sources (basking lamp, ceramic heat emitter, deep heat projector, heat mat) and substrates by species habitat (desert, tropical, snake) [S17].
- Wild-bird feeding: Audubon pairs five feeder designs (tube, hopper, suet, nyjer, ground) with seed types and the birds each attracts [S18]; the RSPB buying guide divides feeders into suet, suet pellet and peanut (steel mesh only), seed and nyjer, and says the first consideration is bird safety, then food and cleaning [S19].
- Saddles: a fitter's guide divides saddles by discipline (dressage, general purpose, jumping, endurance, western, treeless) [S26].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

eBay's antique horse tack listing is searched by tack Type (headstall, one-ear headstall, bosal, halter bridle, bosal hackamore), Brand (mostly unbranded; Showman, Circle Y, Weaver) and Material (rawhide, nylon); individual loose parts (conchos, harness buckles, brass shields) are sold as lots [S32]. eBay's general pet-supplies page returned an error and its sitemap returned no category list (see BLOCKED_HOSTS), so the marketplace's own pet tree is not documented here.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- AAFCO (US) tells owners to select food by species, then life stage and condition (gestation/lactation, growth, maintenance, all life stages) and separates complete foods from treats and supplements, which carry no nutritional adequacy statement [S9].
- WSAVA says to judge a brand by quality control and nutritional adequacy (AAFCO or FEDIAF profile), distinguishes complete diets from "intermittent or complementary" feeding, and notes that unregulated terms such as "premium" or "holistic" tell the buyer little [S10].
- Customs (HS 4201, shown on the EU tariff site): one heading covers "saddlery and harness for any animal" including traces, leads, knee pads, muzzles, saddle cloths, saddlebags and dog coats [S11]. Customs therefore groups animal tack by function across all animal kinds.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- Wikipedia "Horse tack" lists saddles, stirrups, bridles, halters, reins, bits and harnesses; saddles fall into two major categories, English and Western, by riding discipline, with racing, Australian, sidesaddle and endurance saddles not fitting neatly; bits fall into two broad categories, direct-pressure (snaffle) and leverage (curb), despite hundreds of mouthpieces [S25].
- Wikipedia "Beehive": traditional hives (mud, clay/tile, skeps, bee gums) vs modern hives, which are horizontal (long box, top-bar, Dartington long deep) or vertical [S34]. The Museum of English Rural Life treats the skep as one hive type among logs, boxes and frame hives [S33].
- Wikipedia "Aquarium": components and salinity classification as above [S35].

### 1.6 Review sites

Dog Food Advisor rates each dog food only against products of its own form (dry vs dry, wet vs wet, raw vs raw), and its "best of" lists run by form, life stage, grain-free, raw, fresh, freeze-dried, breed size and budget [S24].

### Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** every retailer tree fetched in six regions (US, UK, Austria/Germany, France, Japan, Australia) opens with the animal (Pets at Home, Petco, Pet Circle, Fressnapf, zooplus, Maxi Zoo, Peppy, Tractor Supply). Inside the animal, the second cut is function: food, housing/habitat, care, tack/wear, toys.
- **Split on the same objects:** (a) poultry and horses: Petco files them under Farm & Feed, Pet Circle under "Other pets", Tractor Supply as separate Horse, Livestock and Poultry departments [S21][S13][S30]; (b) ponds: Pets at Home puts pond food and equipment inside Fish [S2]; (c) wild birds: Pets at Home joins wild birds with wildlife [S3], RSPB sells feeders, food, nest boxes and baths as one wild-bird shop [S20]; (d) feed form vs life stage vs health need, which retailers treat sometimes as sub-categories (Pet Circle, Petco) and sometimes as filters (Chewy product fields, Fressnapf hubs); (e) customs and encyclopaedias group by function across animals [S11][S25], retailers by animal.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence for animal first.** All eight retailer families fetched (Pets at Home, Petco, Pet Circle, Fressnapf, zooplus, Maxi Zoo, Peppy, Tractor Supply) put the animal first [S1][S21][S13][S12][S23][S31][S14][S30]. AAFCO's first instruction to buyers is "select a food labeled for the pet's species" [S9]. Beekeeping, aquarium and vivarium catalogues are in effect animal-first, because the whole catalogue is for one animal [S27][S28][S2][S4].

**Why: most kinds are species-bound by design.** Saddles, bits and girths exist only for equines; filters, heaters and test kits only for aquaria; UVB lamps and thermostats for vivaria; supers and smokers for bees; tube and nyjer feeders for wild birds [S5][S16][S17][S27][S18]. My estimate is that about three-quarters of B10 kinds are bound to one animal group by their design, and a quarter are cross-species (bowls, beds, carriers, brushes, flea treatments, microchips).

**Second choice: function or need.** Inside an animal the retailer trees split by what the object does (food, housing, tack, care, toys) [S1][S21][S12][S14]. Where the object is bought for a fit, the third choice is a size or compatibility spec: horse size scale pony/cob/full [S6], dog breed size [S21], hive system and box depth [S27][S29][S7], tank volume [S16].

**The questions buyers ask** in the guides: which saddle for which discipline [S26]; which filter for my tank size and plants [S16]; which feeder for which birds and which seed [S18][S19]; which hive type and frame standard [S8]; whether a food is "complete" for the animal's life stage [S9][S10].

**Where function is first.** Customs and encyclopaedias put the function first and the animal second (HS 4201 "for any animal"; Wikipedia "tack") [S11][S25]. That view is real but it serves a cross-animal reader, not an owner. It can be had as a generated view (all beds, all feeders).

**Is animal kind a modifier?** Decided (K-102): Appendix A lists "user" as a modifier, but the key now carries a written exception (step 13 note and Appendix A of CATEGORIES_v0_7): the kind of animal served is B10's first axis. The framework already uses a kind-of-activity as the first axis in E2 ("by sport or activity first, then equipment type"). Candidates B and C below were not chosen.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common naming for the levels below B10 (C5(a)): sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels, the framework maximum; the named form counts as one).

### Candidate A — animal group first, then function (decided, K-102)
- **Sub-family axis (level 1):** the animal group the object is made for (husbandry group): dogs; cats; small mammals; horses and other equines; fish and aquaria; reptiles and amphibians (terrarium); cage and aviary birds; wild birds and wildlife; bees; poultry and other livestock (backyard); and **several animals** (the multi-use sub-family of C14(c); rule in section 3.1). The tree closes with the parts sub-family **B10.12 Parts and accessories made for one B10 object** (CV-17, C5(d)), whose leaf axis is the object served; it is the only sub-family exempt from the level-1 axis.
- **Leaf axis (level 2), stated for every animal sub-family:** the function the object serves for that animal, drawn from one controlled vocabulary so that cross-animal views can be generated: (1) feed and treats; (2) feeding equipment; (3) housing and containment; (4) habitat life-support (heat, light, filtration, incubation); (5) habitat furnishing (substrate, hides, decoration); (6) beds and furniture; (7) carrying a rider or load (saddlery); (8) steering and restraint (headgear, collars, leads, harnesses); (9) worn by the animal (rugs, coats, boots, masks); (10) keeper's tools; (11) produce handling; (12) carrying and travel; (13) care and hygiene (grooming, litter, water testing and cleaning); (14) health and veterinary; (15) toys and training; (16) identification and tracking. A sub-family uses only the leaves it has kinds for, in this order. A leaf name never joins two functions. (The earlier function "keeper's protection" is gone: bee suits, veils and gloves are A1, K-105.)
- **Kind axis (level 3):** generic type of object (worked tree below). **Named-form axis (level 4):** a design, model or product line below the kind (C2).
- **Example kinds (15):** dry dog food, wet dog food, dog bed, dog harness, English saddle, bridle, snaffle bit, turnout rug, aquarium (tank), canister filter, aquarium heater, vivarium, basking lamp, tube feeder (wild bird), top-bar hive.
- **Where it breaks, and the fix:** (1) a food bowl, bed or brush made for dog and cat has no single animal home: rule 3.1 sends it to "several animals"; (2) a pet carrier for cats and small dogs: same rule; (3) a microchip or a flea treatment sold for dog and cat: same rule; (4) honey extractor: bee equipment, decided K-106 (B10.9.4). Every kind carries an `applies_to` tag so that a generated view per animal ("everything for a dog") exists.

### 3.1 Rule for objects made for more than one animal group (applies C14)
A kind is filed once, by C14 in order: (a) the leaf whose rule or standard defines it (none is attested in this batch); (b) the one animal group that holds all its uses (a dog collar sold for puppies and adult dogs is Dogs); (c) otherwise "Several animals" (B10.11), whose leaf axis is the same function vocabulary. Decided (B-11, K-102): the threshold is two groups; an object made for two or more animal groups goes to B10.11, and the number of groups is not a test beyond "more than one". The `applies_to` tag lists the groups and generates the per-animal views. Hay, sold for horses and for small mammals alike [S21], is filed in B10.11.

### Candidate B — function first, animal as an attribute
- **Level 1 axis:** function (feed, housing, habitat technology, tack and control, wear, carrying, grooming, health, toys, identification). **Level 2:** kind of object. **Animal:** "made for" tag.
- **Example kinds (12):** pet food (dry), treat, hutch/cage/coop, aquarium, vivarium, hive, saddle, bridle, bed, collar, brush, toy.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) "tack" and "habitat technology" are heterogeneous parents (a saddle girth and a fish-tank filter share no buyer or maker); (2) a hive is housing for bees but its parts (frames, supers, smokers) form a sub-system that must stay with the hive system [S27][S7]; (3) the owner-facing retailers all open with the animal, so every generated page would reinvent the animal split; (4) aquarium filter beside rabbit hutch under "housing and habitat" tells no one anything.

### Candidate C — hybrid: function first for cross-animal consumables and health, animal first for species-bound equipment
- **Level 1 axis:** what the object is made for, either "a species' equipment system" (horse, aquarium, vivarium, bees, wild birds, poultry) or "a cross-species function" (companion-animal feed, companion-animal care and health, identification).
- **Example kinds (12):** saddle, bit, girth, hive body, frame, smoker, canister filter, heater, dry pet food, flea treatment, microchip, carrier.
- **Where it breaks:** (1) the level-1 list mixes two kinds of parent, which breaks the one-stated-axis rule; (2) dog and cat then lack a home of their own, though Peppy and Pet Circle give each its own tree [S14][S13]; (3) pet food: companion-animal feed holds dog and cat, but AAFCO and Dog Food Advisor treat dog and cat feed separately [S9][S24]; (4) edge: hay is bought for horses, rabbits and guinea pigs alike [S21].

## 4. Depth and size

- **Rough count of generic kinds:** 700 to 1,000 (estimate). By group: horses 150 to 200, dogs 100 to 130, aquaria 80 to 100, bees 80 to 100, cats 60 to 80, small mammals 50 to 70, reptiles 40 to 60, poultry and livestock 40 to 60, wild birds 30 to 40, cage birds 30 to 40, several-animals 20 to 30. The anchors are the product-type lists of Shires (about 50 types) [S6], SmartPak [S5], Thorne's roughly 40 catalogue sections [S29], and Maxi Zoo's roughly 30 aquarium categories [S31]. These are planning estimates, not caps: kinds, named forms, lines and makers grow as far as the attested evidence goes (depth and breadth rule).
- **Rough count of named forms:** 1,500 to 2,500 (estimate), if only attested designs and types are counted: bit mouthpieces and bridle styles [S5][S6], saddle disciplines [S26], hive designs [S8][S34], feeder designs [S18]. Hive systems and box sizes are platform values and size attributes, not named forms (C13, C2(b)), and are not in this count. Product lines of feed (a brand's recipe line) would multiply the count by ten or more; they are lines rows of the maker ledger (CV-46, C2(e)), uncapped, not named forms and not tree rows (section 6). Large named-form sets, such as the hundreds of bit mouthpieces [S25], live in registers linked to their kind (C2(g), CV-45), uncapped.
- **Where depth concentrates:** horse tack and wear (many parts, many styles per part), beekeeping parts (system × depth, held as platform and size), aquarium technology (type × water type), and feed (form × life stage × diet, by animal).
- **Broad leaves** (many kinds, many makers): dog and cat food and treats; horse headgear; hive parts. **Medium:** aquarium technology; vivarium life support; dog and cat accessories. **Narrow:** wild-bird feeders; observation or top-bar hives; poultry housing.
- **Depth:** four levels below the category are enough: animal group, function, kind, named form (in the parts sub-family: object served, kind, named form). No fifth level is needed (CV-16, C5).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Tier type (decided): preference for equipment, with one exception for feed.** No source fetched sets a tier ordering for saddles, filters, hives, beds or toys; the sources show brand and price facets only [S5][S6][S13]. A tier never sits on a kind (C4(a)).

- **Feed: a spec gate (an attribute), then tier entries on named forms and lines only.**
  - *The gate* is the nutritional adequacy statement: "complete" vs complementary (AAFCO life stages and intended use; AAFCO or FEDIAF nutrient profiles per WSAVA) [S9][S10]. It is a spec on the label with its standard named, so it is a required **attribute** of every feed kind (scale class (i), C4(c)), with the profile met stored as `standard_met` (CV-36); it is not a tier. The life-stage claim is an attribute too (B-32 for dogs and cats).
  - *Above the gate*, a rating is stored as a tier entry (C4(a): `subject_id` a named form or lines row, `scheme` = Dog Food Advisor 1 to 5 stars, an editorial class (iii) scheme, `value`, `direction` higher is better, `test_version`, `stale_after`, `source_url`, `date`) and compared only within the same feed form [S24]. There is no category-wide ladder. The scheme enters on this one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10, CV-12); a second source or region would only add weight and feed `disputed`.
  - Retailer words are not tiers. WSAVA warns that "premium" and "holistic" are unregulated [S10]; Petco's "Premium Food" list for cats [S21] is a dated `platform_tag` (class (vi), C4(c)).
- **Fit specs (attributes with the standard named, C4(c) class (i)):** filter turnover of 6 to 10 times per hour against bio-load [S16], hive frame depth and box width [S27], saddle fit (seat size, tree width; SmartPak's saddle page mentions seat sizes and gullet widths in a search excerpt only †) [S26], rug and tack size (pony, cob, full) [S6]. These set compatibility, not quality.
- **Legal classes** (veterinary medicines' sale classes such as prescription-only and over-the-counter, and any rule on microchips) are rows of the C17 jurisdiction ledger, never a level or a tag on the tree (CV-35). A design or voluntary standard an object meets is `standard_met`.

**Price band:** the domain-wide USD bands of C4(d), derived from a dated, sourced list price (CV-13, CV-14); none is stored in this memo. **How tier appears, never as a level:** tier entries on named forms, product lines or makers; fit specs as attributes; a required "complete or complementary" flag on every feed kind.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Decided: identity, with a platform pocket.**

- **Identity:** saddlery (SmartPak's brand facet lists many third-party makers beside the retailer's own brands SmartPak, Harwich, Plymouth and Nantucket, which are `retail own-label` [S5]; specialist saddle houses such as Stubben, Passier, Wintec and Veredus appear in a search excerpt of the same listing but not on the fetched extract †); feed (Royal Canin, Hill's, Purina and so on are named from general knowledge and need a fetched attestation †; Rakuten organises foods by manufacturer [S15] and Dog Food Advisor reviews by brand [S24]); aquarium technology (Fluval, Tetra, Hikari, Seachem are the brands Pet Circle highlights [S13]); beekeeping suppliers that are also makers (Dadant, Mann Lake, Thorne, class `house or manufacture`) [S27][S28][S29]; collectors search antique tack by brand and by maker marks (Showman, Circle Y, Weaver) [S32].
- **Platform:** hive systems (Langstroth, National, Dadant, Zander, AZ) set which frames and boxes fit [S8][S7][S27][S29]; the dimensions are a standard, not a single maker. They are rows of the C13 compatibility ledger: `platform_id`, `name`, `owner` (the standards body or maker; not stated by any fetched source †), `platform_type` = hive system, `kinds_served` (framed vertical hive, long hive, hive body or super, frame, bee feeder), `standard_ref` (the frame and box size tables in [S8]), `region`, `introduced`, `status`, `sources`. - Harvest order (an ordering rule, never a limit): feed brands (dry/wet/raw/vet diets), saddle houses, aquarium and vivarium technology brands, beekeeping suppliers.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Applies C7 (CV-20, CV-21).** Kinds go canonical form first, then the secondary key that the sub-family states: **ascending specialisation** (the most generic form first). Examples: dry dog food before wet before fresh or frozen before freeze-dried or air-dried before veterinary diet before treat; snaffle bit before curb bit before bitless bridle. Named forms: non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms, by maker (in ledger `name` order), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically. Variants go by `variant_code` or year. Size order (pony, cob, full; deep, medium, shallow) orders attribute values, not kinds or named forms. Maker ladders, benchmark and rating orders are generated views, not stored order. Reason: retailers present forms in the generic-first order [S21][S13] and AAFCO separates complete foods first from treats and supplements [S9].
