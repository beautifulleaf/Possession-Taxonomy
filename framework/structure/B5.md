## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B5 Kitchen & Dining.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 30, first branch; branch note: every plate and tray made to hold food is B5, whatever it is kept for, and display is a tag, S5): *made to prepare, cook, cool, store or serve food and drink: cookware, kitchen tools and machines, kitchen scales and thermometers, cooking appliances, grills, refrigerators and wine fridges, dishwashers, vending machines for food and drink, tableware, glassware, barware, decanters, tea and coffee equipment, food storage and lunch bags, oven gloves and aprons, kitchen timers.* Category list line: "everything made to prepare, cook, cool, store or serve food and drink, powered or not, domestic or commercial."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name B5:**
- Step 10 note (E4): kitchen and utility knives are B5 and C1 (hunting and fighting knives are E4; pocket knives and multi-tools are C1).
- Step 16 note (A5): kitchen timers are B5.
- Step 20 note (F2): vases, bowls and other vessels are B4 or B5, however artistic.
- Step 23 note (C6): kitchen scales and thermometers are B5.
- Step 26 note (E2): portable furniture, stoves, cookware, cool boxes and lanterns are filed by what they do (cookware is B5) wherever they are used; "camping" is a setting.
- Step 30 branch order: B5 is the first branch tried, so a kitchen object that could also be cleaning (B6), climate or water (B7) or furniture (B1) is B5 if it prepares, cooks, cools, stores or serves food and drink.
- S3: parts follow the whole; cases and covers are parts only for one object at a time; furniture, racks and storage that hold several objects are never parts.
- S5: a collected teapot is a teapot; a commemorative plate is a plate.
- Appendix A: power source and technology, connectivity, market (domestic, commercial, professional), installation, material, brand and maker, price band and quality tier, region, era, setting, user and scene are modifiers.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object is filed by its task (so a smart oven or app-linked scale stays B5).
- Step 17 A4 note (K-131): insulated lunch bags and cool bags are B5.
- Step 26 note (K-161): drink bottles and bladders, for sport or not, are B5; a hydration pack sold with its bag is A4.
- Step 3 note (K-58): home-brewing and winemaking ingredients are J5; their equipment is B5.
- Step 23 and 27 notes (K-167): a scale goes with its task (B5, C5, C6); a pocket scale is B5.
- Step 28 note (K-171): a cutting tool is filed by the material or work it is made for; food is B5.
- Step 30 B5 and B1 branch notes (K-182, K-191): every plate and tray made to hold food is B5; a tray made to serve is B5, one made to hold small items on a surface is B1.
- S3a (K-1): a set of objects of one kind is that kind; a mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed; a kit sold, standardised and compared as one object is a kind.
- S3 (K-11): dishwasher salt, descalers and cleaning tablets are B6; water-softener salt and filter cartridges are B7.
- Step 13 note (K-106): honey extraction and bottling gear is B10 with beekeeping equipment.

Where the retailers agree: the first split is by **product family**, not by room or by food. Every tree separates (a) vessels for heat (cookware, bakeware), (b) knives, (c) hand tools, (d) powered machines, (e) table service (tableware), (f) drinking vessels, (g) coffee and tea. Where they split the same objects differently:

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Knives.** Japanese-knife specialists group types by job: versatile (gyuto, santoku, bunka), vegetable and utility (nakiri, petty, Chinese cleaver, kiridashi), slicing, butchery and traditional (sujihiki, kiritsuke, honesuki, deba, yanagiba, usuba) [S17]. A second guide splits first by **bevel** (ryoba double, kataba single) and then lists type, length and purpose [S18]. Wikipedia lists Western types (paring, utility, boning, cook's, bread, carving, cleaver) and then Japanese and Chinese families [S19][S20]. The same shop navigates by knife type, then by handle (Japanese wa or western) and stainless steel [S17].
- **Cookware.** Buying guides organise by **material**: carbon steel, stainless steel, cast iron (and enamelled cast iron), with strengths and drawbacks [S29].
- **Espresso.** Enthusiast and specialist-retailer guides split machines by operating mode (semi-automatic, super-automatic, manual lever) [S23][S24], then by boiler layout (single, heat exchanger, dual) [S23][S24], and describe an **upgrade ladder** (entry all-in-one, grinder, scale, prosumer dual boiler, commercial-grade home) [S25]. Grinders are described by burr size tier and flat or conical geometry [S26].
- **Tea.** One merchant compares four vessels by material, tea suited and care: classic teapot, kyusu, gaiwan, Yixing pot [S28].
- **Glassware.** The collector side lists stemware tiers by maker and format (Riedel and Spiegelau mid-range; Zalto, Josephinenhütte, Grassl hand-blown upper tier; stemless as a separate format) [S37]. Riedel's own guide chooses the glass **shape per grape variety or spirit** across hundreds of entries [S38].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

Replacements, Ltd. [S35][S36] divides the tabletop trade into China-Dinnerware, Crystal-Glassware, Silver-Flatware and Collectibles. Each has "Brands A–Z" and "Popular patterns" and a **piece-type guide with piece codes** (dinner plate DP, cup and saucer sets; water goblet, wine glass, iced tea, juice glass flat and footed, cordial, sherry, liquor cocktail, old fashioned, martini, flat tumbler, brandy). So the collectors' unit is the **pattern** (maker plus design), sold in **piece types**. This is a two-key system: kind = piece type, named form = pattern.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- Customs headings are evidence only (CV-36, C17): the HS and tariff lines below show how trade divides objects; they never define a kind or a leaf, and a legal class such as lead crystal goes to the jurisdiction ledger.
- **HS 7323** (table, kitchen and household articles of iron or steel) divides **by material first**: cast iron not enamelled, cast iron enamelled, stainless steel, enamelled iron or steel, other; it excludes cutlery of 8211–8215 [S30]. The US schedule adds, inside each material, teakettles, bakeware (cookware not suitable for stove top), kitchen ware, cookingware [S31].
- **HS 7013** (table and kitchen glassware) divides by material and making method: glass ceramics, lead crystal stemware, other stemware, drinking glasses (hand-gathered or mechanical, cut or plain), borosilicate and toughened table glass [S32]. Lead crystal is a legal class, which is one of the few places where "crystal" has a definition.
- **UNSPSC family 52150000** (domestic kitchenware and kitchen supplies) has nine classes: disposable kitchenware; kitchen tools and utensils; flatware and cutlery; cookware; bakeware; dishes, servingware and storage containers; drink ware; dishwashing and dish-storage accessories; kitchen supplies [S33]. The tools class alone lists 68 commodities, from rolling pins and graters to kitchen scales, timers, thermometers and knife sharpeners [S34].
- **SCA Certified Home Equipment** gives a test-based list of home brewers and home filter grinders, with a separate commercial programme; it is a pass/fail certification, not a ranking [S27].

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

No museum source was fetched. Wikipedia on the kitchen knife and the Japanese kitchen knife gives the reference layers: construction (steel type, edge, profile), then national traditions (Japanese single-bevel set of yanagiba, deba, usuba; double-bevel gyuto, santoku, nakiri, petty), with Seki as the production centre [S19][S20].

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split; gaps

- **Agree:** knives, cookware, tableware and glassware are different families in every tree; kitchen knives are split by pattern, not by price; the table service unit is the piece type; glass shape is chosen per drink.
- **Split:** appliance aisle vs job grouping; cutlery meaning; whether material is a tree or a filter (trade classification and buying guides lead with material, retailers keep it as a parallel route).
- **Gaps:** China (JD, Tmall) and Korea were searched for but no category page was retrieved; Italy and France (moka pot, cocotte makers) have no fetched source; the sources are skewed to English markets plus Japan and Germany. Commercial equipment is covered by one Australian supplier only. Rakuten, Otto and WMF-style German pages gave little structure. These gaps are stage-3 source-census and harvest tasks (B-34); the list grows with the attested evidence and does not wait for them.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

- **Product family as sold** comes first in navigation: every retailer's top level is a list of families (cookware, knives, tools, appliances, tableware, drinkware) [S1][S9], and UNSPSC does the same (tools, flatware, cookware, bakeware, dishes, drink ware) [S33].
- **Within a family, the second choice differs.** For cookware it is **material and heat source**: Williams Sonoma "Shop by Material" [S1], Kitchen Warehouse stainless and cast iron plus induction pages [S10], buying guides that compare carbon steel, stainless and cast iron [S29], and Otto titles that print hob suitability [S6]. For knives it is **pattern** (gyuto, santoku, nakiri) and then steel and handle [S17][S21]. For espresso it is **operating mode** and then boiler layout [S23][S24]. For tableware it is **piece type**, then pattern and maker [S35]. For glassware it is **shape for the drink** [S36][S38].
- **Questions buyers ask in the guides:** "carbon steel vs stainless vs cast iron" [S29]; "which Japanese knife first: gyuto, santoku or petty" [S17][S18]; "which steel is best for kitchen knives" [S21]; "semi-automatic, super-automatic or lever" [S23]; "what burr size do I need" [S26]; "which teapot for which tea" [S28]; "stemmed or stemless" [S37]. All of them are comparisons between kinds, or between tiers inside one kind.
- **Conclusion:** the first choice is the **job the object does** (heat a vessel, cut, measure, serve, drink), made visible by the retail aisle. Material, power source, brand and tier come second and live as facets.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (decided, B-6): stage of kitchen work, then product family, then form or pattern

Levels (fixed names from convention C5(a); four levels below the category, the named form being the fourth) and the single axis of each:
1. **Sub-family** — axis: *the stage of kitchen work the object serves* (the wording of the B5 test itself: cook, prepare, cool and store, serve and drink at table, make drinks; plus a small remainder), closed by B5.7 Parts and accessories made for one kitchen object (C5(d), CV-17), which is exempt from the stage axis.
2. **Leaf** — axis stated per sub-family (C5(c)); a sub-family with about 12 kinds or fewer skips the leaf level (C5(b), `leaf = null`):

| B5.2 Prepare | product family as sold (knives, boards and sharpeners, hand tools, measuring, food machines) | pattern for knives; the tool itself for hand tools and measuring; appliance type for food machines |

| B5.4 Serve, eat and drink at table | piece family in the place setting (dinnerware, flatware, glassware, cups, serveware and table accessories), as Replacements and the retailers divide it [S35][S36][S4] | piece type, in place-setting order [S35] |

3. **Kind** — as in the right-hand column of the table.
4. **Named form** — models, lines, patterns and iconic designs (a Wüsthof or Shun line, a Breville or Lelit machine, a Riedel shape, a dinnerware pattern, Yixing under teapot), only when the name denotes something a buyer can compare or collect. Each named form carries `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2(a)): a model, line or pattern is maker-bound; a generic cut or traditional variant that many makers make (gyuto, Yixing teapot) is not. Sizes, colourways, certifications and materials are never named forms (C2(b)); colourways, limited editions and hardware revisions are variants (C3), filled wherever a fetched community or catalogue compares them (CV-8), for example espresso-machine and grinder revisions and cookware colourways and limited editions †.

Placement rule inside the sub-family stage axis (resolves B5-1): **a grinder made for coffee is filed with coffee equipment; a spice or pepper mill is a hand tool in B5.2**; every vessel one drinks from at the table (glass, mug, cup) is B5.4, whatever the drink; B5.5 holds only the objects that make the drink.

### Candidate B: retail aisle first, powered vs unpowered (Williams Sonoma and Kitchen Warehouse shape)

- Level 1 — axis *aisle*: Cookware; Knives; Kitchenware (hand tools); **Appliances/Electrics**; Bakeware; Tableware; Drinkware [S1][S9].
- Level 2 — axis *product family inside the aisle* (cooking appliances, kitchen appliances, coffee machines and grinders) [S12].

## 5. Quality and tier

Decided (CV-10 to CV-12, C4): **tier pockets only where a fetched source gives a scheme; preference elsewhere.** A tier never sits on a kind (C4(a)). It sits on a maker, a line or a named form as a tier entry (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`), with the source's own labels as values and a computed `disputed` flag when two sources differ. A spec that is measured on the object is an attribute with its standard named (C4(b)). A pass/fail mark is a certification, not a tier. Every pocket below rests on one fetched page, which is enough to admit its scheme (C4(e), CV-10); a second source or region would only add weight (the `disputed` flag and the scheme's weight in views). Each scheme is registered once in the scheme registry with its scale class (C4(c)).

- **Knife steels:** steel name and hardness (HRC, with the Rockwell C scale named, plus the chromium share, about 13 percent for stainless) are attributes [S22]. The four-property ratings and the "best kitchen" and "best budget" picks [S21] are a published steel comparison. They are not stored as a tier on any kind. As a published comparison they are class (iii), an editorial or community class: tier entries on named forms with the scheme named, admitted on S21 alone.
- **Espresso machines:** the capability ladder (entry all-in-one, grinder upgrade, scale and workflow, prosumer dual boiler, commercial-grade home) [S25] is a scheme whose entries go on named forms (models), with the source's labels as values. The spec table of boiler material, pump type, PID and steam wand across entry, mid and high-end [S24] gives attributes, plus a second scheme from a retailer. Both are class (iii) editorial or community schemes (US retailer or enthusiast blogs), each admitted on its one page.
- **Grinders:** burr diameter (about 40–54 mm small, 55–65 mid, 74–75 large, 83 and above extra-large) [S26] is a spec ladder measured on the object, so it is an attribute (`burr_mm`), not a tier entry. The source itself says burr geometry steers flavour more than diameter.
- **Home brewers and grinders:** the SCA's pass/fail certification [S27] is a `certification` field on the named form (class (i), checked on the object), never a tier.
- **Stemware:** maker tiers (Riedel and Spiegelau mid-range; Zalto, Josephinenhütte and Grassl upper collector tier) [S37] are tier entries on makers, scheme named as the retailer's own (class (iii), editorial or community; admitted on S37 alone). Lead crystal is a material attribute; as a legal class in trade classification [S32] it belongs in the jurisdiction ledger (C17), never a level or a tag on the tree.
- **Cookware, tableware, hand tools, food machines:** no recognised tier ordering was found in the fetched pages; only price bands, construction labels such as "5 Ply" [S5] (an attribute), and brand standing [S9]. Treat as preference.
- **Price band** is the domain-wide USD scheme (C4(d)), derived from a dated, sourced list price. B5 adds no band of its own; the earlier labels "entry, mid, prosumer/premium, commercial" are dropped as a vocabulary (B5-2).
- **Commercial grade is a market modifier** (Appendix A), written as `market = commercial`, never a tier value. Kitchen Warehouse (commercial tableware and drinkware [S13][S14]) and the commercial supplier [S16] carry the same kinds as the home trees. The espresso ladder's top step "commercial-grade home" [S25] is that source's own scheme value, kept verbatim inside its scheme and not read as a market tag. Commercial-only forms (for example the gastronorm pan [S16]) are ordinary kinds with `market = commercial` (B-20).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended: identity**, with a platform pocket still to be sourced.

Knife steel names are materials, not makers, so a knife maker ledger (Seki and other production centres [S20]) is separate from the steel list [S21][S22].
- **Maker ledger: yes, on the core schema of C1:** `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class` (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; retail own-label, such as John Lewis [S5]; licensee or diffusion; artisan or atelier; supplier); `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `known_for_kinds`; `scene_tags`; `price_band` (C4(d)); `sources` (at least one fetched URL). B5 adds no category extension to the maker ledger. A separate table holds **lines and collections** (Le Creuset lines, 5 Ply Thermacore, Riedel O), with `built_by_maker_id`; a second holds **tier entries** (section 5); the **named-form ledger** holds models, shapes and patterns with `maker_bound`; **variants** (C3) are filled wherever a fetched community or catalogue compares them (CV-8) and are uncapped. "Known-for named forms" is derived from the named-form ledger, not stored as a column.
- **School table and maker relations (CV-3, C1).** The standing school table holds traditions of makers: the Seki knife-making tradition [S20] is the first row, and further schools (knife-making towns, tea-ware workshops such as the Yixing potters) enter from attested pages †. Japanese kitchen-knife makers are recorded in the ledger, not as kinds (B-20, K-83).
- **Patterns** (china, crystal, flatware) are maker-bound named forms. Tens of thousands of them are held outside the tree in registers keyed by kind, uncapped (CV-45, C2(g)). A design series that crosses kinds (Riedel O across wine glass and tumbler; a pattern sold as plate, cup and bowl) is a lines row under the maker (C1, C2(d)), and its members are named forms under their own kinds, never one named form that spans two kinds.
- **Compatibility ledger (C13):** capsule and pod systems would make the maker set compatibility, but no fetched page shows it †. When sourced, each system is one row (`platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `sources`), linked to the J6 capsule kinds (J6 files capsules as groceries). No capsule-machine kind is created now; it would have to pass rule T(b) with a fetched comparison source. The only fetched compatibility attribute is hob suitability (induction), which is a spec attribute, not a platform [S5][S6]. Platforms are never levels (CV-29, C13). Machine capsules and pods are one J6 kind, with the system in C13 (J-14).
- **Scene tags (C8):** the scenes in section 7 are held back as tags until each has a definition source †; the pages cited there show that the scene exists, not how it is defined.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical or most general form first, then specialist forms, then spec order where a spec exists** (C7). Named forms: non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), first-introduction year, then alphabetically (C7, CV-21). Maker ladders and spec ladders are generated views, not stored order. Variants go by `variant_code` or year.
- Knives: general-purpose first (chef's knife, santoku), then vegetable and utility, then slicing and specialist, the order in which an enthusiast shop lists them [S17], also the order in which Williams Sonoma lists its own (chef's, steak, santoku, paring, bread, utility, specialty) [S2].
- Pots and pans: by common use (frying pan, saucepan, stockpot) as in the John Lewis quick links [S5].
- Coffee: by operating mode from most manual to most automatic (manual lever, semi-automatic, super-automatic), consistent with the control-versus-convenience framing of the guides [S23].
- Grinders and machines: by spec (burr diameter [S26]) or by the ladder [S25] inside a kind.
- Tableware and glassware: place-setting order, as the Replacements guides run [S35][S36]; serveware and table accessories last.
- Parts and accessories (B5.7): by the object served, in the order that object has in the tree.
Reason: users arrive knowing the common form and look for the specialist; a spec order is only meaningful inside one kind.

## 9. Boundaries

1. **C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment (and E4).** The word "utility knife" names a mid-size kitchen knife [S2][S11] and a box-cutter in trade; the B5 kind is therefore labelled "kitchen utility knife (petty)". *Precedence: a knife made to cut or prepare food at a board or table is B5; a knife made for trade, craft or pocket use (box cutter, pocket knife, multi-tool) is C1; a knife made to fight or hunt is E4.* Chinese cleavers and Japanese slicers are B5 [S17][S19]. Fighting, hunting, butterfly and automatic knives are E4, and everyday-carry locking folders are C1 unless the blade is a weapon kind (K-83). A cutting tool is filed by the work it is made for: one made to cut food (kitchen shears †, named in the Kitchen Warehouse knives menu seen in a search excerpt) is B5, general household scissors are C5 (K-171). Retail also sells pocket knives and Swiss Army knives in the same Knives aisle as kitchen knives (Kitchen Warehouse's mega-menu, search excerpt only), which is a reminder that the aisle name is not the test.
2. **B2 Soft Furnishings.** *Precedence: table linen (cloth, textile placemats, napkins) is B2; hard coasters, trivets and boards are B5; oven gloves and aprons are B5 under the branch text; tea towels and kitchen towels are B2, in B2.5 "Table and kitchen linen" (B2-2).* B2 has still to attest napkin, placemat and table runner (B2-5), and B5 relies on that. Textile placemats and table runners are B2 and hard placemats and coasters are B5 (B5-O1); retail lists placemats and coasters under tableware [S13].
3. **B6 Cleaning & Laundry and B7 Climate, Water & Energy.** *Precedence: a dishwasher is B5; washing-up liquid, sponges, brushes, descalers and dishwasher salt are B6 (cleaning products and tools); a kitchen bin and kitchen and oven cleaner are B6; a refrigerator or wine fridge is B5, not B7; a dish rack and a water filter jug that treats water for drinking are B5 (B-33); filter cartridges, including those for a water filter jug, are B7 (K-11); a plumbed water filter is B7.*
4. **B4 Decor and B1 Furniture & Storage.** *Precedence: a bowl, plate or vessel made to hold or serve food is B5, however decorative or collected (framework rule S5), while a vase or a bowl made only to be looked at is B4; a decanter and a cocktail shaker are B5; a wine rack, bar cart, kitchen cabinet, kitchen island or shelf is B1; a humidor is B1.* Decided (K-182, K-191): every plate and tray made to hold food is B5, whatever it is kept for, and display is a tag (S5); painted ceramic plates made to be hung, such as Kosiv ware, are B5 plates; a tray made to serve is B5 and a tray made to hold small items on a surface is B1. A B4 "decorative plate" kind is therefore not used.
5. **J Groceries, E2 Sports and B10.** *Precedence: coffee beans, tea leaves and wine in bottles are J, but the equipment to brew, grind, pour and drink them is B5 (the Culinary taxonomy holds the drink as a drink); a camping stove, pan or mug is B5 because camping is a setting; drink bottles and bladders are B5 whether or not they are made for a sport [S14], and a hydration pack sold with its bag is A4 (K-161); home-brewing and winemaking ingredients are J5 and their equipment is B5 (K-58); honey extraction and bottling gear is B10 with beekeeping equipment (K-106); a scale goes with its task and a pocket scale is B5 (K-167).* An insulated lunch bag or cool bag is B5: the A4 note says so (K-131), which closes the step 17 conflict.
6. **D (electronics).** *Precedence: a connected oven, scale or kettle is B5 by task (step 24 note); a smart display or voice assistant in a kitchen is D3.*
7. **E1 Toys.** *Precedence (C15): a toy kitchen or play food that cannot cook or serve to the real object's standard is E1; a real pan, knife or cup made in a child's size stays B5, with size and user as attributes and tags.*
8. **Sets and kits (S3a, K-1).** *Precedence: a set of objects of one kind is that kind (a set of wine glasses is wine glass); a mixed set (dinner set, knife block set, cookware set, cup and saucer) is filed by its item of greatest value with its contents listed; a kit sold, standardised and compared as one object is a kind (none is attested in B5 so far); there are no other set kinds.*
9. **Parts (S3, CV-17).** *Precedence: a component, lid, jar or attachment made to be fitted to one kitchen object is B5.7 and follows that object; racks and shelves that hold several objects are B1; cleaning and descaling products are B6 and filter cartridges B7 (K-11).*
