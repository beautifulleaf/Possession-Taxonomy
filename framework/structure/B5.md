## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B5 Kitchen & Dining.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 30, first branch): *made to prepare, cook, cool, store or serve food and drink: cookware, kitchen tools and machines, kitchen scales and thermometers, cooking appliances, grills, refrigerators and wine fridges, dishwashers, vending machines for food and drink, tableware, glassware, barware, decanters, tea and coffee equipment, food storage and lunch bags, oven gloves and aprons, kitchen timers.* Category list line: "everything made to prepare, cook, cool, store or serve food and drink, powered or not, domestic or commercial."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name B5:**
- Step 10 note (E4): kitchen and utility knives are B5 and C1 (hunting and fighting knives are E4; pocket knives and multi-tools are C1).
- Step 16 note (A5): kitchen timers are B5.
- Step 20 note (F2): vases, bowls and other vessels are B4 or B5, however artistic.
- Step 23 note (C6): kitchen scales and thermometers are B5.
- Step 26 note (E2): portable furniture, stoves, cookware, cool boxes and lanterns are filed by what they do (cookware is B5) wherever they are used; "camping" is a setting.
- Step 30 branch order: B5 is the first branch tried, so a kitchen object that could also be cleaning (B6), climate or water (B7) or furniture (B1) is B5 if it prepares, cooks, cools, stores or serves food and drink.
- S3: parts follow the whole; cases and covers are parts only for one object at a time; furniture, racks and storage that hold several objects are never parts.
- S5: a collected teapot is a teapot; a commemorative plate is a plate.
- Appendix A: power source and technology, connectivity, market (domestic, commercial, professional), installation, material, brand and maker, price band and quality tier, region, era, setting, user and scene are modifiers.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object is filed by its task (so a smart oven or app-linked scale stays B5).

Where they split the same objects differently:

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

- **Knives.** Japanese-knife specialists group types by job: versatile (gyuto, santoku, bunka), vegetable and utility (nakiri, petty, Chinese cleaver, kiridashi), slicing, butchery and traditional (sujihiki, kiritsuke, honesuki, deba, yanagiba, usuba) [S17]. A second guide splits first by **bevel** (ryoba double, kataba single) and then lists type, length and purpose [S18]. Wikipedia lists Western types (paring, utility, boning, cook's, bread, carving, cleaver) and then Japanese and Chinese families [S19][S20]. The same shop navigates by knife type, then by handle (Japanese wa or western) and stainless steel [S17].
- **Cookware.** Buying guides organise by **material**: carbon steel, stainless steel, cast iron (and enamelled cast iron), with strengths and drawbacks [S29].
- **Espresso.** Enthusiast and specialist-retailer guides split machines by operating mode (semi-automatic, super-automatic, manual lever) [S23][S24], then by boiler layout (single, heat exchanger, dual) [S23][S24], and describe an **upgrade ladder** (entry all-in-one, grinder, scale, prosumer dual boiler, commercial-grade home) [S25]. Grinders are described by burr size tier and flat or conical geometry [S26].
- **Tea.** One merchant compares four vessels by material, tea suited and care: classic teapot, kyusu, gaiwan, Yixing pot [S28].
- **Glassware.** The collector side lists stemware tiers by maker and format (Riedel and Spiegelau mid-range; Zalto, Josephinenhütte, Grassl hand-blown upper tier; stemless as a separate format) [S37]. Riedel's own guide chooses the glass **shape per grape variety or spirit** across hundreds of entries [S38].

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

Replacements, Ltd. [S35][S36] divides the tabletop trade into China-Dinnerware, Crystal-Glassware, Silver-Flatware and Collectibles. Each has "Brands A–Z" and "Popular patterns" and a **piece-type guide with piece codes** (dinner plate DP, cup and saucer sets; water goblet, wine glass, iced tea, juice glass flat and footed, cordial, sherry, liquor cocktail, old fashioned, martini, flat tumbler, brandy). So the collectors' unit is the **pattern** (maker plus design), sold in **piece types**. This is a two-key system: kind = piece type, named form = pattern.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS 7323** (table, kitchen and household articles of iron or steel) divides **by material first**: cast iron not enamelled, cast iron enamelled, stainless steel, enamelled iron or steel, other; it excludes cutlery of 8211–8215 [S30]. The US schedule adds, inside each material, teakettles, bakeware (cookware not suitable for stove top), kitchen ware, cookingware [S31].
- **HS 7013** (table and kitchen glassware) divides by material and making method: glass ceramics, lead crystal stemware, other stemware, drinking glasses (hand-gathered or mechanical, cut or plain), borosilicate and toughened table glass [S32]. Lead crystal is a legal class, which is one of the few places where "crystal" has a definition.
- **UNSPSC family 52150000** (domestic kitchenware and kitchen supplies) has nine classes: disposable kitchenware; kitchen tools and utensils; flatware and cutlery; cookware; bakeware; dishes, servingware and storage containers; drink ware; dishwashing and dish-storage accessories; kitchen supplies [S33]. The tools class alone lists 68 commodities, from rolling pins and graters to kitchen scales, timers, thermometers and knife sharpeners [S34].
- **SCA Certified Home Equipment** gives a test-based list of home brewers and home filter grinders, with a separate commercial programme; it is a pass/fail certification, not a ranking [S27].

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

No museum source was fetched. Wikipedia on the kitchen knife and the Japanese kitchen knife gives the reference layers: construction (steel type, edge, profile), then national traditions (Japanese single-bevel set of yanagiba, deba, usuba; double-bevel gyuto, santoku, nakiri, petty), with Seki as the production centre [S19][S20].

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split; gaps

- **Agree:** knives, cookware, tableware and glassware are different families in every tree; kitchen knives are split by pattern, not by price; the table service unit is the piece type; glass shape is chosen per drink.
- **Split:** appliance aisle vs job grouping; cutlery meaning; whether material is a tree or a filter (trade classification and buying guides lead with material, retailers keep it as a parallel route).
- **Gaps:** China (JD, Tmall) and Korea were searched for but no category page was retrieved; Italy and France (moka pot, cocotte makers) have no fetched source; the sources are skewed to English markets plus Japan and Germany. Commercial equipment is covered by one Australian supplier only. Rakuten, Otto and WMF-style German pages gave little structure. These gaps should be closed before the owner treats the sub-family list as final.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

- **Product family as sold** comes first in navigation: every retailer's top level is a list of families (cookware, knives, tools, appliances, tableware, drinkware) [S1][S9], and UNSPSC does the same (tools, flatware, cookware, bakeware, dishes, drink ware) [S33].
- **Within a family, the second choice differs.** For cookware it is **material and heat source**: Williams Sonoma "Shop by Material" [S1], Kitchen Warehouse stainless and cast iron plus induction pages [S10], buying guides that compare carbon steel, stainless and cast iron [S29], and Otto titles that print hob suitability [S6]. For knives it is **pattern** (gyuto, santoku, nakiri) and then steel and handle [S17][S21]. For espresso it is **operating mode** and then boiler layout [S23][S24]. For tableware it is **piece type**, then pattern and maker [S35]. For glassware it is **shape for the drink** [S36][S38].
- **Questions buyers ask in the guides:** "carbon steel vs stainless vs cast iron" [S29]; "which Japanese knife first: gyuto, santoku or petty" [S17][S18]; "which steel is best for kitchen knives" [S21]; "semi-automatic, super-automatic or lever" [S23]; "what burr size do I need" [S26]; "which teapot for which tea" [S28]; "stemmed or stemless" [S37]. All of them are comparisons between kinds, or between tiers inside one kind.
- **Conclusion:** the first choice is the **job the object does** (heat a vessel, cut, measure, serve, drink), made visible by the retail aisle. Material, power source, brand and tier come second and live as facets.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): stage of kitchen work, then product family, then form or pattern

Levels and the single axis of each:
1. **Sub-family** — axis: *the stage of kitchen work the object serves* (the wording of the B5 test itself: cook, prepare, cool and store, serve, drink; plus a small remainder).
2. **Leaf** — axis: *product family as sold* (the retail aisle and the UNSPSC class: pans, pots, bakeware, kitchen knives, hand tools, measuring, coffee equipment, tea equipment, tableware, glassware).
3. **Kind** — axis stated per leaf: *vessel form* for cookware; *pattern* for knives; *operating mode* for machines; *piece type* for tableware and glassware.
4. **Named form** — models, lines, patterns and iconic designs (a Wüsthof or Shun line, a Breville or Lelit machine, a Riedel shape, a Gaiwan-style variant, a dinnerware pattern), only when the name denotes something a buyer can compare or collect.

### Candidate B: retail aisle first, powered vs unpowered (Williams Sonoma and Kitchen Warehouse shape)

- Level 1 — axis *aisle*: Cookware; Knives; Kitchenware (hand tools); **Appliances/Electrics**; Bakeware; Tableware; Drinkware [S1][S9].
- Level 2 — axis *product family inside the aisle* (cooking appliances, kitchen appliances, coffee machines and grinders) [S12].
- Example kinds (12): frying pan, saucepan, chef's knife, santoku, spatula, peeler, stand mixer, air fryer, toaster, espresso machine, dinner plate, wine glass.
- Breaks: (1) the Appliances aisle is a power-source split, and Appendix A forbids that on inner levels; an electric kettle and a stovetop kettle would be in different branches; (2) hand-cranked and electric versions of the same kind (grinder, mixer, whisk, can opener) split; (3) a coffee grinder lands in Appliances while a manual grinder lands in Kitchenware; (4) pressure cookers, rice cookers and slow cookers fall between Cookware and Appliances. Retail navigation works because shelves are physical; a taxonomy cannot copy it.
- This candidate is better kept as a **generated view** ("all powered kitchen kinds") over Candidate A.

### Candidate C: material and trade-classification split (HS 7323 / 7013 shape)

- Level 1 — axis *material*: cast iron, enamelled cast iron, stainless steel, other iron or steel, ceramic, glass, crystal [S30][S31][S32].
- Level 2 — axis *article*: cookware, bakeware, kettles, tableware [S31].
- Example kinds (10): cast-iron skillet, enamelled Dutch oven, stainless saucepan, carbon-steel pan, stainless teakettle, stainless kitchenware, lead-crystal wine glass, borosilicate dish, toughened plate, glass-ceramic bowl.
- Breaks: Appendix A makes material a modifier unless it is the form; the same pan form (frying pan) would be spread over four branches; clad cookware has several materials in layers and customs classes it by the outer layer, which hides what buyers compare; knives and coffee machines are not in this classification at all; buying guides compare materials [S29], so material must be kept as a **strong tag and generated view** ("by material"), not a level.

## 5. Quality and tier

Recommended answer: **consensus-ranked in four pockets, preference elsewhere**, always carried as a modifier on the kind or named form, never as a level.

- **Knife steels:** consensus ranking on four properties (edge retention, toughness, corrosion resistance, sharpening ease) in a published chart with "best kitchen" and "best budget" picks [S21], backed by measurable hardness and composition [S22]. Stainless threshold of about 13 percent chromium [S22].
- **Espresso machines:** a consensus capability ladder: entry all-in-one, grinder upgrade, scale and workflow, prosumer dual boiler, commercial-grade home [S25]; a spec table of boiler material, pump type, PID and steam wand across entry, mid and high-end [S24].
- **Grinders:** burr-diameter tiers (about 40–54 mm small, 55–65 mid, 74–75 large, 83 and above extra-large) [S26]. This is a spec ordering, but the source itself says burr geometry steers flavour more than diameter.
- **Home brewers and grinders:** the SCA offers a pass/fail certification; it is a quality mark, not a rank [S27].
- **Stemware:** maker tiers (Riedel and Spiegelau mid-range; Zalto, Josephinenhütte and Grassl upper collector tier) [S37]. Lead crystal is also a legal class in trade classification [S32].
- **Cookware, tableware, hand tools, food machines:** no recognised tier ordering was found in the fetched pages; only price bands, construction labels such as "5 Ply" [S5], and brand standing [S9]. Treat as preference.
- **Commercial grade** is a market modifier. Both Kitchen Warehouse (commercial tableware and commercial drinkware [S13][S14]) and the commercial supplier [S16] carry the same kinds as the home trees, and the espresso ladder treats a commercial-grade machine as the top step [S25]. A "commercial grade" tag on the kind is enough; commercial-only forms (for example the gastronorm pan [S16]) are kinds in their own right.

As a modifier: `tier = {entry, mid, prosumer/premium, commercial}` with the ordering source stated per pocket, and `spec` attributes (HRC, burr mm, boiler layout) beside it.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended: identity**, with a platform pocket still to be sourced.

- Identity: buyers name makers inside the kind: Le Creuset and Wolstead lines [S9]; Breville, Rancilio, Lelit, Profitec, Jura machines [S24]; Eureka and Turin grinders [S26]; Riedel, Spiegelau, Zalto glass [S37][S38]; china, crystal and flatware makers via Replacements' "Brands A–Z" in each of its four departments [S35][S36]; SCA certification lists by maker [S27]. Knife steel names are materials, not makers, so a knife maker ledger (Seki and other production centres [S20]) is separate from the steel list [S21][S22].
- Platform: capsule and pod systems make the maker set compatibility, but no fetched page shows it; this must be sourced before it is claimed. The only fetched compatibility attribute is hob suitability (induction), which is a spec, not a maker platform [S5][S6].
- **Maker ledger: yes.** Columns: maker, known-for kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country and era, each row attested. The ledger carries patterns and lines for tableware, so the tree does not need to hold them.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical or most general form first, then specialist forms, then spec order where a spec exists.**
- Knives: general-purpose first (chef's knife, santoku), then vegetable and utility, then slicing and specialist, the order in which an enthusiast shop lists them [S17], also the order in which Williams Sonoma lists its own (chef's, steak, santoku, paring, bread, utility, specialty) [S2].
- Pots and pans: by common use (frying pan, saucepan, stockpot) as in the John Lewis quick links [S5].
- Coffee: by operating mode from most manual to most automatic (manual lever, semi-automatic, super-automatic), consistent with the control-versus-convenience framing of the guides [S23].
- Grinders and machines: by spec (burr diameter [S26]) or by the ladder [S25] inside a kind.
- Tableware and glassware: place-setting order, as the Replacements guides run [S35][S36].
Reason: users arrive knowing the common form and look for the specialist; a spec order is only meaningful inside one kind.

## 9. Boundaries

1. **C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment.** The word "utility knife" names a mid-size kitchen knife [S2][S11] and a box-cutter in trade. *Precedence: a knife made to cut or prepare food at a board or table is B5; a knife made for trade, craft or pocket use (box cutter, pocket knife, multi-tool) is C1; a knife made to fight or hunt is E4.* Chinese cleavers and Japanese slicers are B5 [S17][S19]. Retail also sells pocket knives and Swiss Army knives in the same Knives aisle as kitchen knives (Kitchen Warehouse's mega-menu, search excerpt only), which is a reminder that the aisle name is not the test.
2. **B2 Soft Furnishings.** Placemats, napkins and tablecloths are table linen. *Precedence: table linen (cloth, textile placemats, napkins) is B2; hard coasters, trivets and boards are B5; oven gloves and aprons are B5 under the branch text; tea towels and kitchen towels are B2 (towels of every kind).* Retail lists placemats and coasters under tableware [S13], so the owner should confirm.
3. **B6 Cleaning & Laundry and B7 Climate, Water & Energy.** *Precedence: a dishwasher is B5; washing-up liquid, sponges and brushes are B6 (cleaning products and tools); a refrigerator or wine fridge is B5, not B7; a water filter jug or plumbed water filter that treats water for drinking is a judgement call between B7 (filter water) and B5 (serve drink) and needs an owner ruling.* A dish rack is also open (clean-up vs store).
4. **B4 Decor and B1 Furniture & Storage.** *Precedence: a bowl, plate or vessel made to hold or serve food is B5, however decorative or collected (framework rule S5), while a vase or a bowl made only to be looked at is B4; a decanter and a cocktail shaker are B5; a wine rack, bar cart, kitchen cabinet or shelf is B1; a humidor is B1.*
5. **J Groceries and E2 Sports.** *Precedence: coffee beans, tea leaves and wine in bottles are J, but the equipment to brew, grind, pour and drink them is B5 (the Culinary taxonomy holds the drink as a drink); a camping stove, pan or mug is B5 because camping is a setting; a drink bottle is B5 [S14] unless it is made for a sport (a cycling or running bottle), which follows step 26 and needs an owner ruling.*
6. **D (electronics).** *Precedence: a connected oven, scale or kettle is B5 by task (step 24 note); a smart display or voice assistant in a kitchen is D3.*

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A. Sub-family by stage of kitchen work (cook, prepare, cool and store, serve and eat, drink, clean-up remainder); leaf by product family as sold; kind by vessel form, pattern, operating mode or piece type; named form for lines, models, shapes and patterns. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Power source, material, commercial grade, tier, brand, region and scene stay as modifiers, ledgers or generated views (appliances view, by-material view, induction view, prosumer view, commercial view).

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. Confirm that powered kitchen objects are spread by job and not gathered into an "Appliances" sub-family (Candidate B), with an appliances view generated instead.
2. Confirm material (cast iron, carbon steel, stainless, copper, clad) as a tag and view, not a level, although guides and trade classification lead with it [S29][S30].
3. Decide whether "gyuto" is a named form of "chef's knife" or its own kind; retailers merge them [S2], enthusiast shops separate them [S17].
4. Decide the status of sets and bundles (knife block set, dinner set, cookware set): recommended as a unit-of-sale tag, not a kind.
5. Decide whether patterns (china, crystal, flatware) are held in a ledger only, given their size [S35].
6. Rule on steak knives and butter knives (B5.2 or B5.4), coffee grinders (prepare or drink) and kettles; the memo placed them by retail evidence.
7. Rule on water filter jugs, dish racks, placemats and coasters.
8. Decide whether commercial-only forms (gastronorm pans, bain maries, commercial ranges [S16]) are ordinary kinds with a "commercial" tag.
9. Approve further research to close the gaps in section 1.6 (China, Korea, Italy, France; museum sources; platform systems such as capsule machines) before the sub-family list is frozen.
