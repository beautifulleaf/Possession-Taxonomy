## 0. Category

**Code and name:** A4 Bags & Accessories (domain A, Worn & Carried).

**Membership test (placement key step 17, fourth branch):** "a bag or luggage of any kind (handbags, backpacks and rucksacks, suitcases, duffels, dry bags, garment bags, tote and shopping bags), wallet or purse, umbrella, sunglasses or other non-corrective eyewear, hand fan, keyring, fashion cane, baby carrier or sling, shopping trolley". Step 17 only applies to things "worn on the body, or carried on the person or in hand as a personal accessory".

**Notes that bind A4:**
- "An object that is only portable, such as a pocket knife, lighter, pen or water bottle, is filed by what it does."
- "Cases made for one object at a time follow it under S3" (phone case, guitar case, golf bag, camera bag, watch strap). "Tool bags are C1."
- G1 note: "Wheeled luggage is A4. Shopping trolleys and trolley bags are A4. ... Baby carriers and slings are A4." Prams and strollers are G1.
- Step 2: "spectacles and contact lenses that correct vision" are H2. Step 17 note: safety glasses and PPE are A1; heated or lit garments stay in A1.
- S3: furniture, racks, boxes and storage that hold several objects or stand in a room are never parts (B1), so a room trunk is not luggage.
- C6 note (step 23): "luggage scales A4". E2 note (step 26): "Rucksacks and bags of every kind are A4, except cases made for one piece of equipment, which follow it under S3." G1 note: "shopping trolleys and trolley bags are A4". Step 17 lists "dry bags" and "fashion cane" as A4 objects.
- Appendix A: brand/maker, price/tier, region, era, scene, user, setting, occasion, material (unless material is the form), condition, edition are modifiers.

**Precedence sentences that touch A4** are collected in section 9.

Resale sites (above) and the V&A show the market treating bags as maker-plus-line objects. ### 1.4 Standards and trade classifications

- **HS / EU CN heading 4202** (https://wisepim.com/hs-codes/4202). The six-digit split is by form class: 4202.1x trunks, suitcases, vanity cases, executive-cases, briefcases, school satchels; 4202.2x handbags; 4202.3x "articles of a kind normally carried in the pocket or in the handbag" (wallets, purses, key-pouches, cigarette-cases); 4202.9x other (travelling bags, toilet bags, rucksacks, shopping bags, sports bags and others). Each class is then split by outer material (leather; plastics or textile; other). The heading also holds spectacle cases, binocular, camera, instrument and gun cases, tool bags and holsters, which the framework files elsewhere under S3. Umbrellas, fans, sunglasses and canes sit in other chapters (unverified; no page fetched).
- **ISO 8624:2020** (https://cdn.standards.iteh.ai/samples/75385/59e6265d21874ffda836e446a9834774/ISO-8624-2020.pdf). Frame measurement vocabulary: boxed lens system, horizontal and vertical boxed lens size, distance between lenses, overall length of side (tolerances in ISO 12870). It standardises size, not shape or type.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **V&A "Bags: Inside Out"** (https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/about-the-bags-inside-out-exhibition). Names objects by maker and line (Hermès Kelly, Lady Dior, Fendi Baguette, Louis Vuitton Speedy by Marc Jacobs) alongside "despatch boxes, vanity cases, military rucksacks" and a design-and-making section.
- **Wikipedia Handbag** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handbag). Types defined by silhouette and carry: baguette, bowling, barrel, bucket, clutch, crossbody, doctor's, east-west, half-moon, hobo, saddlebag, satchel, shoulder, top handle, tote (Birkin cited as an example), wristlet; frame bags vs soft-body bags; traditional types (bilum, Ojibwa bandolier, betel bag). Its own definitions overlap (a baguette "may be worn crossbody"; a satchel may be a top-handle bag).
- **Wikipedia Baggage, Wallet, Sunglasses, Hand fan** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baggage, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallet, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunglasses, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_fan). Luggage: trunk, suitcase, garment bag, duffle, carpet bag, packing cubes, gate-check bag; trunk makers Louis Vuitton, Goyard, Moynat. Wallets: breast, bi-fold, tri-fold, long (popular in Japan), wallet band, wristlet, travel. Sunglasses: fashion styles "not all mutually exclusive"; frames full, half, frameless; clip-on and flip-up. Hand fans: three categories, fixed (uchiwa, tuanshan), folding (sensu, zheshan), powered.
- **Japanese reference** (https://www.modalina.jp/kind/bag.html). 104 bag types, with the note that Japanese separates firm 鞄 (kaban) from soft 袋 (fukuro). It includes house forms (Bolide, Hermès, 1923), traditional forms (furoshiki, azumabukuro, sporran, Korean nubi bag), utility forms (trolley bag, garment bag, pilot case) and rare silhouettes.
- **Chinese reference** (https://baike.so.com/doc/5574401-5788819.html?adapt=1). 包 includes wallet, key bag, coin purse, clutch, handbag, backpack, school bag, satchel, briefcase; it classifies by use (briefcase, camera bag, cosmetic bag) and by carry style (single shoulder, double shoulder, crossbody, hand-carried).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** a bag's type is named by silhouette and carry (tote, crossbody, clutch, backpack) in retail (Selfridges, Zalando), reference (Wikipedia, Modalina, Baike) and enthusiast pages. Luggage is divided by size class (cabin/check-in/trunk) in Rimowa, Samsonite and Tumi. Wallets are divided by format (Leatherology, Wikipedia, LV).
- **Split on wallets:** HS 4202 and the Chinese reference put wallets in the same family as bags; Zalando and Selfridges (men's) shelve wallets separately; LV and Hermès make small leather goods a department parallel to bags.
- **Split on construction:** Samsonite and Tumi treat hardside/softside as a category; Rimowa treats shell as a material filter; HS divides by outer material.
- **Split on shape vs maker:** retailers lead with shape; PurseForum, the V&A and resale pages lead with maker and line.
- **Mixed axes everywhere:** every retail tree mixes silhouette, use, user and object type at one level (Zalando's twelve; Samsonite's facet). One stated axis per level is therefore a design choice, not something any source already does.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence by sub-domain:

- **Handbags, ordinary market:** form/silhouette. Selfridges' type list and counts, Zalando's twelve types and Wikipedia's definitions are all silhouette or carry. Zalando's filters then put Brand first.
- **Handbags, luxury and resale:** maker. PurseForum is shelved by brand; Fashionphile's copy leads with houses; the V&A names bags by house and line.
- **Backpacks and work bags:** function (use). CleverJourney starts "decide what the bag needs to do"; its 23 types are use types with fit checks (laptop sleeve, hip belt, airline underseat dimension).
- **Luggage:** size class and airline rule, then construction and collection. Samsonite's size bands are tied to airline limits; Rimowa leads with Cabin/Check-In/Trunk before collection and material.
- **Wallets and small leather goods:** format and capacity. Leatherology's questions are "how many cards and how much cash" and "which pocket".
- **Eyewear:** frame shape, then rim structure and material, then maker model (Warby Parker, Optiwar, Japan Glasses, Ray-Ban).
- **Umbrellas, fans, baby carriers:** type of mechanism or wearing method (compact/stick/golf; folding/rigid; wrap/sling/buckle carrier).

Conclusion: form (silhouette or format) is the first axis for most of A4; maker is first for luxury handbags; function is first for packs and work bags. Fit/size is a second choice (luggage size class; frame size; carrier age). Performance tier is not a first choice anywhere.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate 1 (recommended): object class, then kind, then named form

Levels and axes (fixed level names from C5(a)):
1. **Sub-family** — axis: what the object is made to do for the owner (carry personal items by hand or shoulder; carry on back or waist; carry for travel; carry papers; hold money and cards; shield the eyes; shield from rain or heat; carry a person; keep, support or haul a small load while out). The last sub-family, "Parts and accessories made for one bag" (C5(d)), is exempt from this axis.
2. **Leaf** — not used. Every sub-family holds about 12 kinds or fewer (A4.1 holds 14; see OWNER item O9), so C5(b) applies: the data stores each kind with `leaf = null`, not under a dummy leaf.
3. **Kind** — axis: silhouette or format (the generic type a buyer names: tote, cabin suitcase, bifold wallet, aviator).
4. **Named form** — axis: maker line or model (maker-bound: Birkin, Speedy, Zippy Wallet, New Wayfarer Classic), or a traditional or regional type that many makers make (not maker-bound: uchiwa, sporran). Every named form carries `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2(a)).

Maximum depth below the category: 3 (sub-family, kind, named form). Argument for fewer than four: no source goes deeper than category > type > line. Hermès is Leather goods > Small leather goods > Wallets > line (sub-family, kind, named form); Vestiaire is Bags > Handbags > Crossbody; Selfridges is Bags > type. An extra leaf level would be an axis no retailer uses (carry position overlaps heavily, see Candidate 2).

Worked tree: 69 kinds in 10 sub-families (a worked example, not a cap). Named forms in brackets are examples; each is attested at the URLs in section 1 unless marked †. The kind placement of a named form is proposed, not attested, until the maker's own page is read (†, section 10). Sizes (Speedy 25/30; Kelly mini to 40) are attributes of the named form, not variants (C2(b), C6). Model or reference codes (RB3025) are variants (C3), not part of the named form's name.

### Form tests (one line each; these resolve the overlaps in F26)

All tests below are proposed by the domain review and no fetched page states them, so each is marked †; Wikipedia's own definitions overlap (a baguette "may be worn crossbody"; a satchel may be a top-handle bag).
- **Shoulder bag** = a single strap worn on one shoulder. **Crossbody bag** = a strap worn across the body, a soft body, no flap fastening at the front. **Messenger bag** = a crossbody bag with a full front flap and a wide gusset. **Sling bag** = a single-strap pack worn on the back or chest. The four are siblings in A4.1 (the sling bag moved there from A4.2).
- **Tote bag**: "shopper" (and Zalando's "shopper bag") is an alias, not a kind.
- **Duffel bag**: "sports bag" and "Boston bag" are aliases. **Wheeled duffel** = a duffel on a wheeled frame with a telescoping handle (wheels change the form; Samsonite lists "Wheeled Duffels" as its own facet).
- **Laptop bag**: not a kind. A laptop bag is filed by form (messenger bag, briefcase or backpack), with `laptop_size` as an attribute. The laptop backpack stays a kind because CleverJourney compares backpacks at use type.
- **Wristlet**: two kinds with one homonym entry (C9). A **wristlet wallet** (A4.5) has card slots inside and no separate interior compartment; otherwise it is a **wristlet bag** (A4.1).
- **Chain wallet and wallet on chain** are two kinds with one homonym entry. The chain wallet is a wallet fixed to a belt-loop chain; the wallet on chain is a small bag-like object carried on a shoulder or cross-body chain (Louis Vuitton "Nano Bags and Chain Wallets").
- **Named form under two kinds** (Birkin: tote in Wikipedia, top-handle in retail typing): the named form goes under the kind its maker uses for it; where the maker gives none, the first retail category on a fetched maker page. Until the Hermès bag pages are read, the Birkin stays under tote bag as in the memo's source, marked † (OWNER O4).
- **Convertible bags** (Tumi "3-way"; a messenger-backpack hybrid): file by the form the maker names first; the other forms are aliases.

### Candidate 2: carry position first (hand / shoulder / crossbody / back / wheeled / pocket), then object

Levels: 1 carry position (axis: how the owner carries it); 2 kind (silhouette or format); 3 named form. Possible fourth level: object class.
Example: hand-held (clutch, wristlet, briefcase, umbrella, fan); shoulder (shoulder bag, hobo, saddle bag); crossbody (crossbody bag, messenger, sling); back (daypack, hiking backpack, baby carrier); wheeled (cabin suitcase, check-in suitcase, shopping trolley); pocket (bifold wallet, cardholder, coin purse).
Evidence: Baike classifies by carry style (single shoulder, double shoulder, crossbody, hand-carried); Wikipedia Handbag defines crossbody and shoulder by carry.
Where it breaks: Wikipedia's own definitions overlap ("a baguette may be worn crossbody"; "a satchel ... often with a pair of top handles and a shoulder strap"); the Birkin and Kelly are hand-carried and shoulder-carried; a wallet on chain is pocket and crossbody; sunglasses, keyrings and fans have no carry position. Carry position is partly a user choice, close to a modifier (setting, user).

### Candidate 3: use case first (everyday, work, travel, sport, school, evening), then object

Levels: 1 use (axis: situation of use); 2 kind; 3 named form.
Evidence: Zalando's "Business & laptop bags", "Sports bags", "Travel bags & cases", "Clutches & evening bags"; Baike's "by use"; CleverJourney's use types.
Where it breaks: Appendix A makes setting and occasion modifiers. Zalando shows the consequence: a sports bag, a travel bag and a duffel are one object under three labels; a tote serves work, beach and school; evening is an occasion (a clutch is a form); pencil cases and diaper bags sit in the same list as handbags. Use-case labels can still be a generated view, and they remain valid inside a kind where the use sets the spec (laptop backpack; hiking backpack).

Rejected as a level: maker-first (PurseForum's shelving), because maker is a modifier under Appendix A; it is handled in section 6.

## 4. Depth and size

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied (C2(a)):** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. Finer distinctions are named forms (maker-bound or not) or attributes. The number of makers is not the test; comparison is.

- **Handbags: stop at silhouette.** Retailers filter at silhouette (Selfridges: 15 types with counts; Zalando: 12), then by brand (Zalando's first filter). Buyers compare quality and makers at the line (Birkin vs Kelly; Speedy 25 vs 30; PurseBlog's "classics" list). Size (Speedy 25/30; Kelly "from 40 to mini") is an attribute of the named form, not a separate form. "Mini bag" appears as a Selfridges type but is a size, not a silhouette. The silhouette kinds that sources define in overlapping ways carry the form tests in section 3.
- **Backpacks and packs: stop at use type.** CleverJourney compares at use type (what to check first: laptop sleeve, hip belt, underseat dimension). These are differences in designed function, so they can be kinds. Capacity (litres) and laptop size are attributes.
- **Luggage: stop at the suitcase and its size class.** OWNER: whether luggage size class (cabin, check-in, trunk) is a kind (the memo's recommendation; Samsonite, Rimowa and Tumi all lead with it) or an attribute (`size_class: personal | cabin | check-in M | check-in L`, with a generated "carry-on" view, as C6 requires for every other size; the review recommends this). The tree above keeps cabin and check-in suitcase as kinds and would merge them into one "suitcase" kind if the owner chooses the attribute; the trunk stays a kind either way because its form differs. Collections (Original, Essential, Classic; Freeform; Voyageur) are named forms or lines, and quality comparison happens at collection level (Rimowa's Original, Essential and Classic differ by shell material). Hardside vs softside is the contested point: a category at Samsonite and Tumi Japan, a material filter at Rimowa, an outer-material split in HS. OWNER: attribute (recommended) or kind split.
- **Wallets: stop at format.** Wikipedia, Leatherology and LV all divide at bifold/trifold/long/cardholder/zip-around/coin. Leather grade (full grain, top grain) and exotic skins are attributes; lines (Zippy, Bearn, Constance Slim) are named forms.
- **Eyewear: stop at frame shape.** Warby Parker and Optiwar sell by shape; Japan Glasses adds rim structure and materials, which are attributes (acetate, titanium, full/half/rimless). Model names (Ray-Ban New Wayfarer Classic, Aviator Classic) are named forms; reference codes such as RB3025 are variants. Lens spec (polarised, UV) is an attribute. ISO 8624 sizes (lens width x bridge x temple) are an attribute with the standard named (C6), not a named form. OWNER: whether frame shape is a kind shared by name with H2 frames (O3).
- **Umbrellas, fans, baby carriers: stop at type.** besttravelumbrella.com, the Wikipedia fan categories and Ergobaby all compare at the mechanism or wearing method. Automatic opening is a mechanism, an attribute. Regional fans (uchiwa, sensu, tuanshan, zheshan) are not-maker-bound named forms under rigid fan and folding fan (C2(c)). No fetched page compares quality for umbrellas (unverified).
- **Small goods (keyring, coin purse, key pouch):** stop at the object. No source compares them deeper.

The choice level does differ across sub-families: handbags stop above the maker line; luggage stops at size class below which collection matters; eyewear stops at shape.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type: no tier stored yet. A tier scheme enters the data only with a fetched source (C4(e)); until then `tier` stays empty in the A4 ledger.** No spec or standard ranks bags by quality. Attributes carry what can be measured or named.

- **Tier entries (C4(a)):** a tier would sit on a maker, a line or a named form as entries (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`), several per subject with a computed `disputed` flag. It never sits on a kind, and a named form does not inherit its maker's tier: a house can have lines at several tiers. The earlier proposal "luxury / premium / mainstream, one value per maker, inherited" is withdrawn; no fetched page publishes it (†, section 10). OWNER: tier vocabulary for A4 (one domain-wide scale or a scale per category; review decision A7).
- **What the market shows, not a tier:** resale and retail pages sort houses implicitly (Fashionphile's copy leads with Louis Vuitton, Chanel, Hermès, Gucci; Selfridges lists houses and contemporary labels together, from a GBP 36 Adanola tote to a GBP 6,380 Bottega Veneta bag). Fashionphile's and Vestiaire's own segment labels, if any are later fetched, are a dated `platform_tag` (C4(c)).
- **Attributes (C4(b), C6):** leather grade (full grain, top grain; named by Leatherology, no standard cited); shell material (aluminium, polycarbonate, ABS; Rimowa and Samsonite filter by it, so it is an attribute, not a tier); hardside or softside (OWNER, section 4b); size (luggage size and weight bands from Samsonite; frame dimensions to ISO 8624; baby-carrier age range) always with the standard or source named.
- **Price band (C4(d)):** the domain-wide USD scheme (under 50, 50-150, 150-500, 500-1,500, 1,500-5,000, 5,000-15,000, over 15,000), derived from a dated, sourced list price. The Selfridges prices are in GBP and must be converted at a dated rate before banding.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

No leaf is used, so the rules apply inside each sub-family (C7).
- **Kinds inside a sub-family:** canonical form first (the commonest, plainest silhouette first: for example tote, then satchel, top-handle), then derived forms. Reason: Wikipedia, Selfridges and Zalando all list in this way, and no spec scale exists for silhouettes.
- **Kinds in luggage:** by size class from smallest to largest (cabin, check-in, trunk), because Samsonite, Rimowa and Tumi sort by size (if the owner makes size class an attribute, the attribute values keep this order).
- **Named forms inside a kind:** by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as the maker's or a fetched page attests (Speedy 1930s; Chanel 2.55, then Classic Flap 1983), then alphabetically where no year is attested. Not-maker-bound named forms follow the maker-bound ones, in the same year-then-alphabet order.
- **Size of one line** (Speedy 25/30; Kelly mini to 40): an attribute on the named form, ordered by size ascending.
- **Variants** go by `variant_code` or year.

## 9. Boundaries

Closest cross-boundary objects and precedence sentences:

1. **Cases and bags made for one object (A4 vs D4, E2, E3, D2, D1, A5, H2).** A case or bag made to hold one object at a time and travel with it goes to that object's category under S3 (phone case, guitar case, golf bag, camera bag, laptop sleeve, watch strap); a bag made to carry personal items in general stays in A4. Sentences from the review: "A pouch made to hold one phone is D2 under S3; a small bag with a phone pocket is A4." "A backpack made with fitted dividers to carry a camera and its lenses is a camera bag, D4 under S3, which names camera bags; a general backpack with a padded sleeve is A4."
2. **Spectacles and sunglasses (A4 vs H2, A1, E2, D2).** Eyewear that corrects vision goes to H2 whatever its shape, and the same frame shape without correction goes to A4; safety glasses against a hazard go to A1; ski or snow goggles made for a sport go to E2; smart glasses go to D2. Clip-on sun lenses made to fit spectacles are parts of the spectacles (S3) and go to H2 when the spectacles correct vision. Frame shapes are named forms or kinds that can occur in both A4 and H2 and are filed by what the owned object is (OWNER O3). Prescription sunglasses: the key says "correct vision" (H2). OWNER: confirm (O8).
3. **Trunks and chests (A4 vs B1).** A trunk made to travel with the owner goes to A4; a chest or trunk made to stand in a room as storage goes to B1, per S3's "furniture ... never parts" and B1's text "chests and trunks".
4. **Wallets, cards and passports (A4 vs F5, I3, D1, D2).** The wallet, purse, card holder and passport cover go to A4; the passport, identity and payment cards inside go to F5 or I3; a hardware crypto wallet goes to D1; a phone case with a card pocket goes to the phone's category (D2) under S3.
5. **Baby carriers, strollers, diaper bags (A4 vs G1).** Baby carriers and slings go to A4 (A4.8) and prams and strollers go to G1 (key note). A diaper bag is a bag filed by form (tote, backpack) with `user: baby` as a tag, because user age is a modifier (C6).
6. **Pencil cases, toiletry bags, lunch bags, cool bags (A4 vs B5, C5, H1).** Step 17 is tried before steps 18, 30 and 31, so a bag of any kind reaches A4 first: a pencil case (A4.9) and a toiletry or wash bag (A4.3) are A4. For lunch bags and cool bags the B5 text names "food storage and lunch bags". OWNER: adopt "insulated lunch and cool bags are B5" (already logged in _framework_conflicts.md) (O6). Zalando shelves pencil cases among bags.
7. **Pet carriers and horse tack (A4 vs B10).** A carrier made for an animal goes to B10 (key step 13 names carriers), not A4.
8. **Belts, scarves, bag charms, packs and vests (A4 vs A1, A3).** A belt or scarf goes to A1; a belt bag (bum bag, waist bag) is a bag and goes to A4. P5 (from A1): "A bag or luggage of any kind, including a chest rig or waist pack, is A4; a garment of vest form with pockets, such as a fishing vest or hi-vis vest, is A1." P6 (from A1): "Safety glasses against a hazard are A1, fashion sunglasses and other non-corrective eyewear are A4, and spectacles that correct vision are H2." A keyring goes to A4. A bag charm hangs on a bag, not on the body; it is an accessory made for one kind of object, so S3 (step 8) sends it to A4 (A4.10), and A3 agrees. Ornaments worn on body, hair or clothing go to A3.
9. **Fans and umbrellas that stand (A4 vs B7, B1, C2).** A hand fan goes to A4; a powered handheld fan (Wikipedia Hand fan lists battery fans) is open between A4 (hand fan) and B7 (fans); beach and patio umbrellas "built to stand" go outside A4, to B1 or C2 by their use. OWNER: both (O6).
10. **Canes (A4 vs H2).** A cane or walking stick made to support a person with difficulty walking, and a white cane, are H2 (step 2: mobility aids, white canes); a fashion cane carried as a dress accessory is A4 (step 17). Proposed sentence for the framework owner, derived from the key; no source fetched for the fashion cane †.
11. **Luggage scales (A4 vs C5, H1).** The key's C6 note files luggage scales under A4, ahead of bathroom and body scales (H1) and postal scales (C5); recorded in A4.9 †.
12. **Furoshiki and carry cloths (A4 vs B2).** A furoshiki is a cloth for wrapping, not a bag: B2, with an A4 cross-reference. OWNER (O5).
