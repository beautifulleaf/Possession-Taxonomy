## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B3 Lighting (domain B, Home).

**Membership test (CATEGORIES_v0_6, key step 30, fifth branch):** made to give light: lamps, light fittings, bulbs (including smart bulbs), lanterns and torches, sunrise lamps.

**Notes that bind (S1b):** candles, string and fairy lights, lava lamps and neon signs made mainly as decoration are B4 (note at B3; B4's branch also lists decorative lighting, illuminated signs, home fragrance). Sunrise lamps are B3 (note at step 16, which sends smart displays and clock radios to D3). Lanterns are filed by what they do, B3, wherever they are used: "camping" is a setting (note at step 26). A connected version of a household object, such as a smart bulb, is filed by its task: connectivity is a modifier (note at step 24). Photo lighting, flashes and ring lights are D4 (step 24). Grow lights are C2 (step 29). Religious lamps made for a rite are F4 (step 14). Vehicle and bicycle lamps are parts of the vehicle (S3, G1). Ceiling fans, even with lights, are B7 (branch order puts B7 before B3). Long utility lighters, matches and firelighters are C8; pocket lighters are B9. Mains cable, plugs and fuses are C8.

**Appendix A modifiers that apply and so may never be a level:** region and culture, era (antique, vintage, retro), price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, user, setting (indoor, outdoor, camping, office, garden), market (domestic, commercial, professional), installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted), power source and technology (manual, electric, gas, battery, and by extension LED versus halogen), connectivity (smart), occasion, motive, origin, how acquired, material, condition, edition.

**Precedence sentences touching B3:** see section 9. Four of them need owner confirmation because the present key is silent (head torches, dive lights, weapon lights, LED strips).

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS/CN heading 9405** (luminaires): chandeliers and other electric ceiling or wall fittings; table, desk, bedside and floor-standing luminaires; Christmas lighting strings; photovoltaic luminaires; other luminaires and spotlights/searchlights; non-electrical luminaires (lanterns, oil lamps); illuminated signs; parts. Customs then splits by **LED-only versus other** and by **material** (plastic, ceramic, glass): technology and material, which Appendix A bars as levels. [HS]
- **EU Ecodesign** groups light sources into six: LED, HID, LFL, CFL, HL (tungsten halogen) and GLS (classic filament); it notes that decorative filament lamps need no label. [EU] (Search excerpts of Regulation 2019/2015, not fetched, also exempt battery-operated products and bicycle light sources.)
- **Cap and base codes** (Edison screw E10 to E40, bi-pin G4 to G12, GU10 twist-lock, bayonet, wedge, fluorescent G13 and G5) are summarised on [WS], which says the US ANSI C81 standards are harmonised with IEC standards. IEC 60061 (lamp caps and holders) and the ILCOS lamp-coding system (IEC 61231) appeared in search results only and were not fetched.
- **ANSI/NEMA FL1** (flashlights): lumens, beam distance, run time, impact, water resistance; voluntary, makers test themselves. [RF]

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** the mass retailers in six countries divide fittings and lamps by **where the light is mounted or stands** (ceiling, pendant, chandelier, wall, recessed/spot/track, table, floor): [IK] (table and floor) [LW] [LP] [LM] [KK] [CN] [HU], and collectors do the same [1D] [PA]. Bulbs are everywhere a separate tree. Portable lights (torch, headlamp, lantern) are separate at every outdoor retailer [RC] [CO] [OL].
- **Split 1, technology:** customs, EU and Wikipedia put **light-source technology** first [HS] [EU] [WE]; retailers and bulb guides put **cap and shape** first [SF] [GE] [HU]; Lampenwelt does both (technology tree, cap sub-facet) [LW].
- **Split 2, room:** Lampenwelt and Leroy Merlin run **room** as a parallel view over the same objects [LW] [LM]; this is the setting modifier and should be a generated view.
- **Split 3, fuel and battery:** outdoor retailers and forums classify portable lights by **fuel or cell** [CO] (BLF indicatively); Appendix A calls this power source.
- **Split 4, desk lamps:** Home Depot separates the desk lamp as a fixture type; Kakaku joins stand light and desk light; the Met describes the Tizio as a redesign of the standard desk lamp [HD1] [KK] [MET].
- **Split 5, lanterns:** a lantern is portable in camping shops and 1stDibs' Q&A, but also a fixed wall or post fixture in the same shops [CO] [1D] [WL] [LW]; "lantern" is a name that crosses two forms.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **For fittings and lamps, form by placement comes first.** Navigation order at Lampenwelt, Leroy Merlin, Kakaku, Lamps Plus and Pamono is: ceiling / pendant / wall / table / floor / spot, with room, style and maker only as later filters; IKEA's lamp page is table and floor first. [LW] [LM] [KK] [LP] [PA] [IK] The Chinese consumer guide makes the same seven-way split by installation form. [CN]
2. **For bulbs the first decision is the cap (compatibility), then shape, then brightness and colour.** Hue's first technical facet is base type; Screwfix and GE open with fitting and shape codes. [HU] [SF] [GE] The cap is a platform-style constraint, not a quality choice.
3. **For portable lights the first choice is how it is carried** (headlamp, flashlight, lantern), then **performance** (lumens, runtime, beam) and power source (cell, fuel). [RC] [RF] [CO] [SW]
4. **For design and antique lamps the first choice is maker or design**, then type and period. [PA] [1D] [CW]
5. Quality is a second-stage question everywhere: spec (lumens, candela, IP, run time) for torches [RF] [GJ], energy and brightness for bulbs [HU], maker and period for collectors [1D].

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): placement-and-role, then mount or carry, then kind, then named form

| Level | Axis | Stated |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | the role the object has in lighting: fixed fitting, standing lamp, carried light, light source, lamp part | what the owner handles |
| Leaf | for the first three: **where it mounts, stands or is carried** (ceiling, wall, recessed and track, ground; table and desk, floor; hand, head, lantern, work light); for light sources: **family by shape and cap form** (cap-screw/bayonet general bulbs, reflector and spot, tubes and discharge) | placement or family |
| Kind | the generic type buyers compare (pendant light, desk lamp, headlamp, candle bulb) | form |
| Named form | models and iconic designs (Tizio, Arco, Akari, Dietz Monarch, Coleman Lantern) | design or model |

Technology (LED, halogen, incandescent), cap code, fuel, battery cell, smart, IP rating, style, material and room are **tags and facets**, never levels. A cap-and-standard table (E27, E14, GU10, B22, G9 ...) is a reference ledger linked to bulb kinds and to fittings that accept them (section 6).

### Candidate B: light-source technology first (as customs, EU and Wikipedia do)

Levels: incandescent / halogen / discharge / LED / fuel flame, then form. Evidence for: [HS] [EU] [WE]. Breaks: (1) a retrofit LED A60 and a 1900 filament A60 are the same buyer object; (2) technology is an Appendix A modifier; (3) fixtures with integrated LED have no replaceable source; (4) fuel lamps and Edison bulbs would sit in unrelated places although collectors group them as lamps. [CW] Not recommended as the primary axis; keep technology as a tag and as the Lampenwelt-style view over bulbs. [LW]

### Candidate C: function or room first (ambient, task, accent, decorative; or living room, bath, outdoor)

Evidence for: Lampenwelt and Leroy Merlin room views [LW] [LM]; desk lamps defined by task [MET]. Breaks: function and room are use and setting, so they are modifiers; the same floor lamp is ambient in one house and a reading light in another; a room tree would give each object several homes (S1). Keep as generated views.

### Where candidate A breaks (edge objects)

1. **Dual-mount lamp**, e.g. an LED "table/wall lamp" sold as both [IK]: needs a tie rule (section 9).
2. **Clamp lamp**: a desk lamp that clips to a surface [HD1] versus a clamp work light with guard and hook [HD2].
3. **Lantern**, which is a fixed wall or post fixture in one shop and a camping light in the next [LW] [WL] [1D]: file by mount or carry, never by the word.
4. **Cordless rechargeable table lamp** and **plug-in night light** [LM]: stands or plugs in place, so power source (modifier) must not move it to portable.
5. **Akari light sculptures** [NG]: lamps designed by a sculptor, in ceiling, floor and table forms: B3 under S5, not F2.
6. **Spotlight**: the word names a fitting (ceiling spot, track head) and a bulb (GU10 spot) [LW] [SF] [HU]: two kinds.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Maximum depth below B3:** four (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). A fifth level is never needed; sizes (A19/A60), caps, outputs and materials are attributes.
- **Generic kinds:** about 90 to 110 (est.): fittings about 30, standing lamps about 12, carried lights about 25, light sources about 22, lamp parts about 8, plus rarely owned historic and professional kinds.
- **Named forms (models, designs):** about 1,000 to 3,000 (est.). Akari alone has over 200 models [NG]; Dietz produced hundreds of lantern models [DZ]; design classics number a few hundred [PA] [1D]; modern flashlight and headlamp models turn over yearly [GJ] [SW] [OL]. Cap codes (about 60 in common use, est. from [WS]) and bulb shape codes (about 40 [GE]) are **attributes and a reference ledger**, not named forms.
- **Where depth concentrates:** (1) design and antique lamps (table, desk, floor, pendant) where the named form carries identity [PA] [1D] [CW]; (2) portable lights, where the class (EDC, duty, headlamp) and a spec ladder set the choice [TT] [SW]; (3) historic and collector lanterns [DZ]; (4) bulbs, where the cap and shape code carry the choice [GE] [SF].
- **Broad leaves:** table lamp, floor lamp, pendant light, flush ceiling light, general-service bulb, pocket flashlight, headlamp. **Medium:** chandelier, wall light, spotlight, track system, desk lamp, electric lantern, reflector bulb, tubes. **Narrow:** miner's lamp, keychain light, floodlight kinds, linear halogen, lamp cord set.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers.

- **Fittings and lamps:** kind = the placement-defined type that retailers and "best X" lists use (pendant light, wall light, table lamp, floor lamp, desk lamp, chandelier). Evidence: category pages at Lampenwelt, Leroy Merlin, Kakaku and Lamps Plus [LW] [LM] [KK] [LP]; maker and designer filters sit at that level on Lampenwelt and Pamono ("Artemide Table Lamps", "Louis Poulsen" in a fitting category) [LW] [PA]. Finer splits (arc, tripod, globe pendant, mushroom, balanced-arm) are **named forms or attributes**: Home Depot's own facets name Style, Lamp Design and Lamp Type below the Fixture Type [HD1]. **Desk lamp stays a separate kind** because Home Depot lists it as its own fixture type and the Met describes it by task function; buyers compare ergonomics and light quality [HD1] [MET]. Choice level differs here: for table lamps the named form is the main object of interest; for flush ceiling lights it is not (makers are incidental) [PA] [LW].
- **Portable lights:** kind = the class buyers rank separately: pocket flashlight, duty/tactical flashlight, penlight, keychain light, headlamp, electric lantern, fuel lantern. Evidence: Tak Tactical ranks three classes separately [TT]; Switchback ranks headlamps by sub-use and lists them against a separate spec table [SW]; REI and Coleman put lantern, flashlight and headlamp in separate buying guides [RC] [RF] [CO]. Running, ultralight and caving headlamps are **named forms or an attribute** (use), not kinds. Battery cell (18650/21700) is a facet; enthusiasts organise threads by it (indicatively, from search excerpts of [BLF]) but it is a compatibility attribute.
- **Bulbs:** kind = shape family as retailers and guides compare (general-service, candle, globe, filament, reflector, spot, capsule, tube); **cap code and technology are facets**, with the cap as a hard compatibility filter [SF] [GE] [HU]. Tubes and discharge lamps are kinds because buyers cannot swap them for each other without a fitting change [EU] [WS].
- **Lamp parts:** kind = the part sold alone (shade, base, cord set, holder) [IK].
- Sub-family choice levels differ: **deep** for design lamps and collector lanterns (maker and model compared), **medium** for portable lights (class and spec compared), **shallow** for mass ceiling lights, general bulbs and lamp parts.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type: spec-ranked for portable lights and bulbs; preference (price band) for fittings; consensus (collector and museum) for named design forms. State the dominant type as spec-ranked.**

- **Portable lights:** measurable ladder defined by ANSI/NEMA FL1 (output, beam distance, run time, impact, water) [RF]; reviewers add price tiers (budget under $30, mid-tier $30 to $90, premium over $90) and note that battery, LED and weatherproofing improve above about $70 [GJ]; headlamps are compared by lumens, weight, beam and IP rating [SW]; output bands run keychain 1-20 lm to high-powered 1000+ [WF].
- **Bulbs:** lumens and colour (Hue's brightness and light-colour facets) [HU]; the EU ecodesign and labelling regime ranks light-source efficiency [EU] (the A-to-G class scale is stated in EU Regulation 2019/2015 as seen in a search excerpt only; fetch it before use).
- **Fittings and lamps:** no recognised tier; IP rating, material and price band only. Design classics are ranked by the collector and museum market (the Tizio is in the Met collection [MET]); that is a maker and edition matter, not a tier.
- **Where it shows:** a tier or grade **modifier** on the kind or named form (e.g. FL1 output class, energy class, price band), never a level.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended: identity for design and collector lighting and for tactical/EDC torches; platform for bulbs and smart lighting; incidental for mass fittings.** Lampenwelt's brand exclusion list names about 55 makers (Anglepoise, Artemide, Flos, Louis Poulsen, Tom Dixon, Foscarini, Ingo Maurer, Philips Hue and others) [LW]; Pamono and 1stDibs sell by maker and designer [PA] [1D]; forums organise around makers (McGizmo, Peak, Fenix, Nitecore, Olight) [CPF] [GJ] [OL]; collectors index by Dietz, Coleman, Aladdin, Tiffany [DZ] [CW] [1D]. **Platform:** cap standards (E27, GU10 ...) and smart ecosystems (Hue base types and smart bulbs) set compatibility [HU] [WS].

**Maker ledger: yes.** Columns: maker, known-for kinds, scene tags (Bauhaus, Mid-Century, Space Age, tactical, camping), tier (spec or price band), price band, country and era, attestation URL. Example candidate rows (all named on fetched pages): Louis Poulsen, Artemide, Flos, Anglepoise, Oluce, Stilnovo, Kaiser Idell, Philips, IKEA [LW] [PA]; Fenix, Nitecore, Olight, Streamlight, SureFire, Nightstick [GJ] [TT] [OL]; Petzl, Black Diamond, BioLite, Coast [SW]; Coleman, Dietz, Aladdin [CO] [DZ] [CW].

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Kinds inside a leaf:** canonical form first (the most common retailer type), then variants, then rare historic kinds. Reason: every retailer lists the common type first and collectors treat historic types as sub-collections. [IK] [LW] [CW]
- **Named forms inside a kind:** chronology by design year, which is how the museum record and Pamono present them (Kamden 1928, Kaiser Idell 1931, Eclisse 1965, Tizio 1972) [PA] [MET].
- **Bulbs and caps:** spec order, large cap to small (E40, E27, E14, E10; then bi-pin by spacing) [WS].
- **Portable lights:** by class size and output (keychain, penlight, pocket, duty, search) as in the Wikipedia output bands [WF].

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (each follows CATEGORIES_v0_6 S1 order and S3):

1. **B3 versus B4.** A lamp is B3 whatever its ornament (Akari, Tiffany-style, andon); a candle, string or fairy light, lava lamp, neon sign or flameless candle made mainly as decoration is B4, and an unlit decorative lantern shell or festival lantern kept for display is B4 (occasion is a modifier). [NG] [CW] [WC]
2. **B3 versus D4.** Photo lighting, flashes and ring lights are D4; a work, desk or clip lamp not made for photography stays B3, and a continuous light sold for stills or video is D4.
3. **B3 versus C2.** A grow light or plant lamp is C2 (step 29 precedes step 30); a general-purpose full-spectrum lamp not sold for plants is B3.
4. **B3 versus G1 under S3.** Vehicle and bicycle headlamps, tail lights and car bulbs are G1 as parts of the vehicle (EU labelling rules also set bicycle light sources apart, per a search excerpt of Regulation 2019/2015 that was not fetched); a handheld torch or a headlamp worn by a cyclist stays B3.
5. **B3 versus B7.** A ceiling fan with a light kit and a bathroom heater-light are B7 (the climate branch comes first); a lamp that merely warms (clamp heat lamp) is B7 if sold as a heater and B3 if sold as a work light. [HD2]
6. **B3 versus F4.** A lamp made for a rite (menorah, diya, sanctuary lamp, Shabbat lamp) is F4; a plain oil or candle lamp is B3. [WC]
7. **B3 versus E2 and the E-gear gap (owner confirmation).** Lanterns are B3 wherever used (note at step 26); the key is silent on head torches, dive lights and weapon-mounted lights. Recommended: headlamps and head torches are B3 like lanterns; dive lights are B3 (waterproof is a feature, not a sport); a weapon-mounted light follows the weapon under S3 (E4 per the rifle-scope note). [SW] [WF] [TT]
8. **B3 versus H2.** A lamp sold to treat a condition (seasonal-affective light therapy) is H2 (step 2); a sunrise or wake-up lamp is B3 by the note, even when it carries a radio.
9. **B3 versus S3 for special-purpose bulbs.** A general-service bulb is B3 (the key names bulbs); a bulb made for one appliance (fridge, oven, sewing-machine, projector) follows that appliance, a projector lamp goes with the projector in D3 (S3). This is the researcher's reading of S3, not a source claim.
10. **B3 versus C8.** Lamp-specific parts (shade, base, cord set, holder) follow the lamp (S3, B3); mains cable, plugs, switches, fuses and ceiling roses sold as electrical supplies are C8.
11. **Tie rule for dual-mount lamps.** An object made to be used in two placements is filed under the first placement in its generic name ("table/wall lamp" is a table lamp). [IK]

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** category B3, sub-family (role: fixed fitting, standing lamp, carried light, light source, lamp part), leaf (placement, carry, or source family), kind (the generic type buyers compare), named form (model or iconic design). **Maximum depth four levels below B3.** Technology, cap, fuel, cell, smart, IP, style, material, room, era, region and price band are tags, facets or generated views; a maker ledger and a cap-and-standard table are separate tables.
