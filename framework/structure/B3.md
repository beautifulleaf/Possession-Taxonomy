## 0. Category

**Code and name:** B3 Lighting (domain B, Home).

**Membership test (CATEGORIES_v0_7, key step 30, B3 branch):** made to give light: lamps, light fittings, bulbs (including smart bulbs), lanterns and torches, sunrise lamps.

**Notes that bind (S1b):** candles, string and fairy lights, lava lamps and neon signs made mainly as decoration are B4 (note at B3; B4's branch also lists decorative lighting, illuminated signs, home fragrance). Sunrise lamps are B3 (note at step 16, which sends smart displays and clock radios to D3). Lanterns are filed by what they do, B3, wherever they are used: "camping" is a setting (note at step 26). A connected version of a household object, such as a smart bulb, is filed by its task: connectivity is a modifier (note at step 24). Photo lighting, flashes and ring lights are D4 (step 24). Grow lights are C2 (step 29). Religious lamps made for a rite are F4 (step 14). Vehicle and bicycle lamps are parts of the vehicle (S3, G1). Ceiling fans, even with lights, are B7 (branch order puts B7 before B3). Long utility lighters, matches and firelighters are C8; pocket lighters are B9. Mains cable, plugs and fuses are C8; so are ceiling roses sold as electrical supplies (B-18). Decided since the first draft: head torches and dive lights are B3 like lanterns, a weapon-mounted light is E4 and a toy light is E1 (note at step 17, K-162); stage lighting is B3 (note at step 22, K-145); a light strip sold to light a space is B3, and string, fairy and novelty lights made mainly as decoration are B4 (K-181); an open holder for a candle is B4, an enclosed lantern with a handle made to be carried as a light is B3, and a lantern made to stand or hang as decoration is B4 (K-180); a Japanese stone lantern is B4 (K-116); candles made for a rite and grave lights are F4 (K-113).

**Appendix A modifiers that apply and so may never be a level:** region and culture, era (antique, vintage, retro), price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene and subculture, user, setting (indoor, outdoor, camping, office, garden), market (domestic, commercial, professional), installation (freestanding, built-in, fitted), power source and technology (manual, electric, gas, battery, and by extension LED versus halogen), connectivity (smart), occasion, motive, origin, how acquired, material, condition, edition. Decided attribute additions: `fuel` (a tag on the placement kinds for non-electric lamps) and `emergency` (an attribute on any kind), both attributes and never levels (B-18).

**Precedence sentences touching B3:** see section 9. The four that were open in the first draft (head torches, dive lights, weapon lights, LED strips) are now in the key (K-162, K-181); sections 9.14 to 9.16 add the sentences decided since.

- **Split 2, room:** Lampenwelt and Leroy Merlin run **room** as a parallel view over the same objects [LW] [LM]; this is the setting modifier and should be a generated view.
- **Split 3, fuel and battery:** outdoor retailers and forums classify portable lights by **fuel or cell** [CO] (BLF indicatively); Appendix A calls this power source.
- **Split 4, desk lamps:** Home Depot separates the desk lamp as a fixture type; Kakaku joins stand light and desk light; the Met describes the Tizio as a redesign of the standard desk lamp [HD1] [KK] [MET].
- **Split 5, lanterns:** a lantern is portable in camping shops and 1stDibs' Q&A, but also a fixed wall or post fixture in the same shops [CO] [1D] [WL] [LW]; "lantern" is a name that crosses two forms.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **For fittings and lamps, form by placement comes first.** Navigation order at Lampenwelt, Leroy Merlin, Kakaku, Lamps Plus and Pamono is: ceiling / pendant / wall / table / floor / spot, with room, style and maker only as later filters; IKEA's lamp page is table and floor first. [LW] [LM] [KK] [LP] [PA] [IK] The Chinese consumer guide makes the same seven-way split by installation form. [CN]
2. **For bulbs the first decision is the cap (compatibility), then shape, then brightness and colour.** Hue's first technical facet is base type; Screwfix and GE open with fitting and shape codes. [HU] [SF] [GE] The cap is a platform-style constraint, not a quality choice.
3. **For portable lights the first choice is how it is carried** (headlamp, flashlight, lantern), then **performance** (lumens, runtime, beam) and power source (cell, fuel). [RC] [RF] [CO] [SW]
4. **For design and antique lamps the first choice is maker or design**, then type and period. [PA] [1D] [CW]
5. Quality is a second-stage question everywhere: spec (lumens, candela, IP, run time) for torches [RF] [GJ], energy and brightness for bulbs [HU], maker and period for collectors [1D].

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): placement-and-role, then mount or carry, then kind, then named form

| Level | Axis | Stated |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | the role the object has in lighting: fixed fitting, standing lamp, carried light, light source, lamp part | what the owner handles |
| Leaf | for the first three: **where it mounts, stands or is carried** (ceiling, wall, recessed and track, ground; table and desk, floor; hand, head, lantern, work light); for light sources: **shape and cap form** (cap-screw/bayonet general bulbs, reflector, spot and capsule bulbs, forms that need their own holder or ballast, strips); for lamp parts: no leaf (about six kinds, C5(b)) | placement, carry or shape and cap form |
| Kind | the generic type buyers compare (pendant light, desk lamp, headlamp, candle bulb) | form |
| Named form | models and iconic designs (Tizio, Arco, Akari ceiling shade, Dietz Monarch, Coleman Lantern), each recorded as `maker_bound: yes` or `no` (C2(a)) | design or model |

Technology (LED, halogen, incandescent), cap code, fuel, battery cell, smart, IP rating, style, material and room are **attributes, tags and facets**, never levels (rule T, C10; Appendix A). Where makers or standards set compatibility, the cap codes (E27, E14, GU10, B22, G9 ...) and the smart-lighting ecosystems go in the **compatibility ledger** (C13, section 6), linked to bulb kinds and to the fittings that accept them. Placement is a level here under rule M (C12): the fittings are made only for their mount and their bodies differ (pendant against floor lamp), so mounting is not the Appendix A "installation" modifier for these kinds. Rule M is decided (CV-28; B-4: B3 by role, then placement), so the leaf axis stands as written. Non-electric lamps (oil, gas, candle) are these same placement kinds with a `fuel` tag, and an emergency or power-cut light is the same kind with an `emergency` attribute (B-18).

### Candidate B: light-source technology first (as customs, EU and Wikipedia do)

Levels: incandescent / halogen / discharge / LED / fuel flame, then form. Evidence for: [HS] [EU] [WE]. Breaks: (1) a retrofit LED A60 and a 1900 filament A60 are the same buyer object; (2) technology is an Appendix A modifier; (3) fixtures with integrated LED have no replaceable source; (4) fuel lamps and Edison bulbs would sit in unrelated places although collectors group them as lamps. [CW] Not adopted as the primary axis (rule T, CV-24; B-4: bulbs by shape family). Technology is an attribute (C10), and the EU six-group light-source classification and the HS 9405 headings go in the **jurisdiction ledger** (C17, section 6), not on the tree. The Lampenwelt-style technology view over bulbs is a generated view. [LW]

### Candidate C: function or room first (ambient, task, accent, decorative; or living room, bath, outdoor)

Evidence for: Lampenwelt and Leroy Merlin room views [LW] [LM]; desk lamps defined by task [MET]. Breaks: function and room are use and setting, so they are modifiers; the same floor lamp is ambient in one house and a reading light in another; a room tree would give each object several homes (S1). Keep as generated views.

### Where candidate A breaks (edge objects) and how each is settled

1. **Dual-mount lamp**, e.g. an LED "table/wall lamp" sold as both [IK]: tie rule in section 9.11 (first placement in the generic name). Where one body is offered with different fixings (clamp, wall plate, stand), the fixing is an installation attribute (rule M, C12).
2. **Clamp lamp**: a desk lamp that clips to a surface is a **desk lamp** with `installation = clamp` [HD1]; a clamp work light with guard and hook, sold as commercial lighting, is the kind **clamp work light** [HD2] (section 9.13).
3. **Lantern**, a fixed wall or post fixture in one shop and a camping light in the next [LW] [WL] [1D]: filed by mount or carry, never by the word. An enclosed lantern with a handle, made to be carried as a light, is B3; a lantern made to stand or hang as decoration is B4 (K-180). The kind labels are kept apart ("Wall-mounted lantern light" against the carried lantern kinds) and the word is a homonym in the alias table (section 6).
4. **Cordless rechargeable table lamp** and **plug-in night light** [LM]: stands or plugs in place, so power source (an attribute) must not move it to the carried lights.
5. **Akari light sculptures** [NG]: lamps designed by a sculptor, in ceiling, floor and table forms: B3 as the object is made (sold by the museum shop as lamps in ceiling, floor and table forms [NG]; S5), not F2. The free-standing Akari models are the test case of F-12 (sculpture-lamps are F2; section 9.14), provisional until attested †. The Akari line (maker ledger, lines table) spans the pendant light, floor lamp and table lamp kinds; each member is a named form under its own kind (C1). The Akari ceiling pieces are hanging shades, so they sit under **Pendant light**, not Flush ceiling light [NG].
6. **Spotlight**: the word names a fitting (ceiling spot, track head) and a bulb (GU10 spot) [LW] [SF] [HU]: two kinds, labelled "Spot fitting" and "Spot bulb", with "spotlight" as a homonym in the alias table.
7. **Balanced-arm lamp on a base** (Tizio type): a desk lamp, not a table lamp (section 9.13).

## 4. Depth and size

- **Maximum depth below B3:** four (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form), the C5(a) limit. A fifth level is never needed; sizes (A19/A60), caps, outputs and materials are attributes (C6). Lamp parts skip the leaf (five kinds, C5(b); the closing parts sub-family states its leaf axis, the object served, C5(d)); the other sub-families keep leaves because each leaf has a different stated axis (C5(c)).
- **Generic kinds:** about 80 to 100 (est.): fittings about 28, standing lamps about 10, carried lights about 20, light sources about 18, lamp parts about 8, plus rarely owned historic and professional kinds. The earlier estimate (90 to 110) counted tube, flashlight and solar kinds separately.
- **Named forms (models, designs):** about 1,000 to 3,000 (est.; an estimate, not a cap, CV-7). Akari alone has over 200 models [NG]; Dietz produced hundreds of lantern models [DZ]; design classics number a few hundred [PA] [1D]; modern flashlight and headlamp models turn over yearly [GJ] [SW] [OL]. Cap codes (about 60 in common use, est. from [WS]) and bulb shape codes (about 40 [GE]) are **attributes and compatibility-ledger rows**, not named forms. Named forms have no cap and are not loaded on demand only (CV-7). Large sets (Akari under pendant light, floor lamp and table lamp; Dietz under hurricane lantern; handheld flashlight models) live in registers keyed by kind (C2(g), CV-45), uncapped, and the tree shows examples only; the "about 50" trigger for opening a register is this memo's rule of thumb (applied by default, section 12).
- **Where depth concentrates:** (1) design and antique lamps (table, desk, floor, pendant) where the named form carries identity [PA] [1D] [CW]; (2) portable lights, where the spec ladder sets the choice [GJ] [SW]; (3) historic and collector lanterns [DZ]; (4) bulbs, where the cap and shape code carry the choice [GE] [SF].
- **Broad leaves:** table lamp, floor lamp, pendant light, flush ceiling light, general-service bulb, handheld flashlight, headlamp. **Medium:** chandelier, wall light, spot fitting, track system, desk lamp, electric lantern, reflector bulb, linear tube lamp. **Narrow:** miner's lamp, keychain light, floodlight, lamp cord set.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule (C2(a)):** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers; a named form is a specific design below it, either maker-bound or not.

- **Fittings and lamps:** kind = the placement-defined type that retailers and "best X" lists use (pendant light, wall light, table lamp, floor lamp, desk lamp, chandelier). Evidence: category pages at Lampenwelt, Leroy Merlin, Kakaku and Lamps Plus [LW] [LM] [KK] [LP]; maker and designer filters sit at that level on Lampenwelt and Pamono ("Artemide Table Lamps", "Louis Poulsen" in a fitting category) [LW] [PA]. Finer splits (arc, tripod, globe pendant, mushroom, balanced-arm) are **named forms or attributes**: Home Depot's own facets name Style, Lamp Design and Lamp Type below the Fixture Type [HD1]. **Desk lamp stays a separate kind** because Home Depot lists it as its own fixture type and the Met describes it by task function; buyers compare ergonomics and light quality [HD1] [MET]. Choice level differs here: for table lamps the named form is the main object of interest; for flush ceiling lights it is not (makers are incidental) [PA] [LW]. Mounting defines these kinds under rule M(a), because each is made only for its mount and its body differs (C12). Stage lighting fixtures (K-145) are one kind for now, rigged on a truss, bar or stand for the professional market; the fixture types stay named forms until a fetched catalogue compares them separately (C2(a), C10) †.
- **Portable lights:** kind = the form buyers compare: handheld flashlight, penlight, keychain light, clip-on light, handheld searchlight, headlamp, the lantern kinds, the work lights. Evidence: REI, Coleman and Olight keep flashlight, headlamp and lantern apart [RC] [RF] [CO] [OL]; Switchback ranks headlamps against a separate spec table [SW]; GearJunkie ranks flashlights as one list against one spec table (lumens, beam distance, runtime, cell) [GJ]. The pocket and duty classes of one retailer blog overlap in output (200 to 1,500 lm and 1,000 to 2,500 lm) and rest on a single source, and "tactical" is a scene word, so they are **not kinds**: output class, carry size and cell (18650 and 21700 are compatibility attributes, indicative from search excerpts of [BLF]) are attributes of the handheld flashlight, and EDC and tactical are scene tags [TT] [WF] (review B3-2). Running, ultralight and caving headlamps are named forms or an attribute (use), not kinds [SW]. Lanterns: the mantle, wick (hurricane) and candle lanterns stay kinds because the burning mechanism changes the object and the sources rank them apart [DZ] [CO] [RC]; battery, rechargeable and solar lanterns are one kind, electric lantern, with power source as an attribute (rule T, C10).
- **Bulbs:** kind = shape family as retailers and guides compare (general-service, candle, globe, reflector, spot, capsule); **cap code, technology, filament style, connectivity and colour are attributes**, with the cap as a hard compatibility filter held in the compatibility ledger [SF] [GE] [HU]. Three kinds are defined by form-and-fitting under rule T(b): the linear tube lamp (its own holder and cap; fluorescent, LED and halogen versions are one kind, because the buyer's object is the same tube), the compact fluorescent lamp (B3-O1, rule T(b); provisional until attested †) and the high-intensity discharge lamp (ballast †) [EU] [WS]. EU Ecodesign's six groups of light source are recorded in the jurisdiction ledger, not as kinds [EU].
- **Lamp parts:** kind = the part sold alone (shade, base, cord set, holder) [IK].
- Sub-family choice levels differ: **deep** for design lamps and collector lanterns (maker and model compared), **medium** for portable lights (form and spec compared), **shallow** for mass ceiling lights, general bulbs and lamp parts.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type: spec-ranked for portable lights and bulbs; preference (price band) for fittings; consensus (collector and museum) for named design forms. State the dominant type as spec-ranked.**

- **Rule (C4, CV-10 to CV-12).** A tier never sits on a kind. It sits on a maker, line, named form or, under C4(b), a variant or plan row, as a tier entry (`tier_entry_id`, `subject_id`, `subject_type`, `scheme_id`, `value`, `rank`, `direction`, `scope`, `test_version`, `stale_after`, `source_url`, `date`, `disputed` computed). A scheme enters on one fetched source; a second source or region only adds weight (the `disputed` flag and the scheme's weight in views) and is never a condition of entry (C4(e), CV-10). Every scale is registered once in the scheme registry and typed by one of the six classes of C4(c).
- **Class (i), spec or certification measured on the object (an attribute with its standard named, never a tier entry).** Portable lights: output, beam distance, run time, impact and water resistance as defined by ANSI/NEMA FL1, a voluntary standard that makers test themselves [RF] (held as `standard_met` = ANSI/NEMA FL1); IP rating, weight and beam for headlamps [SW]; output bands keychain 1-20 lm to high-powered 1000+ [WF]; lumens bands 1-200 to 801-1000 [CO]. Bulbs: lumens and colour (Hue's brightness and light-colour facets) [HU]. The EU energy class (A to G) is a measured class named in a regulation; it is stored as an attribute with the regulation named only after the regulation is fetched † (EU Regulation 2019/2015 was seen in a search excerpt only).
- **Class (v), price-defined band (the price band only, never a tier).** GearJunkie's labels budget under $30, mid-tier $30 to $90 and premium over $90 [GJ] are defined by price, so they are the C4(d) price band and nothing else (the first draft held them as a dated platform tag; the conventions class them as price-defined). The source also says battery, LED and weatherproofing improve above about $70.
- **Class (iii), editorial or community class.** Reviewer lists and grades (GearJunkie's and Switchback Travel's picks and spec tables [GJ] [SW]) may enter at harvest as tier entries on named forms, scheme = the reviewer, one fetched source each, with `test_version` and `stale_after`. None is stored in this memo.
- **Class (iv), maker's own ladder.** A maker's order of its own lines is `line_rank` in the lines table, and a range level may be a tier entry with scheme = the maker's name (CV-12) where a fetched maker page states it; none is stored yet.
- **Class (ii), third-party measured score.** None found for B3 yet; a laboratory score for a flashlight or bulb would enter as a numeric tier entry with `test_version` on one fetched source.
- **Class (vi), a shop's or platform's own label.** Dated `platform_tag` (for example a 1stDibs or Pamono style tag), never a tier.
- **Fittings and lamps.** No recognised tier scheme was found; IP rating and material are attributes, and the price band is the domain scheme. Design classics are ranked by the collector and museum market (the Tizio is in the Met collection [MET]); that is a maker and edition matter. A tier entry on a variant (a re-issue, a limited edition) enters only under C4(b).
- **Price band (C4(d)).** The one domain-wide USD scheme as written: 0 (free), under 50, 50–150, 150–500, 500–1,500, 1,500–5,000, 5,000–15,000, 15,000–50,000, 50,000–150,000, 150,000–500,000, 500,000–1,500,000, 1,500,000–5,000,000, over 5,000,000. Bands that B3 never reaches stay unused. A band is derived from a dated, sourced list price on the maker, line or named form (on maker and line rows where objects are factory-built); an antique or second-hand lamp has no list price and carries no band.
- **Not stored (C16).** Condition, provenance, rarity code and serial numbers of one particular lamp or lantern. The 1stDibs condition grades [1D] and the Dietz rarity code [DZ] are rows in the grading-scale registry (`scale_id`, `name`, `owner`, `scale_type`, `steps`, `kinds_served`, `sources`), typed as the registry allows (condition grade; platform label) and linked to kinds; no grade value is ever stored.
- **Where it shows.** Attributes on the kind or named form (FL1 output class, energy class, IP), the price band on the maker, line or named form, tier entries on makers, lines, named forms and (C4(b)) variants; never a level and never a tier on a kind.

## 8. Order inside a leaf (C7, CV-20, CV-21)

- **Kinds inside a leaf:** canonical form first (the most common retailer type), then the secondary key the sub-family states, then rare historic kinds. Fittings and standing lamps: the secondary key is typical size or complexity (flush, then pendant, then chandelier). Portable lights: class size and output as in the Wikipedia output bands (keychain, penlight, hand torch, searchlight) [WF]. Bulbs: the shape-code order of [GE] (A, candle, globe, reflector, spot, capsule). Reason: every retailer lists the common type first and collectors treat historic types as sub-collections. [IK] [LW] [CW]
- **Named forms inside a kind (CV-21):** non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested (Kamden 1928, Kaiser Idell 1931, Eclisse 1965, Tizio 1972 [PA] [MET]), then alphabetically where no year is attested. Registers keep the same order.
- **Variants:** by `variant_code` or year.
- **Generated views, not stored order:** maker ladders (`line_rank`), newest-first, and spec-ladder or benchmark orders (lumens, run time).
- **Compatibility-ledger rows (caps):** spec order, large cap to small (E40, E27, E14, E10; then bi-pin by spacing) [WS].

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (each follows CATEGORIES_v0_7 S1 order and S3):

1. **B3 versus B4.** A lamp is B3 whatever its ornament (Akari, Tiffany-style, andon). A candle, string or fairy light, lava lamp, neon sign or flameless candle made mainly as decoration is B4 (key note), and an unlit decorative lantern shell or festival lantern kept for display is B4 (occasion is a modifier). [NG] [CW] [WC] Decided (K-180, K-181; review C-7 rows 8 and 9): an open holder for a candle (candlestick, candelabrum, tealight holder, wall sconce for candles) is B4; an enclosed lantern with a handle, made to be carried as a light, is B3; a lantern made to stand or hang as decoration is B4; a light strip sold to light a space (under-cabinet, cove) is B3 (strip light kind), and string, fairy and novelty lights made mainly as decoration are B4. Under K-180 a rush light, bonbori or chochin is B4 unless made to be carried as a light; the stone toro is B4 (K-116).
2. **B3 versus D4.** Photo lighting, flashes and ring lights are D4; a work, desk or clip lamp not made for photography stays B3, and a continuous light sold for stills or video is D4. Studio lights, stands and flashes are D4 (K-17).
3. **B3 versus C2.** A grow light or plant lamp is C2 (step 29 precedes step 30); a general-purpose full-spectrum lamp not sold for plants is B3. Grow lights are one C2 kind with lamp technology an attribute (C-14).
4. **B3 versus G1 under S3.** Vehicle and bicycle headlamps, tail lights and car bulbs are G1 as parts of the vehicle (EU labelling rules also set bicycle light sources apart, per a search excerpt of Regulation 2019/2015 that was not fetched †); a handheld torch or a headlamp worn by a cyclist stays B3.
5. **B3 versus B7.** A ceiling fan with a light kit and a bathroom heater-light are B7 (the climate branch comes first; review C-7 row 28 agrees); a lamp that merely warms (clamp heat lamp) is B7 if sold as a heater (B7 lists the infrared heat lamp, row 29) and B3 if sold as a work light. [HD2]
6. **B3 versus F4.** A lamp made for a rite (menorah, diya, sanctuary lamp, Shabbat lamp) is F4; a plain oil or candle lamp is B3. Candles made for a rite or devotion, grave lights included, are F4 (K-113); a lantern made for Obon to welcome the dead (bon-chōchin) is F4 (B4-O2; provisional until attested: sold as memorial goods †). [WC]
7. **B3 versus E2, E4 and the E-gear gap (K-162, decided).** Lanterns are B3 wherever used (note at step 26); head torches and dive lights are B3 like lanterns (note at step 17); a weapon-mounted light is E4; a toy light is E1 (C15). The memo files headlamps and head torches as one kind, and a dive light as a handheld flashlight with a water or depth rating attribute (waterproof is a feature, not a sport; applied by default, section 12). [SW] [WF] [TT]
8. **B3 versus H2.** A lamp sold to treat a condition (seasonal-affective light therapy) is H2 (step 2); a sunrise or wake-up lamp is B3 by the note, even when it carries a radio.
9. **B3 versus S3 for special-purpose bulbs (B-17, decided).** A general-service bulb is B3 (the key names bulbs); a bulb made for one appliance (fridge, oven, sewing-machine, projector) follows that appliance (S3), and a projector lamp goes with the projector in D3. Aquarium and vivarium lamps go with B10 (S3; review C-7 row 34 now agrees). This is the reading of S3 adopted by B-17, not a source claim.
10. **B3 versus C8 (B-18, decided).** Lamp-specific parts (shade, base, cord set, holder) follow the lamp (S3, B3); a lamp cord set, a pendant cord set included, is B3; mains cable, plugs, switches, fuses and ceiling roses sold as electrical supplies are C8. A battery or charger made for one flashlight follows it (S3; K-4, K-3); a loose standard cell, 18650 and 21700 included, is C8, and a generic charger is D1.
11. **Tie rule for dual-mount lamps.** An object made to be used in two placements is filed under the first placement in its generic name ("table/wall lamp" is a table lamp). [IK] Where the same body is offered with different fixings (clamp, wall plate, stand), the fixing is an installation attribute (rule M, C12).
12. **B3 versus B8 and H1 (B-33, B-18, decided).** A light with no camera or sensor system, including a plug-in or mains security floodlight, is B3 (floodlight kind; B-33); with a camera it is a floodlight camera, B8 (review C-7 row 7; B8 agrees). Emergency and power-cut lights are B3, with an `emergency` attribute on the kind (B-18). A mirror light is a wall light made to light a mirror; a lighted vanity or makeup mirror is H1 (row 12).
13. **Desk lamp, table lamp and clamp lamps.** A lamp with an adjustable arm or head made for task light is a desk lamp (the Met describes the Tizio by that function [MET]; Home Depot lists the desk lamp as its own fixture type [HD1]); a lamp with a fixed shade on a base is a table lamp. A desk lamp that clips on is a desk lamp with `installation = clamp`. A clamp work light with a guard and hook is B3.3.4 [HD2].
14. **B3 versus F2 (F-12, F-25).** Installation, light and kinetic art and sculpture-lamps are F2 when free-standing objects (F-12); a lamp is B3 whatever its ornament, so the test is the object as made: made to give light to a room is B3, made to be looked at as sculpture is F2 (F2 memo, section 9). Akari pendant shades hang and are B3; the free-standing Akari floor and table models are sold by a museum shop as lamps [NG] and are kept B3 by default, provisional until attested (how the maker and the shop name and sell them is the fact to read at harvest) †. A lamp carved from stone is B3 (F-25), with `material` stone, not a kind.
15. **Stage lighting (K-145).** Stage lighting fixtures are B3 (note at step 22); studio lights, stands and flashes are D4 (K-17); fog and haze machines are B4. K-145 does not name controllers, dimmers or truss, so none is entered as a kind until a fetched page shows it sold with stage lighting and the stage-3 harvest places it (provisional until attested †).
16. **Multi-use kinds (C14, CV-31).** A kind used in several leaves goes to the leaf whose rule defines it, else to the general leaf of the family holding all its uses: the night light (plug-in or standing) is filed at B3.2.1, and the floodlight (wall, post or ground bracket is an installation attribute) at B3.1.4. A generated view lists each under every use.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure.** B3 Lighting divides first by role into five sub-families: fixed fittings, standing lamps, carried and worn lights, light sources, and the closing parts sub-family "Parts and accessories made for one lamp or fitting" (C5(d)). The leaf is the axis each sub-family states: placement (rule M, CV-28), how it is carried, or shape and cap form; the parts sub-family skips the leaf (`leaf = null`, C5(b)). The kind is the generic type buyers compare; below it sit named forms with `maker_bound` stated, uncapped, large sets in registers (CV-7, CV-45). Bulbs are divided by shape family, with technology, cap and filament style as attributes (rule T, CV-24; B-4); compact fluorescent and high-intensity discharge lamps are kinds under rule T(b) (B3-O1). There are four levels below B3 at most, and 50 kinds in the worked tree (the stage lighting fixture added by K-145, the ceiling rose moved to C8 by B-18). Fuel, emergency use, output class, connectivity, IP, style, material, room, era, region and price band are attributes, tags or generated views. Homes decided: head torches and dive lights B3, weapon-mounted light E4, toy light E1 (K-162); light strips B3, decorative string lights B4 (K-181); candle holders B4, enclosed carried lanterns B3, decorative lanterns B4 (K-180); light-only security floodlight B3 (B-33); special-purpose bulbs follow the appliance (B-17); lamp cord sets B3, ceiling roses C8 (B-18). Ledgers: the one domain maker ledger (uncapped, one page admits a maker), lines, maker relations, school table, variant ledger (open), tier entries (none stored yet), compatibility ledger, jurisdiction ledger, grading-scale registry, alias table and scene vocabulary.

**Remaining risks.**
1. The compact fluorescent and discharge kinds rest on the pin-base holder and ballast facts (B3-O1); if a fetched source does not show them, the compact fluorescent lamp falls back to a shape attribute of the general bulbs.
2. B-18 puts non-electric lamps in the placement kinds with a `fuel` tag; the mantle, hurricane and candle lantern kinds are kept because the burning mechanism changes the object and the sources rank them apart [DZ] [CO] [RC]. If a stage-3 comparison source does not rank them apart, they merge into a lantern kind with `fuel`.
3. Chochin is a kind only if sellers in two regions give it its own category (C11); otherwise a named form under a lantern kind.
4. The stage lighting fixture is one kind on K-145 alone; its types (PAR can, profile spot, Fresnel, moving head, followspot) may prove to be kinds of their own.
5. F-12 and the Akari floor and table models: a free-standing sculpture-lamp is F2, a lamp is B3; the Akari models are kept B3 by default (section 9.14).
6. C1 has no designer class, so lamp designers are not ledger rows (section 12).
7. The retail evidence is a handful of regions (no India or Latin America tree; the Chinese tree is one guide [CN]); the stage-3 source census covers them (B-34).
