# I2 Digital Media & Items — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md, framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md and framework/structure/_conventions.md (C1 to C13). Research pointers for I2: digital media and items by kind and file format (e-books, audiobooks, music and film files, fonts, photos, in-game items, domain names, NFTs), never by genre or subject. Call count: 13 searches (each search call carried 2 or 3 related queries), 33 fetched pages; the list is in verify/out/S2_I2_sources.tsv and the keys S01 to S32 below are its row numbers (full URLs in the key table of section 1). Regions covered: US, France, Germany, Japan, China (thin) and global registries and standards. Not covered or thin: Korea and the rest of Asia, Latin America, Africa; podcasts kept as files, video-file purchase formats, Chinese and Korean in-game item markets, and the store trees of Kindle, Kobo and BookWalker (found only in search excerpts, so not cited). One host was blocked (Steam Community Market returned only a page header; logged in verify/BLOCKED_HOSTS.txt).*

**One-line result.** Divide I2 by the medium of the thing held (seven sub-families: text and document files; audio files; moving-image files and licences; still-image files; fonts and design assets; in-game and platform items; names and tokens), then by the leaf axis each sub-family states (often skipped), then by the class buyers compare (kind: file-format family for text and images, quality class for music, delivery form for audiobooks, licence scope for fonts, item role for in-game items, eligibility class for domain names, token form for NFTs), then by the specific format, TLD or item design (named form). Rights state (open file, watermarked, account-locked, locker licence), quality ladders, rarity, platform, game, genre and subject stay off the tree as attributes, platform-ledger entries and generated views. Platform, not maker, is the central kind of "maker" here; tiers are measured specifications, not tiers.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** I2 Digital Media & Items, in domain I (Software, Digital & Subscriptions).

**Membership test (placement key, step 5, second branch).** "Anything that exists only as data and is held as a file, licence entitlement or registered token: e-books, audiobooks, digital music and film, podcasts kept as files, fonts, digital artwork, the owner's own photographs, home videos and scanned records held as files (the carrier decides: prints are F5), in-game items (characters, skins, cosmetic and consumable items), domain names, NFTs? I2 Digital Media & Items." Category line: "data-only items held as files, entitlements or tokens: e-books, audiobooks, music and film files, fonts, own photo and video files, in-game items, domain names, NFTs; by kind and file format, never by genre or subject. Study with F1 and D5."

**Precedence sentences and notes that already touch I2.**
- Step 5 lead notes: video games and their expansions are D5 on any carrier or as downloads; game subscriptions and passes are I3; software built into a device is part of the device; a hosted service whose value is the storage, catalogue, connection, place or play it gives access to is I3 "even when an app is used to reach it"; "the account or platform an item sits in is filed with the item's service, the item held in it is in".
- Step 5 I3 note: a bundle is filed by the component it is named and sold for; a playlist or library inside a service is part of that subscription.
- Step 1 notes: "a digital item held as a file, licence entitlement or registered token is not a right in itself" (so it is not OUT as a right); gift-code, wallet and in-game or platform currencies, and credits redeemable for a choice of items such as audiobook credits, are OUT; intellectual property in itself is OUT.
- D5 note (step 24): "cosmetic and consumable in-game items are I2".
- F1 note (step 21): published works fixed on a physical carrier are F1; software on a carrier is I1; "content genre never decides or divides a category"; home recordings of the owner's own life are F5. Step 19 (F5): photographic prints and slides of the owner's own life, home recordings and kept tickets are F5; a ticket for a single event is OUT (step 1).
- Step 19/F5 and step 20/F2: art photographs and posters are F2 as physical pictures; the carrier decides, so the same photograph as a file is I2.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): no S3 item reaches I2 by itself (an expansion is D5, a cosmetic is I2). S5: test the object, not the motive (a collected skin is a skin; a domain bought as an investment is a domain name).
- Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in I2 (never levels): brand and maker (platform, storefront, foundry, registry), region, era, price band and quality tier, edition (limited, signed), connectivity (DRM, online-only), scene and subculture, motive (collecting, investment), how acquired (bought, ripped, own-made, redeemed code), user (child), material (n/a). Content genre and subject are expressly barred.

### Retailers and marketplaces (US, France, Germany, Japan, global)

- **Music downloads, US and France [S08, S09].** Bandcamp sells one item and lets the buyer pick the file format afterwards from a dropdown: MP3 V0, MP3 320, FLAC, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, ALAC, WAV, AIFF [S08]. Qobuz groups its paid downloads first by quality class (24-bit up to 192 kHz; 24-bit up to 96 kHz; 16-bit/44.1 kHz lossless; 320 or 128 kbps lossy) and then names the formats inside each (WAV, AIFF, ALAC, FLAC for the lossless classes; MP3, AAC for lossy), with DSD (1-bit, 2.8 to 22.5 MHz, sold as DSD64 to DSD512) and DXD (24-bit/352.8 kHz) as extra classes; every file is stated to be DRM-free [S09].
- **Music downloads, Japan [S10].** A Japanese audio-press guide lists the three main hi-res shops by the formats they sell: e-onkyo (FLAC, WAV, DSD only), mora (FLAC, DSD, AAC), OTOTOY (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AAC, DSD); all DRM-free, and the shops differ mainly by which formats and which catalogue they carry [S10].
- **Audiobooks [S13, S14, S15].** Libro.fm delivers a DRM-free zip of MP3 files or a single-file M4B, or plays them in its own app [S13]. Audible's formats AA, AAX and AAXC are DRM-protected and tied to the account [S15]. audiobook.jp is audio only (an occasional PDF supplement), bought per title or through an unlimited plan or monthly tickets, and played in its app with repeat downloads [S14]. The delivery form (open single file, open multi-file set, account-locked) is what separates the products.
- **E-books, Germany, France, China [S22, S23, S24, S25].** The German trade FAQ names EPUB (reflowable; novels) and PDF (fixed layout; technical books) as the two main formats, and treats protection as a separate axis: none, personalised watermark, or Adobe DRM (the .acsm file is a licence stub, not the book) [S22]. tolino is an open system of several booksellers whose readers take EPUB, PDF and TXT, Adobe-DRM capable [S24]. Fnac names the format in the product title ("ebook (ePub)") and links its Kobo subscription and readers; the page gave no deeper tree [S23]. A Chinese Kindle guide explains the formats by device: mobi (KF7 and KF8), azw3 (described as mobi in a DRM shell) and epub (universal, readable in every reader app except Kindle's) [S25].
- **Fonts [S12, S27, S30].** MyFonts, an open marketplace for foundries, sells a typeface by licence type: Desktop, Company Desktop, Webfont, App, Electronic Doc, Digital Ad/Email and Server, each priced by users, pageviews, publications or servers [S12]. Adobe Fonts offers a Classification filter of eight classes plus separate expressive tags [S30]. Adobe Stock keeps fonts outside its asset types (they are licensed separately) [S27].
- **Stock and creative assets [S26, S27].** Adobe Stock lists asset types photos, vectors, illustrations, templates, 3D assets, videos, motion graphics templates and audio tracks, with file formats per type (JPEG and PNG; AI, EPS; MOV; OBJ, EXR/HDR, MDL for 3D models, lights and materials; PSDT, AIT, INDT for templates) [S27]. A Japanese guide to BOOTH digital goods lists backgrounds, decorations, frames, textures, brushes for CLIP STUDIO and Photoshop, Photoshop and Canva templates, VRoid textures, 3D models, fonts, e-books and PDFs, with listing fields file format, resolution, supported software, commercial use and redistribution [S26].
- **Digital films [S20].** Movies Anywhere is a locker that synchronises film licences bought from linked retailers and digital copies redeemed from disc-bundle codes; it is a rights record (KeyChest), not a file store [S20]. Its history includes a rival locker (UltraViolet) closed in 2019 and a retailer's films removed and restored in 2025, which shows a locker entitlement can be withdrawn [S20].
- **In-game and platform items [S04, S18, S19].** Steam's community items are trading cards (and foil cards), badges, emoticons, profile backgrounds and coupons, acquired by crafting, trading, the Market or the Points Shop [S04]. The Fortnite fan database filters by item type first (Outfit, Back Bling, Pickaxe, Glider, Emote, Emoji, Wrap, Loading Screen, Skydiving Trails, Spray, Toy, Pet, Lobby Music, Banner, Shoes, Sidekick, Car Trail, Car Boost, LEGO Outfit and Kit, Bundle), then rarity (Common to Legendary and licensed series), then season [S18]. The CS2 database and trade site lists weapon skins by weapon, knives, gloves, cases, collections, stickers (regular, tournament, capsules), agents, graffiti and boxes, pins and capsules, charms, music kits and boxes, patches, keys and a pass, 16,400 items in all, with wear, StatTrak and rarity as properties [S19].
- **Domain names [S21].** Sedo describes premium domains as of two types: registry premiums (price tiers set by the TLD operator, sometimes with higher renewals) and aftermarket names offered by their current owners [S21].
- **NFTs [S05, S06].** OpenSea's help page says it supports two types, 1/1 NFTs (ERC-721) and semi-fungible NFTs (ERC-1155, quantity above one, "great for gaming items and membership passes"), and lists examples: digital art, collectibles, virtual reality items, crypto domain names, ownership records for physical items [S06]. Its browse filters are use categories: Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships, Photography, Music [S05].

### Enthusiast communities and wikis

- **Wikipedia comparison of e-book formats [S03]** compares formats on feature columns (DRM, images, tables, sound, interactivity, word wrap, open standard, annotation, bookmarks, video) and lists Comic book archive (.cbr .cbz .cb7 .cbt .cba), DjVu, Apple Books (.ibook), Kindle (.azw .azw3 .kfx), PDF, EPUB, FictionBook, plain text, RTF, DOC and DOCX, and Apabi (.xeb .ceb), "a popular format for Chinese e-books"; it notes the discontinued Microsoft LIT format. The page is organised by format, with vendors as a separate list.
- **Chinese Kindle guide [S25], Phile-web [S10], fnbr.co [S18], cs.trade [S19]** are the fan-level views: buyers there ask "what is the difference between mobi and azw3", "can Kindle read epub" [S25], "which shop sells which hi-res format" [S10], and "which item types exist" [S18, S19]. Their questions are about format compatibility, not about content.
- **Typography guides [S11, S29].** Monotype's 2026 guide says a font format "is not the same thing as a license": TTF and OTF are desktop containers, WOFF and WOFF2 web wrappers, and a variable font "is not a fifth format" but an OpenType capability [S11]. Google's own font guide says its "genre" classes (serif, sans serif, slab serif, monospaced, script, handwriting, display) "aren't clearly delineated, nor are they mutually exclusive" [S29].

### Collectors and the resale market

- **Skins and in-game items [S19, S18, S04].** The CS2 trade site and the Fortnite database carry price, wear, rarity and season as properties of an item type, not as places in a tree [S18, S19]. Steam's Community Market page could not be read (blocked), but Steamworks documents the item types sold there [S04].
- **Domain aftermarket [S21]** and **NFT marketplaces [S05, S06]** organise by sale route and use category (Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships), not by what the object is.
- **Hi-res download shops [S09, S10]** are the nearest thing to a collector market for files; they compare by sampling rate and format.

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **IANA Root Zone Database [S01]** lists each TLD with Status (Active, Retired, Reserved), Type (Generic, Country Code, Special Use, Test) and writing system; ICANNWiki adds that IANA's finer labels are generic, generic-restricted, sponsored, country-code and infrastructure, with 22 gTLDs before 2012 and 1,241 strings delegated from the 2012 round [S28]. JPRS divides JP names into general-use .JP, prefecture-type, and attribute-type names by registrant class (CO.JP companies, OR.JP organisations, NE.JP network services, AC.JP higher education, ED.JP schools, GO.JP government, GR.JP groups, LG.JP local government, AD.JP), with the regional type closed in 2012 [S02].
- **ethereum.org token standards [S07]** separate fungible tokens (ERC-20) from NFT standards ERC-721 ("a deed for artwork or a song") and ERC-1155 (multi-token, utility tokens and NFTs in one contract).
- **Library of Congress Recommended Formats Statement [S16, S17, S31, S32]** is the nearest thing to a cross-medium standard classification: Textual Works (print, digital, electronic serials), Still Image Works (photographs, other graphic images), Moving Image Works (motion pictures, video), Audio Works, Musical Scores (print, digital), Datasets, Geospatial, Design and 3D (including scanned 3D objects), Software and Video Games, Web Archives, Email [S16]. Within a medium it ranks formats as preferred or acceptable: for digital photographs TIFF, JPEG2000, PNG and JPEG preferred, Photoshop, DNG and camera raw (.nef .crw .arw .iiq) and GIF acceptable [S31]; for file-based video IMF, FFV1 in Matroska and ProRes in QuickTime preferred and MP4 only a viewing proxy [S32]. It requires files to carry no DRM [S17, S32].
- **Adobe Stock's asset-type list [S27]** and **Steamworks' item documents [S04]** act as trade vocabularies for their own sub-markets.

### Museums and reference works

- The LoC statement above is a library-preservation reference. Wikipedia pages on e-book formats and Movies Anywhere [S03, S20], ICANNWiki [S28], Google's Fonts Knowledge [S29] and Adobe's blog [S30] are the reference works. No museum collection page for born-digital items was fetched; this source type is covered by the Library of Congress.

### Where the sources agree and where they split the same object

- **Agree.** Every cross-medium source divides first by medium (text, still image, moving image, audio, 3D, fonts): the LoC contents [S16], Adobe Stock's asset types [S27], BOOTH product ideas [S26]. Within a medium the next cut is a format or quality class [S03, S09, S31]. Fonts, domain names and tokens each have a dedicated standard and a different unit (licence, TLD, token standard).
- **Split.** (1) Protection: the German trade FAQ treats DRM as an independent axis on the same EPUB or PDF [S22], while Audible's AAX and the Chinese azw3 explanation make the protected wrapper part of the format [S15, S25]. (2) Fonts: MyFonts divides purchases by licence, Monotype says format is not licence, and Google admits its genre classes overlap [S11, S12, S29]. (3) Game items: fan databases divide by item type inside one game [S18, S19], Steam's own items are platform-wide and sit outside any game [S04], and OpenSea's "Gaming" is a use category [S05]. (4) NFTs: the standards divide by token form [S06, S07], the marketplaces by use [S05]. (5) Qobuz groups by quality class and then format [S09]; Bandcamp offers all formats of one item [S08].
