# I2 Digital Media & Items — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md, framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md and framework/structure/_conventions.md (C1 to C13). Research pointers for I2: digital media and items by kind and file format (e-books, audiobooks, music and film files, fonts, photos, in-game items, domain names, NFTs), never by genre or subject. Call count: 13 searches (each search call carried 2 or 3 related queries), 33 fetched pages; the list is in verify/out/S2_I2_sources.tsv and the keys S01 to S32 below are its row numbers (full URLs in the key table of section 1). Regions covered: US, France, Germany, Japan, China (thin) and global registries and standards. Not covered or thin: Korea and the rest of Asia, Latin America, Africa; podcasts kept as files, video-file purchase formats, Chinese and Korean in-game item markets, and the store trees of Kindle, Kobo and BookWalker (found only in search excerpts, so not cited). One host was blocked (Steam Community Market returned only a page header; logged in verify/BLOCKED_HOSTS.txt).*

**One-line result.** Divide I2 by the medium of the thing held (seven sub-families: text and document files; audio files; moving-image files and licences; still-image files; fonts and design assets; in-game and platform items; names and tokens), then by the leaf axis each sub-family states (often skipped), then by the class buyers compare (kind: file-format family for text and images, quality class for music, delivery form for audiobooks, licence scope for fonts, item role for in-game items, eligibility class for domain names, token form for NFTs), then by the specific format, TLD or item design (named form). Rights state (open file, watermarked, account-locked, locker licence), quality ladders, rarity, platform, game, genre and subject stay off the tree as attributes, platform-ledger entries and generated views. Platform, not maker, is the central kind of "maker" here; tiers are measured specifications, not tiers.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** I2 Digital Media & Items, in domain I (Software, Digital & Subscriptions).

**Membership test (placement key, step 5, second branch).** "Anything that exists only as data and is held as a file, licence entitlement or registered token: e-books, audiobooks, digital music and film, podcasts kept as files, fonts, digital artwork, the owner's own photographs, home videos and scanned records held as files (the carrier decides: prints are F5), in-game items (characters, skins, cosmetic and consumable items), domain names, NFTs? I2 Digital Media & Items." Category line: "data-only items held as files, entitlements or tokens: e-books, audiobooks, music and film files, fonts, own photo and video files, in-game items, domain names, NFTs; by kind and file format, never by genre or subject. Study with F1 and D5."

**Precedence sentences and notes that already touch I2.**
- Step 5 lead notes: video games and their expansions are D5 on any carrier or as downloads; game subscriptions and passes are I3; software built into a device is part of the device; a hosted service whose value is the storage, catalogue, connection, place or play it gives access to is I3 "even when an app is used to reach it"; "the account or platform an item sits in is filed with the item's service, the item held in it is in".
- Step 5 I3 note: a bundle is filed by the component it is named and sold for; a playlist or library inside a service is part of that subscription.
- Step 1 notes: "a digital item held as a file, licence entitlement or registered token is not a right in itself" (so it is not OUT as a right); gift-code, wallet and in-game or platform currencies, and credits redeemable for a choice of items such as audiobook credits, are OUT; intellectual property in itself is OUT.
- D5 note (step 24): "cosmetic and consumable in-game items are I2".
- F1 note (step 21): published works fixed on a physical carrier are F1; software on a carrier is I1; "content genre never decides or divides a category"; home recordings of the owner's own life are F5. Step 19 (F5): photographic prints and slides of the owner's own life, home recordings and kept tickets are F5; a ticket for a single event is OUT (step 1).
- Step 19/F5 and step 20/F2: art photographs and posters are F2 as physical pictures; the carrier decides, so the same photograph as a file is I2.
- S3 (parts follow the whole): no S3 item reaches I2 by itself (an expansion is D5, a cosmetic is I2). S5: test the object, not the motive (a collected skin is a skin; a domain bought as an investment is a domain name).
- Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in I2 (never levels): brand and maker (platform, storefront, foundry, registry), region, era, price band and quality tier, edition (limited, signed), connectivity (DRM, online-only), scene and subculture, motive (collecting, investment), how acquired (bought, ripped, own-made, redeemed code), user (child), material (n/a). Content genre and subject are expressly barred.

### Retailers and marketplaces (US, France, Germany, Japan, global)

- **Music downloads, US and France [S08, S09].** Bandcamp sells one item and lets the buyer pick the file format afterwards from a dropdown: MP3 V0, MP3 320, FLAC, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, ALAC, WAV, AIFF [S08]. Qobuz groups its paid downloads first by quality class (24-bit up to 192 kHz; 24-bit up to 96 kHz; 16-bit/44.1 kHz lossless; 320 or 128 kbps lossy) and then names the formats inside each (WAV, AIFF, ALAC, FLAC for the lossless classes; MP3, AAC for lossy), with DSD (1-bit, 2.8 to 22.5 MHz, sold as DSD64 to DSD512) and DXD (24-bit/352.8 kHz) as extra classes; every file is stated to be DRM-free [S09].
- **Music downloads, Japan [S10].** A Japanese audio-press guide lists the three main hi-res shops by the formats they sell: e-onkyo (FLAC, WAV, DSD only), mora (FLAC, DSD, AAC), OTOTOY (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AAC, DSD); all DRM-free, and the shops differ mainly by which formats and which catalogue they carry [S10].
- **Audiobooks [S13, S14, S15].** Libro.fm delivers a DRM-free zip of MP3 files or a single-file M4B, or plays them in its own app [S13]. Audible's formats AA, AAX and AAXC are DRM-protected and tied to the account [S15]. audiobook.jp is audio only (an occasional PDF supplement), bought per title or through an unlimited plan or monthly tickets, and played in its app with repeat downloads [S14]. The delivery form (open single file, open multi-file set, account-locked) is what separates the products.
- **E-books, Germany, France, China [S22, S23, S24, S25].** The German trade FAQ names EPUB (reflowable; novels) and PDF (fixed layout; technical books) as the two main formats, and treats protection as a separate axis: none, personalised watermark, or Adobe DRM (the .acsm file is a licence stub, not the book) [S22]. tolino is an open system of several booksellers whose readers take EPUB, PDF and TXT, Adobe-DRM capable [S24]. Fnac names the format in the product title ("ebook (ePub)") and links its Kobo subscription and readers; the page gave no deeper tree [S23]. A Chinese Kindle guide explains the formats by device: mobi (KF7 and KF8), azw3 (described as mobi in a DRM shell) and epub (universal, readable in every reader app except Kindle's) [S25].
- **Fonts [S12, S27, S30].** MyFonts, an open marketplace for foundries, sells a typeface by licence type: Desktop, Company Desktop, Webfont, App, Electronic Doc, Digital Ad/Email and Server, each priced by users, pageviews, publications or servers [S12]. Adobe Fonts offers a Classification filter of eight classes plus separate expressive tags [S30]. Adobe Stock keeps fonts outside its asset types (they are licensed separately) [S27].
- **Stock and creative assets [S26, S27].** Adobe Stock lists asset types photos, vectors, illustrations, templates, 3D assets, videos, motion graphics templates and audio tracks, with file formats per type (JPEG and PNG; AI, EPS; MOV; OBJ, EXR/HDR, MDL for 3D models, lights and materials; PSDT, AIT, INDT for templates) [S27]. A Japanese guide to BOOTH digital goods lists backgrounds, decorations, frames, textures, brushes for CLIP STUDIO and Photoshop, Photoshop and Canva templates, VRoid textures, 3D models, fonts, e-books and PDFs, with listing fields file format, resolution, supported software, commercial use and redistribution [S26].
- **Digital films [S20].** Movies Anywhere is a locker that synchronises film licences bought from linked retailers and digital copies redeemed from disc-bundle codes; it is a rights record (KeyChest), not a file store [S20]. Its history includes a rival locker (UltraViolet) closed in 2019 and a retailer's films removed and restored in 2025, which shows a locker entitlement can be withdrawn [S20].
- **In-game and platform items [S04, S18, S19].** Steam's community items are trading cards (and foil cards), badges, emoticons, profile backgrounds and coupons, acquired by crafting, trading, the Market or the Points Shop [S04]. The Fortnite fan database filters by item type first (Outfit, Back Bling, Pickaxe, Glider, Emote, Emoji, Wrap, Loading Screen, Skydiving Trails, Spray, Toy, Pet, Lobby Music, Banner, Shoes, Sidekick, Car Trail, Car Boost, LEGO Outfit and Kit, Bundle), then rarity (Common to Legendary and licensed series), then season [S18]. The CS2 database and trade site lists weapon skins by weapon, knives, gloves, cases, collections, stickers (regular, tournament, capsules), agents, graffiti and boxes, pins and capsules, charms, music kits and boxes, patches, keys and a pass, 16,400 items in all, with wear, StatTrak and rarity as properties [S19].
- **Domain names [S21].** Sedo describes premium domains as of two types: registry premiums (price tiers set by the TLD operator, sometimes with higher renewals) and aftermarket names offered by their current owners [S21].
- **NFTs [S05, S06].** OpenSea's help page says it supports two types, 1/1 NFTs (ERC-721) and semi-fungible NFTs (ERC-1155, quantity above one, "great for gaming items and membership passes"), and lists examples: digital art, collectibles, virtual reality items, crypto domain names, ownership records for physical items [S06]. Its browse filters are use categories: Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships, Photography, Music [S05].

### Enthusiast communities and wikis

- **Wikipedia comparison of e-book formats [S03]** compares formats on feature columns (DRM, images, tables, sound, interactivity, word wrap, open standard, annotation, bookmarks, video) and lists Comic book archive (.cbr .cbz .cb7 .cbt .cba), DjVu, Apple Books (.ibook), Kindle (.azw .azw3 .kfx), PDF, EPUB, FictionBook, plain text, RTF, DOC and DOCX, and Apabi (.xeb .ceb), "a popular format for Chinese e-books"; it notes the discontinued Microsoft LIT format. The page is organised by format, with vendors as a separate list.
- **Chinese Kindle guide [S25], Phile-web [S10], fnbr.co [S18], cs.trade [S19]** are the fan-level views: buyers there ask "what is the difference between mobi and azw3", "can Kindle read epub" [S25], "which shop sells which hi-res format" [S10], and "which item types exist" [S18, S19]. Their questions are about format compatibility, not about content.
- **Typography guides [S11, S29].** Monotype's 2026 guide says a font format "is not the same thing as a license": TTF and OTF are desktop containers, WOFF and WOFF2 web wrappers, and a variable font "is not a fifth format" but an OpenType capability [S11]. Google's own font guide says its "genre" classes (serif, sans serif, slab serif, monospaced, script, handwriting, display) "aren't clearly delineated, nor are they mutually exclusive" [S29].

### Collectors and the resale market

- **Skins and in-game items [S19, S18, S04].** The CS2 trade site and the Fortnite database carry price, wear, rarity and season as properties of an item type, not as places in a tree [S18, S19]. Steam's Community Market page could not be read (blocked), but Steamworks documents the item types sold there [S04].
- **Domain aftermarket [S21]** and **NFT marketplaces [S05, S06]** organise by sale route and use category (Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships), not by what the object is.
- **Hi-res download shops [S09, S10]** are the nearest thing to a collector market for files; they compare by sampling rate and format.

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **IANA Root Zone Database [S01]** lists each TLD with Status (Active, Retired, Reserved), Type (Generic, Country Code, Special Use, Test) and writing system; ICANNWiki adds that IANA's finer labels are generic, generic-restricted, sponsored, country-code and infrastructure, with 22 gTLDs before 2012 and 1,241 strings delegated from the 2012 round [S28]. JPRS divides JP names into general-use .JP, prefecture-type, and attribute-type names by registrant class (CO.JP companies, OR.JP organisations, NE.JP network services, AC.JP higher education, ED.JP schools, GO.JP government, GR.JP groups, LG.JP local government, AD.JP), with the regional type closed in 2012 [S02].
- **ethereum.org token standards [S07]** separate fungible tokens (ERC-20) from NFT standards ERC-721 ("a deed for artwork or a song") and ERC-1155 (multi-token, utility tokens and NFTs in one contract).
- **Library of Congress Recommended Formats Statement [S16, S17, S31, S32]** is the nearest thing to a cross-medium standard classification: Textual Works (print, digital, electronic serials), Still Image Works (photographs, other graphic images), Moving Image Works (motion pictures, video), Audio Works, Musical Scores (print, digital), Datasets, Geospatial, Design and 3D (including scanned 3D objects), Software and Video Games, Web Archives, Email [S16]. Within a medium it ranks formats as preferred or acceptable: for digital photographs TIFF, JPEG2000, PNG and JPEG preferred, Photoshop, DNG and camera raw (.nef .crw .arw .iiq) and GIF acceptable [S31]; for file-based video IMF, FFV1 in Matroska and ProRes in QuickTime preferred and MP4 only a viewing proxy [S32]. It requires files to carry no DRM [S17, S32].
- **Adobe Stock's asset-type list [S27]** and **Steamworks' item documents [S04]** act as trade vocabularies for their own sub-markets.

### Museums and reference works

- The LoC statement above is a library-preservation reference. Wikipedia pages on e-book formats and Movies Anywhere [S03, S20], ICANNWiki [S28], Google's Fonts Knowledge [S29] and Adobe's blog [S30] are the reference works. No museum collection page for born-digital items was fetched; this source type is covered by the Library of Congress.

### Where the sources agree and where they split the same object

- **Agree.** Every cross-medium source divides first by medium (text, still image, moving image, audio, 3D, fonts): the LoC contents [S16], Adobe Stock's asset types [S27], BOOTH product ideas [S26]. Within a medium the next cut is a format or quality class [S03, S09, S31]. Fonts, domain names and tokens each have a dedicated standard and a different unit (licence, TLD, token standard).
- **Split.** (1) Protection: the German trade FAQ treats DRM as an independent axis on the same EPUB or PDF [S22], while Audible's AAX and the Chinese azw3 explanation make the protected wrapper part of the format [S15, S25]. (2) Fonts: MyFonts divides purchases by licence, Monotype says format is not licence, and Google admits its genre classes overlap [S11, S12, S29]. (3) Game items: fan databases divide by item type inside one game [S18, S19], Steam's own items are platform-wide and sit outside any game [S04], and OpenSea's "Gaming" is a use category [S05]. (4) NFTs: the standards divide by token form [S06, S07], the marketplaces by use [S05]. (5) Qobuz groups by quality class and then format [S09]; Bandcamp offers all formats of one item [S08].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Medium first, then format or compatibility, then quality or rights.** The evidence, source by source:

- **Cross-medium navigation is by medium.** The Library of Congress contents run Textual, Still Image, Moving Image, Audio, Scores and so on [S16]; Adobe Stock opens with photos, design assets (vectors, illustrations, templates), 3D assets, then motion and audio [S27]; BOOTH sellers think in backgrounds, brushes, templates, 3D models, fonts, e-books [S26]. No source fetched opens with the file extension across media, and none opens with genre.
- **Inside a medium, the buyer's next question is "will it work on my device or app", i.e. format.** The Chinese Kindle guide asks "why does mobi become azw3 on Kindle" and "can Kindle read epub" [S25]; the German FAQ says EPUB suits novels and PDF suits technical books [S22]; Phile-web compares shops by the formats they sell [S10]; Bandcamp shows one item and a format dropdown [S08].
- **Quality and rights come after format.** Qobuz names the quality class (24-bit/192 kHz down to 320 kbps) before the format [S09]; the German FAQ then adds protection (none, watermark, Adobe DRM) [S22]; Libro.fm and Qobuz advertise DRM-free as a selling point [S09, S13]; the Library of Congress requires no DRM [S17, S32].
- **For fonts the licence scope is the purchase decision** (users, pageviews, publications, servers) once the typeface is chosen [S12], and Monotype stresses that the file format does not settle it [S11].
- **For in-game items the game comes first for the buyer but the item type comes first on every database filter.** Fortnite's filter order is item type, rarity, season [S18]; the CS2 database opens with weapons, knives, gloves, cases, stickers, agents [S19]; Steam's community items are platform-wide [S04].
- **For domain names the TLD class is the first filter** (IANA: status, type, writing system [S01]; JPRS: registrant class [S02]) and the aftermarket adds registry premium against aftermarket [S21].
- **For NFTs the standards give token form** (1/1 against semi-fungible) [S06, S07] while marketplaces filter by use (Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships) [S05].

**Reading.** Owners and buyers decide first by what the thing is when opened (read, listen, watch, view, install as a typeface, equip, resolve, trade), then by the compatibility format, then by rights state and quality. Medium is therefore the right sub-family axis. The leaf and kind axes differ by sub-family, because the next decision differs: format for text and images, quality class for music, delivery form for audiobooks, licence scope for fonts, role for game items, eligibility for domain names.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): medium, then a stated leaf axis, then compared class, then specific format or design

| Level | Axis |
|---|---|
| Sub-family | medium of the thing held: what it is when it is used (text, audio, moving image, still image, font or design asset, in-game item, name or token) |
| Leaf | per sub-family: not used in text, moving-image or still-image sub-families (12 kinds or fewer, C5b); kind of recording in audio; asset class in fonts and design assets; role in the game in game items; register (DNS or blockchain) in names and tokens |
| Kind | the class buyers compare: file-format family (text, still image); quality class (music); delivery form (audiobooks); licence scope (fonts); item role (game items); TLD eligibility class (domain names); token form (NFTs) |
| Named form | the specific format or codec (FLAC, AZW3, TIFF), TLD (.jp), item design (a CS2 knife finish) or token standard (ERC-721) |

Shared attributes, never levels: rights state (open file, personalised watermark, account-locked, locker licence, token); unit (track, album, volume, season, set); quality ladder values (bit depth, sampling rate, bitrate, resolution) with the standard named (C4b); source of the file (bought, redeemed, own-made, ripped; Appendix A "how acquired"); language and script (IDN); region; game or platform (platform ledger, C13); rarity and wear (platform tags, C4c); licence scope for assets other than fonts; genre and subject (barred).

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. *A Kindle book in a Kobo-readable EPUB, or a PDF magazine issue.* The same work exists in several kinds; each file is its own object. Format-family kinds let the owner hold two files of one title, but the "same title" link needs a work-level identifier outside the tree.
2. *A digital film licence with a downloaded file.* A locker licence (kind 18) and a DRM-free video file (kind 19) are different objects: only one of them survives the locker closing [S20]. The tree separates them but the owner may prefer one "film" row.
3. *A Steam trading card or a Fortnite Wrap.* Steam items live on a platform, not inside a game [S04], so "in-game" is slightly wrong; wraps and trails cross roles. The leaf axis "role" blurs at the edges (a Glider is a tool, a vehicle skin or an expression).
4. *A domain name in a blockchain registry or an NFT ticket.* A blockchain name is called a "crypto domain name" and an NFT by OpenSea [S06], so it could be kind 55 to 57 or 58; an NFT that is a membership pass or event ticket crosses I3 and OUT (section 9).

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds.** The worked tree has 59. A complete tree would add podcast and e-serial kinds, more game roles (vehicles, land and building items, consumables), further asset kinds and video and score format kinds: about 70 to 90 kinds.
- **Named forms.** About 60 file formats and codecs appear in the tree (14 e-book formats, about 11 music formats, M4B and MP3 audiobook sets plus 3 Audible formats, 3 video containers, 15 image formats, templates and 3D file types) [S03, S08, S09, S27, S31, S32]. Beyond that: domain extensions (1,241 delegated gTLD strings from the 2012 round and 22 before it [S28], plus country codes [S01]; JP has nine attribute-type labels [S02]); CS2 item designs (16,400 [S19]); typeface families, Fortnite item designs and NFT collections (not counted; each runs to many thousands).
- **Where depth concentrates.** (1) In-game items: many kinds are cheap, but named forms are unbounded; the right depth is the role kind with named forms entered only on demand. (2) Fonts: depth is in licence scope and in family names (identity). (3) Music: depth is in quality and format, a short, closed list. (4) Domain names and tokens: depth is in the registry list, not the kind list.
- **Leaf sizes.** Broad: In-game roles (16 kinds, five leaves), Fonts (7 kinds, all licence scopes), Templates and production packs. Medium: Music recordings (4), Domain names (3), Still-image files (5, no leaf), Text and document files (9, no leaf). Narrow: Spoken-word books (3), Moving-image files and licences (2), Tokens (2), Podcast episodes (1), 3D assets (3).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule.** A kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or compatibility; below that the item is a named form or an attribute. The level differs by sub-family.

| Sub-family | Kind stops at | Named form is | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Text and document files | file-format family (EPUB, Kindle-format, PDF, comic archive) | the specific variant (AZW3, KFX; CBZ, CBR) | Format decides device, reflow and layout: S03, S22, S24, S25. Retailers print the format in the product title (S23). Quality is not compared across titles. |
| Music files | quality class (lossy, lossless compressed, uncompressed, studio-resolution) | the format (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, MP3 V0) | Qobuz groups by quality class and then names formats (S09); Bandcamp offers all formats of one item (S08); Japanese shops differ by formats (S10). Bit depth and sampling rate are spec attributes (C4b). |
| Audiobooks | delivery form (single file, multi-file set, account-locked) | the format (M4B, MP3, AAX, AAXC) | Libro.fm and Audible differ by openness, not audio quality (S13, S15); audiobook.jp is app-delivered (S14). |
| Moving-image | holding form (licence, file) | the container or retailer (Movies Anywhere library, MP4, MOV, MKV) | S20 and S32; thin evidence, no fetched video-store tree. |
| Still-image | file-format family (raw, lossy, lossless, layered, vector) | the file type (DNG, JPEG, TIFF, SVG) | The LoC splits raster and vector and ranks formats (S31); Adobe Stock lists format per asset type (S27). |
| Fonts | licence scope | the typeface family (maker-bound by foundry) | MyFonts sells by licence scope (S12); Monotype says format is not licence (S11); genre classes overlap (S29). |
| Design assets | asset class (template, brush pack, 3D model, motion template) | the product or pack | S26, S27. |
| In-game items | item role (outfit, weapon skin, emote, container, trading card) | the item design within a game | Item type is the first filter on the fan databases and Steam's documented item list (S04, S18, S19); price and rarity per item are tags. |
| Domain names | TLD eligibility class (open generic, country-code, restricted) | the TLD (.jp, .agency) | IANA type and JPRS registrant class (S01, S02); aftermarket premium is a price tag (S21). |
| Tokens | token form (1/1, semi-fungible) | the standard (ERC-721, ERC-1155) | OpenSea and ethereum.org (S06, S07); use categories are tags (S05). |

**Contrast with other categories.** Unlike the physical F1 where the carrier family (hardback, LP) is the compared class, I2 compares format family or quality class, because there is no physical form. Like D5, platform is a compatibility attribute and a ledger entry, never a level.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Choice: spec-ranked, but as measured attributes, not tiers.** For sampled media (music, video, images) a measurable specification orders the files: bitrate and mode (MP3 V0, 320, 128 kbps), bit depth and sampling rate (16-bit/44.1 kHz, 24-bit/96 kHz, 24-bit/192 kHz), 1-bit DSD rates (DSD64 to DSD512) and DXD 24-bit/352.8 kHz [S08, S09, S10]; resolution (HD, 4K) for video [S27]. Three sources in three regions (US, France, Japan) agree on the ladder for music. Under C4(b) these are attributes with the standard named, not tier entries, and they never become levels. For other sub-families there is no tier: the order is a matter of preference.

- **Consensus-ranked orderings that exist.** The Library of Congress ranks formats as preferred or acceptable for preservation (TIFF, JPEG2000, PNG, JPEG ahead of raw and PSD) [S31, S32]. It is one source in one region and a preservation view, so it is held as a candidate attribute `preservation_rank`, not entered as a scheme (C4e).
- **Platform-set labels.** Fortnite rarities (Common to Legendary, licensed series), CS2 rarity, wear and StatTrak [S18, S19] and registry premium price tiers [S21] are platform labels: dated `platform_tag` entries (C4c), never tiers.
- **Price band.** Applies to priced purchases (NFTs, domains, fonts, hi-res albums) using the shared USD bands of C4(d); not a level.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Choice: platform.** The maker or operator sets what works with what: Amazon's own Kindle formats [S25], Apple's .ibook [S03], Audible's account-bound formats [S15], Adobe DRM and the .acsm licence stub in Germany [S22], the tolino retailer alliance [S24], Movies Anywhere's linked retailers [S20], Steam and the game publishers [S04, S18, S19], the Ethereum token standards [S07] and the registry operators (IANA lists a TLD manager for each; JPRS runs .jp) [S01, S02]. Identity matters in two pockets: font foundries (MyFonts is a marketplace of foundries [S12]; Adobe Fonts offers a Classification filter and tags [S30]) and NFT collections ("PFPs and identity", membership perks [S05]).

**Recommendation.**
- A **compatibility ledger** (C13) first: platform_id, name, owner, kinds_served, standard_ref, sources. Rows such as Kindle, Kobo, tolino, Apple Books, Adobe DRM, Audible, Movies Anywhere, Steam, Fortnite, CS2, ERC-721 and ERC-1155, DNS root. Platforms are never levels.
- A **light maker ledger** (C1 core columns) with the extra `maker_class` values storefront, platform holder, format steward, foundry, registry operator and game publisher. `known_for_kinds` derived from the named-form ledger (C1 note). Each row needs one fetched source and a second where available. A maker is not a level and tier entries sit on makers, lines or named forms only (C4a).
- Named forms of fonts and game items are maker-bound (foundry or publisher) = yes; file formats are maker-bound = no unless proprietary (ALAC, KFX, .ibook, Audible AAX: yes).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Text and images:** open standard first (EPUB, TIFF), then proprietary formats by first-introduction year as attested; reason: the open form is the canonical one and carries least lock-in (C7).
- **Music and video:** spec order from lossy to uncompressed to studio-resolution, because the spec ladder is the buyer's own comparison (S09).
- **Audiobooks:** open files before account-locked, then single file before multi-file.
- **Fonts:** licence scope in the order a cart prints it (Desktop, Company Desktop, Webfont, App, Electronic Doc, Digital Ad/Email, Server) [S12]; named forms by foundry, then year, then alphabetical.
- **Game items:** roles in the order wear, tools, expression, containers, collectibles; named forms by game, then first-introduction year.
- **Domain names:** open generic, country-code, restricted (IANA type before eligibility); TLDs alphabetical [S01].
- **Tokens:** 1/1 before semi-fungible; standards by year of adoption.
- Named forms generally follow C7: maker (ledger order), then first-introduction year, then alphabetical.

**Framework notes found while writing.**
- Step 5 and step 19/F1: "home recordings" is F5 in the F1 note and "home videos ... held as files" is I2 in step 5; the carrier rule resolves it, but the F5 line should say "on a physical carrier".
- Step 5 says "in-game items", while Steam's trading cards, emoticons and backgrounds are platform items outside any single game [S04]; the line should read "in-game and platform items".

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure.** Candidate A: seven sub-families by medium; leaf only where it carries a distinct axis (audio: kind of recording; design assets: asset class; game items: role; names and tokens: register); kind = the class buyers compare (stated per sub-family in 4b); named form = format, TLD, token standard or item design. Maximum depth is four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); the text, moving-image and still-image sub-families use three because they hold 12 kinds or fewer (C5b). Rights state, unit, quality values, rarity, platform, game, language, scene and genre stay off the tree as attributes, platform-ledger rows and generated views; fonts' classification classes (serif, sans, script) are non-exclusive tags [S29].

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. **Medium-first sub-families.** Accept seven (text, audio, moving image, still image, fonts and design assets, game items, names and tokens), or prefer Candidate B (holding form first)?
2. **Audio leaf by kind of recording** (music, spoken-word, podcast). It separates products that stores and DRM systems separate (S13, S15), but it is a content-type split; the brief bars genre. Accept it as form, not genre, or fold audiobooks into one audio kind list?
3. **Fonts.** Kinds by licence scope with the family as named form (recommended), or one "font family" kind with licence scope as an attribute (fewer kinds, one row per family)?
4. **Variable fonts.** Attribute (recommended, Monotype says it is a capability [S11]) or kind (C10(b) might allow it)?
5. **In-game items.** Game as ledger and view, not a level; named forms entered only on demand (16,400 CS2 designs [S19]). Accept?
6. **NFT edge cases.** A token that is a game item, ticket or membership pass: I2 as token (recommended), or I2.6, I3 or OUT by its use?
7. **Rights state** (open, watermark, account-locked, locker licence) as an attribute with a generated view, not a sub-family. Accept?
8. **Unit** (track, album, set, season, volume) as an attribute. Accept?
9. **Domain names.** Kinds by TLD eligibility class (recommended), with TLDs as named forms and premium or aftermarket as a price tag, or one kind "domain name"?
10. **Stock and production assets.** Keep stock images, templates and 3D files in I2 (recommended, they are data files) or send licensed-asset libraries to I3?
11. **Provisional kinds without a fetched source** (S6): podcast episode file (17), digital score file (9) and the named forms of the moving-image sub-family beyond MP4, MOV and MKV; and Korean, further Chinese and Latin American markets, which this memo did not reach.
12. **Attestation of deeper tiers.** Whether to adopt the Library of Congress ranking as `preservation_rank` after a second source is fetched.
