**One-line result (decided).** Divide I2 by what the held data is for (seven sub-families: read; heard; watched; viewed; built with; used in a game or platform account; registered; I-2), then by the leaf axis each sub-family states (skipped where a sub-family holds 12 kinds or fewer), then by the class buyers compare (kind: layout form for text, one kind each for music, audiobooks, podcasts, video and fonts, file-format family for still images, asset class for production assets, item role for game and platform items, eligibility class for domain names, token form for NFTs), then by the specific format, TLD, typeface family, item design or token standard (named form). Platform, maker and region never name a kind; rights state (open file, watermark, account-locked, locker licence), unit, compression, quality values, licence scope, rarity, platform, game, genre and subject stay off the tree as attributes, variants, compatibility-ledger rows and generated views. Platform, not maker, is the central kind of "maker" here; quality ladders are measured specifications, not tiers. Named forms (formats, typeface families, TLDs, item designs, NFT collections) are harvested from attested catalogues in stage 3 without a cap.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** I2 Digital Media & Items, in domain I (Software, Digital & Subscriptions).

**Membership test (placement key CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 5, second branch; wording after K-69).** "Anything that exists only as data and is held as a file, licence entitlement or registered token: e-books, audiobooks, digital music and film, podcasts kept as files, fonts, digital artwork, the owner's own photographs, home videos and scanned records held as files (the carrier decides: prints are F5), in-game and platform items (characters, skins, cosmetic and consumable items, platform profile items, messaging stickers), domain names, NFTs? I2 Digital Media & Items." Category line: "data-only items held as files, entitlements or tokens: e-books, audiobooks, music and film files, fonts, own photo and video files, in-game and platform items, domain names, NFTs; by kind and file format, never by genre or subject. Study with F1 and D5."

**Precedence sentences and notes that already touch I2.**
- Step 5 lead notes: video games and their expansions are D5 on any carrier or as downloads; game subscriptions and passes are I3; software built into a device is part of the device; a hosted service whose value is the storage, catalogue, connection, place or play it gives access to is I3 "even when an app is used to reach it"; "the account or platform an item sits in is filed with the item's service, the item held in it is in". Added in v0_7 (K-71): game launchers and their free accounts are not filed (their makers stay in the maker ledger); an asset-library plan is I3 and each licensed asset I2; files a program loads without code (sample libraries, presets, LUTs, model weights) are I2, plug-ins I1, game mods D5; a reference work as an e-book file is I2, as its own program I1; a plan that renews credits is I3 and loose credits OUT; an NFT-carried membership is I2 unless it is also sold as a plan.
- Step 5 I3 note: a bundle is filed by the component it is named and sold for; a playlist or library inside a service is part of that subscription.
- Step 1 notes: "a digital item held as a file, licence entitlement or registered token is not a right in itself" (so it is not OUT as a right); "a domain-name registration is I2, not a right" (K-29); gift-code, wallet and in-game or platform currencies, and credits redeemable for a choice of items such as audiobook credits, are OUT; intellectual property in itself is OUT.
- D5 note (step 24, after K-69): "in-game and platform items (cosmetic and consumable) are I2".
- F1 note (step 21): published works fixed on a physical carrier are F1; software on a carrier is I1; "content genre never decides or divides a category"; "home recordings on a physical carrier are F5" (K-141). Step 19 (F5, after K-139): a photographic print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2; every other photographic print, slide or negative, whoever is shown, is F5; home recordings and kept tickets are F5; a ticket for a single event is OUT (step 1).
- Step 19/F5 and step 20/F2: art photographs and posters are F2 as physical pictures; the carrier decides, so the same photograph as a file is I2. Video works held as files are I2 (F-12).
- S3 (parts follow the whole): no S3 item reaches I2 by itself (an expansion is D5, a cosmetic is I2). S5: test the object, not the motive (a collected skin is a skin; a domain bought as an investment is a domain name).
- Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in I2 (never levels): brand and maker (platform, storefront, foundry, registry), region, era, price band and quality tier, edition (limited, signed), connectivity (DRM, online-only), scene and subculture, motive (collecting, investment), how acquired (bought, ripped, converted, own-made, redeemed code), user (child), market (personal, company), material (n/a). Content genre and subject are expressly barred.
- C16 (CV-33): the data holds kinds, named forms, variants and the ledgers only. A particular registered domain name, a particular token ID, the wear float or StatTrak count of one skin, and one file's provenance are individual-item facts and are not stored.

### Retailers and marketplaces (US, France, Germany, Japan, global)

- **Music downloads, US and France [S08, S09].** Bandcamp sells one item and lets the buyer pick the file format afterwards from a dropdown: MP3 V0, MP3 320, FLAC, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, ALAC, WAV, AIFF [S08]. Qobuz groups its paid downloads first by quality class (24-bit up to 192 kHz; 24-bit up to 96 kHz; 16-bit/44.1 kHz lossless; 320 or 128 kbps lossy) and then names the formats inside each (WAV, AIFF, ALAC, FLAC for the lossless classes; MP3, AAC for lossy), with DSD (1-bit, 2.8 to 22.5 MHz, sold as DSD64 to DSD512) and DXD (24-bit/352.8 kHz) as extra classes; every file is stated to be DRM-free [S09].
- **Music downloads, Japan [S10].** A Japanese audio-press guide lists the three main hi-res shops by the formats they sell: e-onkyo (FLAC, WAV, DSD only), mora (FLAC, DSD, AAC), OTOTOY (FLAC, ALAC, WAV, AAC, DSD); all DRM-free, and the shops differ mainly by which formats and which catalogue they carry [S10].
- **Audiobooks [S13, S14, S15].** Libro.fm delivers a DRM-free zip of MP3 files or a single-file M4B, or plays them in its own app [S13]. Audible's formats AA, AAX and AAXC are DRM-protected and tied to the account [S15]. audiobook.jp is audio only (an occasional PDF supplement), bought per title or through an unlimited plan or monthly tickets, and played in its app with repeat downloads [S14]. The delivery form (open single file, open multi-file set, account-locked) is what separates the products.
- **E-books, Germany, France, China [S22, S23, S24, S25].** The German trade FAQ names EPUB (reflowable; novels) and PDF (fixed layout; technical books) as the two main formats, and treats protection as a separate axis: none, personalised watermark, or Adobe DRM (the .acsm file is a licence stub, not the book) [S22]. tolino is an open system of several booksellers whose readers take EPUB, PDF and TXT, Adobe-DRM capable [S24]. Fnac names the format in the product title ("ebook (ePub)") and links its Kobo subscription and readers; the page gave no deeper tree [S23]. A Chinese Kindle guide explains the formats by device: mobi (KF7 and KF8), azw3 (described as mobi in a DRM shell) and epub (universal, readable in every reader app except Kindle's) [S25].
- **Fonts [S12, S27, S30].** MyFonts, an open marketplace for foundries, sells a typeface by licence type: Desktop, Company Desktop, Webfont, App, Electronic Doc, Digital Ad/Email and Server, each priced by users, pageviews, publications or servers [S12]. Adobe Fonts offers a Classification filter of eight classes plus separate expressive tags [S30]. Adobe Stock keeps fonts outside its asset types (they are licensed separately) [S27].
- **Stock and creative assets [S26, S27].** Adobe Stock lists asset types photos, vectors, illustrations, templates, 3D assets, videos, motion graphics templates and audio tracks, with file formats per type (JPEG and PNG; AI, EPS; MOV; OBJ, EXR/HDR, MDL for 3D models, lights and materials; PSDT, AIT, INDT for templates) [S27]. A Japanese guide to BOOTH digital goods lists backgrounds, decorations, frames, textures, brushes for CLIP STUDIO and Photoshop, Photoshop and Canva templates, VRoid textures, 3D models, fonts, e-books and PDFs, and digital planners, with listing fields file format, resolution, supported software, commercial use and redistribution [S26].
- **Digital films [S20].** Movies Anywhere is a locker that synchronises film licences bought from linked retailers and digital copies redeemed from disc-bundle codes; it is a rights record (KeyChest), not a file store [S20]. Its history includes a rival locker (UltraViolet) closed in 2019 and a retailer's films removed and restored in 2025, which shows a locker entitlement can be withdrawn [S20].
- **In-game and platform items [S04, S18, S19].** Steam's community items are trading cards (and foil cards), badges, emoticons, profile backgrounds and coupons, acquired by crafting, trading, the Market or the Points Shop [S04]. The Fortnite fan database filters by item type first (Outfit, Back Bling, Pickaxe, Glider, Emote, Emoji, Wrap, Loading Screen, Skydiving Trails, Spray, Toy, Pet, Lobby Music, Banner, Shoes, Sidekick, Car Trail, Car Boost, LEGO Outfit and Kit, Bundle), then rarity (Common to Legendary and licensed series), then season [S18]. The CS2 database and trade site lists weapon skins by weapon, knives, gloves, cases, collections, stickers (regular, tournament, capsules), agents, graffiti and boxes, pins and capsules, charms, music kits and boxes, patches, keys and a pass, 16,400 items in all, with wear, StatTrak and rarity as properties [S19].
- **Domain names [S21].** Sedo describes premium domains as of two types: registry premiums (price tiers set by the TLD operator, sometimes with higher renewals) and aftermarket names offered by their current owners [S21].
- **NFTs [S05, S06].** OpenSea's help page says it supports two types, 1/1 NFTs (ERC-721) and semi-fungible NFTs (ERC-1155, quantity above one, "great for gaming items and membership passes"), and lists examples: digital art, collectibles, virtual reality items, crypto domain names, ownership records for physical items [S06]. Its browse filters are use categories: Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships, Photography, Music [S05].

### Enthusiast communities and wikis

- **Wikipedia comparison of e-book formats [S03]** compares formats on feature columns (DRM, images, tables, sound, interactivity, word wrap, open standard, annotation, bookmarks, video) and lists Comic book archive (.cbr .cbz .cb7 .cbt .cba), DjVu, Apple Books (.ibook), Kindle (.azw .azw3 .kfx), PDF, EPUB, FictionBook, plain text, RTF, DOC and DOCX, and Apabi (.xeb .ceb), "a popular format for Chinese e-books"; it notes the discontinued Microsoft LIT format. The page is organised by format, with vendors as a separate list.
- **Chinese Kindle guide [S25], Phile-web [S10], fnbr.co [S18], cs.trade [S19]** are the fan-level views: buyers there ask "what is the difference between mobi and azw3", "can Kindle read epub" [S25], "which shop sells which hi-res format" [S10], and "which item types exist" [S18, S19]. Their questions are about format compatibility, not about content.
- **Typography guides [S11, S29].** Monotype's 2026 guide says a font format "is not the same thing as a license": TTF and OTF are desktop containers, WOFF and WOFF2 web wrappers, and a variable font "is not a fifth format" but an OpenType capability [S11]. Google's own font guide says its "genre" classes (serif, sans serif, slab serif, monospaced, script, handwriting, display) "aren't clearly delineated, nor are they mutually exclusive" [S29].

### Collectors and the resale market

- **Skins and in-game items [S19, S18, S04].** The CS2 trade site and the Fortnite database carry price, wear, rarity and season as properties of an item type, not as places in a tree [S18, S19]. Steam's Community Market page could not be read (blocked), but Steamworks documents the item types sold there [S04].
- **Domain aftermarket [S21]** and **NFT marketplaces [S05, S06]** organise by sale route and use category (Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships), not by what the object is.
- **Hi-res download shops [S09, S10]** are the nearest thing to a collector market for files; they compare by sampling rate and format.

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **IANA Root Zone Database [S01]** lists each TLD with Status (Active, Retired, Reserved), Type (Generic, Country Code, Special Use, Test) and writing system; ICANNWiki adds that IANA's finer labels are generic, generic-restricted, sponsored, country-code and infrastructure, with 22 gTLDs before 2012 and 1,241 strings delegated from the 2012 round [S28]. JPRS divides JP names into general-use .JP, prefecture-type, and attribute-type names by registrant class (CO.JP companies, OR.JP organisations, NE.JP network services, AC.JP higher education, ED.JP schools, GO.JP government, GR.JP groups, LG.JP local government, AD.JP), with the regional type closed in 2012 [S02].
- **ethereum.org token standards [S07]** separate fungible tokens (ERC-20) from NFT standards ERC-721 ("a deed for artwork or a song") and ERC-1155 (multi-token, utility tokens and NFTs in one contract).
- **Library of Congress Recommended Formats Statement [S16, S17, S31, S32]** is the nearest thing to a cross-medium standard classification: Textual Works (print, digital, electronic serials), Still Image Works (photographs, other graphic images), Moving Image Works (motion pictures, video), Audio Works, Musical Scores (print, digital), Datasets, Geospatial, Design and 3D (including scanned 3D objects), Software and Video Games, Web Archives, Email [S16]. Within a medium it ranks formats as preferred or acceptable: for digital photographs TIFF, JPEG2000, PNG and JPEG preferred, Photoshop, DNG and camera raw (.nef .crw .arw .iiq) and GIF acceptable; for vector images SVG preferred, EPS and PDF acceptable [S31]; for file-based video IMF, FFV1 in Matroska and ProRes in QuickTime preferred and MP4 only a viewing proxy [S32]. It requires files to carry no DRM [S17, S32].
- **Adobe Stock's asset-type list [S27]** and **Steamworks' item documents [S04]** act as trade vocabularies for their own sub-markets.

### Museums and reference works

- The LoC statement above is a library-preservation reference. Wikipedia pages on e-book formats and Movies Anywhere [S03, S20], ICANNWiki [S28], Google's Fonts Knowledge [S29] and Adobe's blog [S30] are the reference works. No museum collection page for born-digital items was fetched; this source type is covered by the Library of Congress.

### Where the sources agree and where they split the same object

- **Agree.** Every cross-medium source divides first by medium (text, still image, moving image, audio, 3D, fonts): the LoC contents [S16], Adobe Stock's asset types [S27], BOOTH product ideas [S26]. Within a medium the next cut is a format or quality class [S03, S09, S31]. Fonts, domain names and tokens each have a dedicated standard and a different unit (licence, TLD, token standard).
- **Split.** (1) Protection: the German trade FAQ treats DRM as an independent axis on the same EPUB or PDF [S22], while Audible's AAX and the Chinese azw3 explanation make the protected wrapper part of the format [S15, S25]. (2) Fonts: MyFonts divides purchases by licence, Monotype says format is not licence, and Google admits its genre classes overlap [S11, S12, S29]. (3) Game items: fan databases divide by item type inside one game [S18, S19], Steam's own items are platform-wide and sit outside any game [S04], and OpenSea's "Gaming" is a use category [S05]. (4) NFTs: the standards divide by token form [S06, S07], the marketplaces by use [S05]. (5) Qobuz groups by quality class and then format [S09]; Bandcamp offers all formats of one item [S08].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Medium first, then format or compatibility, then quality or rights.** The evidence, source by source:

- **Cross-medium navigation is by medium.** The Library of Congress contents run Textual, Still Image, Moving Image, Audio, Scores and so on [S16]; Adobe Stock opens with photos, design assets (vectors, illustrations, templates), 3D assets, then motion and audio [S27]; BOOTH sellers think in backgrounds, brushes, templates, 3D models, fonts, e-books [S26]. No source fetched opens with the file extension across media, and none opens with genre.
- **Inside a medium, the buyer's next question is "will it work on my device or app", i.e. format.** The Chinese Kindle guide asks "why does mobi become azw3 on Kindle" and "can Kindle read epub" [S25]; the German FAQ says EPUB suits novels and PDF suits technical books [S22]; Phile-web compares shops by the formats they sell [S10]; Bandcamp shows one item and a format dropdown [S08].
- **Quality and rights come after format.** Qobuz names the quality class (24-bit/192 kHz down to 320 kbps) before the format [S09]; the German FAQ then adds protection (none, watermark, Adobe DRM) [S22]; Libro.fm and Qobuz advertise DRM-free as a selling point [S09, S13]; the Library of Congress requires no DRM [S17, S32].
- **For fonts the licence scope is the purchase decision** (users, pageviews, publications, servers) once the typeface is chosen [S12], and Monotype stresses that the file format does not settle it [S11].
- **For in-game items the game comes first for the buyer but the item type comes first on every database filter.** Fortnite's filter order is item type, rarity, season [S18]; the CS2 database opens with weapons, knives, gloves, cases, stickers, agents [S19]; Steam's community items are platform-wide [S04].
- **For domain names the TLD class is the first filter** (IANA: status, type, writing system [S01]; JPRS: registrant class [S02]) and the aftermarket adds registry premium against aftermarket [S21].
- **For NFTs the standards give token form** (1/1 against semi-fungible) [S06, S07] while marketplaces filter by use (Art, PFPs, Gaming, Memberships) [S05].

**Reading.** Owners and buyers decide first by what the held data is for (read, listen, watch, view, build other work with, equip in a game or platform account, register), then by the compatibility format, then by rights state and quality. That purpose is therefore the sub-family axis. The kind axes differ by sub-family because the next comparison differs: layout form for text (S22, S03), compression for music (S08, S09; decided as an attribute of one music kind, I-8), format-family for still images (S31), the typeface for fonts (licence scope is chosen after it, S12), asset class for production assets (S26, S27), role for game items (S18, S19), eligibility for domain names (S01, S02). Device compatibility is recorded at named-form level through the compatibility ledger (C13), not as a kind.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (decided, I-2): purpose of the held data, then a stated leaf axis, then compared class, then specific format or design

| Level | Axis |
|---|---|
| Sub-family | **what the held data is for**: read (text); heard (audio); watched (moving image); viewed (still image); built with (fonts and production assets); used inside a game or platform account (game and platform items); registered (names and tokens). Decided (I-2): level 1 as restated here, not holding form first (Candidate B). |
| Leaf | per sub-family: `leaf = null` in text, moving-image and still-image sub-families (12 kinds or fewer, C5(b)); kind of recording in audio (music, spoken word, podcast; a form of medium, not a genre; I-9); asset class in fonts and production assets; role of the item in the game or platform account in game and platform items; register (DNS or blockchain) in names and tokens |
| Kind | the class buyers compare: layout form (text); one kind each for music (I-8), audiobooks and video (I-10), fonts (I-11) and podcasts; file-format family (still image); asset class (production assets); item role (game and platform items); TLD eligibility class (domain names; I-12); token form (NFTs) |
| Named form | the specific format or codec (FLAC, AZW3, TIFF), typeface family, TLD (.jp), item design (a CS2 knife finish), token standard (ERC-721) or NFT collection |

Shared attributes, never levels: `rights_state` (open file, personalised watermark, account-locked, locker licence, token); `unit` (track, album, volume, title, episode, season, set); `compression` (lossy, lossless compressed, uncompressed; I-8); quality values (bitrate, bit depth, sampling rate, resolution) with the standard named (C4(c) class (i)); font file format (OTF, TTF, WOFF2) and the variable capability (I-11); source of the file (bought, redeemed, converted, own-made, ripped; Appendix A "how acquired"); language and script (IDN); region; `delivery` (the one C13 list, CV-30; I2 uses download, app store, web or hosted and stream); `preservation_rank` (I-23, section 5); game or platform (compatibility ledger, C13); rarity (dated `platform_tag`, C4(c) class (vi)); licence scope for assets (a C3 variant of type `licence scope` for fonts, an attribute for single stock assets); genre and subject (barred). Wear of one skin is an individual-item fact and is not stored (C16, I-14).

**Precedence sentences inside I2** (from review I2-1, I2-5 and I2-8; the three for level 1 adopted with I-2, the token sentence confirmed by I2-O1 and K-71(k)):
- *"A file sold as a pack, template, brush, texture, model, preset or sample for making other work is I2.5, whatever its file format; a single image, clip or track held to be looked at, watched or heard is filed by its medium (I2.2, I2.3, I2.4), even when it was licensed as a stock asset."*
- *"An avatar, outfit or item that is equipped inside a game or platform account is I2.6; the same model held as a file to import into tools is I2.5."*

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**

2. *A digital film licence with a downloaded file.* Both are the kind "digital video"; the locker entitlement and the DRM-free file differ by `rights_state` and named form (null against MP4), and only the file survives the locker closing [S20]. 3. *A Steam trading card or a Fortnite Wrap.* Steam items live on a platform, not inside a game [S04], so the sub-family reads "game and platform items"; wraps and trails cross roles. The leaf axis "role" blurs at the edges (a Glider is a tool, a vehicle skin or an expression); the tree places each by the role its source names.
4. *A blockchain domain name.* It is a token (alias table: "crypto domain name" is an NFT, not a DNS name [S06]): I2.7 > Tokens > Unique (1/1) NFT. An NFT that is a membership pass or event ticket is the token; its access is filed on its own terms (section 9).
5. *A stock asset.* A single stock photo, vector, clip or audio track is filed by its medium with its licence as an attribute; a stock template, brush or 3D model is I2.5 (first precedence sentence above). The asset-library plan is I3.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Choice: spec-ranked, but as measured attributes, not tiers.** For sampled media (music, video, images) a measurable specification orders the files: bitrate and mode (MP3 V0, 320, 128 kbps), bit depth and sampling rate (16-bit/44.1 kHz, 24-bit/96 kHz, 24-bit/192 kHz, DXD 24-bit/352.8 kHz), 1-bit DSD rates (DSD64 to DSD512) [S08, S09, S10]; resolution (HD, 4K) for video [S27]. Three sources in three regions (US, France, Japan) agree on the ladder for music. Under C4(c) class (i) these are attributes with the standard named, not tier entries (CV-10), and they never become levels or kinds (which is why the draft's "studio-resolution" kind was removed). The hi-res view is generated from them (section 7). For other sub-families there is no tier: the order is a matter of preference.

- **Consensus-ranked orderings that exist.** The Library of Congress ranks formats as preferred or acceptable for preservation (TIFF, JPEG2000, PNG, JPEG ahead of raw and PSD) [S31, S32]. Decided (I-23, departing from the memo's "hold for a second source" under the owner's one-source rule): `preservation_rank` (preferred, acceptable) is an attribute of the format named forms, entered from one fetched LoC page, with no second source; provisional until attested (the LoC Recommended Formats pages, fetched at stage-3 harvest). It is a preservation view, never a tier or a level.
- **Platform-set labels.** Fortnite rarities (Common to Legendary, licensed series), CS2 rarity [S18, S19] and registry premium price tiers [S21] are platform labels: dated `platform_tag` entries (C4c), never tiers. Wear and StatTrak describe one item and are not stored (C16); a CS2 wear class is not stored either, as variant or otherwise (I-14).
- **Maker ladders.** No maker ladder was found in I2's sources. Font licence scopes are not a ladder; they are `licence scope` variants without rank. Tier entries on variant or plan rows (C4(b), CV-11) are allowed but have no I2 use yet. A scheme found at harvest enters on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10).
- **Price band.** Applies to priced purchases using the shared USD bands of C4(d) (CV-13, CV-14), not a level. Decided (CV-14, I-12): a domain name is banded at its TLD's standard annual renewal price, with registry premium as a `platform_tag`; a font is banded per `licence scope` variant; free items band "0 (free)"; recurring prices are annualised; NFTs and albums by dated list price.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Choice: platform.** The maker or operator sets what works with what: Amazon's own Kindle formats [S25], Apple's .ibook [S03], Audible's account-bound formats [S15], Adobe DRM and the .acsm licence stub in Germany [S22], the tolino retailer alliance [S24], Movies Anywhere's linked retailers [S20], Steam and the game publishers [S04, S18, S19], the Ethereum token standards [S07] and the registry operators (IANA lists a TLD manager for each; JPRS runs .jp) [S01, S02]. Identity matters in two pockets: font foundries (MyFonts is a marketplace of foundries [S12]; Adobe Fonts offers a Classification filter and tags [S30]) and NFT collections ("PFPs and identity", membership perks [S05]).

**Decided.**
- A **compatibility ledger** (C13, CV-29, CV-30) first, with the v1 columns `platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `platform_type`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `region`, `introduced`, `status`, `sources`, and `compatibility_relation` rows (a format named form "supports" or "requires" a platform). Rows are platforms: Kindle, Kobo, tolino, Apple Books, Apabi reader †, Adobe DRM, Audible, Movies Anywhere, Steam, Fortnite and CS2 (as game platforms for items), LINE † and Telegram † (as messaging platforms for sticker packs), Ethereum, the DNS root. ## 8. Order inside a leaf

Decided order (C7 as amended by CV-21; CV-20): kinds canonical form first, then the sub-family's stated secondary key; named forms non-maker first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically, then maker-bound named forms by maker (ledger `name` order), first-introduction year, then alphabetically; variants by `variant_code` or year. The bracketed lists in the worked tree (section 3) are examples, not stored order. Generated views give other orders (by platform, by compression, by rarity, alphabetical).
- **Text and images:** kinds in tree order (reflowable before fixed-layout; raw before derived formats). Named forms: open, non-maker formats first (EPUB, FB2; TIFF, PNG; SVG), then proprietary maker-bound formats by maker (KFX, AZW3, MOBI under their maker; .ibook; Apabi). Reason: the open form is the canonical one and carries least lock-in; this is the C7 rule, not a separate I2 key.
- **Music:** one kind (I-8); named forms open first (MP3, AAC, Ogg Vorbis, FLAC, WAV, AIFF, DSD, by first-attested year), then maker-bound (ALAC, WMA Lossless †). The buyer's comparison from lossy to lossless compressed to uncompressed is a generated view on `compression` (S08, S09).
- **Audiobooks and video:** named forms open formats first (M4B, MP3; MP4, MKV), then proprietary (AA, AAX, AAXC), then null (locker or app library).
- **Fonts:** named forms (families) are all maker-bound: by foundry, then year, then alphabetical (C7). Licence-scope variants in the order a cart prints them (Desktop, Company Desktop, Webfont, App, Electronic Doc, Digital Ad/Email, Server) [S12], the variants' `variant_code` order where the foundry gives one.
- **Game and platform items:** leaves in the order wear, tools, expression, containers, collectibles; named forms (item designs, maker-bound) by maker (game publisher or platform holder), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically; the by-game view is generated from the C13 rows (I-14).
- **Domain names:** kinds open generic, country-code, restricted (IANA type before eligibility); TLDs in C7 order, with an alphabetical view generated [S01].
- **Tokens:** kinds 1/1 before semi-fungible; named forms the non-maker token standards first by year of adoption, then NFT collections by issuer (C2(d)).

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure.** Candidate A (I-2): seven sub-families by what the held data is for (read, heard, watched, viewed, built with, used in a game or platform account, registered); a leaf only where it carries a distinct axis (audio: kind of recording, I-9; fonts and production assets: asset class; game and platform items: role; names and tokens: register), `leaf = null` elsewhere (C5(b)); kind = the class buyers compare (section 4b): e-book and document kinds by layout form with formats as named forms and Kindle, Apple Books, Apabi and tolino as C13 rows (I-7), one "digital music file" kind with compression and resolution as attributes (I-8), one audiobook kind and one moving-image kind with rights state and unit as attributes (I-10), one "digital font" kind with the typeface family as named form, licence scope a `licence scope` variant and the variable capability an attribute (I-11), still images by file-format family, production assets by asset class with the sample, preset, MIDI, AI-model, planner and sticker-pack kinds added (I-13; AI model weights are I2), game and platform items by role with the game a C13 row and a view (I-14), domain names by TLD eligibility class with each TLD a named form and premium or aftermarket a platform tag (I-12), and NFTs by token form, a token staying I2 whatever it depicts or unlocks (I2-O1); named form = format, typeface family, TLD, token standard, NFT collection or item design, harvested from attested catalogues in stage 3 without a cap, large sets in registers (CV-7, CV-45).
