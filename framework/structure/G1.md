## 0. Category

**Code and name:** G1 Vehicles (domain G, Vehicles & Property).

**Membership test (placement key step 11):** "Made **mainly to transport people, animals or goods from place to place** on land, water, rail, snow or air, however moved (engine, motor, muscle, wind, animal or tow): cars, motorcycles, bicycles and e-bikes, e-scooters and other powered personal transport, boats, canoes, kayaks and sailing craft, aircraft including sailplanes, trailers and horseboxes, touring caravans and motorhomes, horse-drawn vehicles, prams and strollers". Category description: "everything made mainly to transport people, animals or goods, with its fitted parts and electronics."

**Notes that bind (S1b), from step 11:** every boat, canoe, kayak, bicycle and aircraft is G1, including those made for racing. Unpowered boards, skates, kick scooters, skis, sledges, hang gliders and paragliders are E2; powered paragliders are aircraft. A machine that carries its operator while it does other work (tractor, ride-on mower, excavator) goes by that work. Hand carts, sack trucks and dollies are C1; wheelbarrows and garden carts are C2; shopping trolleys and trolley bags are A4; wheeled luggage is A4. Ride-on toys, children's tricycles and balance bikes are E1; adult tricycles are G1. Baby carriers and slings are A4. The v0.7 key adds K-89 to K-98 to this note (e-scooters, powered boards, children's pedal bicycles, racing and off-road machines, utility carriers, trailers, pet strollers, parachutes, drones, mobility scooters).

**Rules that touch G1:**
- Step 4: a vehicle or vessel made to stay in one place as a dwelling (houseboat, static caravan, tiny house on wheels) is G2.
- S3, parts follow the whole: "a child car seat to Vehicles". Step 30 B7 note: wall-box chargers for electric vehicles are G1 under S3. Step 30 B8: locks made for a bicycle or vehicle are G1 under S3. Fuels are C8 and cleaning products B6 whatever they are used in. Anything worn on the body is step 17 (A1/A2/E2), not a part.
- Appendix A and S4: region, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, **market (domestic, commercial, professional)**, installation, **power source and technology (manual, electric, gas, battery)**, occasion, motive and how acquired never split an inner level. The last two bold items matter for G1; decisions G-2 and G-3 settle them (a motorsailer is a kind with `propulsion`; trucks and buses are kinds by body, not a market; rule T, CV-24).
- Category sentence for the family: G1 is "with its fitted parts and electronics", which is why section 3 ends with a parts sub-family.

**Precedence sentences that already exist elsewhere and bind G1 (restated, not new):** A4 memo: baby carriers go to A4, prams and strollers to G1. B10 memo: a trailer, horsebox, carriage or wheeled pet stroller is G1 (decided: K-95). C7 memo, P5 and P6: a tipper, car or horse trailer made only to carry is G1 (step 11 is tried before step 12); ATVs and utility vehicles are G1 by the same reasoning (decided: K-93, K-94). E2 memo, P6 and P7: a bicycle, kayak, canoe or boat is G1; an electric kick scooter is G1; a bike pump, rack or bottle cage follows the bicycle; a turbo trainer holding a real bicycle follows the bicycle. B3 and D2 memos: a vehicle's own lighting is part of the vehicle; a portable or aftermarket sat-nav is D2.

### 1.2 Trade classifications, regulation and standards

- **Google product taxonomy** [GPC]. "Vehicles & Parts" has two children: Vehicle Parts & Accessories and Vehicles. Vehicles has three: Aircraft; Motor Vehicles (Cars, Trucks & Vans; Golf Carts; Motorcycles & Scooters; Off-Road and All-Terrain Vehicles with ATVs & UTVs and Go Karts & Dune Buggies; Recreational Vehicles; Snowmobiles); Watercraft (Motor Boats, Personal Watercraft, Sailboats, Yachts). Fifteen categories in all. Shopify's crosswalk to this taxonomy adds Heavier-Than-Air and Lighter-Than-Air aircraft and Commercial, Service & Utility and Motor Sports vehicles (†: no row in the sources file, so this claim is unverified and carries no weight in the recommendation). Bicycles do not appear in this branch. Two further facts were seen in search excerpts only: travel trailers sit under Parts > Vehicle Storage & Cargo > Motor Vehicle Trailers, and Merchant Center vehicle ads require brand, model, year, mileage, colour, condition and VIN.
- **UNECE consolidated resolution** [UNECE] and **EU** [EC]. Four categories: L (two, three or four wheels, L1 to L7: mopeds, motorcycles, quads, light quadricycles), M (at least four wheels, passengers: M1 up to eight seats plus driver; M2 more than eight seats and up to 5 t; M3 above 5 t), N (goods: N1 up to 3.5 t, N2, N3) and O (trailers: O1 up to 750 kg, O2 up to 3,500 kg, O3 up to 10,000 kg, O4 above; also [ESC]). G is an off-road suffix (N1G). A **motor caravan** is a "special purpose M1" vehicle with seats and table, sleeping, cooking and storage. M1 bodywork codes run AA saloon, AB hatchback, AC station wagon (estate), AD coupé, AE convertible, AF multi-purpose vehicle, referring to ISO 3833:1977 terms. The standard is road-only and mass-, wheel- and function-based.
- **Auto Care Association VCdb** [VCDB]. A normalised "Year, Make, Model" fitment database with 175,500+ vehicle applications, 2.4 million configurations and 16,500+ off-highway vehicles and equipment, covering light, medium and heavy vehicles, powersports and off-highway equipment. It is licensed by subscription. It shows that the parts trade keys on make, model and year.
- **Cycles** [ISO4210]. ISO 4210-2:2023 covers young-adult bicycles (saddle height 635 to under 750 mm), city and trekking, mountain and racing bicycles including folding. It excludes delivery bikes, recumbents, tandems, BMX and competition/stunt bikes; smaller bikes fall under ISO 8098 or toy rules. E-bikes [EBK, a secondary explainer]: in the EU and UK an electrically power-assisted cycle (EN 15194) is limited to 250 W continuous, assistance cut-off at 25 km/h and pedal assist only (walk assist to 6 km/h), and counts as a bicycle; assistance to 45 km/h is a speed pedelec and a moped (L1e-B); the US uses Class 1, 2 and 3 (pedal assist 20 mph; throttle 20 mph; pedal assist 28 mph; up to 750 W).
- **Boats** [RCD]. Directive 2013/53/EU covers recreational craft of 2.5 to 24 m hull length and personal watercraft. Design categories A, B, C, D by wind force and wave height are printed on the builder's plate and are "a design classification and not a navigational authorisation". The Directive excludes canoes, kayaks, gondolas, pedalos, surfboards, racing boats, historical craft and own-build craft.
- **Aircraft** [FAA]. For pilots the FAA lists categories (airplane, glider, rotorcraft, lighter-than-air, powered-lift, powered parachute, weight-shift control, and rocket) with classes inside (airplane: single-engine land, single-engine sea, multi-engine land, multi-engine sea; rotorcraft: helicopter, gyroplane; lighter-than-air: airship, balloon). "Type" means a specific make and model (Boeing 737). "Category" also has two other meanings: aircraft certification category (normal, utility, acrobatic, transport; special: experimental, light sport, restricted) and instrument-approach category A to E. The first axis in aviation is therefore how the aircraft flies.
- **US sizes and others** [W-CLASS]. Car classification schemes include the European Commission segments (A mini, B small, C medium, D large, E executive, F luxury, J sport utility, M multi-purpose, S sport coupé), Euro NCAP classes, US EPA classes by interior volume, NHTSA by curb weight, IIHS by footprint, the Chinese national standard GB/T 3730.1 (2021; sedans by length, hatchbacks, SUVs and MPVs by wheelbase), and Japan's kei class. ISO 3833 defines the terms.

### 1.3 Enthusiast and reference guides

- **Cars** [W-BODY]. Wikipedia lists current styles (buggy, convertible/cabriolet, coupé, coupé SUV, crossover, fastback, hatchback, hearse, Kammback, liftback, limousine, microvan, minivan/MPV, notchback, panel van, panel truck, pickup, roadster, sedan/saloon, shooting-brake, SUV, station wagon/estate, targa top, ute) and says styles "vary depending on intended use, market position, location, and the era they were made". Regional synonyms are many (saloon/sedan, estate/wagon, berlina/berline, break, bakkie, ute).
- **Motorcycles** [W-MOTO]. "There is no universal system for classifying all types of motorcycles"; "some authors argue" for six categories (standard, cruiser, touring, sport, off-road, dual-purpose), and scooters, mopeds, minibikes, pocket bikes and three-wheelers "are often excluded". Sport-body and legal definitions are separate.
- **Bicycles** [W-BIKE]. Bicycles "can be categorized by function, number of riders, gearing, sport, means of propulsion, position of the rider or frame type"; "the categories are not mutually exclusive". The list covers cargo, tandem, recumbent, unicycle, tricycle, velomobile, cycle rickshaw, e-bike and moped.
- **Small boats** [BSM]. A guide groups small boats (under about 25 ft, trailerable) as paddlecraft (kayaks sit-in and sit-on-top and fishing; canoes; rowboats; SUPs), inflatables (low pressure about 3 PSI, high pressure up to 15), dinghies ("two meanings": a small racing sailboat, or any yacht tender), RIBs, PWCs, aluminium and jon boats, and trailerable cruisers. It names models and makers (Hake Seaward 25, Pacific Seacraft Flika, Farrier F-22, Corsair F-24, MacGregor 26, Ranger Tugs, C-Dory 25, Lowe Boats).
- **Baby transport** [W-BABY]. Wheeled devices are "prams" (newborn lying, facing the pusher) and "strollers" (sitting, forward, to about three years). UK and US terms differ: pram/baby carriage, pushchair/buggy/stroller; a buggy in the US is a quad. Carrycot, travel system, twin and jogging variants exist, and strollers can be "a highly visible symbol" of class and parenting philosophy. On-body carriers are listed separately (A4).
- **Handcars** [W-HAND]. Pump and push trolleys, draisines, railbikes, velocipedes and "jiggers" are one object under many regional names, built by Sheffield, Kalamazoo and Buda, and now used for tourism (France's vélorail) and vintage-railway racing.
- **Sailing classes** [W-470]. The 470 is a World Sailing International Class and a "strict one-design class" whose builders must be "Licensed Builders"; the template lists other classes (420, J/24, J/70, Melges 24). A one-design class is therefore a named form that several makers build.

### 1.4 Collectors, resale and museums

- **Collectors** [HAG, EBAY]. Hagerty says there is "not a lot of agreement" on age labels: vintage 1919 to 1930; antique (the Antique Automobile Club of America lets cars over 25 years old join its touring events) with the earlier "Brass Era"; "Full Classic" 1915 to 1948 on the Classic Car Club of America's approved list; and every US state sets its own historic-vehicle age (Michigan 26 years, Colorado 32). eBay offers Classics, Collector cars and Exotics as shopping lists. Age and status are labels on a car, not types of car.
- **Resale** [BUY]. A Japanese resale shop sorts bicycles by use group and names models inside each.
- **Museums** [SMG]. The Science Museum Group keeps railways as a separate theme and runs the National Railway Museum and Locomotion as separate sites. The page gave no vehicle-type tree, so museum evidence here is weak; museums are consistent with a mode-first reading (rail, road, air, water as different collections) but this memo does not rest on it.

### 1.5 Where the sources agree and where they split

**They agree:** (1) The first split is by mode or class of vehicle: Google (aircraft, motor vehicles, watercraft), eBay (cars and trucks, motorcycles, boats, powersports, other), mobile.de and AutoScout24 (cars, motorcycles, caravans, trucks, trailers), UNECE (L, M, N, O). (2) In every language the second split for cars is a short list of body styles (six to nine) and for bicycles a list of use types. (3) The model line (Golf, 911, F-150, Backroad, RMK) is the unit people name and the unit that parts and prices attach to [EBAY, VCDB, POL].

**They split the same objects differently:**
- **Pickups:** a car with a bed in eBay's body list (Truck), a goods vehicle N1 in UNECE, a "vehicle type" of its own in China (皮卡) and a body type in Japan.
- **Caravans and trailers:** a trailer (O) in UNECE, separate tabs in AutoScout24, one family with motorhomes in RVIA and mobile.de, a "Recreational Vehicle" in Google's Motor Vehicles but "Travel Trailers" under Parts > Vehicle Storage & Cargo (search excerpt only).
- **E-bikes:** a bicycle in EN 15194 and in Rose's cross-listing, a separate tab beside motorcycles in mobile.de, a motorcycle-facet value in Cycle Trader.
- **Personal watercraft and snowmobiles:** Watercraft (PWC) and Motor Vehicles (snowmobile) in Google, Powersports in eBay, "Pwc" and "Open Snowmobile" among motorcycles in Cycle Trader, excluded from RVs by RVIA.
- **Size against body:** China (Autohome), Europe (EC segments) and Japan (kei, compact) put a size class first; the US, Germany (Kombi, Limousine) and Wikipedia put body style first. Germany's list even includes "Kleinwagen".
- **Sail against power:** YachtWorld, Google and eBay split boats by sail and power first, yet YachtWorld lists "Motorsailer" under both and Decathlon splits kayaks by construction (inflatable or rigid) first.
- **Words:** US Class A/B/C (RVIA) and UK low-profile/overcab/A-class (PM) name different things; "A-class" and "Class A" are not the same; "dinghy" has two senses [BSM]; "buggy" means a stroller in the UK and a quad in the US [W-BABY].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Observed order of choice** (navigation order, filter order and buying-guide questions in the fetched sources):

1. **What it travels on (mode or medium).** Every marketplace has tabs for road cars, motorcycles, boats, aircraft, caravans or trailers before any other filter [MOB, AS24, EBAY, CT, YW, CTRL, GPC]. This is the only axis that never changes between countries.
2. **The legal class and licence gate.** For bicycles and light vehicles the first practical question is whether the machine counts as a bicycle, moped or motorcycle: 250 W and 25 km/h for an EPAC, 45 km/h for a speed pedelec, the kei limits (660 cc, 3.4 m), the O1 to O4 mass bands, the CE design category A to D [EBK, GOO, ESC, RCD]. These are measured specifications or classes a jurisdiction assigns, so they are attributes (design standards) or C17 ledger rows (legal classes), never levels.
3. **Use or terrain.** Buyers say what they will do: commute, family, leisure, fish, race, cross snow [GOO, MOB, ROSE, POL, PM]. Rose, Polaris and the Japanese bicycle guide organise their whole range by use. Use is therefore the first axis for bicycles, snowmobiles, kayaks and caravans, but it is not stable across makers (a "trail" bike differs between brands).
4. **Body or form.** For cars this is the body-style list (eBay nine, mobile.de seven, Goo-net eight, ISO and UNECE six codes AA to AF).
5. **Size.** Euro segments, Autohome levels, kei and compact in Japan, boat length, trailer mass.
6. **Make and model.** The model line is the unit of comparison and of parts fit [EBAY, VCDB], and people shop by it as often as by body.

**Conclusion for the tree.** The stable, source-agreed axis for the first inner split is **mode**; the stable axis for the next is the **class of vehicle as it is registered, sold or flown**; the axis after that is **body, form or discipline**; and the model line is the named form. Use, size, age and price are attributes, tags or views; legal class is a C17 ledger row.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates use the framework's fixed level names (C5a): category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, with at most four levels below the category. They differ in what the sub-family and leaf levels are.

### Candidate A (recommended): mode, then class of vehicle, then type, then model

- **Sub-family** (axis: **mode of travel**, the medium the vehicle is made to move through or on): Land vehicles; Rail vehicles; Watercraft; Aircraft; and, last, Parts and accessories made for one vehicle (the C5d sub-family, exempt from this axis).
- **Leaf** (axis stated per sub-family, C5c): in Land, the **kind of vehicle by body and drive, as it is sold** (cars; vans, trucks and buses; motorcycles and other powered light vehicles; off-road, snow and utility vehicles; bicycles; other human-powered cycles; trailers; caravans, motorhomes and campers; animal-drawn vehicles; hand-pushed carriers). The legal registration class, mass band and seat count are never the axis (C6, C17). In Watercraft, the **means of propulsion** (paddle or oar, wind, engine), which passes Rule T, C10(b): the mast and keel against the engine and helm change the form, and YachtWorld, Google and eBay list the types separately. In Aircraft, two leaves: airplanes (axis: engine class, which passes C10(b) because the engine changes the airframe and its installation and Controller and Trade-A-Plane list the classes separately [CTRL, TAP]) and other aircraft (axis: how it flies, the FAA category). Rail has about 7 kinds, so it skips the leaf level (C5b).
- **Kind** (axis per leaf): cars by **body style** in UNECE code order; vans, trucks and buses by body; motorcycles by type; bicycles by discipline or use; trailers by load carried; caravans by body and whether towed or driven; sailing craft by hull configuration and accommodation, motor craft by use class; aircraft by engine class (airplanes) or by how they fly (others).
- **Named form**: the **model line or class design** (Golf, Backroad, RMK, Piper PA-28 Cherokee, the 470 class). Maker-bound unless a class or generic design (C2). **There is one named-form level only (C5a):** a generic body sub-style (roadster, targa, crossover, shooting brake) is an attribute `body_substyle` on the maker-bound named form, not a second named-form level.
- **Example sub-families and leaves with 10 to 15 real kinds:** Cars: saloon, hatchback, estate, coupé, convertible, SUV, MPV, microcar. Motorcycles: standard, cruiser, sport, touring, adventure/dual-sport, scooter. Bicycles: road, gravel, cross-country MTB, downhill MTB, BMX, city, folding, mini-velo, cargo. Sailing craft: sailing dinghy, open keelboat, cruising monohull, beach catamaran, cruising multihull, motorsailer. Aircraft: single-engine piston airplane, turboprop airplane, sailplane, helicopter, hot-air balloon.
- **Evidence for it:** mode tabs on every marketplace [MOB, AS24, EBAY, CTRL, YW]; Google's three-way Aircraft / Motor Vehicles / Watercraft [GPC]; FAA categories by how the aircraft flies [FAA]; the Directive that already treats boats apart from road vehicles [RCD]; the museum split of rail from the rest [SMG]; body-style lists of 6 to 9 values in four countries [EBAY, MOB, GOO, W-BODY].
- **Where it breaks (edge objects):**
  1. **Motorsailer** (MacGregor 26 in [BSM]): YachtWorld lists "Motorsailer" under both Power and Sail [YW]. The propulsion leaf cannot give it two homes. Rule: a kind in the sailing leaf, with `propulsion = wind + combustion engine` and a primary flag (decided: G-2, CV-37).
  2. **Speed pedelec** (assist to 45 km/h): a moped (L1e-B) in the EU but a US Class 3 bicycle, and sold as an e-bike (mobile.de lists E-Bikes as its own tab) [EBK, MOB]. A rule that follows the local limit would give one object two homes. Rule (P2, section 9; decided G-4): a cycle made to be pedalled, with or without electric assist of any power or speed, is a bicycle filed by its discipline; its legal class goes to the C17 jurisdiction ledger. The earlier rule "a moped when over the local limit" is dropped.
  3. **Campervan conversion** (Panama P12 on a Ford Transit Custom [PM]): a van in law (M1 or N1) and in the parts trade, a leisure vehicle for its owner. The caravans leaf takes it on function, while the base van is recorded as `base_named_form_id` on the conversion's named form (C1, CV-3).
  4. **Kei car** (Suzuki Wagon R, Daihatsu Hijet [GOO, W-BODY]): a legal class that spans hatchback, van and truck. It goes to the C17 ledger; it is never a kind or a leaf.
  5. **Horse-drawn sleigh**: a sledge (E2 by the step 11 note) or an animal-drawn vehicle (G1). Decided (G-10, section 9, P6): a horse-drawn or machine-drawn sleigh is G1; a play or sport sledge is E2.
  6. **Cruising houseboat and narrowboat**: a hull with its own propulsion and steering is G1 under the step 11 wording and S5, while a moored non-navigating houseboat is G2 (step 4). The G2 memo's draft rule differed; K-60 and the key's step 4 settle it as P4 states (narrowboat and cruising houseboat G1, tiny house on wheels G2).

### Candidate B: propulsion first (muscle, engine, wind, animal, tow), then mode

- **Sub-family** (axis: how the vehicle is moved, the list in the step 11 wording): muscle-powered; engine-powered; wind-powered; animal-drawn; towed.
- **Leaf** (axis: mode): road, rail, water, air. **Kind**: type. **Named form**: model.
- **Example kinds (12):** muscle: bicycle, kayak, pram, handcar; engine: car, motorcycle, motorboat, helicopter, snowmobile; wind: sailing dinghy, sailplane; towed: caravan, trailer.
- **Evidence:** the step 11 wording itself, and Wikipedia's bicycle list, which has a "by means of propulsion" section [W-BIKE]. No marketplace, standard or museum in the fetched set uses propulsion as a first level.
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **E-bike**: Appendix A lists "power source (manual, electric, gas, battery)" as a modifier, so an e-bike must sit with the bicycle, not in an engine sub-family; the candidate cannot hold that.
  2. **Motorsailer, motor-assisted glider, pedal-drive kayak** [BSM, YW, DEC]: all have two means of propulsion.
  3. **Caravan against motorhome**: B puts them in different sub-families (towed against engine) while RVIA, mobile.de and eBay keep them together [RVIA, MOB, EBAY].
  4. **Pram, bicycle, kayak and handcar** share the muscle sub-family, which no buyer would browse.

### Candidate C: legal category first (UNECE and EU L, M, N, O), then type

- **Sub-family** (axis: regulatory vehicle category): L (two- to four-wheeled light vehicles), M (passenger vehicles with four or more wheels), N (goods vehicles), O (trailers), plus "not registered as road vehicles" (cycles, prams, boats, aircraft, rail).
- **Leaf** (axis: type or bodywork code). **Kind**: size or use class. **Named form**: model.
- **Example kinds (12):** L: motorcycle, scooter, moped, quad; M: saloon, hatchback, estate, MPV, bus; N: panel van, pickup, rigid truck; O: box trailer.
- **Evidence:** UNECE and EU categories [UNECE, EC, ESC]; UNECE bodywork codes AA to AF; the EU registration-document directives use these categories [EC].
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **Boats, aircraft and rail** fall outside the regulation, so one sub-family becomes a leftover for everything else.
  2. **Pickup (N1) against SUV (M1)** look the same to buyers and are listed together by eBay and Goo-net [EBAY, GOO].
  3. **Motorhome** is a special-purpose M1 and a caravan an O trailer [UNECE], yet owners compare them together [RVIA, MOB].
  4. **Kei** crosses M1 and N1, and **bicycles and strollers**, the most numerous vehicles owned, have no category at all.

**Recommendation (decided, G-1).** Candidate A. Use C's categories as rows in the C17 jurisdiction ledger (UNECE and EU L, M, N, O and the mass bands O1 to O4), and B's propulsion as an attribute (`propulsion`, with `assist` and `rig` as sub-attributes), so that the standards stay searchable without being a level.

**Where depth concentrates:** cars (body style by model line), bicycles (disciplines, then maker lines), motor craft (a class list of 55 values at YachtWorld [YW]), motorhomes and campervans (layouts and converters [PM]) and parts (outside this count).

**Leaf breadth.**
- *Broad (more than 15 kinds, or thousands of named forms):* Cars; Bicycles; Motorcycles and other powered light vehicles; Motor craft.
- *Medium (6 to 15 kinds):* Vans, trucks and buses; Off-road, snow and utility; Other human-powered cycles; Trailers; Caravans, motorhomes and campers; Hand-pushed carriers; Sailing craft; Airplanes; Other aircraft.
- *Narrow (fewer than 6 kinds):* Paddle and oar craft; Animal-drawn vehicles. Rail (about 7) has no leaf level.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule.** A kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers. For vehicles that is the **body style, discipline or class** inside a vehicle class; the **model line** is the named form beneath it; the **segment or size band, legal class and engine** are attributes or C17 ledger rows. A generic body sub-style is an attribute on the model, not a level.

**Evidence, by sub-family.**
- **Cars.** The body-style list is the filter every large marketplace shows: nine values at eBay, seven at mobile.de, eight (plus kei and others) at Goo-net, six codes in the UNECE annex [EBAY, MOB, GOO, UNECE]. Buyers then pick a make and model: eBay's "popular models" are model-level pages [EBAY]. So a saloon and an estate are separate kinds (they are separate filters and separate standard codes), while "compact" and "mid-size" are size bands of the same kind in Europe, China and Japan [W-CLASS, AH, GOO]. Roadster, targa, crossover and coupé SUV are sub-styles of the body style and are held as `body_substyle` with a generated view. Decided (G-5): `body_substyle` is an attribute with a generated view. Where Chinese and Japanese sources put size first, the size band becomes a region-tagged generated view (G-3).
- **Vans, trucks and buses.** Sources split by body (panel van, pickup, truck, bus), not by a 3.5 t line: eBay and Goo-net list the pickup beside cars, and Autohome lists pickup, micro van, light bus and micro truck as vehicle types [EBAY, GOO, AH]. UNECE N1 to N3 and M2 and M3 are mass-based legal classes and go to C17. Decided (G-9): one leaf, kinds by body, mass and seats as attributes, no split at 3.5 t; trucks and buses stay kinds (G-3).
- **Bicycles.** Rose, the Japanese resale guide and Wikipedia all separate road, gravel, mountain, city, folding and BMX, and the Japanese shop publishes a best-value guide for road bikes alone ("12 road bikes under 100,000 yen") [ROSE, BUY, W-BIKE]. Rose and Wikipedia split mountain bikes further into cross-country, trail, downhill and dirt, with distinct geometry and suspension travel [ROSE, W-BIKE], so these are kinds. Rose also splits road bikes (race, endurance) and gravel bikes (race, everyday, adventure), but these are gradients of one geometry, not separate sports. Decided (G-7): the road and gravel gradients are the attribute `geometry` (C2(b)), with models filed directly under the kind; mountain-bike disciplines are kinds.
- **Motorcycles.** Cycle Trader's thirty-value facet and Wikipedia's six categories agree on standard, cruiser, touring, sport and dual-sport as the comparable types [CT, W-MOTO]. Wikipedia also warns "there is no universal system".
- **Caravans and motorhomes.** The comparison level is the layout: low-profile against overcab against A-class [PM]; towable against driven [RVIA]. The three layouts, the campervan, the truck camper (a body made only for a pickup bed, C12(a)) and the fifth-wheel are therefore kinds (G-6). Floor plans such as a bunkhouse or a toy-hauler garage are attributes or variants (seen in search excerpts only); RVIA lists the sport utility RV with a built-in garage as a type [RVIA]. Decided (G-6): one leaf; US Class A, B and C are C9 homonym rows (CV-23).
- **Boats.** Buyers compare within a class: bowrider, centre console, cabin cruiser, trawler [YW]. YachtWorld's class lists mix hull, use, rig and size, so the kind list is a cleaned version of them: rig names (sloop, ketch, yawl, cutter, schooner) and size names (superyacht, mega yacht) become attributes (`rig`, `loa`). Sailing kinds follow hull configuration and accommodation (dinghy, open keelboat, cruising monohull, beach catamaran, cruising multihull, motorsailer); YachtWorld lists the beach catamaran apart from the catamaran [YW]. Inflatable against rigid kayaks are split first at Decathlon [DEC], and the RIB is already a kind defined by construction. Decided (G-8): construction (inflatable or rigid) is an attribute, so the first draft's two kayak kinds are one kind "Kayak" with `construction`; it becomes a kind split only where retailers in two regions split it, which decides the level and never whether an item is in. † Provisional until attested: a second-region retailer splitting inflatable and rigid kayaks or boats. The RIB stays a kind, as YachtWorld lists it as a boat class [YW].
- **Aircraft.** The buyer's category is engine class: single-engine piston, multi-engine piston, turboprop, jet, helicopter [CTRL, TAP]. Models within them (Cherokee, 172) carry the variants.
- **Prams and strollers.** The comparison is pram against stroller against jogging stroller; brand is a strong signal [W-BABY]. † Only one reference page supports this; one fetched baby-goods retailer or reference page per kind attests it at stage 3 (a second region is not needed).

**Depth differs by sub-family.** Cars: body style only (shallow), with depth in named forms. Bicycles: one level deeper at mountain bikes. Everything else: one kind level.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Chosen type: preference (price band), with measured specifications as attributes and legal classes in the C17 ledger; no consensus tier was found in the fetched sources.**

- **Spec-ranked and legal schemes that exist, and where they go.** None of these ranks quality.
  - *Design standards and design classifications an object is built to* are attributes `standard_met`, with the standard named (C4(b), C17; CV-36): ISO 4210 and ISO 8098 saddle-height bands [ISO4210]; EN 15194 design [EBK]; CE design category A to D, "a design classification and not a navigational authorisation" [RCD]; ISO 3833 body terms [W-CLASS].
  - *Size schemes* are size attributes (C6) with the scheme named: EC segments A to F, J, M, S; GB/T 3730.1 A00 to C/D; US EPA volume classes; NHTSA weight; IIHS footprint [W-CLASS, AH].
  - *Classes a jurisdiction assigns for registration, licence or tax* go to the C17 ledger (`subject_id`, `jurisdiction`, `scheme`, `class`, `criteria`, `source_url`, `date`): kei [GOO]; UNECE and EU L1 to L7, M1 to M3, N1 to N3, O1 to O4 [UNECE, EC, ESC]; EPAC, speed pedelec and US Class 1 to 3 [EBK]; FAA aircraft category and class and the certification categories (normal, utility, experimental, light sport) [FAA].
- **Consensus-ranked tiers:** none for whole vehicles in the fetched set. Euro NCAP appears in the classification table only as a class name [W-CLASS]; its star ratings were not fetched; if added they are third-party measured scores, tier entries (scale class ii) on the variant, with `test_version` and entering on one fetched source (C4(b), CV-10, CV-11).
- **Brand perception and status labels** are the nearest thing to a tier: eBay's "Exotics" list (Alfa Romeo, Aston Martin, Bentley, Bugatti, Ferrari, Jaguar, Lamborghini, Land Rover, Lotus) and "Classics" and "Collector cars" [EBAY]; the Classic Car Club of America's approved "Full Classics" list [HAG]; mobile.de's "Premium" and "Luxury cars" shortcuts [MOB]. These are dated `platform_tag` entries (C4c) or status tags, never tiers or levels.
- **Trim levels** (for example PRO RMK, RMK SP, INDY VR1 against INDY SP at Polaris; Rose Backroad AL against Backroad FF) are maker lines or variants (`trim`) under a model [POL, ROSE]. Polaris's own wording ("the value leader", "the ultimate") is a maker's description of its own range, not a ranking; a maker's own ladder of trims is a tier entry with `scheme` = the maker's name (C4(c) class iv, CV-12) and `line_rank`, entering on the maker's own page alone (one fetched source, CV-10); an independent ranking, if one is later fetched, adds weight and the `disputed` flag, never entry.
- **Component tiers** (bicycle groupsets and similar maker-set ladders) are not researched; † a scheme enters on one fetched maker or reviewer page (CV-10) and sits on lines (C4(a)), never on kinds.
- **Price.** Prices come from a dated, sourced list price (C4d). New-vehicle prices run from a few hundred USD (an inflatable kayak) to millions, so the shared bands collapse nearly every car and boat into "over 15,000 USD". The memo uses the domain-wide bands of C4(d) (CV-13, CV-14): 15,000 to 50,000; 50,000 to 150,000; 150,000 to 500,000; 500,000 to 1,500,000; 1,500,000 to 5,000,000; over 5,000,000 USD. Fetched price anchors: RV new-price ranges by type [RVIA]; snowmobile list prices [POL]; an inflatable kayak kit from about 75 GBP and a rigid one-person leisure kayak at about 280 GBP [DEC].
- **How it appears:** as `price_band`, `platform_tag` and `tier` entries on makers, lines and named forms; as attributes on the object; never as a level. VIN and hull-identification values are never stored (C16); only the identifier scheme may be (section 6).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Kinds:** canonical form first, then the sub-family's secondary key. Cars follow the UNECE bodywork-code order (saloon, hatchback, estate, coupé, convertible, SUV or multi-purpose), then the remaining body styles alphabetically; the order is a published standard sequence [UNECE]. Motorcycles: standard, cruiser, touring, sport, then dual-purpose and off-road, then scooters and mopeds, then three-wheelers (the sequence of Wikipedia's six categories with the excluded classes last [W-MOTO]). Bicycles: road, gravel, mountain (cross-country to downhill, by suspension travel), BMX, city, folding, mini-velo, cargo [ROSE, W-BIKE]. Caravans, motorhomes and campers: towed first (touring caravan, folding camper, fifth-wheel), then truck camper, campervan, then motorhomes by size (low-profile, overcab, A-class) [RVIA, PM]. Sailing craft: dinghy, open keelboat, cruising monohull, beach catamaran, cruising multihull, motorsailer. Boats overall: smallest propulsion effort first (paddle, sail, engine) then by length. Aircraft: airplanes by engine class, then other aircraft by FAA category order (glider, rotorcraft, lighter-than-air, others) [FAA].
- **Named forms:** non-maker named forms come first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; maker-bound named forms follow, by maker (maker-ledger `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically. This is C7 as decided (CV-21, CONVENTIONS_v1); ladder views by `line_rank` are generated, not stored.
- **Variants:** by `variant_code` or year (C3).
- **Reason:** a canonical-first order matches how the sources list their own values (the body-style filters and the standard code order); a chronological order would help only collectors.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

### Final structure

G1 Vehicles follows Candidate A (G-1): five sub-families by mode of travel (Land, Rail, Watercraft, Aircraft, then Parts and accessories made for one vehicle), with Rail skipping the leaf level; ten leaves in Land by body and drive as sold, three in Watercraft by means of propulsion, two in Aircraft, four in Parts by the vehicles served; about 150 kinds by body style, discipline, class or type (70 shown in the worked tree); and the model line or class design as the one named-form level, maker-bound or not, uncapped. Legal and registration classes (kei, UNECE and EU L, M, N, O, EPAC, US e-bike classes, FAA categories) are C17 rows; design standards are `standard_met`; body sub-style, road and gravel `geometry`, `construction`, `propulsion`, `assist`, `rig`, size, age, price band and scene stay off the tree. Makers, builders, coachbuilders, yards and designers are ledger rows, uncapped. The parts sub-family has leaves by vehicle sub-family, kinds by vehicle system, and fit in the C13 ledger. Nothing in this paragraph needs a further owner answer.

### Remaining risks

- Several kinds and rules rest on one reference page or on no fetched page: prams and strollers, the pet stroller, the microcar, the open keelboat, the cruising houseboat and narrowboat, the light-sport airplane, the MPV and minibus tie-break (P15), and the mini-velo and other kinds with a regional name (R-19). They are provisional until attested at stage 3.
- G-8 (inflatable against rigid) and G-6 (an equivalence of US Class B and the UK campervan) wait for fetched sellers; if a second-region retailer splits inflatable and rigid kayaks or boats, the kayak kind splits (level only).
- The v0.7 key still has sentences that disagree with decisions (section 12, problems); they are collected as R-16 amendments for v0.7.1.
- Named-form depth is large (of the order of 10,000 model lines, many more variants); harvesting from existing catalogues (not the licensed VCdb) is stage 3 work.
- The parts system list and the C13 candidate rows are unsourced until stage 3.
