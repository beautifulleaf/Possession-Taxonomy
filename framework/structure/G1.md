# G1 Vehicles — structure memo (research draft)

Stage 2 research memo for the owner. It describes how the world already divides vehicles and recommends a structure with evidence. It is not a decision. Every claim about how a source divides the category cites a page fetched in batch S2_G1; the same URLs, with a one-line description each, are in `verify/out/S2_G1_sources.tsv`. A fact seen only in a search excerpt is marked "(search excerpt only)". Framework: CATEGORIES_v0_6.md. Owner decisions: BRIEF.md. Shared conventions C1 to C9: `framework/structure/_conventions.md`. Counts of kinds and named forms are estimates and are marked as such.

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** G1 Vehicles (domain G, Vehicles & Property).

**Membership test (placement key step 11):** "Made **mainly to transport people, animals or goods from place to place** on land, water, rail, snow or air, however moved (engine, motor, muscle, wind, animal or tow): cars, motorcycles, bicycles and e-bikes, e-scooters and other powered personal transport, boats, canoes, kayaks and sailing craft, aircraft including sailplanes, trailers and horseboxes, touring caravans and motorhomes, horse-drawn vehicles, prams and strollers". Category description: "everything made mainly to transport people, animals or goods, with its fitted parts and electronics."

**Notes that bind (S1b), from step 11:** every boat, canoe, kayak, bicycle and aircraft is G1, including those made for racing. Unpowered boards, skates, kick scooters, skis, sledges, hang gliders and paragliders are E2; powered paragliders are aircraft. A machine that carries its operator while it does other work (tractor, ride-on mower, excavator) goes by that work. Hand carts, sack trucks and dollies are C1; wheelbarrows and garden carts are C2; shopping trolleys and trolley bags are A4; wheeled luggage is A4. Ride-on toys, children's tricycles and balance bikes are E1; adult tricycles are G1. Baby carriers and slings are A4.

**Rules that touch G1:**
- Step 4: a vehicle or vessel made to stay in one place as a dwelling (houseboat, static caravan, tiny house on wheels) is G2.
- S3, parts follow the whole: "a child car seat to Vehicles". Step 30 B7 note: wall-box chargers for electric vehicles are G1 under S3. Step 30 B8: locks made for a bicycle or vehicle are G1 under S3. Fuels are C8 and cleaning products B6 whatever they are used in. Anything worn on the body is step 17 (A1/A2/E2), not a part.
- Appendix A and S4: region, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, **market (domestic, commercial, professional)**, installation, **power source and technology (manual, electric, gas, battery)**, occasion, motive and how acquired never split an inner level. The last two bold items matter for G1 (section 10, questions 2 and 3).
- Category sentence for the family: G1 is "with its fitted parts and electronics", which is why section 3 ends with a parts sub-family.

**Precedence sentences that already exist elsewhere and bind G1 (restated, not new):** A4 memo: baby carriers go to A4, prams and strollers to G1. B10 memo: a trailer, horsebox, carriage or wheeled pet stroller is G1 (open question there). C7 memo, P5 and P6: a tipper, car or horse trailer made only to carry is G1 (step 11 is tried before step 12); ATVs and utility vehicles are G1 by the same reasoning (open there). E2 memo, P6 and P7: a bicycle, kayak, canoe or boat is G1; an electric kick scooter is G1; a bike pump, rack or bottle cage follows the bicycle; a turbo trainer holding a real bicycle follows the bicycle. B3 and D2 memos: a vehicle's own lighting is part of the vehicle; a portable or aftermarket sat-nav is D2.

### 1.2 Trade classifications, regulation and standards

- **Google product taxonomy** [GPC]. "Vehicles & Parts" has two children: Vehicle Parts & Accessories and Vehicles. Vehicles has three: Aircraft; Motor Vehicles (Cars, Trucks & Vans; Golf Carts; Motorcycles & Scooters; Off-Road and All-Terrain Vehicles with ATVs & UTVs and Go Karts & Dune Buggies; Recreational Vehicles; Snowmobiles); Watercraft (Motor Boats, Personal Watercraft, Sailboats, Yachts). Fifteen categories in all. Shopify's crosswalk to this taxonomy adds Heavier-Than-Air and Lighter-Than-Air aircraft and Commercial, Service & Utility and Motor Sports vehicles. Bicycles do not appear in this branch. Two further facts were seen in search excerpts only: travel trailers sit under Parts > Vehicle Storage & Cargo > Motor Vehicle Trailers, and Merchant Center vehicle ads require brand, model, year, mileage, colour, condition and VIN.
- **UNECE consolidated resolution** [UNECE] and **EU** [EC]. Four categories: L (two, three or four wheels, L1 to L7: mopeds, motorcycles, quads, light quadricycles), M (at least four wheels, passengers: M1 up to eight seats plus driver; M2 more than eight seats and up to 5 t; M3 above 5 t), N (goods: N1 up to 3.5 t, N2, N3) and O (trailers: O1 up to 750 kg, O2 up to 3,500 kg, O3 up to 10,000 kg, O4 above; also [ESC]). G is an off-road suffix (N1G). A **motor caravan** is a "special purpose M1" vehicle with seats and table, sleeping, cooking and storage. M1 bodywork codes run AA saloon, AB hatchback, AC station wagon (estate), AD coupé, AE convertible, AF multi-purpose vehicle, referring to ISO 3833:1977 terms. The standard is road-only and mass-, wheel- and function-based.
- **Auto Care Association VCdb** [VCDB]. A normalised "Year, Make, Model" fitment database with 175,500+ vehicle applications, 2.4 million configurations and 16,500+ off-highway vehicles and equipment, covering light, medium and heavy vehicles, powersports and off-highway equipment. It is licensed by subscription. It shows that the parts trade keys on make, model and year.
- **Cycles** [ISO4210]. ISO 4210-2:2023 covers young-adult bicycles (saddle height 635 to under 750 mm), city and trekking, mountain and racing bicycles including folding. It excludes delivery bikes, recumbents, tandems, BMX and competition/stunt bikes; smaller bikes fall under ISO 8098 or toy rules. E-bikes [EBK, a secondary explainer]: in the EU and UK an electrically power-assisted cycle (EN 15194) is limited to 250 W continuous, assistance cut-off at 25 km/h and pedal assist only (walk assist to 6 km/h), and counts as a bicycle; assistance to 45 km/h is a speed pedelec and a moped (L1e-B); the US uses Class 1, 2 and 3 (pedal assist 20 mph; throttle 20 mph; pedal assist 28 mph; up to 750 W).
- **Boats** [RCD]. Directive 2013/53/EU covers recreational craft of 2.5 to 24 m hull length and personal watercraft. Design categories A, B, C, D by wind force and wave height are printed on the builder's plate and are "a design classification and not a navigational authorisation". The Directive excludes canoes, kayaks, gondolas, pedalos, surfboards, racing boats, historical craft and own-build craft.
- **Aircraft** [FAA]. For pilots the FAA lists categories (airplane, glider, rotorcraft, lighter-than-air, powered-lift, powered parachute, weight-shift control, and rocket) with classes inside (airplane: single-engine land, single-engine sea, multi-engine land, multi-engine sea; rotorcraft: helicopter, gyroplane; lighter-than-air: airship, balloon). "Type" means a specific make and model (Boeing 737). "Category" also has two other meanings: aircraft certification category (normal, utility, acrobatic, transport; special: experimental, light sport, restricted) and instrument-approach category A to E. The first axis in aviation is therefore how the aircraft flies.
- **US sizes and others** [W-CLASS]. Car classification schemes include the European Commission segments (A mini, B small, C medium, D large, E executive, F luxury, J sport utility, M multi-purpose, S sport coupé), Euro NCAP classes, US EPA classes by interior volume, NHTSA by curb weight, IIHS by footprint, the Chinese national standard GB/T 3730.1 (2021; sedans by length, hatchbacks, SUVs and MPVs by wheelbase), and Japan's kei class. ISO 3833 defines the terms.

### 1.3 Enthusiast and reference guides

- **Cars** [W-BODY]. Wikipedia lists current styles (buggy, convertible/cabriolet, coupé, coupé SUV, crossover, fastback, hatchback, hearse, Kammback, liftback, limousine, microvan, minivan/MPV, notchback, panel van, panel truck, pickup, roadster, sedan/saloon, shooting-brake, SUV, station wagon/estate, targa top, ute) and says styles "vary depending on intended use, market position, location, and the era they were made". Regional synonyms are many (saloon/sedan, estate/wagon, berlina/berline, break, bakkie, ute).
- **Motorcycles** [W-MOTO]. "There is no universal system for classifying all types of motorcycles"; "some authors argue" for six categories (standard, cruiser, touring, sport, off-road, dual-purpose), and scooters, mopeds, minibikes, pocket bikes and three-wheelers "are often excluded". Sport-body and legal definitions are separate.
- **Bicycles** [W-BIKE]. Bicycles "can be categorized by function, number of riders, gearing, sport, means of propulsion, position of the rider or frame type"; "the categories are not mutually exclusive". The list covers cargo, tandem, recumbent, unicycle, tricycle, velomobile, cycle rickshaw, e-bike and moped.
- **Small boats** [BSM]. A guide groups small boats (under about 25 ft, trailerable) as paddlecraft (kayaks sit-in and sit-on-top and fishing; canoes; rowboats; SUPs), inflatables (low pressure about 3 PSI, high pressure up to 15), dinghies ("two meanings": a small racing sailboat, or any yacht tender), RIBs, PWCs, aluminium and jon boats, and trailerable cruisers. It names models and makers (Hake Seaward 25, Pacific Seacraft Flika, Farrier F-22, Corsair F-24, MacGregor 26, Ranger Tugs, C-Dory 25, Lowe Boats).
- **Baby transport** [W-BABY]. Wheeled devices are "prams" (newborn lying, facing the pusher) and "strollers" (sitting, forward, to about three years). UK and US terms differ: pram/baby carriage, pushchair/buggy/stroller; a buggy in the US is a quad. Carrycot, travel system, twin and jogging variants exist, and strollers can be "a highly visible symbol" of class and parenting philosophy. On-body carriers are listed separately (A4).
- **Handcars** [W-HAND]. Pump and push trolleys, draisines, railbikes, velocipedes and "jiggers" are one object under many regional names, built by Sheffield, Kalamazoo and Buda, and now used for tourism (France's vélorail) and vintage-railway racing.
- **Sailing classes** [W-470]. The 470 is a World Sailing International Class and a "strict one-design class" whose builders must be "Licensed Builders"; the template lists other classes (420, J/24, J/70, Melges 24). A one-design class is therefore a named form that several makers build.

### 1.4 Collectors, resale and museums

- **Collectors** [HAG, EBAY]. Hagerty says there is "not a lot of agreement" on age labels: vintage 1919 to 1930; antique (the Antique Automobile Club of America lets cars over 25 years old join its touring events) with the earlier "Brass Era"; "Full Classic" 1915 to 1948 on the Classic Car Club of America's approved list; and every US state sets its own historic-vehicle age (Michigan 26 years, Colorado 32). eBay offers Classics, Collector cars and Exotics as shopping lists. Age and status are labels on a car, not types of car.
- **Resale** [BUY]. A Japanese resale shop sorts bicycles by use group and names models inside each.
- **Museums** [SMG]. The Science Museum Group keeps railways as a separate theme and runs the National Railway Museum and Locomotion as separate sites. The page gave no vehicle-type tree, so museum evidence here is weak; museums are consistent with a mode-first reading (rail, road, air, water as different collections) but this memo does not rest on it.

### 1.5 Where the sources agree and where they split

**They agree:** (1) The first split is by mode or class of vehicle: Google (aircraft, motor vehicles, watercraft), eBay (cars and trucks, motorcycles, boats, powersports, other), mobile.de and AutoScout24 (cars, motorcycles, caravans, trucks, trailers), UNECE (L, M, N, O). (2) In every language the second split for cars is a short list of body styles (six to nine) and for bicycles a list of use types. (3) The model line (Golf, 911, F-150, Backroad, RMK) is the unit people name and the unit that parts and prices attach to [EBAY, VCDB, POL].

**They split the same objects differently:**
- **Pickups:** a car with a bed in eBay's body list (Truck), a goods vehicle N1 in UNECE, a "vehicle type" of its own in China (皮卡) and a body type in Japan.
- **Caravans and trailers:** a trailer (O) in UNECE, separate tabs in AutoScout24, one family with motorhomes in RVIA and mobile.de, a "Recreational Vehicle" in Google's Motor Vehicles but "Travel Trailers" under Parts > Vehicle Storage & Cargo (search excerpt only).
- **E-bikes:** a bicycle in EN 15194 and in Rose's cross-listing, a separate tab beside motorcycles in mobile.de, a motorcycle-facet value in Cycle Trader.
- **Personal watercraft and snowmobiles:** Watercraft (PWC) and Motor Vehicles (snowmobile) in Google, Powersports in eBay, "Pwc" and "Open Snowmobile" among motorcycles in Cycle Trader, excluded from RVs by RVIA.
- **Size against body:** China (Autohome), Europe (EC segments) and Japan (kei, compact) put a size class first; the US, Germany (Kombi, Limousine) and Wikipedia put body style first. Germany's list even includes "Kleinwagen".
- **Sail against power:** YachtWorld, Google and eBay split boats by sail and power first, yet YachtWorld lists "Motorsailer" under both and Decathlon splits kayaks by construction (inflatable or rigid) first.
- **Words:** US Class A/B/C (RVIA) and UK low-profile/overcab/A-class (PM) name different things; "A-class" and "Class A" are not the same; "dinghy" has two senses [BSM]; "buggy" means a stroller in the UK and a quad in the US [W-BABY].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Observed order of choice** (navigation order, filter order and buying-guide questions in the fetched sources):

1. **What it travels on (mode or medium).** Every marketplace has tabs for road cars, motorcycles, boats, aircraft, caravans or trailers before any other filter [MOB, AS24, EBAY, CT, YW, CTRL, GPC]. This is the only axis that never changes between countries.
2. **The legal class and licence gate.** For bicycles and light vehicles the first practical question is whether the machine counts as a bicycle, moped or motorcycle: 250 W and 25 km/h for an EPAC, 45 km/h for a speed pedelec, the kei limits (660 cc, 3.4 m), the O1 to O4 mass bands, the CE design category A to D [EBK, GOO, ESC, RCD]. These are measured specifications, so they are attributes.
3. **Use or terrain.** Buyers say what they will do: commute, family, leisure, fish, race, cross snow [GOO, MOB, ROSE, POL, PM]. Rose, Polaris and the Japanese bicycle guide organise their whole range by use. Use is therefore the first axis for bicycles, snowmobiles, kayaks and caravans, but it is not stable across makers (a "trail" bike differs between brands).
4. **Body or form.** For cars this is the body-style list (eBay nine, mobile.de seven, Goo-net eight, ISO and UNECE six codes AA to AF).
5. **Size.** Euro segments, Autohome levels, kei and compact in Japan, boat length, trailer mass.
6. **Make and model.** The model line is the unit of comparison and of parts fit [EBAY, VCDB], and people shop by it as often as by body.

**Conclusion for the tree.** The stable, source-agreed axis for the first inner split is **mode**; the stable axis for the next is the **class of vehicle as it is registered, sold or flown**; the axis after that is **body, form or discipline**; and the model line is the named form. Use, size, legal class, age and price are attributes, tags or views.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates use the framework's fixed level names (C5a): category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, with at most four levels below the category. They differ in what the sub-family and leaf levels are.

### Candidate A (recommended): mode, then class of vehicle, then type, then model

- **Sub-family** (axis: **mode of travel**, the medium the vehicle is made to move through or on): Land vehicles; Rail vehicles; Watercraft; Aircraft; and, last, Parts and accessories made for one vehicle (the C5d sub-family, exempt from this axis).
- **Leaf** (axis stated per sub-family, C5c): in Land, the **class of vehicle as it is registered or sold** (cars, vans and pickups, trucks and buses, motorcycles and light powered vehicles, off-road and snow vehicles, bicycles, other human-powered cycles, trailers, caravans and motorhomes, animal-drawn and hand-pushed vehicles); in Watercraft, the **means of propulsion** (paddle or oar, wind, engine); in Aircraft, **how it flies** (the FAA category: airplane, glider, rotorcraft, lighter-than-air, powered parachute and weight-shift). Rail has about eight kinds, so it skips the leaf level (C5b).
- **Kind** (axis per leaf): cars by **body style** in UNECE code order; vans by load layout; motorcycles by type; bicycles by discipline or use; trailers by load carried; caravans by body and whether towed or driven; boats by hull and use class; aircraft by engine class and purpose.
- **Named form**: the **model line or class design** (Golf, Backroad, RMK, Piper PA-28 Cherokee, the 470 class). Maker-bound unless a class or generic design (C2).
- **Example sub-families and leaves with 10 to 15 real kinds:** Cars: saloon, hatchback, estate, coupé, convertible, SUV, MPV. Motorcycles: standard, cruiser, sport, touring, adventure/dual-sport, scooter. Bicycles: road, gravel, cross-country MTB, downhill MTB, BMX, city, folding, cargo. Sailing craft: sailing dinghy, cruising sailboat, catamaran, trimaran. Aircraft: single-engine piston airplane, turboprop airplane, sailplane, helicopter, hot-air balloon.
- **Evidence for it:** mode tabs on every marketplace [MOB, AS24, EBAY, CTRL, YW]; Google's three-way Aircraft / Motor Vehicles / Watercraft [GPC]; FAA categories by how the aircraft flies [FAA]; the Directive that already treats boats apart from road vehicles [RCD]; the museum split of rail from the rest [SMG]; body-style lists of 6 to 9 values in four countries [EBAY, MOB, GOO, W-BODY].
- **Where it breaks (edge objects):**
  1. **Motorsailer** (MacGregor 26 in [BSM]): YachtWorld lists "Motorsailer" under both Power and Sail [YW]. The propulsion leaf cannot give it two homes. Rule needed: file by the first-listed class or by hull form; recommended: a kind in the sailing leaf, with an `engine_primary` attribute (section 10, question 2).
  2. **Speed pedelec** (assist to 45 km/h): a moped (L1e-B) in law but sold as an e-bike (mobile.de lists E-Bikes as its own tab) [EBK, MOB]. A bicycle by the bicycle leaf, a motorcycle-class vehicle by UNECE. Rule: a machine that meets the EPAC limits is a bicycle with `assist = EPAC`; one that does not is a moped in the motorcycle leaf.
  3. **Campervan conversion** (Panama P12 on a Ford Transit Custom [PM]): a van in law (M1 or N1) and in the parts trade, a leisure vehicle for its owner. The caravans leaf takes it on function, while the base van is a part-of-lineage attribute (`base_vehicle`).
  4. **Kei car** (Suzuki Wagon R, Daihatsu Hijet [GOO, W-BODY]): a legal class that spans hatchback, van and truck. It stays an attribute, not a leaf.
  5. **Horse-drawn sleigh**: a sledge (E2 by the step 11 note) or an animal-drawn vehicle (G1). Needs a precedence sentence (section 9, P6).

### Candidate B: propulsion first (muscle, engine, wind, animal, tow), then mode

- **Sub-family** (axis: how the vehicle is moved, the list in the step 11 wording): muscle-powered; engine-powered; wind-powered; animal-drawn; towed.
- **Leaf** (axis: mode): road, rail, water, air. **Kind**: type. **Named form**: model.
- **Example kinds (12):** muscle: bicycle, kayak, pram, handcar; engine: car, motorcycle, motorboat, helicopter, snowmobile; wind: sailing dinghy, sailplane; towed: caravan, trailer.
- **Evidence:** the step 11 wording itself, and Wikipedia's bicycle list, which has a "by means of propulsion" section [W-BIKE]. No marketplace, standard or museum in the fetched set uses propulsion as a first level.
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **E-bike**: Appendix A lists "power source (manual, electric, gas, battery)" as a modifier, so an e-bike must sit with the bicycle, not in an engine sub-family; the candidate cannot hold that.
  2. **Motorsailer, motor-assisted glider, pedal-drive kayak** [BSM, YW, DEC]: all have two means of propulsion.
  3. **Caravan against motorhome**: B puts them in different sub-families (towed against engine) while RVIA, mobile.de and eBay keep them together [RVIA, MOB, EBAY].
  4. **Pram, bicycle, kayak and handcar** share the muscle sub-family, which no buyer would browse.

### Candidate C: legal category first (UNECE and EU L, M, N, O), then type

- **Sub-family** (axis: regulatory vehicle category): L (two- to four-wheeled light vehicles), M (passenger vehicles with four or more wheels), N (goods vehicles), O (trailers), plus "not registered as road vehicles" (cycles, prams, boats, aircraft, rail).
- **Leaf** (axis: type or bodywork code). **Kind**: size or use class. **Named form**: model.
- **Example kinds (12):** L: motorcycle, scooter, moped, quad; M: saloon, hatchback, estate, MPV, bus; N: panel van, pickup, rigid truck; O: box trailer.
- **Evidence:** UNECE and EU categories [UNECE, EC, ESC]; UNECE bodywork codes AA to AF; the EU registration-document directives use these categories [EC].
- **Where it breaks:**
  1. **Boats, aircraft and rail** fall outside the regulation, so one sub-family becomes a leftover for everything else.
  2. **Pickup (N1) against SUV (M1)** look the same to buyers and are listed together by eBay and Goo-net [EBAY, GOO].
  3. **Motorhome** is a special-purpose M1 and a caravan an O trailer [UNECE], yet owners compare them together [RVIA, MOB].
  4. **Kei** crosses M1 and N1, and **bicycles and strollers**, the most numerous vehicles owned, have no category at all.

**Recommendation.** Candidate A. Use C's categories as attributes (`unece_category`, with the mass bands O1 to O4) and B's propulsion as an attribute (`assist`, `engine_primary`, `rig`), so that the standards stay searchable without being a level.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule.** A kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers. For vehicles that is the **body style, discipline or class** inside a vehicle class; the **model line** is the named form beneath it; the **segment or size band, legal class and engine** are attributes.

**Evidence, by sub-family.**
- **Cars.** The body-style list is the filter every large marketplace shows: nine values at eBay, seven at mobile.de, eight (plus kei and others) at Goo-net, six codes in the UNECE annex [EBAY, MOB, GOO, UNECE]. Buyers then pick a make and model: eBay's "popular models" are model-level pages [EBAY]. So a saloon and an estate are separate kinds (they are separate filters and separate standard codes), while "compact" and "mid-size" are size bands of the same kind in Europe, China and Japan [W-CLASS, AH, GOO]. Where Chinese and Japanese sources put size first, the size band becomes a generated view; this is question 3.
- **Bicycles.** Rose, the Japanese resale guide and Wikipedia all separate road, gravel, mountain, city, folding and BMX, and the Japanese shop publishes a best-value guide for road bikes alone ("12 road bikes under 100,000 yen") [ROSE, BUY, W-BIKE]. Rose and Wikipedia split mountain bikes further into cross-country, trail, downhill and dirt, with distinct geometry and suspension travel [ROSE, W-BIKE], so these are kinds. Rose also splits road bikes (race, endurance) and gravel bikes (race, everyday, adventure), but these are gradients of one geometry, not separate sports, so they are non-maker named forms. This is a judgement and is question 4.
- **Motorcycles.** Cycle Trader's thirty-value facet and Wikipedia's six categories agree on standard, cruiser, touring, sport and dual-sport as the comparable types [CT, W-MOTO]. Wikipedia also warns "there is no universal system".
- **Caravans and motorhomes.** The comparison level is the layout: low-profile against overcab against A-class [PM]; towable against driven [RVIA]. Floor plans such as a bunkhouse or a toy-hauler garage are attributes or variants (seen in search excerpts only); RVIA lists the sport utility RV with a built-in garage as a type [RVIA].
- **Boats.** Buyers compare within a class: bowrider, centre console, cabin cruiser, trawler [YW]. YachtWorld's class lists mix hull, use, rig and size, so the kind list is a cleaned version of them: rig names (sloop, ketch, yawl, cutter, schooner) and size names (superyacht, mega yacht) become attributes (`rig`, `loa`). Inflatable against rigid kayaks are split first at Decathlon [DEC], which is why they are kept as two kinds pending question 5.
- **Aircraft.** The buyer's category is engine class: single-engine piston, multi-engine piston, turboprop, jet, helicopter [CTRL, TAP]. Models within them (Cherokee, 172) carry the variants.
- **Strollers and prams.** The comparison is pram against stroller against jogging stroller; brand is a strong signal [W-BABY].

**Depth differs by sub-family.** Cars: body style only (shallow), with depth in named forms. Bicycles: one level deeper at mountain bikes. Everything else: one kind level.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Chosen type: preference (price band), with measured specifications as attributes; no consensus tier was found in the fetched sources.**

- **Spec-ranked schemes that exist, and where they go.** None of these ranks quality; each classifies size, power or design strength. All are attributes with the standard named (C4b), not tier entries:
  - car size: EC segments A to F, J, M, S; GB/T 3730.1 A00 to C/D; US EPA volume classes; NHTSA weight; IIHS footprint; kei [W-CLASS, AH, GOO];
  - legal class: UNECE L1 to L7, M1 to M3, N1 to N3, O1 to O4 [UNECE, EC, ESC]; EPAC, speed pedelec and US Class 1 to 3 [EBK]; ISO 4210 and ISO 8098 saddle-height bands [ISO4210];
  - boat: CE design category A to D [RCD];
  - aircraft: FAA category and class, aircraft certification category (normal, utility, experimental, light sport) [FAA].
- **Consensus-ranked tiers:** none for whole vehicles in the fetched set. Euro NCAP appears in the classification table only as a class name [W-CLASS]; its star ratings were not fetched and would be an attribute if added.
- **Brand perception and status labels** are the nearest thing to a tier: eBay's "Exotics" list (Alfa Romeo, Aston Martin, Bentley, Bugatti, Ferrari, Jaguar, Lamborghini, Land Rover, Lotus) and "Classics" and "Collector cars" [EBAY]; the Classic Car Club of America's approved "Full Classics" list [HAG]; mobile.de's "Premium" and "Luxury cars" shortcuts [MOB]. These are dated `platform_tag` entries (C4c) or status tags, never tiers or levels.
- **Trim levels** (for example PRO RMK, RMK SP, INDY VR1 against INDY SP at Polaris; Rose Backroad AL against Backroad FF) are maker lines or variants under a model [POL, ROSE]. They carry a `tier` entry only if a fetched source ranks them (Polaris calls the SP "the value leader" and the VR1 "the ultimate"; one maker's own wording, so it is a `tier` entry with source = the maker).
- **Price.** Prices come from a dated, sourced list price (C4d). New-vehicle prices run from a few hundred USD (an inflatable kayak) to millions, so the shared bands collapse nearly every car and boat into "over 15,000 USD". **Proposal:** for G1 add the bands 15,000 to 50,000, 50,000 to 150,000, 150,000 to 500,000 and over 500,000 USD (section 10, question 11). Fetched price anchors: RV new-price ranges by type [RVIA]; snowmobile list prices [POL]; an inflatable kayak kit from about 75 GBP and a rigid one-person leisure kayak at about 280 GBP [DEC].
- **How it appears:** as `price_band`, `platform_tag` and `tier` entries on makers, lines and named forms; as attributes on the object; never as a level.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity, with a platform role in the parts sub-family.**
- *Identity.* The maker is part of what is sought: eBay's navigation is by make and model; YachtWorld offers "1,110 more makes" beyond ten shown; Trade-A-Plane's copy opens with Cessna, Mooney and Piper; Polaris and Rose organise their pages by named lines; Buychari names the model beside each bicycle type [EBAY, YW, TAP, POL, ROSE, BUY].
- *Platform.* Parts fit by make, model and year (the VCdb exists for that) [VCDB]. That belongs to the parts sub-family and to the named-form key, not to the kind levels.
- *Not a level.* The framework (S4, Appendix A) puts brand and maker among the modifiers.

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger, using the core C1 schema, with G1 extensions.**

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Kinds:** canonical form first, then the sub-family's secondary key. Cars follow the UNECE bodywork-code order (saloon, hatchback, estate, coupé, convertible, SUV or multi-purpose), then the remaining body styles alphabetically; the order is a published standard sequence [UNECE]. Motorcycles: standard, cruiser, touring, sport, then dual-purpose and off-road, then scooters and mopeds, then three- and four-wheelers (the sequence of Wikipedia's six categories with the excluded classes last [W-MOTO]). Bicycles: road, gravel, mountain (cross-country to downhill, by suspension travel), BMX, city, folding, cargo [ROSE, W-BIKE]. Boats: smallest propulsion effort first (paddle, sail, engine) then by length. Aircraft: by FAA category order (airplane, glider, rotorcraft, lighter-than-air, others) [FAA].
- **Named forms:** by maker (maker-ledger `name`), then first-introduction year as attested (C7), then alphabetically. Non-maker named forms (roadster, shooting brake, one-design classes) come first, then maker-bound forms.
- **Variants:** by `variant_code` or year (C3).
- **Reason:** a canonical-first order matches how the sources list their own values (the body-style filters and the standard code order); a chronological order would help only collectors.

**Precedence sentences (proposed; P1 and P2 and P4 restate or sharpen existing ones).**
- **P1 (E2 / G1, powered boards).** *A board, skate or kick scooter with no motor is E2; a scooter or board with a motor, made to carry a rider from place to place, is G1 in the leaf of motorcycles and other powered light vehicles.* (E2 memo P6; open for hoverboards and powered skateboards.)
- **P2 (G1 inside, e-bikes).** *A bicycle with a pedal-assist motor that meets the local limit for a bicycle (EU and UK 250 W and 25 km/h; US Class 1 to 3) is a bicycle with `assist = EPAC` or its class; one that exceeds the limit (assist to 45 km/h, or a throttle in the EU) is filed as a moped in the motorcycle leaf.* [EBK]
- **P3 (E1 / G1, children's cycles).** *A balance bike or a children's tricycle is E1 (key note); a children's pedal bicycle above the toy size limit (saddle height over 435 mm, ISO 4210-2 note [ISO4210]) is a bicycle in G1.* Needs owner approval (question 8).
- **P4 (G2 / G1).** *A caravan, boat or van made to be towed, driven or sailed from place to place is G1 even when it is kept parked for years; one made to stay in one place as a dwelling (houseboat moored for good, static caravan, tiny house on wheels) is G2.* (S5: test the object, not the motive; step 4.)
- **P5 (C7 / G1, trailers and utility vehicles).** *A trailer, tipper or wagon made only to carry goods or animals is G1; one that also loads, mixes, spreads or processes what it carries is C7. An ATV or side-by-side made to carry people or goods is G1.* (C7 memo P5 and P6.)
- **P6 (E2 / G1, sledges).** *A sledge or sleigh drawn by an animal or machine to carry people or goods is G1 as an animal-drawn vehicle; one slid downhill for play or sport is E2.* Needs owner approval.
- **P7 (parts, S3).** *A part, accessory or consumable made for one kind of vehicle follows it (G1.5); a product made for several kinds of object goes by what it does (tools C1, cleaning products B6, fuels C8).* Vehicle lights and factory navigation are parts; an aftermarket portable sat-nav is D2.
- **P8 (step 17, worn gear).** *A helmet, jacket or pair of shoes worn by a rider is filed by step 17 and not as a vehicle part; a bike lock or child car seat made for one vehicle is G1.* (S3.)
- **P9 (E1 / G1, models).** *A scale model, toy vehicle or pedal car is E1; a full-size vehicle kept for display is G1 whatever the motive* (S5).
- **P10 (A4 / G1, baby transport).** *A carrier or sling carried on the body is A4; a wheeled pram or stroller is G1; an infant car seat is G1 under S3 (named in S3).* [W-BABY]

### Owner decisions needed (numbered)

1. **Mode as the first inner axis.** Approve sub-families Land, Rail, Watercraft, Aircraft and Parts, with Rail skipping the leaf level.
2. **Propulsion as a leaf axis in Watercraft.** Appendix A lists "power source and technology (manual, electric, gas, battery)" as a modifier, while sail, power and paddle craft are separate in YachtWorld, Google and eBay. Approve reading "power source" as the energy type of an engine (petrol, diesel, electric) and "means of propulsion" (paddle, wind, engine) as part of the form. Decide the **motorsailer** rule (recommended: a kind in sailing craft with an `engine_primary` attribute).
3. **Legal and size classes as attributes, not kinds.** Approve kei, EPAC, UNECE L, M, N, O, CE design category and size segments as attributes with generated views, and the **speed-pedelec rule** (P2). Decide whether Chinese and Japanese size-first systems need their own generated view (recommended: yes, region-tagged). Confirm that Appendix A's "market (commercial, professional)" does not forbid trucks and buses as kinds (recommended: they are forms with a load bed or a bus body, not a market).
4. **Mountain-bike disciplines.** Kinds (recommended) or non-maker named forms; and the same rule for road (race, endurance) and gravel (race, everyday, adventure) (recommended: named forms).
5. **Inflatable and rigid kayaks.** Two kinds (recommended, following Decathlon) or one kind with a `construction` attribute (material-is-the-form test).
6. **Caravans.** A leaf of its own with motorhomes (recommended) or caravans under Trailers and motorhomes under Cars; decide the UK/US vocabulary alias.
7. **Pickups and vans.** A leaf "Vans, pickups and light goods vehicles" (recommended) or pickups under Cars.
8. **Edge small vehicles.** Children's pedal bicycles (P3 and the 435 mm line), unicycles, hoverboards, powered skateboards, e-scooters outside the L categories, mobility scooters and wheelchairs (not placed by the key; H1 may hold them).
9. **Parts sub-family.** Decide whether G1.5 is a hierarchy at all. The Google taxonomy splits vehicle parts by system (braking, climate, electrical, engine, exhaust, frame and body, fuel, interior, lighting, mirrors, seating, sensors, suspension, towing; search excerpt only) and the parts trade keys on fitment (year, make, model) [VCDB]. Recommended: a parts table keyed by `fits_named_form_id`, with a small kind list, not a vehicle-system tree under each vehicle. Decide whether roof racks, boxes and bike racks count as parts or as "racks" (S3; E2 memo P7 says bike racks follow the bicycle).
10. **Named-form depth.** Model line only (recommended), with generation, year, trim and engine as variants and the extra `variant_type` values.
11. **Price bands above 15,000 USD** (15,000 to 50,000; 50,000 to 150,000; 150,000 to 500,000; over 500,000).
12. **Boundary calls:** P6 (sleigh), pet strollers and dog bike trailers (B10), camera and toy drones (D4, E1, G1), wall-box EV chargers (key places them in G1), dashcams (D4 memo).

### Framework conflicts found for the log (not applied)

1. Appendix A's **power source** and **market** modifiers against the sail-or-power and truck-or-bus splits that every source makes (question 2 and 3).
2. Step 11 lists **touring caravans** with trailers, but the regulation treats a caravan as an O trailer and a motorhome as a special-purpose M1, while owners and marketplaces group them (question 6).
3. The step 11 note says **children's tricycles and balance bikes are E1** and says nothing about children's pedal bicycles, which ISO 4210/ISO 8098 treat as bicycles above a saddle height (question 8).

5. **Horse-drawn sleighs** against "sledges" in E2 (P6).
6. **Pet strollers** and **dog bike trailers**: step 11 reaches them before step 13 (B10 memo, open).
