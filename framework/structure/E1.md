## 0. Category

**Code and name:** E1 Toys & Games.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 25; wording unchanged from v0_6): *made for play, or for a game of skill or chance played at a table, on a board or floor, or with cards, dice, tiles or tokens: toys, dolls, figures (character, action, vinyl and collectible-series figures, bobbleheads), puzzles, board, card, dice and tile games (including trading card games), tabletop and coin-operated games, table football and air hockey, casino and poker chips, toy vehicles and ride-on toys, scale models, model kits and miniatures (built or not, ready-made or not), model railways, dress-up accessories made as toys (toy crowns, wands, play make-up), piñatas, children's play equipment (swings, slides, climbing frames, playhouses, treehouses, trampolines made for play, paddling pools), gambling equipment.* Category line: "toys, figures, dolls, models and kits, table, board, card and dice games, children's play equipment, gambling equipment."

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

**Retailers and marketplaces.**
- **UK, Hamleys** [S1]: Shop Toys is split LEGO & Construction Toys (LEGO, Playmobil, Construction Toy Sets); Soft Toys (Teddy Bears, Plush Figures & Puppets); Preschool Learning Toys (Bath Toys, Toys for Babies, Roleplay Toys, Early Learning); Action Toys & Vehicles (Action Figures, Toy Vehicle Sets, Remote Control Toys & Robots); Dolls & Fashion (Baby Dolls, Fashion Dolls, Hair/Beauty/Jewellery); Games & Puzzles (Board Games, Other Games, Puzzles); Sports Toys & Leisure (Ride On Toys, Indoor, Outdoor); Lifestyle Toys (Batteries, School Supplies, Gifts, Collectables); Creative. Price point, age and brand are separate top menus. The first level is a hybrid of object form (soft toys, dolls), play action (construction, games), user (preschool) and setting (sports/outdoor), plus one catch-all (Lifestyle).
- **UK/Ireland, Smyths** [S2]: Category, then Age (0–6 months to 8 years), then Price, then Brand (Barbie, Bluey, Crayola) are the filters, in that order; categories include Action Figures & Playsets, Arts Crafts & Music. 6,379 products on the page. Category first, age second.
- **Australia, Kmart** [S3]: Toys splits out Impulse & Novelty Toys and Board Games (board games and puzzles) as their own groups.
- **US, Target** [S4]: Toys & Games has Blind Box Collectible Surprise Toys and Building Toys as named groups next to deal pages, and a second axis "Toys for all ages" in six bands (0–24 months, 2–4, 5–7, 8–10, 11–13, 14+). Only six of 29 sub-links were readable.
- **Germany/Switzerland, Galaxus** [S5]: 324,138 products under Spielzeug. Popular categories are generic types and a brand together: LEGO, Brettspiele, Plüschtier, Hörspiel, Spielzeugfigur, Kartenspiele, **Klemmbausteine** (generic interlocking bricks, beside the brand LEGO), Spielzeugauto, **Sammelfigur** (collectible figure, beside Spielzeugfigur), Sammelkarten, Baukasten, Puppe, Experimentierkasten, Lernspiel, Bastelset. Inside a type the filters are brand and line (LEGO Technic, Pokémon cards, Tonie figures). Age is stored on every product.
- **Japan, AmiAmi** [S6]: Figures (Bishoujo, Character, Foreign, Dolls); Toys (Gundam, Robots, Tokusatsu); Scale Models (Scale/Military, Car Plastic Model Kits, Car Models, Train Models, Tools/Paints); Trading Cards (Card Games, Trading Cards, Card Supplies); Toys & Goods (Kid's Toys, Plush, Jigsaw Puzzles). Several labels are franchise genres (Bishoujo, Tokusatsu, Gundam), which the owner's rule keeps off the tree.
- **Japan, HobbyLink Japan** [S7]: Scale Figures, Big Scale Figures, Figure Kits, Plush Dolls; Completed Models versus Injection Kits versus Racing Car Kits (finished or kit is the first split in models); makers (Bandai, Banpresto, Kotobukiya, MegaHouse, Taito, FuRyu) are a main facet.
- **Japan, Yodobashi** [S8]: Board Games sits under Game; the main facet is maker (Hobby JAPAN, Group SNE, Arclight, Oink Games, CMON Japan, Hanayama).
- **Specialist game shops:** Zatu (UK/US) [S9] filters 4,063 board games by Playing Time, Player Count, Awards, Key Features, Brand Collection and Puzzle Pieces, and types them Board Games 3,838 versus Accessories 121. Philibert (FR) [S10] filters by player count, game length, age, publisher and language, with a Category facet of Classic games, Trading Card Games and Living Card Games, Kid games, Miniatures games, Wargames, Roleplaying games, Board games, Accessories (form, audience and genre mixed). Ravensburger [S11] filters by Genre (board, card, dice, construction, brain, logic, memory, party, strategy game), Collection (3D Puzzle, Booster Pack, Card Sleeves, GraviTrax), Puzzle Format and Piece Count. WooCards (UK) [S12] sorts trading cards by game brand, then by sealed-product form: Booster Pack, Booster Box, Elite Trainer Box, Ultra Premium Box, Special Collection Box, Tins, Starter Deck, Structure Deck, Battle Deck, Collector Chest, Blister, Binders.
- **Casino and game-room suppliers (US)** [S13][S14]: Casino Game Supplies is split by game (Poker, Blackjack, Roulette, Craps, Bingo, Prize Wheels) and by equipment (Layouts, Chips & Cases, Hardware, Playing Cards). American Gaming Supply lists Poker Tables (customizable, folding, furniture, pedestal), Poker Chips, Playing Cards, casino products by game (baccarat, blackjack, craps, pai gow, raffle drums, roulette, slot machines) and a Game Room group (air hockey, foosball, pool tables, darts, arcade games, chess sets, backgammon).
- **China/global, Pop Mart** [S15]: the Category facet is Bags & Pendants, Art Figures, Plush Pendants, Plush Dolls, Action Figures, MEGA, Accessories, Blocks, POP BEAN, Display Containers; characters (Labubu, Molly, Skullpanda, Dimoo, Disney) are a second, separate facet.
- **Marketplace, eBay US** [S16]: Top categories are Action figures, Automotive models and kits, Building toys, Collectible card games, Diecast and slot cars, Funko, Model trains, Mystery toys, Tabletop games. Brand is a very large facet (Hornby, Märklin, Bachmann, Tamiya, Hot Toys, Sideshow, Mezco, Minichamps, Traxxas).

**Enthusiast communities and wikis.**
- **BoardGameGeek** [S17][S18][S19] keeps three parallel vocabularies, not a tree: *Type* (eight "subdomains": Abstract Strategy, Customizable, Thematic, Family, Children's, Party, Strategy, Wargames; set by user poll, up to three per game), *Category* (a mix of theme and form: American West, Animals, Trains, Zombies, Word Game, Print & Play, Action/Dexterity, Third-party Expansion), and *Mechanism* (a long list including Acting, Auction/Bidding, Cooperative Game, Dice Rolling, Hand Management, Worker Placement). One game carries many mechanisms, so mechanism is multi-valued by design. Type mixes audience (Family, Children's, Party) with complexity (Strategy) and genre (Thematic, Wargames).
- **Pagat** [S20] (card games): the main classification is by mechanism in five groups (outplay, exchange, comparison, layout, other), also by objective, and separately by *type of cards or tiles* (French-suited, German-suited, domino, number cards, single-suited, other). The author, following Parlett, says no classification is wholly satisfactory. The equipment classification is the only single-valued one.
- **Deck types** [S21]: poker size, bridge size, jumbo/large print, mini, tarot (78 cards), custom/novelty; all share the 52-card structure except tarot.
- **Model railways** [S22]: scale (ratio) and gauge (track width) are separate; OO 1:76 on 16.5 mm, N 1:148 on 9 mm and O 1:43.5 on 32 mm are the main UK systems; gauge decides which track and rolling stock fit together.
- **Collector guides, figures** [S23][S24][S25]: figures are divided by *format class*: prize figure (4–6 in, arcade/Ichiban Kuji origin, Banpresto, Sega, FuRyu, Taito), scale figure (pre-posed, 1/7, 1/8, 1/10, makers such as Good Smile, Alter, Kotobukiya, Max Factory), Nendoroid (about 10 cm chibi with swap parts) and figma (20+ joints); Western action figures by scale class: 1/12 (5–7 in), 1/6 (11–13 in) and statue scale 1/4 and larger. One import guide says Nendoroid and figma "should be separate categories". Doll collectors [S26] name Barbie, porcelain, vintage, artist, fashion (Integrity Toys, Blythe, Momoko), character and ball-jointed dolls.
- **Japanese buying guide** [S27]: board games are selected by player count and age; genre (bluff, hidden identity, deduction) is an aid; shogi, chess, go, Othello, sugoroku and Game of Life are all counted as board games.

**Collectors and the resale market.** eBay [S16] is the clearest: form first (action figures, building toys, diecast and slot cars, model trains, tabletop games, collectible card games, mystery toys), maker as the second large axis. HobbyLink and AmiAmi pre-owned and "completed model versus kit" [S6][S7] show the same pattern.

**Industry bodies and market trackers.** The Toy of the Year categories [S28] are Action Figure, Collectible, Construction, Creative, Doll, Educational, Game, Infant/Toddler, Kidult, License, Outdoor, Playset, Plush, Preschool, Specialty, Tech, Vehicle: form (doll, plush, vehicle, construction), play pattern (playset, creative), user (infant, preschool, kidult), technology (tech), channel (specialty) and rights (license) side by side. Circana's 11 supercategories [S29] are Action Figures and Accessories, Arts and Crafts, Building Sets, Dolls, Explorative and Other Toys, Games and Puzzles, Infant/Toddler/Preschool, Outdoor and Sports, Plush, Vehicles, Youth Electronics; here Beyblade X sits under action figures, trading cards under Games and Puzzles (Pokémon) or Explorative and Other (NFL cards), and Hot Wheels 1:64 under Vehicles.

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **EU combined nomenclature, heading 9503** [S30] lists, in order: tricycles/scooters/pedal cars and dolls' carriages; dolls representing human beings; doll parts; electric trains and scale model assembly kits; construction sets (plastic, other); stuffed animal toys; other animal-creature toys; toy musical instruments; wooden puzzles; other puzzles; toys in sets; motorised toys; toy weapons; die-cast miniature models; electronic education toys; other toys. Material and motor appear as splits (plastic versus other; stuffed versus not; motor versus not).
- **Heading 9504** [S31]: billiards; coin-operated games *with* screen and *without* screen; playing cards; video game consoles; casino tables, bowling alley equipment and "other funfair, table or parlour games" (pintables, dartboards, foosball); electric car racing sets as games. Trade law puts billiards, darts, casino tables and board games in one heading, which the framework splits between E1 and E2.
- **ISO 8124-1** [S32]: a toy is a product designed or clearly intended for play by children under 14. Outside the scope: bicycles with saddle height above 435 mm, model kits and hobby items whose finished item is not mainly play, collectibles not meant for under-14s, specialist puzzles (over 500 pieces or without a picture), public playground equipment, combustion-engined models, faithful firearm reproductions; sporting goods are out, "however toys which are their counterparts are included".
- **EN 71-8** [S33]: "activity toys for domestic use" are swings, slides, carousels, climbing frames, rigid playhouses, paddling pools (water depth up to 400 mm) and inflatable activity toys; ride-ons are not activity toys; public playground equipment is a separate series (EN 1176).

**Museums and reference works.** The Strong [S35] describes its collection as toys, dolls, board games, puzzles, construction sets, mechanical banks, video games, pinball machines. The Brighton Toy and Model Index [S36] (10,169 pages, toys to the 1960s) is organised by maker and line (Meccano's rivals, Märklin, Dinky, Matchbox, Airfix, Scalextric, Minic, Britains), with categories for wooden construction toys and card models. Wikipedia's List of toys [S34] has 20 type sections (Action figures, Animals, Cars and radio-controlled, Construction, Creative, Dolls, Educational, Electronic, Games, Physical activity and dexterity, Playground, Puzzle/assembly, Spinning toys, Wooden toys, Vehicles...), and its navbox mixes type, user (boys', girls'), material (stuffed) and function (educational).

**Where sources agree.** Dolls, action/collectible figures, construction (bricks and blocks), vehicles, games and puzzles are separate in every retail, trade and industry source [S1][S5][S6][S28][S29][S30]. Jigsaws are separate from games in AmiAmi, Hamleys and the EU nomenclature [S1][S6][S30]. Model railways and scale models are kept apart from play toys by hobby retailers and eBay [S6][S16].

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- *Plush*: a department of its own (Hamleys, Circana, Pop Mart), part of dolls (Pop Mart "Plush Dolls"), or part of animal toys (CN) [S1][S29][S15][S30].
- *Collectible figures*: with action figures (Circana), with their own award and shop group (TOTY "Collectible", Target "Blind Box", eBay "Funko"), or by format (prize/scale/Nendoroid) in Japan [S29][S28][S4][S16][S24].
- *Preschool and infant*: a department in Hamleys, TOTY and Circana (an age-defined group), but the items are ordinary forms (bath toys, stacking toys, play mats) [S1][S28][S29].
- *Die-cast cars*: toy vehicles (Circana: Hot Wheels), scale models (AmiAmi Car Models) or "die-cast metal miniature models" (CN) [S29][S6][S30].
- *Trading cards*: games (Pokémon in Games and Puzzles, Philibert TCG category), collectibles (NFL cards in Explorative and Other), their own group (AmiAmi) [S29][S10][S6].
- *Billiards, darts, foosball, casino tables*: one trade heading, but the framework puts cue and target games in E2 and table football in E1 [S31].
- *Board games*: by audience and complexity (BGG Type: Family, Children's, Party, Strategy), by mechanism (BGG, Pagat), by theme (BGG Category), by publisher (Yodobashi), or by player count and time (Zatu, Philibert) [S17][S18][S19][S9][S10][S8].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence points to three different "first choices", one per part of the category, plus age as a universal filter:

1. **Toys proper: object form or play pattern, with age as the filter beside it.** Hamleys and Smyths open with a department (construction, dolls, action and vehicles, games and puzzles) and put age and price next [S1][S2]; Target and Galaxus carry age on every product [S4][S5]; TOTY and Circana reflect the same mix [S28][S29]. Age is the owner's "user" modifier and stays off the tree.
2. **Collectible figures and models: scale or format class.** Collector guides open with scale as "the most fundamental decision" (1/12 versus 1/6 versus statue) or with format (prize, scale, Nendoroid, figma); price, articulation and display space follow from it [S23][S24][S25]. Model railway guides start with scale and gauge because it fixes compatibility [S22]. In the tree, scale is a required attribute (C6) and the kinds are set by articulation and form (section 3), because scale classes, sales channels and single makers' lines are three different axes.
3. **Games: player count, playing time and audience, then mechanism; physical form only for non-board games.** Zatu and Philibert filters lead with playing time, player count, age and publisher [S9][S10]; BGG and Pagat are about mechanism [S18][S20]; the only single-valued, object-facing split is the physical component set (board and pieces, a standard 52-card deck, German-suited deck, dominoes, dice, sealed TCG product) [S20][S21][S12].
4. **Trading card games and casino gear: the game system first, the product form second.** WooCards sorts by game brand then by product form [S12]; casino suppliers sort by game then by equipment [S13][S14].

Conclusion for the tree: the buyer's first decision is the **play family**, the form-and-play group buyers shop first (dolls and figures, construction, scale models, play vehicles, games, puzzles, role play and other toys, play equipment), as Hamleys, Galaxus and Circana show [S1][S5][S29]. This agrees with the framework's "primary designed function" axis and is decided (E-1). Age, character or franchise, gender, price and licence are filters and tags. Game mechanism, type, theme, player count and playing time, which the game shops lead with, are not even tags here: they are at most a link field to the future Interactive Entertainment taxonomy (owner rule; E-9).

## 3. Candidate structures

Three candidates. Candidate A is the one the evidence favours, and it is decided (E-1). Every one keeps age, franchise, licence, price, era, scene and sales format (blind box, booster, kit versus finished, prize) off the tree, as Appendix A requires.

### Candidate A — play family, then a leaf axis stated per sub-family, then kind, then named form (decided, E-1)

| Level | Name | One stated axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | play family: the form-and-play group buyers shop first (dolls and figures, construction, scale models, play vehicles, games, puzzles, role play and other toys, play equipment) [S1][S5][S29]; the closing parts sub-family is exempt (C5d) |
| 2 | Leaf | one axis per sub-family, stated in its heading below (C5c); sub-families of about 12 kinds or fewer have no leaf and store `leaf = null` (C5b) |
| 3 | Kind | the generic type buyers compare within a leaf (articulation and form, system class, object modelled, component set, structure type) |
| 4 | Named form | a line or series inside one kind, system, iconic design, published game (publisher the maker) or traditional pattern, with `maker_bound: yes | no` (C2(a)); uncapped (E-6, CV-7): Nendoroid, Hot Wheels 1:64, LEGO Technic, Monopoly, Ticket to Ride, German-suited pattern |

Nine sub-families (decided, E-1): E1.1 Dolls, figures & soft toys; E1.2 Construction toys; E1.3 Scale models & model railways; E1.4 Play vehicles & ride-ons; E1.5 Games; E1.6 Puzzles; E1.7 Role play & other toys; E1.8 Play equipment; E1.9 Parts and accessories made for one toy or game. Maximum depth stays four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; CV-16).

Scale, subject (car, aircraft, mecha, character), material, power source, how acquired (the `assembly` attribute: kit or built, E-14), sales channel (prize, blind box, E-12), age and franchise are attributes or tags in every sub-family. Variants (C3) hold set numbers, figure releases, kit numbers, TCG sets and prints, colourways, editions and printings, filled wherever a community compares them (CV-8, E-6).

**The line for games: form, not mechanism or theme.** A game is filed by the physical component set the owner holds (a board with pieces, a standard deck, a domino set, dice, a sealed TCG product, a miniatures starter set, a wheel or table). Three tests support this:
1. *Single-valued.* A copy of a game has one component form; it has many mechanisms. BGG assigns many mechanisms to one game and lets a game sit in up to three Types [S17][S18]; Pagat admits that no classification is "wholly satisfactory" [S20]. Mechanism fails the one-axis rule.
2. *Object-facing.* Rules are not owned; a deck or a boxed set is. Pagat's one classification that is about the object is "type of cards or tiles used" [S20], and WooCards sorts by sealed product form [S12].
3. *Stable.* Type labels mix audience (Family, Children's, Party), complexity (Strategy) and theme (Thematic, Wargames, American West, Zombies) [S17][S19]; the owner's rule keeps genre and audience off the tree. Mechanism families, BGG Type, BGG Category, player count and play time are never tags or generated views here; they are at most a link field to the future Interactive Entertainment taxonomy (owner rule; E-9). The age a game is sold for is the ordinary user tag (CV-18); awards (Spiel des Jahres, Kinderspiel and Kennerspiel des Jahres) are sourced `award` tags on named forms (section 5), never tiers.
The cost is that "worker placement" or "trick-taking" cannot be browsed in this taxonomy at all; that browsing belongs to the Interactive Entertainment taxonomy, reached through the link field.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (my estimate):** about 150 (range 120–200); the worked tree has 110. Nine sub-families; 18 leaves in four sub-families (E1.1, E1.3, E1.5, E1.7) and five leafless sub-families (E1.2, E1.4, E1.6, E1.8, E1.9, `leaf = null`); 2–8 kinds per leaf.
- **Named forms: no cap (decided, E-6; CV-7).** Named forms are lines and series inside one kind, systems, iconic designs, traditional patterns, game systems and every published board, card, tile, dice or tabletop game, with the publisher as maker and no "of note" filter (Nendoroid, figma, Hot Wheels 1:64, LEGO Technic, Playmobil, Scalextric, Pokémon TCG, Warhammer, Monopoly, Ticket to Ride, German-suited pattern). One fetched page admits each (C2(f)); they are harvested in stage 3 from the catalogues that already list them (the source census comes first: BoardGameGeek's game and publisher database [S17], the Brighton Toy and Model Index [S36], Pagat [S20], maker and shop catalogues) and grow as far as the attested evidence goes. My estimate: tens of thousands, dominated by published game titles (Zatu lists 4,063 board games in one store [S9]); the game titles are held in a C2(g) register (CV-45). Figure releases, model-kit numbers, LEGO set numbers, card sets and prints, editions and printings are C3 variants, filled wherever a community compares them (CV-8), also uncapped: Smyths UK has 6,379 toy products on one page [S2], Galaxus 324,138 [S5], the Brighton index 10,169 pages for toys to the 1960s [S36].
- **Where depth concentrates:** figures and collectibles (kind times line times maker, with releases as variants); games (component set times title, with TCG product form and game system); model railways (scale and gauge times maker times locomotive, stock, track); construction systems (compatibility systems, C13); collector dolls.
- **Broad leaves and kinds** (thousands of forms or variants): boxed board games; articulated action figures and static figures; fashion dolls; interlocking brick sets; die-cast vehicles; jigsaw puzzles; trading card games.
- **Medium:** plush; assembly scale models; model railways; ride-ons; playsets; card, tile and dice games; casino and gaming equipment; play equipment.
- **Narrow:** pinball machines; metal construction sets; puppets; toy soldiers; ball-jointed dolls; mechanical puzzles; roulette wheels; prize wheels; mechanical banks.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: preference.** No recognised, community-wide or spec-based ordering of quality exists across toys and games. Each leaf has price bands and awards, not a ladder of tiers. A `tier` never sits on a kind (C4a).

- **Figures: a price-and-finish ladder of formats, entered as C4 tier entries on lines.** Collector guides rank by finish and price: prize figure ($5–15, "basic paint", arcade origin), Nendoroid ($30–60), figma ($50–100+), scale figure ($40–200+, "professional paint", the "collector's standard"); Western classes run 1/12 ($25–100), 1/6 ($200–500+), statue scale ($500–2,000+) [S23][S24]. Japanese import guides give yen bands: prize figures about ¥1,500–4,000, new scale figures ¥15,000–30,000+ [S25]. In the data this becomes the scheme "collector format ladder", with C4(a) tier entries (`subject_id`, `subject_type`, `scheme_id`, `value`, `rank`, `direction`, `source_url`, `date`) on the named forms and lines it names (Nendoroid, figma, and maker lines once harvested), and a computed `disputed` flag where sources differ. Formats that are attributes (prize channel, scale class) are not tier subjects; they get price bands instead. The scheme is a scale of class (iii), an editorial or community class (C4(c)), and is registered once in the scheme registry. The three sources are small commercial blogs [S23][S24][S25]; under C4(e) and CV-10 the scheme enters the data on one fetched source, and further sources (including any from another region) only add weight and feed `disputed`.
- **Maker ladders (C4(c)(iv), CV-12).** Where a maker ranks its own lines or grades, the order is `line_rank` in the lines table, with optional tier entries with `scheme` = the maker's name; for example Bandai's Gunpla grades (HG, RG, MG, PG †), attested on the maker's page in stage 3.
- **Price bands (C4(d), CV-13).** One domain-wide USD scheme (0 (free), under 50, 50–150, 150–500, 500–1,500, 1,500–5,000, 5,000–15,000, 15,000–50,000 and the upper bands of CONVENTIONS_v1 C4(d), unused here), derived from a dated, sourced list price per named form; a band is never a tier. The guide ranges above map roughly: prize figures under 50; Nendoroid under 50 to 50–150; figma 50–150; scale figures 50–150 to 150–500; 1/12 under 50 to 50–150; 1/6 150–500 to 500–1,500; statues 500–1,500 to 1,500–5,000. Yen bands are converted at a dated rate at harvest.
- **Puzzles and games: awards and measured attributes, not tiers.** Piece count, puzzle format and age on Ravensburger filters [S11] are attributes (C4b); playing time, player count and awards (Zatu Platinum/Gold/Silver, Spiel des Jahres, Golden Geek, Dice Tower) on Zatu [S9] are attributes and sourced `award` tags. Zatu's award badges are a dated `platform_tag` (C4c), not a tier.
- **Elsewhere:** model railways and kits compete on scale and detail, but scale is a compatibility key and an attribute, not quality [S22]; ISO and EN safety standards are pass/fail requirements, not tiers [S32][S33]; a standard a toy meets (ISO 8124, EN 71) is the attribute `standard_met`, and legal toy-safety and gambling classes go to the C17 jurisdiction ledger (CV-35, CV-36).

**How it appears (decided, CV-10):** tiers never on a kind; tier entries on lines and named forms (figures first; dolls and model lines wherever a fetched source states a ladder); `price_band` on named forms elsewhere; `award` as an attributed tag.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states, then named forms in the C7 order as amended by CV-21 (non-maker named forms first, then maker-bound ones by maker and year).** Reasons: (1) buyers and sources open with the plain generic type (Galaxus lists "Klemmbausteine" before LEGO; Smyths and Hamleys list the generic department before brands) [S5][S1][S2]; (2) where one measurable spec defines the sequence, it is the secondary key, always in one direction, **smallest model first** (largest ratio denominator first): model railway scale N 1:148, OO 1:76, O 1:43.5 [S22]; figure scale 1:12, 1:6, 1:4 statue [S23]; puzzle piece count ascending [S11]; TCG `product_form` by contents (booster pack, starter or structure deck, collection box, booster box) [S12]; (3) non-maker named forms (French-suited pattern, sugoroku, foam dart blaster, himitsu-bako) come first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; maker-bound named forms follow, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically where no year is attested, because collectors track lines by year (Brighton) [S36] (CV-21); variants go by `variant_code` or year; maker ladders (`line_rank`, such as Gunpla grades) are generated views, not stored order. Secondary keys per sub-family: E1.1 articulation (most articulated first) then scale; E1.2 piece size; E1.3 scale; E1.4 motion (pushed, remote, ridden); E1.5 component count; E1.6 piece count; E1.7 none (canonical order); E1.8 size; E1.9 host kind order. Chronology is not the main axis because the era is a modifier.

## 9. Boundaries

Neighbouring categories and the precedence sentences, all decided. The sentences this memo first proposed as new were adopted (E1-O1, with the K items named in each); most are now in CATEGORIES_v0_7, and the two that are not yet are marked "to add to the key". The others restate the key.

1. **E2 Sports, Fitness & Recreation.** *A game whose play is a sport with a ball, racket, bat, cue or target is E2 even when it is sold in a game-room range: billiards and pool tables, darts and dartboards and table-tennis tables are E2, while table football, air hockey, pinball and board and card games are E1.* The EU nomenclature and game-room suppliers keep them together [S31][S14], so the cut is the framework's, not the trade's. *Toy or real (C15, adopted CV-32; K-160, step-25 note):* an object made for play by children that imitates a sport object is E1 when it cannot do the real object's job to that object's own standard (a child's toy golf set, a toy ball, a toy flying disc, a toy kite); a regulation ball or a sport kite made in a child's size stays E2. ISO 8124 likewise includes "toys which are counterparts" of sporting goods [S32]. Kick scooters stay E2; children's tricycles and balance bikes stay E1. **Adopted (K-159, step-25 note):** *A trampoline with a frame of about 1.2 m or less, with or without a handlebar, sold for exercise (a rebounder) is E2; a garden or play trampoline is E1* (provisional until attested: the 1.2 m frame cut †). **Adopted (K-84, step-10 note):** *boomerangs are E2*, whether sold for throwing sport or as a toy.
2. **D5 Video Games.** *A game played on a screen, including a coin-operated video cabinet, is D5; a table or coin-operated game without a screen (pinball, claw crane, redemption game, air hockey) is E1.* The EU nomenclature splits coin-operated games by screen [S31]. **Adopted (K-155, step-24 note):** *Casino slot and skill-stop machines, whatever display they use, are gambling equipment and E1*, so that a casino supplier's range [S14] is not split between D5 and E1; their legal classes go to the C17 ledger (CV-35).
3. **F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards.** *A trading card or sealed product made to be played in a game (Pokémon, Magic: The Gathering, Yu-Gi-Oh!, One Piece) is E1; a card made to be collected for its player or image with no game is F3; card sleeves and deck boxes follow the cards (S3), and an empty binder or album for several cards is B1 (K-9).* **Adopted (E1-O1, CV-33; to add to the key):** *A trading-card-game single card is E1 as the kind; a card made for collecting with no game is F3; the particular card is an individual item and out (C16).* A graded trading-card-game card stays E1 (F3-O3); grading scales are rows of the C16 grading-scale registry and no grade is stored (CV-34). Collectible sticker series (Panini type) are F3 (K-121). Circana files Pokémon packs under Games and Puzzles and NFL cards under Explorative and Other [S29].
4. **C4 Craft & Art Supplies; C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments.** *A kit sold to be assembled into a model or construction (assembly scale model, figure kit, brick set) is E1; the paints, glues, brushes and tools for model-making, and craft compounds and kits sold for making things (clay, slime, beads), are C4 even when sold in a toy aisle.* AmiAmi puts Tools/Paints inside Scale Models, Circana has an Arts and Crafts supercategory, and Hamleys has a Creative department [S6][S29][S1]. **Key (K-149, step-23 note):** *a kit made to teach science through experiments is C6 whatever its parts; a programmable robot or coding toy made for play is E1; a board or component kit without an experiment programme is D1* (play robots and coding toys are kinds 111 and 112 in E1.2, R-22, provisional until attested).
5. **F2 Art and B4 Decor.** *A figure of a character or creature, whether vinyl, PVC or resin, and including statue scale, is E1; an ornamental figurine of a person or animal made as decorative art is F2; a replica weapon that cannot injure and is made for decoration is B4.* A cast, replica or carved figure is filed by what it is (F2 sculpture, E1 toy or kit; K-77, step-7 note); a foam reproduction of a weapon is E1 (K-86). Statue-scale 1/4 collectibles are the pressure point [S23].
6. **G1 Vehicles.** *Ride-on toys, children's tricycles and balance bikes are E1; children's pedal bicycles (C15), other pedal bicycles and adult tricycles are G1* (K-91, step-11 note). **Adopted as a key note (E-13; to add to the key):** ISO 8124 treats a cycle as a toy only up to a saddle height of 435 mm [S32]; the note marks the toy range of ride-ons, tricycles and balance bikes and does not move a pedal bicycle to E1 (K-91; section 12). Combustion-engined hobby RC cars, which ISO leaves outside toy rules [S32], are E1 kind 30 (K-97, E-11).
7. **C2 Garden & Grounds.** *A paddling pool made for play is E1 (up to 400 mm of water depth, EN 71-8); a deeper above-ground or inflatable swimming pool is C2.* [S33] (the 400 mm cut adopted as a key note, E-13; to add to the key). The same standard defines the E1 play structures: swings, slides, carousels, climbing frames, rigid playhouses, inflatable activity toys. A children's garden tool set is E1 (kind 73) when it cannot do the job, C2 otherwise (K-179, step-29 note).
8. **D3 Audio & Video, D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices, D4 Cameras, D5 Video Games.** *A device whose job is playing sound or stories for children (Toniebox, children's headphones) is D3; a children's tablet is D2.* **Key (K-16, rule S3b):** *Toniebox-type figures are D3 parts under S3; a toys-to-life figure is E1 (filed by its form among the figure kinds of E1.1), and its portal or reader is a D5 part.* **Adopted (K-97, step-11 note):** *An RC or FPV drone sold as a hobby model is E1 (kind 30); one made for filming is D4; camera-less toy drones are E1.* Electronic toys stay E1 by what they do; "electronic" is an attribute (C10, CV-24).
9. **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects.** *Tarot and oracle decks sold for divination are F4; playing cards, including German-suited and other regional decks, and a pack sold for card play (tarock, French tarot), are E1* (K-109, step-14 note; named forms of kind 42). The 78-card tarot pack is also a game deck [S21]. Spirit boards, pendulums, runes and I Ching sets are F4 when made for divination and E1 (kind 35) when made as a board game (K-114).
10. **B10 Animal Equipment, A1 Clothing, A3 Jewellery, H1 Personal Care, E3 Music, E4 Weapons, B3 Lighting.** *Pet toys are B10; costumes are A1 and dress-up accessories made as toys (crowns, wands, play make-up) are E1 (K-123, K-130), while a real ornament in a child's size stays A3; pacifiers and teethers are H1; toy musical instruments are E1 (kind 77) and real, tuned instruments E3 (C15); a toy weapon (foam dart blaster, water blaster, play sword, foam replica) is E1 (kind 76), and a weapon that can injure is E4 (C15).* [S30] lists toy musical instruments and toy weapons in the toy heading. **Adopted (K-84, step-10 note):** *slingshots, blowguns and shuriken are E4 unless made as toys (E1, kind 76).* A toy light is E1 (K-162; kind 113 in E1.7, a torch or nightlight shaped as a toy, R-22, provisional until attested).
11. **A4 Bags & Accessories.** *A display case made to hold one figure follows the figure (E1.9, S3).* **Key (K-136, step-17 note):** *a plush or figure pendant with a clip or chain is E1 (kind 11 for plush, the figure kind by form for a figure pendant); any other bag charm or keyring is A4.* Pop Mart Plush Pendants [S15] are therefore E1.

## 14. Structure decisions for the build (DPH, session 7)
From verify/out/DPH_E_report.md; binds the build like memo text (R-118 a).

- **E1-1 Player count (attribute; partial revision of E-9, provisional).** Player count is printed on the box. It is a fact about the object, not a genre, mechanic or theme, so the owner's IE rule (BRIEF) does not bar it. Store `player_count` (min and max players) on the game named forms of E1.5.1.01, E1.5.2.02, E1.5.3.03, E1.5.3.04, E1.5.4.01, E1.5.5.01 and E1.5.6.07, as a facet. The builder should change the E-9 wording in the memo to say that mechanism, BGG Type and Category, theme, audience labels and complexity weight stay out (they are at most the IE link field), while printed player count, playing time and box age are attributes.
- **E1-2 Playing time (attribute, same revision).** Store `playing_time_min` (the printed range in minutes) on the same game named forms, as a facet. Weight and complexity stay in the IE link field.
- **E1-3 Box age (attribute).** Store `min_age` (the printed minimum age) on every E1 named form. The C6 user tag (baby, child, teen, adult) is derived from it, and the age-band filter reads it. Age is never a level.
- **E1-4 Warhammer-style faction (attribute, provisional).** Faction names what the miniatures in the box depict, as a character or franchise tag does (LEGO themes and Pop Mart characters are already tags). It is not lore-as-genre or a mechanic. Store `faction` on named forms of E1.5.5.01, E1.5.5.02 and E1.1.2.02. Its values are controlled per game system (the C13 row), taken from the publisher's own faction list. It is a facet only inside the game-system view. Rationale: Access Models filters on faction, and the Meeple Mountain guide says players choose by faction first.
- **E1-5 Miniatures-game army or unit box E1.5.5.02 (noted).** The kind stays †. Attest it in the E1 harvest on a publisher or shop page that sells army or unit boxes; faction boxes are its named forms.
- **E1-6 Paints, glues and tools (display_only; other_category C4).** These stay C4 (memo section 9.4). The Models group (G-E1-3) gets a generated cross-link "Paints, glues & tools (C4)" to the C4 modelling-supplies kinds once C4 is built. groups.tsv does not change, because a cross-link is not a member.
- **E1-7 Diorama and scenic kits (covered).** A building, ruin, terrain or diorama kit sold for scale models or miniatures is E1.3.1.01 Assembly scale model, with `subject` = "structure, terrain or diorama". Scenery sold for a model railway (gauge or railway scale stated) stays E1.3.2.05. Loose groundwork materials (static grass, flock, scatter, water effects) are model-making supplies and go to C4. Terrain packed in a game-system starter box is part of that box. E1.3.2.05 is not widened: its leaf is Model railways, so widening it would give placers a wrong home.
- **Display (display_only, from level2_note).** The radio-controlled vehicle (E1.4.03) and the slot-car set (E1.4.04) are also listed in the Models group as a hobby shelf. This is generated; their home does not change. Every group gets an age-band filter.
- **Needs.** Spiel des Jahres awards; maker kit skill levels; Gunpla grades (provisional); collector figure format ladder; BoardGameGeek rank (provisional); EN 71, ASTM F963 and CPSIA, ISO 8124 (gate standards, `standard_met`); TCG card grading scales (provisional); C13 rows for construction and track systems, railway scales and gauges, and TCG and miniatures game systems. The C13 `platform_type` list needs three new values: "toy construction or track system", "model scale and gauge" and "card or miniatures game system".
- **Maker role.** way_in (maker_roles.tsv row E1 *) confirmed. The brand and publisher tab sits beside the type path.
