## 0. Category

**Code and name:** E1 Toys & Games.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 25): *made for play, or for a game of skill or chance played at a table, on a board or floor, or with cards, dice, tiles or tokens: toys, dolls, figures (character, action, vinyl and collectible-series figures, bobbleheads), puzzles, board, card, dice and tile games (including trading card games), tabletop and coin-operated games, table football and air hockey, casino and poker chips, toy vehicles and ride-on toys, scale models, model kits and miniatures (built or not, ready-made or not), model railways, dress-up accessories made as toys (toy crowns, wands, play make-up), piñatas, children's play equipment (swings, slides, climbing frames, playhouses, treehouses, trampolines made for play, paddling pools), gambling equipment.* Category line: "toys, figures, dolls, models and kits, table, board, card and dice games, children's play equipment, gambling equipment."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name E1 (all from CATEGORIES_v0_6):**
- Step 4 (G2): "Play structures, including treehouses, are E1."
- Step 10 (E4): "Toy and foam replicas are E1"; airsoft and paintball markers are E2; decorative replicas that cannot injure are B4.
- Step 11 (G1): "Ride-on toys, children's tricycles and balance bikes are E1; adult tricycles are G1"; kick scooters and skates are E2.
- Step 13 (B10): pet toys are B10. Step 14 (F4): "Card and board games are E1"; tarot and oracle decks are F4.
- Step 15 (F3): "Casino and poker chips are E1. Collectible figures, dolls and model vehicles are E1"; non-game trading cards are F3.
- Step 18 (H1): pacifiers and teethers are H1. Step 20 (F2): "figures of characters, action figures, vinyl and collectible-series figures, and bobbleheads are E1"; ornamental figurines are F2.
- Step 22 (E3): "toy instruments are E1." Step 23 (C6): science-education kits are C6.
- Step 24 (D5): arcade machines and video games are D5 (tried before step 25). Step 26 (E2): "games that are sports, including ball, racket, bat, stick, cue and target games and darts, are E2. Chess, draughts, go and other board games are E1. Costumes are A1."
- Step 28 (C4): paints, glues and tools made for model-making are C4. Step 29 (C2): "paddling pools made for play are E1"; above-ground and inflatable swimming pools are C2. Step 30 (B4): decorative replica weapons, party decorations and festival decorations are B4.
- Rule S3: parts made for one kind of object follow it (doll clothes, model railway track, TCG sleeves follow the toy or game).

**Enthusiast communities and wikis.**
- **BoardGameGeek** [S17][S18][S19] keeps three parallel vocabularies, not a tree: *Type* (eight "subdomains": Abstract Strategy, Customizable, Thematic, Family, Children's, Party, Strategy, Wargames; set by user poll, up to three per game), *Category* (a mix of theme and form: American West, Animals, Trains, Zombies, Word Game, Print & Play, Action/Dexterity, Third-party Expansion), and *Mechanism* (a long list including Acting, Auction/Bidding, Cooperative Game, Dice Rolling, Hand Management, Worker Placement). One game carries many mechanisms, so mechanism is multi-valued by design. Type mixes audience (Family, Children's, Party) with complexity (Strategy) and genre (Thematic, Wargames).
- **Pagat** [S20] (card games): the main classification is by mechanism in five groups (outplay, exchange, comparison, layout, other), also by objective, and separately by *type of cards or tiles* (French-suited, German-suited, domino, number cards, single-suited, other). The author, following Parlett, says no classification is wholly satisfactory. The equipment classification is the only single-valued one.
- **Deck types** [S21]: poker size, bridge size, jumbo/large print, mini, tarot (78 cards), custom/novelty; all share the 52-card structure except tarot.
- **Model railways** [S22]: scale (ratio) and gauge (track width) are separate; OO 1:76 on 16.5 mm, N 1:148 on 9 mm and O 1:43.5 on 32 mm are the main UK systems; gauge decides which track and rolling stock fit together.
- **Collector guides, figures** [S23][S24][S25]: figures are divided by *format class*: prize figure (4–6 in, arcade/Ichiban Kuji origin, Banpresto, Sega, FuRyu, Taito), scale figure (pre-posed, 1/7, 1/8, 1/10, makers such as Good Smile, Alter, Kotobukiya, Max Factory), Nendoroid (about 10 cm chibi with swap parts) and figma (20+ joints); Western action figures by scale class: 1/12 (5–7 in), 1/6 (11–13 in) and statue scale 1/4 and larger. One import guide says Nendoroid and figma "should be separate categories". Doll collectors [S26] name Barbie, porcelain, vintage, artist, fashion (Integrity Toys, Blythe, Momoko), character and ball-jointed dolls.
- **Japanese buying guide** [S27]: board games are selected by player count and age; genre (bluff, hidden identity, deduction) is an aid; shogi, chess, go, Othello and Game of Life are all counted as board games.

**Collectors and the resale market.** eBay [S16] is the clearest: form first (action figures, building toys, diecast and slot cars, model trains, tabletop games, collectible card games, mystery toys), maker as the second large axis. HobbyLink and AmiAmi pre-owned and "completed model versus kit" [S6][S7] show the same pattern.

**Industry bodies and market trackers.** The Toy of the Year categories [S28] are Action Figure, Collectible, Construction, Creative, Doll, Educational, Game, Infant/Toddler, Kidult, License, Outdoor, Playset, Plush, Preschool, Specialty, Tech, Vehicle: form (doll, plush, vehicle, construction), play pattern (playset, creative), user (infant, preschool, kidult), technology (tech), channel (specialty) and rights (license) side by side. Circana's 11 supercategories [S29] are Action Figures and Accessories, Arts and Crafts, Building Sets, Dolls, Explorative and Other Toys, Games and Puzzles, Infant/Toddler/Preschool, Outdoor and Sports, Plush, Vehicles, Youth Electronics; here Beyblade X sits under action figures, trading cards under Games and Puzzles (Pokémon) or Explorative and Other (NFL cards), and Hot Wheels 1:64 under Vehicles.

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- **EU combined nomenclature, heading 9503** [S30] lists, in order: tricycles/scooters/pedal cars and dolls' carriages; dolls representing human beings; doll parts; electric trains and scale model assembly kits; construction sets (plastic, other); stuffed animal toys; other animal-creature toys; toy musical instruments; wooden puzzles; other puzzles; toys in sets; motorised toys; toy weapons; die-cast miniature models; electronic education toys; other toys. Material and motor appear as splits (plastic versus other; stuffed versus not; motor versus not).
- **Heading 9504** [S31]: billiards; coin-operated games *with* screen and *without* screen; playing cards; video game consoles; casino tables, bowling alley equipment and "other funfair, table or parlour games" (pintables, dartboards, foosball); electric car racing sets as games. Trade law puts billiards, darts, casino tables and board games in one heading, which the framework splits between E1 and E2.
- **ISO 8124-1** [S32]: a toy is a product designed or clearly intended for play by children under 14. Outside the scope: bicycles with saddle height above 435 mm, model kits and hobby items whose finished item is not mainly play, collectibles not meant for under-14s, specialist puzzles (over 500 pieces or without a picture), public playground equipment, combustion-engined models, faithful firearm reproductions; sporting goods are out, "however toys which are their counterparts are included".
- **EN 71-8** [S33]: "activity toys for domestic use" are swings, slides, carousels, climbing frames, rigid playhouses, paddling pools (water depth up to 400 mm) and inflatable activity toys; ride-ons are not activity toys; public playground equipment is a separate series (EN 1176).

**Museums and reference works.** The Strong [S35] describes its collection as toys, dolls, board games, puzzles, construction sets, mechanical banks, video games, pinball machines. The Brighton Toy and Model Index [S36] (10,169 pages, toys to the 1960s) is organised by maker and line (Meccano's rivals, Märklin, Dinky, Matchbox, Airfix, Scalextric, Minic, Britains), with categories for wooden construction toys and card models. Wikipedia's List of toys [S34] has 20 type sections (Action figures, Animals, Cars and radio-controlled, Construction, Creative, Dolls, Educational, Electronic, Games, Physical activity and dexterity, Playground, Puzzle/assembly, Spinning toys, Wooden toys, Vehicles...), and its navbox mixes type, user (boys', girls'), material (stuffed) and function (educational).

**Where sources agree.** Dolls, action/collectible figures, construction (bricks and blocks), vehicles, games and puzzles are separate in every retail, trade and industry source [S1][S5][S6][S28][S29][S30]. Jigsaws are separate from games in AmiAmi, Hamleys and the EU nomenclature [S1][S6][S30]. Model railways and scale models are kept apart from play toys by hobby retailers and eBay [S6][S16].

**Where they split the same objects differently.**
- *Plush*: a department of its own (Hamleys, Circana, Pop Mart), part of dolls (Pop Mart "Plush Dolls"), or part of animal toys (CN) [S1][S29][S15][S30].
- *Collectible figures*: with action figures (Circana), with their own award and shop group (TOTY "Collectible", Target "Blind Box", eBay "Funko"), or by format (prize/scale/Nendoroid) in Japan [S29][S28][S4][S16][S24].
- *Preschool and infant*: a department in Hamleys, TOTY and Circana (an age-defined group), but the items are ordinary forms (bath toys, stacking toys, play mats) [S1][S28][S29].
- *Die-cast cars*: toy vehicles (Circana: Hot Wheels), scale models (AmiAmi Car Models) or "die-cast metal miniature models" (CN) [S29][S6][S30].
- *Trading cards*: games (Pokémon in Games and Puzzles, Philibert TCG category), collectibles (NFL cards in Explorative and Other), their own group (AmiAmi) [S29][S10][S6].
- *Billiards, darts, foosball, casino tables*: one trade heading, but the framework puts cue and target games in E2 and table football in E1 [S31].
- *Board games*: by audience and complexity (BGG Type: Family, Children's, Party, Strategy), by mechanism (BGG, Pagat), by theme (BGG Category), by publisher (Yodobashi), or by player count and time (Zatu, Philibert) [S17][S18][S19][S9][S10][S8].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence points to three different "first choices", one per part of the category, plus age as a universal filter:

1. **Toys proper: object form or play pattern, with age as the filter beside it.** Hamleys and Smyths open with a department (construction, dolls, action and vehicles, games and puzzles) and put age and price next [S1][S2]; Target and Galaxus carry age on every product [S4][S5]; TOTY and Circana reflect the same mix [S28][S29]. Age is the owner's "user" modifier and stays off the tree.
2. **Collectible figures and models: scale or format class.** Collector guides open with scale as "the most fundamental decision" (1/12 versus 1/6 versus statue) or with format (prize, scale, Nendoroid, figma); price, articulation and display space follow from it [S23][S24][S25]. Model railway guides start with scale and gauge because it fixes compatibility [S22].
3. **Games: player count, playing time and audience, then mechanism; physical form only for non-board games.** Zatu and Philibert filters lead with playing time, player count, age and publisher [S9][S10]; BGG and Pagat are about mechanism [S18][S20]; the only single-valued, object-facing split is the physical component set (board and pieces, a standard 52-card deck, German-suited deck, dominoes, dice, sealed TCG product) [S20][S21][S12].
4. **Trading card games and casino gear: the game system first, the product form second.** WooCards sorts by game brand then by product form [S12]; casino suppliers sort by game then by equipment [S13][S14].

Conclusion for the tree: the buyer's first decision is *what kind of thing it is for* (hold and pose, build, drive or ride, play by rules, solve, climb on), which agrees with the framework's "primary designed function" axis. Age, character or franchise, gender, price and licence are filters and tags.

## 3. Candidate structures

Three candidates. Candidate A is the one the evidence favours. Every one keeps age, franchise, licence, price, era, scene and sales format (blind box, kit versus finished) off the tree, as Appendix A requires.

**The line for games: form, not mechanism or theme.** A game is filed by the physical component set the owner holds (a board with pieces, a standard deck, a domino set, dice, a sealed TCG product, a miniatures starter set, a wheel or table). Three tests support this:
1. *Single-valued.* A copy of a game has one component form; it has many mechanisms. BGG assigns many mechanisms to one game and lets a game sit in up to three Types [S17][S18]; Pagat admits that no classification is "wholly satisfactory" [S20]. Mechanism fails the one-axis rule.
2. *Object-facing.* Rules are not owned; a deck or a boxed set is. Pagat's one classification that is about the object is "type of cards or tiles used" [S20], and WooCards sorts by sealed product form [S12].
3. *Stable.* Type labels mix audience (Family, Children's, Party), complexity (Strategy) and theme (Thematic, Wargames, American West, Zombies) [S17][S19]; the owner's rule keeps genre and audience off the tree. Mechanism families, BGG Type, BGG Category, player count, play time, age, awards and "Kinderspiel/Kennerspiel" style tiers become tags or generated views.
The cost is that "worker placement" or "trick-taking" cannot be browsed as a branch; a view built from tags gives them back.

**Where Candidate A breaks.**
- *Tonies* (Toniebox and Tonie figures): the Toniebox is the top infant/preschool seller [S29] and Smyths lists Toniebox starter sets with toys [S2]; it is a children's audio player (D3) and its figures are made to play on it (S3 would send them to D3), yet Galaxus files "Hörspiel" under Spielzeug [S5]. No clean E1 home.
- *Slot-car racing sets:* EU trade law treats electric car racing sets as games (95049010) while model-railway and toy-vehicle sets are 9503 [S30][S31]; Scalextric is a toy vehicle system here.
- *Pinball and coin-operated machines:* step 24 sends arcade machines to D5, step 25 lists "coin-operated games" in E1; EU trade law splits by screen versus no screen [S31]; the Strong lists pinball machines among play objects [S35].
- *Die-cast cars:* Hot Wheels 1:64 is a toy [S29]; Minichamps and AUTOart are scale models [S16]; the same material and scale family covers both. The line is the maker's market, which is not a test; scale ratio plus purpose of the line is the best proxy.
- *Wargame miniatures:* an unpainted figure is both a model kit and a game piece; Philibert and Zatu file them as games [S9][S10], hobby shops as models [S6].

### Candidate B — the market's supercategories

Sub-families copied from Circana and Toy of the Year: Action Figures and Accessories; Building Sets; Dolls; Games and Puzzles; Vehicles; Plush; Infant/Toddler/Preschool; Outdoor and Sports; Explorative and Other; Youth Electronics [S28][S29]. Leaves: the retailers' departments.
- *Example sub-families and leaves (13 kinds):* Action Figures (action figure, collectible figure, Beyblade); Building Sets (brick set, Playmobil); Dolls (fashion doll, baby doll); Games and Puzzles (board game, TCG pack, jigsaw); Vehicles (die-cast car, RC car); Plush; Outdoor and Sports (trampoline, balance bike).
- *Breaks:* (1) it mixes axes: Infant/Preschool is user, Youth Electronics is technology, Outdoor and Sports is setting. A baby doll is Dolls or Infant. (2) Balance bikes and trampolines sit in "Outdoor" next to E2 sports goods. (3) Arts and Crafts is C4 in the framework. (4) The same cards are Games and Puzzles (Pokémon) or Explorative and Other (NFL cards) [S29], so one object kind has two homes. Rejected for the tree; its labels survive as generated views.

### Candidate C — trade-law object type

Sub-families from heading 9503/9504: wheeled toys and doll carriages; dolls; electric trains and model kits; construction sets; stuffed and other creature toys; toy instruments; puzzles; toy weapons; die-cast models; casino and table games; playing cards; billiards [S30][S31].
- *Example (12 kinds):* tricycle, dolls' carriage, doll, electric train, model assembly kit, construction set, stuffed toy, toy instrument, wooden puzzle, die-cast model, playing cards, pintable.
- *Breaks:* (1) it splits one kind by material or motor (construction sets "of plastic" versus "other"; wooden versus other puzzles; stuffed versus not; motorised versus not) which Appendix A forbids. (2) Billiards, darts, casino tables and board games share a heading [S31], against the framework's E1/E2 line. (3) "Toys n.e.s." residuals take action figures, designer vinyl and most novelty. (4) Collectible card products have no home beyond "playing cards". Useful as a cross-check of completeness; not as a structure.

*Japanese hobby shops* (AmiAmi, HobbyLink [S6][S7]) split figures by finish (completed model, kit), format and scale, which Candidate A already uses at the kind level; their franchise-genre groups (Bishoujo, Tokusatsu, Gundam) are tags.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (my estimate):** about 150 (range 120–200). Seven sub-families, 30–35 leaves, 3–6 kinds per leaf.
- **Named forms (estimate):** about 600–900 if named forms stop at the line, system, brand-range or sealed-product game system (Barbie, Nendoroid, figma, Hot Wheels 1:64, LEGO Technic, Playmobil, Scalextric, Pokémon TCG, Warhammer, Hornby OO). If every board-game title, figure release, model-kit number and card set counts, the number is in the tens of thousands: Zatu lists 4,063 board games in one store [S9], Smyths UK 6,379 toy products on one page [S2], Galaxus 324,138 toy products [S5], the Brighton index 10,169 pages for toys to the 1960s [S36]. See open question 2.
- **Where depth concentrates:** figures and collectibles (format class times line times maker); games (component form times title, with TCG product form times game system); model railways (scale and gauge times maker times locomotive, wagon, track); construction systems (compatibility systems); collector dolls.
- **Broad leaves** (many kinds and thousands of forms): boxed board games; action and collectible figures; fashion dolls; interlocking brick sets; die-cast toy cars; jigsaw puzzles; trading card games.
- **Medium leaves:** plush; plastic scale model kits; model railways; ride-ons; playsets; card and dice games; gambling equipment; play equipment.
- **Narrow leaves:** pinball machines; metal construction sets; puppets; toy soldiers; ball-jointed dolls; mechanical puzzles; roulette wheels; prize wheels.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: preference.** No recognised, community-wide or spec-based ordering of quality exists across toys and games. Each leaf has price bands and awards, not a ladder of tiers. Two local exceptions should be recorded as a modifier, not a level.

- **Figures: a consensus ladder of format classes.** Collector guides rank by finish and price: prize figure ($5–15, "basic paint", arcade origin), Nendoroid ($30–60), figma ($50–100+), scale figure ($40–200+, "professional paint", the "collector's standard"); Western classes run 1/12 ($25–100), 1/6 ($200–500+), statue scale ($500–2,000+) [S23][S24]. Japanese import guides give yen bands: prize figures about ¥1,500–4,000, new scale figures ¥15,000–30,000+ [S25]. This is a price-and-finish consensus attached to format classes, which are already kinds in Candidate A; it should appear as a tier tag on the kind (entry, mid, premium, statue), not as an extra level.
- **Puzzles and games: awards and measurable attributes, not tiers.** Piece count, puzzle format and age on Ravensburger filters [S11]; playing time, player count, awards (Zatu Platinum/Gold/Silver, Spiel des Jahres, Golden Geek, Dice Tower) on Zatu [S9]. These are attributes and tags.
- **Elsewhere:** model railways and kits compete on scale and detail, but scale is a compatibility key, not quality [S22]; ISO and EN safety standards are pass/fail requirements, not tiers [S32][S33].

**How it appears:** a `tier` modifier with the values entry / mid / premium / display-statue, defined per leaf and used only for figures, dolls and models where a source states bands; a `price_band` modifier elsewhere; `award` as an attributed tag.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Type: identity, with platform effects.** Makers and lines are part of what is sought: a Nendoroid is a Good Smile product, a Hot Wheels 1:64 is a Mattel line, a Pop Mart blind-box series is part of a character universe [S15][S24][S29]. Retail and resale sources use the maker as a main facet: HobbyLink and Yodobashi by maker, Galaxus by brand inside a generic type, eBay with a very long brand list, Smyths with brand as a filter [S7][S8][S5][S16][S2]. The Brighton Index is organised by maker and line [S36].

*Platform cases (maker sets compatibility):* LEGO System, Duplo and Technic bricks; Playmobil figures and scenes; model railway scale and gauge (OO, N, O) and maker systems (Hornby, Märklin); trading card game systems (a Pokémon booster pack plays only in Pokémon); Toniebox and its figures [S5][S22][S12][S2]. The platform key (the system a product belongs to) should be recorded on the kind or the named form, not used as a level.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then spec order, then named forms by line.** Reasons: (1) buyers and sources open with the plain generic type (Galaxus lists "Klemmbausteine" before LEGO; Smyths and Hamleys list the generic department before brands) [S5][S1][S2]; (2) where a single measurable spec defines the sequence, it is the order people already use: figure scale (statue, then 1:6, 1:12) [S23], model railway scale and gauge from small to large [S22], puzzle piece count [S11], sealed TCG products by contents (booster pack, starter or structure deck, collection box, booster box) [S12]; (3) named forms are ordered by maker line, then by first release, because collectors track lines by year (Brighton) [S36]. Chronology is not the main axis because the era is a modifier.

## 9. Boundaries

Neighbouring categories and the precedence sentences proposed. Sentences marked **new** are not in CATEGORIES_v0_6 and are for the owner to approve; the others restate the key.

1. **E2 Sports, Fitness & Recreation.** *A game whose play is a sport with a ball, racket, bat, cue or target is E2 even when it is sold in a game-room range: billiards and pool tables, darts and dartboards and table-tennis tables are E2, while table football, air hockey, pinball and board and card games are E1.* The EU nomenclature and game-room suppliers keep them together [S31][S14], so the cut is the framework's, not the trade's. **New:** *A toy counterpart made for play (a child's toy golf set or play hoop) is E1 because step 25 is tried before step 26; the same item made for the sport is E2.* ISO 8124 likewise includes "toys which are counterparts" of sporting goods [S32]. Kick scooters stay E2; children's tricycles and balance bikes stay E1.
2. **D5 Video Games.** *A game played on a screen, including a coin-operated video cabinet, is D5; a table or coin-operated game without a screen (pinball, claw crane, air hockey) is E1.* The EU nomenclature splits coin-operated games by screen [S31]. **New:** *Casino slot and skill-stop machines, whatever display they use, are gambling equipment and E1*, so that a casino supplier's range [S14] is not split between D5 and E1; owner to confirm.
3. **F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards.** *A trading card or sealed pack made to be played in a game (Pokémon, Magic: The Gathering, Yu-Gi-Oh!, One Piece) is E1; a card made to be collected for its player or image with no game is F3; card sleeves and binders follow the cards.* Circana files Pokémon packs under Games and Puzzles and NFL cards under Explorative and Other [S29]. Single cards are individual items and out of scope.
4. **C4 Craft & Art Supplies.** *A kit sold to be assembled into a model or construction (plastic model kit, brick set) is E1; the paints, glues, brushes and tools for model-making, and craft compounds and kits sold for making things (clay, slime, beads), are C4 even when sold in a toy aisle.* AmiAmi puts Tools/Paints inside Scale Models, Circana has an Arts and Crafts supercategory, and Hamleys has a Creative department [S6][S29][S1].
5. **F2 Art and B4 Decor.** *A figure of a character or creature, whether vinyl, PVC or resin, and including statue scale, is E1; an ornamental figurine of a person or animal made as decorative art is F2; a replica weapon that cannot injure and is made for decoration is B4.* Statue-scale 1/4 collectibles are the pressure point [S23].
6. **G1 Vehicles.** *Ride-on toys, children's tricycles and balance bikes are E1; a pedal bicycle and an adult tricycle are G1.* ISO 8124 treats a cycle as a toy only up to a saddle height of 435 mm [S32], a measurable cut the owner may adopt (**new**).
7. **C2 Garden & Grounds.** *A paddling pool made for play is E1 (up to 400 mm of water depth, EN 71-8); a deeper above-ground or inflatable swimming pool is C2.* [S33] The same standard defines the E1 play structures: swings, slides, carousels, climbing frames, rigid playhouses, inflatable activity toys.
8. **D3 Audio & Video and D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices.** *A device whose job is playing sound or stories for children (Toniebox, children's headphones) is D3; a children's tablet is D2.* The figures made to play on a Toniebox [S2][S5] are parts under S3 and would follow it to D3 although they are also figures: **unresolved**, see section 10.
9. **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects.** *Tarot and oracle decks are F4; playing cards, including German-suited and other regional decks, are E1.* The 78-card tarot pack is also a game deck [S21]; a pack sold for tarock or French tarot play is the conflict.
10. **B10 Animal Equipment, A1 Clothing, H1 Personal Care, E3 Music.** *Pet toys are B10; costumes are A1 and dress-up accessories made as toys (crowns, wands, play make-up) are E1; pacifiers and teethers are H1; toy musical instruments are E1 and real instruments E3.* [S30] lists toy musical instruments in the toy heading.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** Level 1 sub-family by play action (seven sub-families), level 2 leaf by object form, level 3 kind by the format class buyers compare, level 4 named form (line, system, model). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). **Choice level:** at the format class (figures, construction, sealed TCG product), the component set (games), or the structure type (play equipment); shallow for pretend and novelty toys (section 4b). **Tier type:** preference, with a consensus price-and-finish ladder for figure format classes carried as a tier tag. **Makers:** identity, with platform effects in building systems, trading card games and model railways; a maker ledger is needed. Age, character, franchise, licence, scene (anime, kidult, hobby gaming), era, price, audience, mechanism, theme and sales format (blind box, kit versus finished) are tags or generated views, never levels. Candidate B's labels survive as views; Candidate C is a completeness cross-check.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. Approve the level-1 axis (what the owner does with it) and the seven sub-families, against a retail-style department list (Candidate B).
2. **Granularity of named forms.** Stop at the line, system, brand range or sealed-product game system (about 600–900 forms), or admit every board-game title, figure release, kit number and card set (tens of thousands)? Recommended: line and system level, plus a short attested list of landmark titles per game kind, with the rest held as external tags.
3. **Games: form not mechanism.** Confirm that mechanism, BGG Type, theme, audience, player count and play time never divide the games leaf (section 3), and that rules-only games (poker, bridge) are not objects.
4. **Scale and gauge.** Appendix A does not mention scale. Recommended: scale and gauge are required platform attributes for model railways and kits, while figure scale classes (1:12, 1:6, statue) are kinds because they name different objects. Confirm.
5. **Slot machines, pinball, claw cranes** (D5 versus E1), and whether the screen test or the stake-and-pay-out test decides (section 9, sentence 2).
6. **Die-cast cars:** toy (Hot Wheels 1:64) versus scale model (Minichamps 1:43 and 1:18) [S29][S16]. Recommended: the toy kind is the 1:64-class play line, the scale model is declared by ratio and sold to display; owner to confirm the proxy.
7. **Tonies and audio toys;** and electronic toys generally (EU heading for electronic education toys [S30]): D3, D2 or E1.
8. **Tarot decks** used for card games (F4 versus E1).
9. **Blind boxes** as a sales-format tag rather than a kind, and **kidult** as a user tag [S4][S28].
10. **Adopt external cut points:** ISO 8124 saddle height 435 mm for cycles as toys, EN 71-8 water depth 400 mm for paddling pools [S32][S33]; or leave the cuts to the key's wording.
11. **Wargame miniatures** (games) versus model figure kits (models) [S9][S10][S6]; and hobby-grade RC aircraft, boats and cars with combustion engines, which ISO leaves outside toy rules [S32].
12. **Items the key does not name:** kites, flying discs (Frisbee is in Wikipedia's spinning toys [S34]), toy balls, bubble toys (TOTY Outdoor [S28]) and water toys. Proposed home: E1.6 when sold as toys, E2 when made for a sport.
13. **Sealed TCG products are in, single cards are out;** confirm card sleeves and binders as parts under S3 (E1 for game cards, F3 for collected cards).

**Framework conflicts to log (not applied):** (a) step 24 lists arcade machines in D5 and step 25 lists coin-operated games in E1 with no screen test; (b) step 14 sends all tarot decks to F4 although tarot packs are game decks; (c) Toniebox figures under S3 conflict with step 25 "figures"; (d) kites, flying discs, toy balls and bubble toys are in no step; (e) step 25 lists both "casino and poker chips" and "gambling equipment", which overlap and neither names slot machines; (f) trampolines "made for play" (E1) versus "fitness trampolines" (E2) have no stated cut.
