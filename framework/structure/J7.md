### 1.5 Where sources agree and where they split the same objects

- **Agree:** the top level is the drink family (wine, beer, spirits, cider; alcohol-free apart) in Wine.com, Systembolaget, Vinatis and the Whisky Exchange [S1][S2][S14][S5]. Still wine is split by colour first [S1][S2][S14][S25]. A spirit is typed by the legal class and type [S9][S12].
- **Split, sake:** Wine.com files saké as a wine type (36 items) [S1]; the Whisky Exchange pairs "Beer & Sake" [S5]; the tax agency treats it as its own product with its own standard [S8]; Culinary files it with rice wines (local file). Production is closer to beer; the label standard is its own.
- **Split, liqueurs and gin:** TTB makes cordials and liqueurs a class and gin a separate class [S9]; Whisky.de puts liqueur under a "Spirits" department and gin top-level [S4]; Vinatis calls both "compound" spirits [S15].
- **Split, fortified wine:** Wine.com puts Dessert, Sherry & Port inside wine [S1]; Liv-ex keeps Port as a separate index [S28]; Wine Folly joins dessert and fortified in one style [S25].
- **Split, Champagne:** a sparkling wine type at Wine.com [S1][S11], a department at Vinatis [S14], an index at Liv-ex [S28].
- **Split, whisky:** country first and variety second at Whisky.de [S4]; a mix of country and style in one list at Wine.com [S12]; the law gives five Scotch categories and a separate US type list [S7][S9]. The variety words recur across countries (single malt in Scotland and Japan, blend in Scotland, Ireland and Japan) [S4]; country is the part that varies.
- **Split, beer:** BJCP groups for judging and says the groups are arbitrary [S22]; Vinatis sorts by flavour profile [S17]; Systembolaget by "öltyp" [S19].
- **Split, rum:** by base (molasses, cane, agricole) and by colour at once at Whisky.de [S4][S21]; TTB defines rum by the sugar-cane base and treats Cachaça as a type [S9].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Drink family** (wine, beer, spirits, cider) is the first branch on every retailer fetched [S2][S5][S14][S18]. Sake, liqueurs, gin and fortified wine are the drinks whose home varies from shop to shop (1.5).
2. **Within wine, colour or process class** comes before grape or region. Wine.com's wine-type facet (red, white, sparkling, dessert and port, rosé, saké, orange) comes first, then grape within red [S1][S10]; Systembolaget filters white, red, rosé, sparkling, fortified [S2]; Vinatis opens with colour departments [S14]; Wine Folly's nine styles are colour and body [S25].
3. **Within spirits, the legal class or base spirit, then type, then age.** Wine.com and the Whisky Exchange start with drink type [S5][S12]; Whisky.de goes country then variety [S4] (here the country is a tag and the variety is the kind); the law uses class then type [S9]; the auction house filters distillery, bottler, age, ABV last [S29].
4. **Filter order on Wine.com** is Gift Type, Occasion, Variety, Varietal, Region, Availability, Size & Type, Fine Wine, Vintage, Reviewed By [S1]. Style and variety precede region; vintage and critic score come late and act as filters. Price, ABV, vintage and rating are sliders on every list [S1][S12][S13].
5. **Maker and bottler are first for collectors.** At the auction house the series, distillery and bottler lists are longer than any other facet [S29]; Liv-ex indices are named-wine lists [S28].
6. **Questions buyers ask** are style questions (BJCP: which style, how sweet, how carbonated [S24]; Wine Folly: body and food [S25]; Whisky.de gift finder: smoky or not, sherry-fruity or not, origin, budget [S4]).

The first axis is therefore **what the drink is** (family, then class or style). Region, age, vintage and price are filters.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's levels: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, at most four below the category (C5).

### Candidate A (chosen): drink family by raw material and process, then class or style

- **Level 1, sub-family: the drink's base material and how it is made (fermented, distilled, or compounded from a distillate).** The axis is production stage within raw material, in the Culinary DR-E to DR-I order. Seven sub-families: Wine (grape); Beer (grain ales and lagers); Cider, perry, mead and other fruit and honey ferments; Sake and other rice and grain wines; Spirits (distilled, unsweetened); Liqueurs and flavoured spirits (spirit compounded with a flavouring); Mixed alcoholic drinks (ready-to-drink, a spirit or wine mixed with a flavouring or mixer).
- **Level 2, leaf: one axis stated per sub-family** (C5c). A sub-family with about 12 kinds or fewer stores its kinds with `leaf = null` (C5b).
  - *Wine:* wine class, assigned by a **first-yes key**: (1) fortified with spirit? Fortified wine. (2) Sweet by botrytis, ice, dried grapes or late harvest? Sweet wine. (3) Sparkling? Sparkling wine. (4) A white made with long skin contact? Orange wine. (5) Pink? Rosé wine. (6) Otherwise still red or still white wine, by colour. Then a sparkling rosé is sparkling, a vin doux naturel is fortified, and Lambrusco is sparkling. Dosage, sweetness and body are attributes. A seventh leaf, Aromatised wine (vermouth, retsina; Culinary DR-F2-4), is added once it is sourced (†).
  - *Beer:* style family (BJCP family tags [S22]).
  - *Cider, perry, mead and other fruit ferments:* base fruit or honey.
  - *Sake and other rice wines:* `leaf = null` (2 sourced kinds so far).
  - *Spirits:* legal class or base spirit [S9].
  - *Liqueurs and flavoured spirits:* `leaf = null` while it holds about 12 kinds or fewer (6 now; about 15 expected); if it grows past that the leaf axis is flavouring [S9][S4].
  - *Mixed drinks:* `leaf = null` (1 kind).
- **Level 3, kind:** the type the buyer compares: grape or blend type (wine), BJCP style (beer, cider, mead), composition class (sake), legal or production type (spirits, liqueurs). Country or region alone never makes a kind (Appendix A).
- **Level 4, named form:** a maker's line or cuvée (maker-bound), or a traditional variant that many makers produce (not maker-bound), per C2. Vintage, age statement, bottle size and polishing ratio are not levels (4b, 5).

**Where Candidate A breaks.**
1. *Fortified wines and vermouth:* Port and Sherry are wines by material (grape) and spirits by strengthening; Wine.com and Liv-ex keep Port with wine [S1][S28]. The wine first-yes key above settles the fortified case. Aromatised wine (vermouth) is not listed on any fetched page; Culinary DR-F2-4 has it (local file); unattested here (†).
2. *Ready-bottled cocktails and hard seltzer* sit between J7.7 and Culinary DR-I. Systembolaget separates "blanddrycker" from cider and from spirits [S20]; no fetched page lists their kinds. Decided: K-24 files sealed ready-to-drink cocktails, hard seltzer and canned gin and tonic in J7.7; a cocktail mixed to order is Culinary DR-I.
3. *Gin:* distilled with juniper, so spirit, but Vinatis counts it "compound" with liqueurs [S15]; the class rule in [S9] is unambiguous.
4. *Sake:* the NTA names mix two properties, added alcohol and polishing ratio [S8]. Resolved: the added-alcohol split is the kind (junmai sake against sake with brewer's alcohol); the eight NTA names are values of the attribute `designation`, and the polishing ratio (50 / 60 / 70 %) is an attribute with its standard (C4b). Decided: J-16 (sake by composition) and CV-42 (a legal name that fixes a different composition is a kind; a ladder is an attribute).
5. *Single bottles with several categories:* a whisky bottled by an independent bottler (Jura 1975 by Signatory Vintage) has a distillery, a bottler and a vintage [S30]: one kind, one named form, two makers (section 6).

**Alternate not adopted.** The pre-decision memo wrote out an alternative in which the (R) kinds become non-maker named forms under generic method kinds (traditional-method sparkling wine, fortified wine, aged grape brandy, agave spirit and the like). CV-40 and J-16 keep wine and spirit designations as kinds, so it is not used; its text is kept in verify/work/S2U_J7/J7.pre.md. The tree shape and depth are the same either way.

### Candidate B: retail departments (Wine; Beer; Spirits; Cider and mixed), sake inside wine, liqueurs inside spirits

Mirrors Systembolaget [S2][S18] and Whisky.de [S4]. Levels: department → type facet → kind → named form (three levels). Leaf axis: the retailer's own "type" facet. Examples: Wine → Red → Pinot Noir; Spirits → Whisky → Single malt whisky; Cider & blanddrycker → Cider. Breaks where: (1) sake placed in wine contradicts the tax agency's product definition [S8] and Culinary; (2) mead, fruit wine and perry have no department (Systembolaget folds perry into cider [S20], BJCP groups them [S23]); (3) liqueurs inside spirits hide a TTB class [S9]; (4) the department list is the retailer's, not the product's, so adding a new retailer changes the tree.

### Candidate C: origin-first (country → region → appellation → style)

Matches how Old World wine is sold and how Liv-ex indices are built [S28], and how Vinatis sorts Champagne [S16]. Breaks: Appendix A makes region a modifier, so region cannot be a level; the same grape appears in dozens of regions; a spirit's legal type already carries origin only for a few cases (Scotch, Irish, Cognac [S9]); rejected for the tree. The origin remains a tag and a generated view (section 7).

## 5. Quality and tier

**There is no single tier ordering across J7.** The categories fall in three classes:

- **Spec ladders, as attributes with their standard named (C4b):** sake polishing ratio (50 / 60 / 70 %) and the eight NTA names, which are a ladder on it (NTA 清酒の製法品質表示基準 [S8]); Scotch age statement in years of the youngest component [S26] and the five legal categories [S7]; ABV (the TTB 40 % minimum [S9]); beer strength and colour tags (BJCP session / standard / high / very high [S22]); Champagne dosage [S16]. These are measured or legally defined on the object. They never become tier entries or levels. Each ladder is a row of the shared `standard` table (CV-40, JC1), and an object's value points to it (`subject_id`, `standard_id`, `value`).
- **Consensus ranking, as tier entries on a maker, line or named form (C4a):** fine wine. Critic scores appear as a facet with named sources and counts (James Suckling 4,969, Wine Spectator 3,891, Robert Parker's Wine Advocate 3,585, Jeb Dunnuck, Wine Enthusiast, Vinous, Decanter and others) [S1] and as a per-wine list ("Top 100 of 2025") [S1]. Liv-ex's index membership (Fine Wine 50, Fine Wine 100, Bordeaux Legends 40) is a second, market-based scheme [S28]. Each is a dated `scheme` / `value` entry with a source; wines may carry several, and a `disputed` flag is computed when they disagree. Each scheme enters on one fetched source (CV-10, C4(e)); a second region only adds weight. In the C4(c) registry the critic scores are class (iii), an editorial or community class (a reviewer's grade enters on one source, CV-12), and Liv-ex index membership is class (ii), a third-party rank. Official regional classifications (for example estate rankings) were not fetched and need a page before they enter (†). Critic scores and Liv-ex prices attach to a vintage, which is a C3 variant: C4(b) and CV-11 allow a tier entry on a variant for an external score of that version, vintage scores included, so a vintage score sits on the vintage variant row and a score of the wine as a label sits on the named form.
- **Platform tags (C4c):** Wine.com's Boutique, Collectible, Older Vintages, Bordeaux Futures, Rare and Green [S1]; Whisky Auctioneer's Private Selection, Travel Retail Exclusive, Single Cask, One of ≤25/50/100 bottles [S29]. They are dated `platform_tag` values, not tiers.
- **Preference only:** beer, cider and liqueurs; the sources fetched list no tier scheme, so the price band alone applies until one is found; a scheme enters on one fetched page (CV-10).

**No individual-item facts (C16).** A cask number, a bottle number in a limited run, a warehouse receipt, a lot record and a condition or fill-level grade belong to one particular object and are never stored. The lot record [S30] is evidence that the attributes (distillery, age, vintage, production type, cask type, region, bottler, ABV, size) exist on kinds and named forms; it is not data to load.

**Tier type for the category: mixed.** Sake and spirits are spec-ranked (attributes); fine wine is consensus-ranked (tier entries); beer, cider and liqueurs are preference. **Price:** the shared USD item bands (C4d) from a dated list price, computed on a 750 ml (wine) or 700 ml (spirits) equivalent bottle, because bottles are compared by the bottle. CV-15 (JC2): a `unit_price_band` in USD per litre is also derived from the dated list price, bands set at the first data load.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought: collectors filter by distillery and by bottler [S29], wine indices list named wines [S28], and Wine.com lists critic scores by named wine [S1]. Compatibility (platform) does not apply.

### 6.1 Maker ledger

One maker ledger on the C1 core schema (one ledger for the whole domain, `maker_class` multi-valued; CV-2), with `maker_class` keeping its C1 values (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; retail own-label; licensee or diffusion; artisan or atelier; supplier). The supply-chain roles this category needs go in the multi-valued column `supply_role` (CV-41, JC3), with these values: winery or estate; négociant; cooperative; distillery; brewery; cider maker; independent bottler; importer or trader; retail own-label. A Champagne house is a house or manufacture in C1 terms with `supply_role` négociant or winery as the source says. Consortia and appellation bodies are not makers: they are the `authority` of a designation row. **Admission and growth (CV-5, BRIEF).** The ledger has no cap. One fetched page showing that a house, estate, winery, négociant, cooperative, distillery, brewery, cider maker, meadery, sake brewery, independent bottler, importer or retail own-label makes or sells something admits it. Persons (a winemaker, master blender, head distiller or brewer) are ledger rows too (class artisan or atelier), tied to their house through the relations table. Traditions of makers (for example sake master-brewer guilds, †) go in the standing school table. Seeding starts from the fetched lists (Liv-ex index components [S28], the named wines on Wine.com [S1], the distillery and bottler lists of the auction house [S29], Systembolaget's range [S18], the cider makers named in [S24]) and from a source census of existing catalogues made before harvesting; candidates to census, named from general knowledge and not fetched (†): producer and bottle registers such as Vivino and Wine-Searcher, community beer, whisky, wine, sake and cider databases, national appellation and distillery registers. Sake breweries and meaderies take `supply_role` brewery.

**Distillery against bottler.** The maker of a named form or line is the bottler or brand owner; the distillery that made the spirit is `built_by_maker_id` (C1 addition). Evidence: the rum filter "Original bottler / Independent bottler" [S21]; the whisky auction's Distillery Bottling 15,500 versus Signatory Vintage 1,682 [S29]; a lot record names distillery and bottler as different entities [S30]. The first draft's `bottler_role` column is dropped: a distillery's own bottling is a named form whose maker and `built_by_maker_id` are the same distillery; an independent bottling has two different makers. `appellation_ids` is not needed in the maker row; it is derived from the named-form ledger.

### 6.2 Designation and standard tables (replace the first draft's "origin ledger")

Two tables (CV-40, JC1):
- **`designation`** (protected or legal name of origin or method): `designation_id`, name, native name, `scheme` (a legal type such as the US "distinctive product" class, a Scotch category, a Japanese national name), `register_id`, country, `authority` (the body, never a maker), `kinds_served`, rule summary, sources. Rows fetched in this batch: Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy de Jerez, Calvados, Pisco, Tequila and Mezcal (TTB "distinctive product of" a country [S9]); the five Scotch categories and Scotch Whisky [S7]; the eight Japanese tokutei meisho-shu names [S8]. Champagne, Port and the others rest on retailer and index pages [S1][S14][S28] and need a legal register source before they enter the table (†).
- **`standard`** (a measured or legal grading scheme): `standard_id`, name, authority, jurisdiction, `subject`, ordered scale values, `kinds_served`, source. Rows: the NTA polishing ratio [S8], the Scotch age statement [S26], the TTB minimum strength [S9], the BJCP strength and colour tags [S22] and Champagne dosage terms [S16].
- **Placement (JC1(c)).** A designation whose rules bind one kind is a non-maker named form carrying `designation_id` (Old Tom under gin is not a designation; Barolo under Nebbiolo would be). A designation spanning several kinds is a tag on products and named forms: Scotch (it spans single malt, single grain, blended malt and blended whisky [S7]), and each country of origin. A designation is a kind only under C11 or the written exception of CV-40 and JC1: wine and spirit designations (Champagne, Port, Cognac and similar) are kinds. The (R) kinds of this tree are that exception; each still has a `designation` row whose `kinds_served` names the kind, and other appellations (Barolo under Nebbiolo) stay non-maker named forms carrying `designation_id`, or tags (J-16).
- Origin is a tag on named forms and bottles, never a level (Appendix A). Legal classes a jurisdiction assigns for tax, excise, licence or age of sale go to the C17 jurisdiction ledger; none was fetched here.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

C7 says canonical form first, then the sub-family's secondary key. Keys (adopted, R-0):
- **Wine leaves:** leaves in the order Still red, Still white, Rosé, Orange, Sparkling, Sweet, Fortified (the first-yes key reversed, still wines first). Inside Still red and Still white, single-grape kinds first and blend kinds after, each group ordered by **body, light to full**, the Wine Folly order [S25]; named forms in the C7 order (CV-21: non-maker named forms first by first-attested year, then maker-bound forms by maker, year and alphabet). Reason: Wine Folly's styles already order grapes by body.
- **Beer leaves:** **BJCP category and letter order** [S6] (1A, 1B, 1C, 2A…): an attested, stable order that is not a ranking.
- **Cider and mead:** BJCP order (C1A to C1E, C2, C3, C4; M1 to M3) [S24][S23][S6].
- **Sake:** canonical form first: junmai sake (no added alcohol), then sake with brewer's alcohol [S8]. The attribute `designation` lists its values in the NTA table order, which runs from ginjo through junmai to honjozo [S8]; the polishing ratio is an attribute, not the sort key.
- **Spirits:** the legal-type order inside each class: for whisky, the Scotch categories in the regulation's order (Single Malt, Single Grain, Blended Malt, Blended Grain, Blended) [S7], then pot still, bourbon and rye; for brandy, the US type list (grape brandy first, then Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy de Jerez, Calvados, Pisco) [S9]. Country is not a sort key.
- **Variants:** by year, then bottler (C7: by `variant_code` or year); age statements ascending inside a named form.
- **Closing mixed kinds (K-46):** a final kind "mixed <class>", where one exists, is stored last in its sub-family.

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (first *yes* wins; S1 one home):

1. **J7 vs Culinary DR-F / DR-E / DR-G:** a wine, beer, cider, sake or spirit in a sealed bottle, can, keg or cask is J7 as the product bought; the same drink as a drink poured, a dish with wine, or a cocktail made to serve is Culinary. A ready-bottled cocktail or mixed drink in a sealed can or bottle is J7.7 (Systembolaget's blanddrycker [S20]); a cocktail mixed to order is Culinary DR-I. K-24 files them in J7.7; the kinds of J7.7 (hard seltzer, canned gin and tonic, other ready-to-drink cocktails, spritzers) are unsourced (†) and provisional until attested.
2. **J7 vs J5:** wine, spirits and extracts made for cooking or flavouring (mirin, cooking wine, bitters) are J5 (step 3 note). K-48 (in key v0.7, step 3): *bitters sold as a flavouring by the dash are J5; a bitter liqueur sold to drink by the glass is J7* (here J7.6: amaro, Fernet and similar). R-17: drinking bitters stay J7, and bitters sold as a flavouring go to J5's condiments and sauces leaf; mirin and cooking wines go to its vinegars and cooking-liquids leaf. No fetched page names amaro; the TTB liqueur class is broad [S9] (†). Wine vinegar and sake lees are J5, not J7.
3. **J7 vs OUT (rights and claims):** wine or spirits **bought but not yet delivered** is OUT (step 1): Bordeaux futures (the "Bordeaux Futures" facet [S1], a 2022 vintage "Pre-sale: ships after 03/15/2026" listing [S1]), en primeur offers, and a warehouse receipt for a cask held in a bonded store [S27] are claims until the wine or spirit is delivered. A bottle or cask physically delivered to the owner is J7 as stock. K-25 (in key v0.7) settles it: a cask held in bond, on a warehouse receipt or otherwise not delivered is OUT; a delivered cask, physically with the owner, is J7.
4. **J7 vs B5, B1 and gift or mixed packs:** decanters, wine glasses, barware, corkscrews and wine fridges are B5; the liquid is J7. A cellar rack or wine shelf is B1 (S3, "never parts"). A gift set is filed by its item of greatest value (a Champagne and chocolates set is J7 because the Champagne costs more [S1]); the box kept afterwards is B1. A mixed case of different wines (Wine.com "Mixed Collections" [S1]) follows K-46: it is filed under the kind that makes up half or more of its weight, otherwise under a final kind "mixed <class>" (here "mixed wine"; likewise "mixed beer", "mixed spirits"), created when one fetched page attests a mixed pack. A set that mixes categories is filed by its item of greatest value.
5. **J7 vs B9:** a cannabis-, CBD-, kratom- or areca-bearing drink or beer is B9 whatever the form (step 27 note); an ordinary alcoholic drink is J7.
6. **J7 vs prepared drinks (Culinary):** alcohol-free versions of beer, wine and spirits (K-22), non-alcoholic cider (the US sense of "sweet cider" is unfermented [S23]), kombucha and juices are Culinary (step 1); Systembolaget keeps alcohol-free beside, not inside, the alcoholic categories [S2].
7. **J7 vs C3 and home-brewing:** grape vines and hop plants are plants (C3); the grape juice or malt sold to make a drink is J5 or Culinary. K-58 (in key v0.7, step 3): home-brewing and winemaking ingredients are J5 and their equipment is B5; R-17 puts malt extract, hops and brewing yeast in J5's baking and fermenting-aids leaf. A kit sold as one unit follows the sets rule (K-1, K-46). Living yeast cultures kept to grow are C3 (step 6; C-15, provisional until attested). Makers of kits, ingredients and equipment stay in the maker ledger.
8. **J7 vs F1/F2/B4/F5:** a collectible empty bottle or label is filed as the object it is (S5; a glass bottle is B4 or B5 by its use); a wine label or menu is kept printed ephemera (F5, K-140; a type becomes a kind only by the C2 test, otherwise a named form). These edges are not attested in the fetched pages (†).

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure.** Candidate A: seven sub-families by raw material and production method (J7.1 Wine; J7.2 Beer; J7.3 Cider, perry, mead and other fruit and honey ferments; J7.4 Sake and other rice wines; J7.5 Spirits; J7.6 Liqueurs and flavoured spirits; J7.7 Mixed alcoholic drinks, which now holds the sealed ready-to-drink cocktails, hard seltzer and canned gin and tonic of K-24), a leaf per sub-family where it holds more than about 12 kinds (`leaf = null` otherwise) on one stated axis (wine class by a first-yes key with Sweet wine and Fortified wine as leaves and an Aromatised wine leaf once attested; BJCP style family; base fruit or honey; legal class or base spirit); kinds at grape or blend type (J-16), BJCP style, sake composition class (junmai sake; sake with brewer's alcohol) and legal or production type (whisky by production type, country a tag), together with the 17 wine and spirit designation kinds that CV-40 writes in as a written exception (Champagne, Cava, Prosecco, Crémant, Sauternes, Tokaj, Port, Sherry, Madeira, Cognac, Armagnac, Calvados, Pisco, Cachaça, Tequila, Mezcal, and Bordeaux red blend, which is also a blend type); and named forms for lines, cuvées and expressions, with no cap on named forms, lines, variants or makers. Maximum depth: four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Vintage and bottler edition are variants; age statement, ABV, polishing ratio, sake designation, dosage, sweetness, bottle size, container, closure and country are attributes or tags; region and appellation are tags and a generated view. Tier is mixed: spec ladders are attributes with their standard; critic scores and Liv-ex index membership are tier entries on named forms and on vintage variants, each entering on one fetched source; beer, cider and liqueurs have no tier scheme yet.
