- **Split, liqueurs and gin:** TTB makes cordials and liqueurs a class and gin a separate class [S9]; Whisky.de puts liqueur under a "Spirits" department and gin top-level [S4]; Vinatis calls both "compound" spirits [S15].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Drink family** (wine, beer, spirits, cider) is the first branch on every retailer fetched [S2][S5][S14][S18]. Sake, liqueurs, gin and fortified wine are the drinks whose home varies from shop to shop (1.5).
2. **Within wine, colour or process class** comes before grape or region. Wine.com's wine-type facet (red, white, sparkling, dessert and port, rosé, saké, orange) comes first, then grape within red [S1][S10]; Systembolaget filters white, red, rosé, sparkling, fortified [S2]; Vinatis opens with colour departments [S14]; Wine Folly's nine styles are colour and body [S25].
3. **Within spirits, the legal class or base spirit, then type, then age.** Wine.com and the Whisky Exchange start with drink type [S5][S12]; Whisky.de goes country then variety [S4] (here the country is a tag and the variety is the kind); the law uses class then type [S9]; the auction house filters distillery, bottler, age, ABV last [S29].
4. **Filter order on Wine.com** is Gift Type, Occasion, Variety, Varietal, Region, Availability, Size & Type, Fine Wine, Vintage, Reviewed By [S1]. Style and variety precede region; vintage and critic score come late and act as filters. Price, ABV, vintage and rating are sliders on every list [S1][S12][S13].
5. **Maker and bottler are first for collectors.** At the auction house the series, distillery and bottler lists are longer than any other facet [S29]; Liv-ex indices are named-wine lists [S28].
6. **Questions buyers ask** are style questions (BJCP: which style, how sweet, how carbonated [S24]; Wine Folly: body and food [S25]; Whisky.de gift finder: smoky or not, sherry-fruity or not, origin, budget [S4]).

The first axis is therefore **what the drink is** (family, then class or style). Region, age, vintage and price are filters.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's levels: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, at most four below the category (C5).

### Candidate A (recommended): drink family by raw material and process, then class or style

- **Level 1, sub-family: the drink's base material and how it is made (fermented, distilled, or compounded from a distillate).** The axis is production stage within raw material, in the Culinary DR-E to DR-I order. Seven sub-families: Wine (grape); Beer (grain ales and lagers); Cider, perry, mead and other fruit and honey ferments; Sake and other rice and grain wines; Spirits (distilled, unsweetened); Liqueurs and flavoured spirits (spirit compounded with a flavouring); Mixed alcoholic drinks (ready-to-drink, a spirit or wine mixed with a flavouring or mixer).
- **Level 2, leaf: one axis stated per sub-family** (C5c). A sub-family with about 12 kinds or fewer stores its kinds with `leaf = null` (C5b).
  - *Wine:* wine class, assigned by a **first-yes key**: (1) fortified with spirit? Fortified wine. (2) Sweet by botrytis, ice, dried grapes or late harvest? Sweet wine. (3) Sparkling? Sparkling wine. (4) A white made with long skin contact? Orange wine. (5) Pink? Rosé wine. (6) Otherwise still red or still white wine, by colour. Then a sparkling rosé is sparkling, a vin doux naturel is fortified, and Lambrusco is sparkling. Dosage, sweetness and body are attributes. A seventh leaf, Aromatised wine (vermouth, retsina; Culinary DR-F2-4), is added once it is sourced (†).
  - *Beer:* style family (BJCP family tags [S22]).
  - *Cider, perry, mead and other fruit ferments:* base fruit or honey.
  - *Sake and other rice wines:* `leaf = null` (2 sourced kinds so far).
  - *Spirits:* legal class or base spirit [S9].
  - *Liqueurs and flavoured spirits:* `leaf = null` while it holds about 12 kinds or fewer (6 now; about 15 expected); if it grows past that the leaf axis is flavouring [S9][S4].
  - *Mixed drinks:* `leaf = null` (1 kind).
- **Level 3, kind:** the type the buyer compares: grape or blend type (wine), BJCP style (beer, cider, mead), composition class (sake), legal or production type (spirits, liqueurs). Country or region alone never makes a kind (Appendix A).
- **Level 4, named form:** a maker's line or cuvée (maker-bound), or a traditional variant that many makers produce (not maker-bound), per C2. Vintage, age statement, bottle size and polishing ratio are not levels (4b, 5).

**Where Candidate A breaks.**
1. *Fortified wines and vermouth:* Port and Sherry are wines by material (grape) and spirits by strengthening; Wine.com and Liv-ex keep Port with wine [S1][S28]. 3. *Gin:* distilled with juniper, so spirit, but Vinatis counts it "compound" with liqueurs [S15]; the class rule in [S9] is unambiguous.
4. *Sake:* the NTA names mix two properties, added alcohol and polishing ratio [S8]. Resolved: the added-alcohol split is the kind (junmai sake against sake with brewer's alcohol); the eight NTA names are values of the attribute `designation`, and the polishing ratio (50 / 60 / 70 %) is an attribute with its standard (C4b). OWNER: O6 (whether composition, not designation, is a kind).
5. *Single bottles with several categories:* a whisky bottled by an independent bottler (Jura 1975 by Signatory Vintage) has a distillery, a bottler and a vintage [S30]: one kind, one named form, two makers (section 6).

Sparkling wine: "traditional-method sparkling wine" (Champagne, Crémant, Cava) and "tank-method sparkling wine" (Prosecco) [S11]. Fortified wine: one kind, "fortified wine" (Port, Sherry, Madeira). Sweet wine: "late-harvest wine" (Sauternes, Tokaj). Still red: "Bordeaux red blend" becomes the named form of "red blend" (Other Red Blends [S10]). Brandy: "aged grape brandy" (Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy de Jerez †), with Calvados under fruit brandy and Pisco under unaged grape brandy (generic labels need a fetched source †). Rum: Cachaça under "rum from cane juice". Agave: "agave spirit" (Tequila, Mezcal) [S9]. The tree shape and depth do not change.

### Candidate B: retail departments (Wine; Beer; Spirits; Cider and mixed), sake inside wine, liqueurs inside spirits

Mirrors Systembolaget [S2][S18] and Whisky.de [S4]. Levels: department → type facet → kind → named form (three levels). Leaf axis: the retailer's own "type" facet. Examples: Wine → Red → Pinot Noir; Spirits → Whisky → Single malt whisky; Cider & blanddrycker → Cider. Breaks where: (1) sake placed in wine contradicts the tax agency's product definition [S8] and Culinary; (2) mead, fruit wine and perry have no department (Systembolaget folds perry into cider [S20], BJCP groups them [S23]); (3) liqueurs inside spirits hide a TTB class [S9]; (4) the department list is the retailer's, not the product's, so adding a new retailer changes the tree.

### Candidate C: origin-first (country → region → appellation → style)

Matches how Old World wine is sold and how Liv-ex indices are built [S28], and how Vinatis sorts Champagne [S16]. Breaks: Appendix A makes region a modifier, so region cannot be a level; the same grape appears in dozens of regions; a spirit's legal type already carries origin only for a few cases (Scotch, Irish, Cognac [S9]); rejected for the tree. The origin remains a tag and a generated view (section 7).

## 5. Quality and tier

**There is no single tier ordering across J7.** The categories fall in three classes:

- **Spec ladders, as attributes with their standard named (C4b):** sake polishing ratio (50 / 60 / 70 %) and the eight NTA names, which are a ladder on it (NTA 清酒の製法品質表示基準 [S8]); Scotch age statement in years of the youngest component [S26] and the five legal categories [S7]; ABV (the TTB 40 % minimum [S9]); beer strength and colour tags (BJCP session / standard / high / very high [S22]); Champagne dosage [S16]. These are measured or legally defined on the object. They never become tier entries or levels. Under JC1(a) each ladder is a row of the shared `standard` table (OWNER: O3), and an object's value points to it (`subject_id`, `standard_id`, `value`).
- **Consensus ranking, as tier entries on a maker, line or named form (C4a):** fine wine. Critic scores appear as a facet with named sources and counts (James Suckling 4,969, Wine Spectator 3,891, Robert Parker's Wine Advocate 3,585, Jeb Dunnuck, Wine Enthusiast, Vinous, Decanter and others) [S1] and as a per-wine list ("Top 100 of 2025") [S1]. Liv-ex's index membership (Fine Wine 50, Fine Wine 100, Bordeaux Legends 40) is a second, market-based scheme [S28]. Each is a dated `scheme` / `value` entry with a source; wines may carry several, and a `disputed` flag is computed when they disagree. Both schemes are US and UK sources: C4(e) wants a second source from another region before the critic scheme enters the data (†). Official regional classifications (for example estate rankings) were not fetched and need a source before they enter the data (†). Critic scores and Liv-ex prices attach to a vintage, which is a C3 variant, while C4(a) lets tier entries sit only on makers, lines and named forms. OWNER: O12 (allow a variant as the subject of a tier entry); until then the entries sit on the named form (the wine as a label), without a vintage.
- **Platform tags (C4c):** Wine.com's Boutique, Collectible, Older Vintages, Bordeaux Futures, Rare and Green [S1]; Whisky Auctioneer's Private Selection, Travel Retail Exclusive, Single Cask, One of ≤25/50/100 bottles [S29]. They are dated `platform_tag` values, not tiers.
- **Preference only:** beer, cider and liqueurs; the sources fetched list no tier; price band alone applies.

**No individual-item facts (C16).** A cask number, a bottle number in a limited run, a warehouse receipt, a lot record and a condition or fill-level grade belong to one particular object and are never stored. The lot record [S30] is evidence that the attributes (distillery, age, vintage, production type, cask type, region, bottler, ABV, size) exist on kinds and named forms; it is not data to load.

**Tier type for the category: mixed.** Sake and spirits are spec-ranked (attributes); fine wine is consensus-ranked (tier entries); beer, cider and liqueurs are preference. **Price:** the shared USD item bands (C4d) from a dated list price, computed on a 750 ml (wine) or 700 ml (spirits) equivalent bottle, because bottles are compared by the bottle. OWNER: O4 (JC2): a `unit_price_band` in USD per litre is also derived from the dated list price, bands set at the first data load.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought: collectors filter by distillery and by bottler [S29], wine indices list named wines [S28], and Wine.com lists critic scores by named wine [S1]. Compatibility (platform) does not apply.

### 6.1 Maker ledger

One maker ledger on the C1 core schema, with `maker_class` keeping its C1 values (house or manufacture; independent; microbrand; retail own-label; licensee or diffusion; artisan or atelier; supplier). The supply-chain roles this category needs go in the multi-valued column `supply_role` (OWNER: O5, JC3), with these values: winery or estate; négociant; cooperative; distillery; brewery; cider maker; independent bottler; importer or trader; retail own-label. A Champagne house is a house or manufacture in C1 terms with `supply_role` négociant or winery as the source says. Consortia and appellation bodies are not makers: they are the `authority` of a designation row.

**Distillery against bottler.** The maker of a named form or line is the bottler or brand owner; the distillery that made the spirit is `built_by_maker_id` (C1 addition). Evidence: the rum filter "Original bottler / Independent bottler" [S21]; the whisky auction's Distillery Bottling 15,500 versus Signatory Vintage 1,682 [S29]; a lot record names distillery and bottler as different entities [S30]. The first draft's `bottler_role` column is dropped: a distillery's own bottling is a named form whose maker and `built_by_maker_id` are the same distillery; an independent bottling has two different makers. `appellation_ids` is not needed in the maker row; it is derived from the named-form ledger.

### 6.2 Designation and standard tables (replace the first draft's "origin ledger")

OWNER: O3 (JC1). Two tables, as proposed in JC1:
- **`designation`** (protected or legal name of origin or method): `designation_id`, name, native name, `scheme` (a legal type such as the US "distinctive product" class, a Scotch category, a Japanese national name), `register_id`, country, `authority` (the body, never a maker), `kinds_served`, rule summary, sources. Rows fetched in this batch: Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy de Jerez, Calvados, Pisco, Tequila and Mezcal (TTB "distinctive product of" a country [S9]); the five Scotch categories and Scotch Whisky [S7]; the eight Japanese tokutei meisho-shu names [S8]. Champagne, Port and the others rest on retailer and index pages [S1][S14][S28] and need a legal register source before they enter the table (†).
- **`standard`** (a measured or legal grading scheme): `standard_id`, name, authority, jurisdiction, `subject`, ordered scale values, `kinds_served`, source. Rows: the NTA polishing ratio [S8], the Scotch age statement [S26], the TTB minimum strength [S9], the BJCP strength and colour tags [S22] and Champagne dosage terms [S16].
- **Placement (JC1(c)).** A designation whose rules bind one kind is a non-maker named form carrying `designation_id` (Old Tom under gin is not a designation; Barolo under Nebbiolo would be). A designation spanning several kinds is a tag on products and named forms: Scotch (it spans single malt, single grain, blended malt and blended whisky [S7]), and each country of origin. A designation is a kind only under C11. OWNER: O1 asks whether the wine and spirit names in this tree are a written exception.
- Origin is a tag on named forms and bottles, never a level (Appendix A). Legal classes a jurisdiction assigns for tax, excise, licence or age of sale go to the C17 jurisdiction ledger; none was fetched here.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

C7 says canonical form first, then the sub-family's secondary key. Proposed keys:
- **Wine leaves:** leaves in the order Still red, Still white, Rosé, Orange, Sparkling, Sweet, Fortified (the first-yes key reversed, still wines first). Inside Still red and Still white, single-grape kinds first and blend kinds after, each group ordered by **body, light to full**, the Wine Folly order [S25]; named forms by maker, then first-introduction year. Reason: Wine Folly's styles already order grapes by body.
- **Beer leaves:** **BJCP category and letter order** [S6] (1A, 1B, 1C, 2A…): an attested, stable order that is not a ranking.
- **Cider and mead:** BJCP order (C1A to C1E, C2, C3, C4; M1 to M3) [S24][S23][S6].
- **Sake:** canonical form first: junmai sake (no added alcohol), then sake with brewer's alcohol [S8]. The attribute `designation` lists its values in the NTA table order, which runs from ginjo through junmai to honjozo [S8]; the polishing ratio is an attribute, not the sort key.
- **Spirits:** the legal-type order inside each class: for whisky, the Scotch categories in the regulation's order (Single Malt, Single Grain, Blended Malt, Blended Grain, Blended) [S7], then pot still, bourbon and rye; for brandy, the US type list (grape brandy first, then Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy de Jerez, Calvados, Pisco) [S9]. Country is not a sort key.
- **Variants:** by year, then bottler (C7: by `variant_code` or year); age statements ascending inside a named form.

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (first *yes* wins; S1 one home):

1. **J7 vs Culinary DR-F / DR-E / DR-G:** a wine, beer, cider, sake or spirit in a sealed bottle, can, keg or cask is J7 as the product bought; the same drink as a drink poured, a dish with wine, or a cocktail made to serve is Culinary. A ready-bottled cocktail or mixed drink in a sealed can or bottle is J7.7 (Systembolaget's blanddrycker [S20]); a cocktail mixed to order is Culinary DR-I. OWNER: O8 (J7.7, and hard seltzer and spritzers, are unsourced †).
2. **J7 vs J5:** wine, spirits and extracts made for cooking or flavouring (mirin, cooking wine, bitters) are J5 (step 3 note). The J review's proposed sentence (X7, in `_framework_conflicts.md`): *bitters sold as a flavouring by the dash are J5; a bitter liqueur sold to drink by the glass (amaro, Fernet) is J7.6.* No fetched page names amaro; the TTB liqueur class is broad [S9] (†). OWNER: O7 (framework sentence). Wine vinegar and sake lees are J5, not J7.
3. **J7 vs OUT (rights and claims):** wine or spirits **bought but not yet delivered** is OUT (step 1): Bordeaux futures (the "Bordeaux Futures" facet [S1], a 2022 vintage "Pre-sale: ships after 03/15/2026" listing [S1]), en primeur offers, and a warehouse receipt for a cask held in a bonded store [S27] are claims until the wine or spirit is delivered. A bottle or cask physically delivered to the owner is J7 as stock. OWNER: O11 (confirm OUT until delivered, and say whether a cask physically in the owner's cellar, or held on a warehouse receipt, is a claim).
4. **J7 vs B5, B1 and gift or mixed packs:** decanters, wine glasses, barware, corkscrews and wine fridges are B5; the liquid is J7. A cellar rack or wine shelf is B1 (S3, "never parts"). A gift set is filed by its item of greatest value (a Champagne and chocolates set is J7 because the Champagne costs more [S1]); the box kept afterwards is B1. A mixed case of different wines (Wine.com "Mixed Collections" [S1]) is filed by the step 3 set rule today; JC5(c) would file a mix under the kind that is half or more of its weight and otherwise under a final "mixed" kind. OWNER: O13 (JC5(c)).
5. **J7 vs B9:** a cannabis-, CBD-, kratom- or areca-bearing drink or beer is B9 whatever the form (step 27 note); an ordinary alcoholic drink is J7.
6. **J7 vs prepared drinks (Culinary):** alcohol-free beer, wine and spirits, non-alcoholic cider (the US sense of "sweet cider" is unfermented [S23]), kombucha and juices are Culinary (step 1); Systembolaget keeps alcohol-free beside, not inside, the alcoholic categories [S2].
7. **J7 vs C3 and home-brewing:** grape vines and hop plants are plants (C3); the grape juice or malt sold to make a drink is J5 or Culinary. No key names home-brewing: kits and ingredients (†) are proposed as ingredients J5 and equipment B5. OWNER: O14.
