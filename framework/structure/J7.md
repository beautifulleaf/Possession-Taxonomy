## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J7 Wine, Beer & Spirits (domain J, Groceries).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 3, last branch): *wine, beer, cider, sake, spirits and other alcoholic drinks in sealed containers.* The step lead is "an alcoholic drink in a sealed container (bottle, can, keg, cask, box)". Category line: "alcoholic drinks in sealed containers, by style, appellation and age."

**Precedence sentences and notes that touch J7:**
- Step 1: wine or spirits **bought but not yet delivered** is a right or claim and OUT; a prepared drink (soft drinks, juices, waters, ready-to-drink coffee and tea, kombucha, alcohol-free beer) is left to Culinary. The same step's list ends "alcohol-free beer, wine and spirits", which can be misread as putting all wine and spirits out; section 9 and question 6 treat this as a wording defect (step 3 and the category line say alcoholic drinks are in).
- Step 3 note: Culinary describes foods as eaten; here each is filed once, as the product as bought, divided by product and by quality of ingredient, and Culinary links to it. **Wines and spirits made for cooking or flavouring (mirin, cooking wine, bitters, extracts) are J5.** A set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value (OUT if that item is a prepared food); the basket or box kept afterwards is B1.
- Step 27 note (B9): alcoholic drinks are J7; cannabis, kratom and areca drinks are B9 whatever their food form.
- Step 17 neighbours: glassware, decanters, barware and wine fridges are B5; humidors and cellar racks are B1 (S3 "never parts": storage that holds several objects is furniture).
- S2 (kinds are generic types; models, cuts and designs are named forms; individual items are never kinds), S5 (test the object, not the motive: a collected bottle is a bottle), S6 (attestation), Appendix A (region, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, material, edition and condition are modifiers).
- Owner decisions in BRIEF.md: "wine and spirits stay in scope as B9 stock by object (all alcoholic drinks in sealed containers)" (this now reads J7 in v0.6); grocery products that Culinary also holds (cheese, cured meats, fruit, tea, coffee, wine) are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind.
- Cross-link to Culinary (local file `/home/user/culinary-taxonomy/culinary/FRAMEWORK_v1.md`, read for this batch, not fetched): DR-E Beer & Grain Ferments (beers E1, rice and grain wines E3), DR-F Wine & Fermented Fruit, Honey & Sap (still wines by style F1, sparkling, sweet, fortified and aromatised F2, ciders, fruit wines and meads F3), DR-G Spirits (grain G1, fruit, cane and plant G2, botanical G3), DR-H Liqueurs & Infused Spirits. ### 1.5 Where sources agree and where they split the same objects

- **Agree:** the top level is the drink family (wine, beer, spirits, cider; alcohol-free apart) in Wine.com, Systembolaget, Vinatis and the Whisky Exchange [S1][S2][S14][S5]. Still wine is split by colour first [S1][S2][S14][S25]. A spirit is typed by the legal class and type [S9][S12].
- **Split, sake:** Wine.com files saké as a wine type (36 items) [S1]; the Whisky Exchange pairs "Beer & Sake" [S5]; the tax agency treats it as its own product with its own standard [S8]; Culinary files it with rice wines (local file). Production is closer to beer; the label standard is its own.
- **Split, liqueurs and gin:** TTB makes cordials and liqueurs a class and gin a separate class [S9]; Whisky.de puts liqueur under a "Spirits" department and gin top-level [S4]; Vinatis calls both "compound" spirits [S15].
- **Split, fortified wine:** Wine.com puts Dessert, Sherry & Port inside wine [S1]; Liv-ex keeps Port as a separate index [S28]; Wine Folly joins dessert and fortified in one style [S25].
- **Split, Champagne:** a sparkling wine type at Wine.com [S1][S11], a department at Vinatis [S14], an index at Liv-ex [S28].
- **Split, whisky:** country first and variety second at Whisky.de [S4]; a mix of country and style in one list at Wine.com [S12]; the law gives five Scotch categories and a separate US type list [S7][S9].
- **Split, beer:** BJCP groups for judging and says the groups are arbitrary [S22]; Vinatis sorts by flavour profile [S17]; Systembolaget by "öltyp" [S19].
- **Split, rum:** by base (molasses, cane, agricole) and by colour at once at Whisky.de [S4][S21]; TTB defines rum by the sugar-cane base and treats Cachaça as a type [S9].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Drink family** (wine, beer, spirits, cider) is the first branch on every retailer fetched [S2][S5][S14][S18]. Sake, liqueurs, gin and fortified wine are the drinks whose home varies from shop to shop (1.5).
2. **Within wine, colour or process class** comes before grape or region. Wine.com's wine-type facet (red, white, sparkling, dessert and port, rosé, saké, orange) comes first, then grape within red [S1][S10]; Systembolaget filters white, red, rosé, sparkling, fortified [S2]; Vinatis opens with colour departments [S14]; Wine Folly's nine styles are colour and body [S25].
3. **Within spirits, the legal class or base spirit, then type, then age.** Wine.com and the Whisky Exchange start with drink type [S5][S12]; Whisky.de goes country then variety [S4]; the law uses class then type [S9]; the auction house filters distillery, bottler, age, ABV last [S29].
4. **Filter order on Wine.com** is Gift Type, Occasion, Variety, Varietal, Region, Availability, Size & Type, Fine Wine, Vintage, Reviewed By [S1]. Style and variety precede region; vintage and critic score come late and act as filters. Price, ABV, vintage and rating are sliders on every list [S1][S12][S13].
5. **Maker and bottler are first for collectors.** At the auction house the series, distillery and bottler lists are longer than any other facet [S29]; Liv-ex indices are named-wine lists [S28].
6. **Questions buyers ask** are style questions (BJCP: which style, how sweet, how carbonated [S24]; Wine Folly: body and food [S25]; Whisky.de gift finder: smoky or not, sherry-fruity or not, origin, budget [S4]).

The first axis is therefore **what the drink is** (family, then class or style). Region, age, vintage and price are filters.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's levels: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form, at most four below the category (C5).

### Candidate A (recommended): drink family by raw material and process, then class or style

- **Level 1, sub-family: the base material and how it was fermented or distilled.** Seven sub-families: Wine (grape); Beer (grain ales and lagers); Cider, perry, mead and other fruit and honey ferments; Sake and other rice and grain wines; Spirits (distilled, unsweetened); Liqueurs and flavoured spirits; Mixed alcoholic drinks (ready-to-drink). This is the Culinary DR-E to DR-H order with the cocktails made ready-bottled.
- **Level 2, leaf: one axis stated per sub-family** (C5c): wine, wine class (colour for still wines; method for sparkling, fortified and sweet, the Wine.com type facet [S1]); beer, style family (BJCP family tags [S22]); cider and others, base fruit or honey; sake, brewing class (with or without brewer's alcohol, NTA [S8]); spirits, legal class (base spirit) [S9]; liqueurs, flavouring [S9][S4]; mixed drinks, build.
- **Level 3, kind:** the type the buyer compares: grape or blend type (wine), BJCP style (beer, cider, mead), tokutei meisho-shu designation (sake), legal type designation (spirits, liqueurs).
- **Level 4, named form:** a maker's line or cuvée (maker-bound), or a regional or traditional variant that many makers produce (not maker-bound), per C2. Vintage and age statement are not levels (4b, 5).

**Where Candidate A breaks.**
1. *Fortified wines and vermouth:* Port and Sherry are wines by material (grape) and spirits by strengthening; Wine.com and Liv-ex keep Port with wine [S1][S28]. Aromatised wine (vermouth) is not listed on any fetched page; Culinary DR-F2-4 has it (local file); unattested here.
2. *Ready-bottled cocktails and hard seltzer* sit between J7.7 and Culinary DR-I. Systembolaget separates "blanddrycker" from cider and from spirits [S20]; no fetched page lists their kinds.
3. *Gin:* distilled with juniper, so spirit, but Vinatis counts it "compound" with liqueurs [S15]; the class rule in [S9] is unambiguous.
4. *Sake with added spirit vs. honjozo-style names:* the NTA names mix two properties, added alcohol and polishing ratio [S8]. A single "leaf axis" holds only if the leaf is read as the added-alcohol class and the polishing ratio goes to an attribute (4b, 5).
5. *Single bottles with several categories:* a whisky bottled by an independent bottler (Jura 1975 by Signatory Vintage) has a distillery, a bottler and a vintage [S30]: one kind, one named form, two makers (section 6).

### Candidate B: retail departments (Wine; Beer; Spirits; Cider and mixed), sake inside wine, liqueurs inside spirits

Mirrors Systembolaget [S2][S18] and Whisky.de [S4]. Levels: department → type facet → kind → named form (three levels). Leaf axis: the retailer's own "type" facet. Examples: Wine → Red → Pinot Noir; Spirits → Whisky → Single Malt Scotch; Cider & blanddrycker → Cider. Breaks where: (1) sake placed in wine contradicts the tax agency's product definition [S8] and Culinary; (2) mead, fruit wine and perry have no department (Systembolaget folds perry into cider [S20], BJCP groups them [S23]); (3) liqueurs inside spirits hide a TTB class [S9]; (4) the department list is the retailer's, not the product's, so adding a new retailer changes the tree.

### Candidate C: origin-first (country → region → appellation → style)

Matches how Old World wine is sold and how Liv-ex indices are built [S28], and how Vinatis sorts Champagne [S16]. Breaks: Appendix A makes region a modifier, so region cannot be a level; the same grape appears in dozens of regions; a spirit's legal type already carries origin only for a few cases (Scotch, Irish, Cognac [S9]); rejected for the tree. The origin remains a tag and a generated view (section 7).

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds (estimates, not counted from a source unless marked):**
- Wine: about 110. The red-wine facet alone lists 24 types [S10]; add whites, sparkling, fortified and sweet types.
- Beer: about 100. BJCP 2021 has 34 categories [S6]; the lettered styles within were not counted in this batch.
- Cider, perry, mead and other fruit ferments: about 15 (BJCP C1A–C1E, C2, C3, C4 plus mead categories [S23][S24][S6]).
- Sake: 8 designations [S8] plus a few non-designated types.
- Spirits: about 55 (US type list [S9] plus the Wine.com and Whisky.de lists [S12][S4]).
- Liqueurs and flavoured spirits: about 15 [S9][S4].
- Mixed drinks: about 5.
- **Total: about 310 kinds.**

**Named forms:** very large. The markets fetched hold 13,295 items at one US wine store [S1], 9,377 products in stock at one Swedish retailer [S18] and 137,440 catalogued bottles at one whisky auction house [S29]; most of those are vintages, bottler editions and sizes, which are variants. Cuvée-level and expression-level named forms (a line, not a year) are probably in the low tens of thousands if everything were loaded. Policy: load only those with an attested source (S6), and use a seed list from the fetched index lists [S28] and catalogues [S29].

**Where depth concentrates:** single malt Scotch and bourbon (bottler editions, distillery lines) [S29]; Bordeaux, Burgundy, Champagne and Rhône cuvées [S28]; beer styles [S6].

**Leaves by breadth:**
- *Broad:* Still red wine, Still white wine, Whisky, Pale lager family, Sparkling wine.
- *Medium:* Brandy, Rum, Fortified and sweet wine, Apple cider, Amber and dark lagers, Liqueurs, Junmai family.
- *Narrow:* Orange wine, Rosé (few kinds, many wines), Perry, Mead, Agave, Gin, Vodka (few kinds, many named forms), Sake non-designated.

### 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers.** The evidence differs by sub-family:

| Sub-family | Where buyers compare | Evidence | Choice level |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wine, still | Grape or blend type, then maker and vintage | Wine.com's third-level facet is the grape or blend, with counts [S10]; Wine Folly says the style shortcut replaces variety and region for beginners [S25] | Kind = grape or blend type; appellation is a region tag or a non-maker named form |
| Wine, sparkling and fortified | Appellation-named type | Champagne has its own department at Vinatis [S14], an index at Liv-ex [S28], dosage and blend types [S16]; Port has an index [S28]; Wine.com explains Champagne, Crémant, Cava and Prosecco as separate methods and places [S11] | Kind = appellation-named type (C2(c)), with blend type (Blanc de Blancs) and dosage as non-maker named form and attribute |
| Beer | Style | BJCP style is the unit of judging and the commercial-example list [S6][S22]; Systembolaget beer type is a facet [S19] | Kind = BJCP style; style family is the leaf |
| Cider and mead | Style | BJCP C1A–C1E and M1–M3 [S24][S6] | Kind = BJCP style |
| Sake | Designation | The eight names are a legal list and a purchase choice [S8] | Kind = designation; polishing ratio is an attribute with its standard |
| Spirits | Type designation, then distillery or brand, then age | TTB class and type [S9]; Scotch categories [S7]; Wine.com type lists [S12]; the auction facets rank distillery, bottler and age [S29] | Kind = legal type; distillery and bottler are makers; age is an attribute |

## 5. Quality and tier

**There is no single tier ordering across J7.** The categories fall in three classes:

- **Spec ladders, as attributes with their standard named (C4b):** sake polishing ratio (50 / 60 / 70 %, NTA 清酒の製法品質表示基準 [S8]); Scotch age statement in years of the youngest component [S26] and the five legal categories [S7]; ABV (the TTB 40 % minimum [S9]); beer strength and colour tags (BJCP session / standard / high / very high [S22]); Champagne dosage [S16]. These are measured or legally defined on the object. They never become tier entries.
- **Consensus ranking, as tier entries on a maker, line or named form (C4a):** fine wine. Critic scores appear as a facet with named sources and counts (James Suckling 4,969, Wine Spectator 3,891, Robert Parker's Wine Advocate 3,585, Jeb Dunnuck, Wine Enthusiast, Vinous, Decanter and others) [S1] and as a per-wine list ("Top 100 of 2025") [S1]. Liv-ex's index membership (Fine Wine 50, Fine Wine 100, Bordeaux Legends 40) is a second, market-based scheme [S28]. Each is a dated `scheme` / `value` entry with a source; wines may carry several, and a `disputed` flag is computed when they disagree. Official regional classifications (for example estate rankings) were not fetched in this batch and need a source before they enter the data.
- **Platform tags (C4c):** Wine.com's Boutique, Collectible, Older Vintages, Bordeaux Futures, Rare and Green [S1]; Whisky Auctioneer's Private Selection, Travel Retail Exclusive, Single Cask [S29]. They are dated `platform_tag` values, not tiers.
- **Preference only:** beer, cider and liqueurs; the sources fetched list no tier; price band alone applies.

**Tier type for the category: mixed.** Sake and spirits are spec-ranked (attributes); fine wine is consensus-ranked (tier entries); beer, cider and liqueurs are preference. Price bands use the shared USD bands (C4d) from a dated list price.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Identity.** The maker is part of what is sought: collectors filter by distillery and by bottler [S29], wine indices list named wines [S28], and Wine.com lists critic scores by named wine [S1]. Compatibility (platform) does not apply.

**Recommend a maker ledger** on the C1 core schema, with maker classes extended for this category: *winery or estate*; *négociant or merchant*; *distillery*; *brewery*; *independent bottler*; *Champagne house* (a house or manufacture in C1 terms); *cooperative*; *cider maker*; *own-label retailer*. Extensions (optional, per C1):
- `bottler_role` (distillery bottling, independent bottler, négociant, contract). Evidence: the rum filter "Original bottler / Independent bottler" [S21]; the whisky auction's Distillery Bottling 15,500 versus Signatory Vintage 1,682 [S29]; a lot record names distillery and bottler as different entities [S30].
- `appellation_ids` is not needed in the maker row; it is derived from the named-form ledger.

**Origin ledger (recommended, like C13's compatibility ledger).** Appellations and legal origin names (Champagne, Cognac, Scotch, Tequila) carry rules that bound which kinds they apply to: `origin_id`, name, native name, country, kind of designation as the source states it, `kinds_served`, rule source. The legal wording is fetched for Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy de Jerez, Calvados, Pisco, Tequila and Mezcal (TTB "distinctive product of" a country [S9]), for Scotch [S7] and for Japanese sake names [S8]. Origin is a tag on named forms and bottles, never a level (Appendix A).

**Lines and variants.** Collections and series (Macallan Fine & Rare, Balvenie Tun 1401, Springbank Society [S29]) go to the line table; the vintage, a bottler's edition or a collaboration goes to the variant table (C3, `variant_type` extended with *vintage* and *bottler edition*; the vintage year is the `year` column).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

C7 says canonical form first, then the sub-family's secondary key. Proposed keys:
- **Wine leaves:** single-grape kinds first and blend kinds after, each group ordered by **body, light to full**, the Wine Folly order [S25]; named forms by maker, then first-introduction year. Reason: Wine Folly's styles already order grapes by body.
- **Beer leaves:** **BJCP category and letter order** [S6] (1A, 1B, 1C, 2A…): an attested, stable order that is not a ranking.
- **Cider and mead:** BJCP order (C1A to C1E, C2, C3, C4; M1 to M3) [S24][S23][S6].
- **Sake:** NTA table order, which runs from ginjo through junmai to honjozo [S8]; the polishing ratio is an attribute, not the sort key.
- **Spirits:** the legal-type order inside each class: for whisky, the Scotch five categories in the regulation's order (Single Malt, Single Grain, Blended Malt, Blended Grain, Blended) [S7], then the other countries; for brandy, the US type list (grape brandy first, then Cognac, Armagnac, Brandy de Jerez, Calvados, Pisco) [S9].
- **Variants:** by year, then bottler; age statements ascending inside a named form.

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (first *yes* wins; S1 one home):

1. **J7 vs Culinary DR-F / DR-E / DR-G:** a wine, beer, cider, sake or spirit in a sealed bottle, can, keg or cask is J7 as the product bought; the same drink as a drink poured, a dish with wine, or a cocktail made to serve is Culinary. A ready-bottled cocktail or mixed drink in a sealed can or bottle is J7.7 (Systembolaget's blanddrycker [S20]); a cocktail mixed to order is Culinary DR-I.
2. **J7 vs J5:** wine, spirits and extracts made for cooking or flavouring (mirin, cooking wine, bitters) are J5 (step 3 note); a bitter spirit sold to drink (amaro, Fernet) is a liqueur in J7.6 (the TTB liqueur class is broad [S9]; no fetched page names amaro, so this is a proposal). Wine vinegar and sake lees are J5, not J7.
3. **J7 vs OUT (rights and claims):** wine or spirits **bought but not yet delivered** is OUT (step 1): Bordeaux futures (the "Bordeaux Futures" facet [S1], a 2022 vintage "Pre-sale: ships after 03/15/2026" listing [S1]), en primeur offers, and a warehouse receipt for a cask held in a bonded store [S27] are claims until the wine or spirit is delivered. A bottle or cask physically delivered to the owner is J7 as stock (question 4).
4. **J7 vs B5:** decanters, wine glasses, barware, corkscrews and wine fridges are B5; the liquid is J7. A cellar rack or wine shelf is B1 (S3, "never parts"). A gift set is filed by its item of greatest value (a Champagne and chocolates set is J7 because the Champagne costs more [S1]); the box kept afterwards is B1.
5. **J7 vs B9:** a cannabis-, CBD-, kratom- or areca-bearing drink or beer is B9 whatever the form (step 27 note); an ordinary alcoholic drink is J7.
6. **J7 vs prepared drinks (Culinary):** alcohol-free beer, wine and spirits, non-alcoholic cider (the US sense of "sweet cider" is unfermented [S23]), kombucha and juices are Culinary (step 1); Systembolaget keeps alcohol-free beside, not inside, the alcoholic categories [S2].
7. **J7 vs C3:** grape vines and hop plants are plants (C3); the grape juice or malt sold to make a drink is J5 or Culinary (no J7 key names home-brewing; question 8).
8. **J7 vs F1/F2/B4:** a collectible empty bottle or label is filed as the object it is (S5; a glass bottle is B4 or B5 by its use); a wine label or menu is a document (F5). These edges are not attested in the fetched pages.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure:** Candidate A, seven sub-families by raw material and process (Wine; Beer; Cider, perry, mead and other fruit and honey ferments; Sake and other rice wines; Spirits; Liqueurs and flavoured spirits; Mixed alcoholic drinks), a leaf per sub-family on one stated axis (wine class, style family, base fruit or honey, brewing class, legal class, flavouring), kinds at the grape or blend type, BJCP style, tokutei meisho-shu designation and legal type, and named forms for lines, cuvées and expressions. **Maximum depth: four levels below the category.** Vintage, age statement, ABV, polishing ratio, dosage, sweetness, size and closure are attributes or variants. Region and appellation are tags and a generated view. A maker ledger with a `bottler_role` extension and an origin ledger carry the identity layer. Tier is mixed: spec ladders as attributes, fine-wine critic and index entries as tier entries.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. **Regional-name kinds.** Keep Champagne, Port, Sherry, Cognac and similar as kinds (evidence: department, index and facet in [S14][S28][S12]; C2(c)), or make generic kinds by method (traditional-method sparkling, fortified wine) with the regional names as named forms (C11)? This memo recommends kinds.
2. **Wine kind level.** Grape or blend type as the kind, with appellation as a tag and non-maker named form, against appellation as the kind for Old World wines (Candidate C pressure).
3. **Sake designations** as kinds, with the polishing ratio as an attribute (C4b): the eight names are a legal list and also read as a grade ladder.
4. **Cask and futures ownership.** Confirm OUT until delivered (step 1), and say whether a cask physically in the owner's cellar or a warehouse-receipt cask is a claim.
5. **Ready-bottled cocktails and hard seltzer:** J7.7, or Culinary DR-I?
6. **Step 1 wording.** The list "alcohol-free beer, wine and spirits" should read "alcohol-free versions of beer, wine and spirits" (log in `_framework_conflicts.md`).
7. **Bitters and amaro boundary:** drinking bitters in J7.6; cooking and cocktail bitters in J5 (step 3 names bitters under J5).
8. **Home-brewing and winemaking:** kits and ingredients have no key; propose equipment B5, ingredients J5 (unsourced).
9. **Vintage in the variant ledger:** add `variant_type = vintage` and `bottler edition` to C3.
10. **Coverage:** authorise a second pass for East Asian and other regional drinks (makgeolli, huangjiu, baijiu, shochu, soju, arak, palm wines) with sources from those markets, and for official regional wine classifications.
11. **Maker seeding:** load makers at scale only from attested lists (Liv-ex components, Wine.com, the auction catalogue); decide how many.
