## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J4 Fruit & Vegetables, in domain J Groceries & Cellar.

**Membership test (placement key step 3, fourth branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** "**fruit, vegetables, herbs, edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen**, whole, cut or washed, but not dressed, sauced, fried or coated". Category line: "fruit, vegetables, herbs, edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen, by species and cultivar."

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch J4:**
- Step 3 head: groceries are filed once, as the product as bought, "divided by product and by the quality of the ingredient"; Culinary describes the same foods as eaten and links to it. Nuts, seeds, dried fruit, dried vegetables, dried mushrooms and dried seaweed are J5; pickles, preserves, tinned and jarred fruit, vegetables and tomatoes are J5; sauces and condiments are J5 "whatever they are made from"; pulses are J5.
- Step 6 note: "cut vegetables, herbs, sprouts and microgreens sold to eat are step 3; a plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is [C3] even when sold to eat, such as potted basil or living microgreens."
- Step 1: a salad (a dressed mixture), a dish, a soup or a ready meal is prepared and left to Culinary; "curing, smoking, ageing, fermenting, drying, salting, pickling, tinning, freezing or plain cooking of one main ingredient does not make a food prepared".
- Step 7 (F6): natural objects kept as specimens are F6; a natural material sold for a use is filed by that use.
- Brief (owner, 2026-10-17): grocery products that Culinary also holds are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind; groceries are judged by the quality of the ingredient.
- Culinary link plan (FRAMEWORK_v1.md, SW-A): Culinary already holds fruit species as kinds and cultivars as variants ("Fruit species are kinds, cultivars variants", rule s13), with leaves Citrus SW-A1-1, Pome SW-A1-2, Stone SW-A1-3, Persimmons SW-A1-4, Berries SW-A1-5, Grapes SW-A1-6, Melons SW-A1-7, Pomegranates and dryland fruit SW-A1-8, and the tropical families SW-A2-1 to SW-A2-17; edible seaweeds SV-H5-2, wild mushrooms and truffles SV-H5-5, chillies and peppers SV-H5-7. - *Australia, Woolworths* (S18, S19, S1). The aisle Fruit & Veg opens into Fruit, Vegetables, Salad, Prepared Vegetables, Organic, Fresh Herbs/Garlic/Chillies and Snacking. Fruit divides into Apples & Pears, Bananas, Berries & Cherries, Citrus, Grapes, Melons & Mangoes, Avocados, Pineapples/Kiwi/Tropical, Asian & Exotic, and Cut Fruit. Vegetables divide into crop-named groups (Zucchini/Eggplant/Squash, Sprouts, Mushrooms, Fresh Herbs/Garlic/Chillies, Organic, Prepacked; links on the aisle page also show Carrots/Onions/Root veg, Potatoes/Pumpkins, Lettuce/Kale/Leafy greens, Celery). The page explains its own rule: tomatoes, cucumbers, avocados and capsicums "are botanically fruits", but "because they aren't sweet, we categorise them as vegetables" (S18); it also teaches the plant-part view (flower with seeds, root, leaf, stem).
- *UK, Waitrose* (S26, S34, S27, S28, S29). Fresh Fruit (Berries, Loose Fruit, Bananas, Apples, Pears, Grapes, Oranges/Lemons/Citrus, Mangos/Melons/Exotic, Peaches & Nectarines, Plums/Apricots, Prepared Fruit, Nuts/Seeds/Dried Fruit, Cherries, Organic, Avocado, Perfectly Ripe) and Fresh Vegetables are separate aisles from Fresh Salad & Herbs (cucumber and radish sit there) and from In Season Fruit & Vegetables; frozen fruit (Cherries, Blueberry, smoothie mixes) and frozen vegetables (Peas, Sweetcorn & Carrots, Beans, Spinach/Broccoli/Cauliflower, Brussels Sprouts, Mixed Vegetables, Parsnips/Celeriac/Squash/Beetroot, Onions, Herbs) are aisles in the Frozen department.
- *Germany, REWE* (S2): mushrooms are their own group, split into Frische Champignons, Frische Pilze and Getrocknete Pilze, so fresh and dried mushrooms sit together in one shop category (the framework sends the dried ones to J5).
- *France, Carrefour* (S3): the Fruits et Légumes rayon divides into Bio, Fruits, Légumes, Prêt à consommer, Jus de fruits frais, Fruits et légumes secs and Corbeilles; seasonal lists (légumes de saison, fruits de saison) cut across it; the filters offer quality labels (Label Rouge, IGP, AOP, Bio) and country of origin.
- *Japan, Rakuten greengrocer* (S7): single fruit, single vegetable, boxed fruit by species (strawberry, mikan, grapefruit, persimmon, grape, peach, banana, apple, melon, pineapple, watermelon, mango, kiwi, lychee), and sweet potato split by named cultivar (Naruto Kintoki, Amakun, Annou).
- *India, BigBasket* (S21): Fresh Fruits, Fresh Vegetables, Exotic Fruits & Veggies, Herbs & Seasonings, Organic, Cuts & Sprouts; facets for banana varieties (Nendran, Poovan, Robusta, Yelakki, Green, Red) and mango varieties (Alphonso, Kesar, Dasheri, Langra, Himsagar, Totapuri...) as cultivar filters.
- *Singapore, FairPrice* (S31): Vegetables divide into Frozen Vegetables, Leafy Vegetables, Mushrooms, Potatoes & Roots, Garlic/Ginger/Onions, so frozen sits inside the vegetable group here.
- *United States* (S20, S25): Instacart divides Fresh Produce into Fresh Fruit, Fresh Vegetables and party trays, with Frozen Food as a separate top category; Kroger files Frozen Vegetables in its Frozen Foods department.

**Enthusiast communities and wikis.** Orange Pippin (S10, S35) lists tree-fruit species (apples, crab-apples, apricots, cherries, peaches, plums, pears) with named varieties inside each; its apple directory holds about 800 entries (sum of the A to Z counts), each described as dessert, culinary or cider, with synonyms (Albermarle Pippin is a synonym for Newtown Pippin). Know the Pepper (S24) sorts 215+ chillies into five Scoville tiers. Wikipedia (S11) groups Capsicum cultivars under five species and notes that British English calls sweet ones "peppers" and hot ones "chillies" while Australian and Indian English use "capsicum" for bell peppers.

**Collectors, resale and trade encyclopedias.** There is no collector market. The Specialty Produce encyclopedia of a wholesaler (S13) catalogues thousands of articles by produce family and shows the licensing pattern: Cripps Pink is the varietal name and "only around 10 percent" of the crop is sold under the trademark Pink Lady, whose name is owned and managed by a state department and an Australian industry body.

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- UNECE (S4): one standard per commodity or commodity group (Aubergines, Berry fruits, Certain small fruits, Ceps, Truffles, Leafy vegetables, Root and tubercle vegetables, Shallots, Strawberries...), plus an informal citrus variety list (S36).
- EU/UK marketing standards (S5, S33): Extra Class, Class I, Class II for ten specifically listed products (apples, citrus, kiwi, lettuces, peaches/nectarines, pears, strawberries, sweet peppers, table grapes, tomatoes); a General Marketing Standard (no classes) for most other fresh fruit, vegetables, nuts, herbs and cultivated mushrooms; excluded: potatoes, chilli peppers, sweetcorn, olives, wild mushrooms, sweet potatoes, ripened bananas, ginger.
- USDA AMS (S6, S17, S12): voluntary U.S. grade standards for each vegetable and fruit, fresh and for processing; frozen vegetables have their own grades A, B, C and Substandard, scored 90, 80 and 70 points.
- Japan (S14, S15): the national standard shipping specifications were abolished in 2002; each prefecture now sets its own. Fukushima grades fruit and fruit-like vegetables Tokushu/Shu/Yu/Ryo (特秀・秀・優・良), vegetables and mushrooms A/B, with size classes by count per box or weight and colour tables per cultivar (Fuji, Tsugaru, Jonagold...).
- China (S16): GB/T 26906-2024 grades sweet cherry special, first and second class, with size by diameter and soluble solids of at least 14 percent.
- IFPS PLU (S22, S23): over 1,500 codes identifying commodity, variety, growing method (prefix 9 for organic) and size; mushrooms alone have 21 codes (button small/large, cremini, portabella, shiitake, oyster, enoki, chanterelle, morel, cep, wood ear...).
- GS1 GPC (S8): Family Fruits Unprepared/Unprocessed (Fresh) with classes such as Pome Fruits, Kiwifruits and Miscellaneous Fruit, and bricks by species (Apples with an Apple Variety attribute, Strawberries, Dates, Durian, Mangos, Lychees); separate families for Vegetables (Non Leaf), Leaf Vegetables and Fresh Cut; an attribute "Quality (UNECE Standard)".

**Museums and reference works.** The UC Riverside Citrus Variety Collection (S9) holds about 1,000 citrus cultivars and relatives, browsable alphabetically and "by category or type". The CIAT guide (S32) divides vegetables into ten classes by plant part (leafy, root, bulb, stem, flower, fruit, pod, tuber, seed, fungi) and admits overlaps (potato root or tuber; pea pod or legume). A trade guide on seaweed (S30) groups it by colour (green, brown, red) with named types (nori, wakame, kombu, dulse, arame, hijiki, sea grapes).

**Where sources agree.** Every retailer with a full tree opens with fruit against vegetable (S1, S3, S20, S21, S26, S34); herbs are split from vegetables in three of them (S1, S21, S27); the commodity (apple, pear, tomato, mushroom) is the unit of standards, trade codes and shop sub-aisles (S4, S6, S8, S17, S22). **Where they split the same objects differently:** avocado (fruit aisle and vegetable aisle at Woolworths, S18/S19); tomato and cucumber (fruit botanically, vegetables on every shelf); mushrooms (inside vegetables at Woolworths and FairPrice, S1, S31; their own group at REWE, S2; UNECE has separate Ceps and Truffles standards, S4); frozen (a department apart at Waitrose, Kroger, Instacart: S25, S28, S29, S20; inside vegetables at FairPrice, S31); cherries (with berries at Woolworths, S19; stone fruit botanically, and Waitrose gives them an aisle of their own, S26); chillies (with herbs and garlic at Woolworths, S18; excluded from the EU standards, S33); nuts and dried fruit (inside Fresh Fruit at Waitrose, S26; the framework puts them in J5).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose in this order, from the shop trees above: (1) **fruit or vegetable, by culinary use** (all of S1, S3, S18, S20, S21, S26, S34); (2) **the crop or crop group** (apples and pears, citrus, berries; roots, leafy greens, alliums), which is also the unit of the standards (S4, S17, S6) and of the trade codes (S8, S22); (3) **the cultivar**, but only where it is sold by name: BigBasket mango and banana facets (S21), Japanese shops by sweet-potato cultivar (S7), Fukushima colour and size tables per apple cultivar (S15), the Orange Pippin directory (S10, S35); (4) **filters that are not types**: origin (S3), organic (S1, S3, S21, S26), quality labels (S3), season (S3, S18, S27 menu), ripeness ("Perfectly Ripe", S26), brand (S21), pack (loose, prepacked, cut: S1, S19, S26). Nobody navigates by botanical family, because tomato, pepper and potato (all Solanaceae) sit in different shop groups (S1, S18), and Woolworths says it files by sweetness, not botany (S18). Nobody navigates by plant part either, except guides (S32) and the Woolworths explainer (S18).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates keep depth at the framework maximum: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels below J4; C5a).

### Candidate A (recommended): class of produce, then crop group, then species, then cultivar; state as an attribute

- **Level 1 sub-family. Axis: class of produce as sold** (fruit; vegetable; herb; fungi and other edible organisms). Sources: S1, S18, S21, S26, S31; fungi alone in S2, S4, S23; herbs alone in S1, S21, S27. Sea vegetables fold into the fourth sub-family, named "Fungi and other edible organisms" (J-10; the separate sea-vegetable sub-family of the earlier draft is gone). There is no sub-family for mixed packs (J4-1, C5c): a mix is a final kind "mixed <class>" at the end of the lowest level that holds all its parts (rule R3 below, K-46).
- **Level 2 leaf. Axis: crop group** (botanical-culinary group as the shops and standards use it). Fruit leaves (8): pome, stone, citrus, berries and small fruit, grapes and vine fruit, melons, tropical and subtropical, other temperate and dryland. Vegetable leaves (7): leaf vegetables, brassicas, roots tubers and rhizomes, alliums, fruiting vegetables, pods beans and peas, stems and shoots. The "Mixed" leaf of the earlier draft is gone: under K-46 a mix is a final kind at the end of the lowest level that holds all its parts, so a mix inside one leaf ends that leaf, a mix across the leaves of one sub-family ends the sub-family with `leaf = null` (J5.4 Mixed nuts is the precedent), and a mix across sub-families ends J4 (R3). Herbs and the fungi and other edible organisms sub-family have about 12 kinds or fewer in the sourced set, so they skip the leaf level (C5b: the data stores `leaf = null`). In a full load the fourth sub-family will pass 12 kinds (about 15 fungus market types, S23, plus the seaweeds, S30); its leaf axis is then the kind of organism (edible fungi; sea vegetables; other edible organisms), stated now as C5(c) asks. Herbs likewise get a leaf only if the list passes 12.
- **Level 3 kind. Axis: species or commodity** (the unit of the grade standards, PLU and GPC; section 4b).
- **Level 4 named form. Axis: cultivar or commercial type.** Almost all are **not maker-bound** (`maker_bound = no`): traditional and open cultivars (Gala, Fuji, Alphonso, Habanero, Naruto Kintoki) **and Cripps Pink**, because only the trade mark Pink Lady® is licensed, not the cultivar (S13). Cripps Pink points to a row of the shared variety table (JC7, CV-44) whose `holder` is not in the fetched pages (†). Commercial types (cherry tomato, Roma and vine tomatoes, new and baking potatoes) are non-maker named forms too (J-9). Every cultivar and commercial type enters on one fetched page showing it is or was sold, with no cap (J-10, CV-7). **Pink Lady® is a C9 alias of Cripps Pink with relation `trade mark`**, and the licensor is a row in the maker ledger. A club cultivar sold under one marketer only stays **maker-bound** (SunGold kiwifruit with Zespri: S20; the exclusivity itself rests on one retailer page: †).
- **State, form and grade are attributes**: storage state (`storage_state`: fresh, chilled-cut or frozen; S25, S29, S12; J-10, JC4); whole, cut, washed, prepacked (S1, S19, S26); grade class as a value of a named `standard` row (JC1; S5, S12, S15, S16; C4b); size class (S15, S22; C6); organic, origin and quality label (S3, S22); ripeness (S26). PLU and GPC codes are an `external_codes` field on the kind or named form, not variants (J4-2).

*Rules inside J4 (all from the review, J4-1 to J4-4, now confirmed or decided; the decision ids are in section 12).*
- **R1 Fruit or vegetable: sweetness as sold (decided J-9).** Tomato, avocado, cucumber, pepper, chilli, aubergine, courgette and squash, sweetcorn: Vegetables > Fruiting vegetables, because the one retailer that states its rule files them by sweetness, not botany (S18). Avocado has one home. Cherry: Stone fruit (Waitrose gives it its own aisle, S26; Woolworths files it with berries, S19). Commercial types (cherry tomato, Roma, cherry vine, new potato, baking potato) are non-maker named forms of their kind. Plantain: Tropical and subtropical fruit, with the green-cooking use as an attribute (plantain kind †: no fetched page lists it as a product). Fresh olive: Fruiting vegetables (K-54 makes fresh olives J4, and an olive is not sweet as sold; kind †, no fetched J4 page lists it).
- **R2 Herbs.** Herbs are "leafy or soft-stemmed plants sold in bunches or packs to flavour food" (coriander, parsley, basil, mint, lemongrass). Rocket goes to leaf vegetables; ginger to roots, tubers and rhizomes; bean sprouts and cut microgreens to stems and shoots; sweetcorn to fruiting vegetables; seaweed sold fresh or frozen to Fungi and other edible organisms (J-10). Kinds for rocket, lemongrass, basil, mint and plantain are not yet attested on a fetched page and are listed but not counted (†).
- **R3 Mixed products (decided K-46, JC5(c)).** A mix of kinds is filed under the kind that makes up half or more of its weight (a berry bag that is over half blueberry is blueberry). Otherwise it is a final kind "mixed <class>" at the end of the lowest level that holds all its parts: a mix inside one leaf ends that leaf (mixed berries for a forest-fruit mix, S28; mixed salad leaves for an undressed salad-leaf mix, S18); a mix across the leaves of one sub-family ends the sub-family with `leaf = null` (mixed fruit for a smoothie mix of several crop groups, S28; mixed vegetables for frozen mixed vegetables, S12, S29); a mix of fruit and vegetables with no half-weight kind (a party tray, S20) is the final kind "mixed fruit and vegetables" at the end of J4, with `sub_family = null` and `leaf = null`. A mix across categories (a tray with dip, a hamper) is filed by its item of greatest value and is OUT if that item is a prepared food; the basket kept afterwards is B1 (step 3 set rule).
- **R4 Nuts, chestnuts, coconut, olives (decided K-54, J4-O1).** Every nut, chestnut and coconut, tender coconut included, is J5; fresh olives are J4 (R1); cured table olives are J5 (preserved). J5.4 carries chestnut and coconut (J5 memo).

## 4. Depth and size

Basis: over 1,500 PLU codes for fresh produce, each a commodity, variety and size (S22); mushrooms alone have 21 PLU codes, of which about 15 are distinct species or market types (S23); UNECE has a separate standard per commodity or group (at least ten titles seen: S4); GPC lists fresh-fruit bricks by species (at least 15 seen in the partial view: S8).
- **Named forms (cultivars and commercial types):** very large if everything is loaded: about 800 apple entries (S35), about 1,000 citrus cultivars and relatives (S9), an estimated 5,000 Capsicum cultivars (S11), plus potatoes, tomatoes, grapes, mangoes and bananas. - **Where depth concentrates:** apples, citrus, chillies, tomatoes, potatoes, grapes, mangoes and bananas; Japanese fruit (cultivar tables per fruit, S15) and sweet potato (S7); mushrooms by market type (S23).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers (C2a). For produce that level is **the species or commodity**, which is where grade standards (S4, S6, S17, S5), trade codes (S22, S8), shop sub-aisles (S1, S19, S26, S34) and brand/maker comparison (S20, S25, S31) sit. Cultivars are the named-form level, where a licence or breeder can be attached.

Evidence for species as the kind:
- Grade standards are written per commodity: UNECE (S4), USDA fruit list of apples, apricots, bananas, berries, cherries, citrus, grapes, kiwifruit, mangos, melons, peaches, pears, pineapple, plums (S17), USDA vegetable list (S6), EU specific standards for ten commodities (S33), Fukushima per fruit (S15), China's sweet-cherry standard (S16).
- GPC bricks are species; cultivar is an attribute ("Apple Variety", S8). PLU codes the variety and size under the commodity (S22).
- Shops name the species in sub-aisles (S1, S19, S26, S34, S7) and use cultivar as a filter or a product name (S21 mango and banana facets; S20 "Gala Apple", "Roma Tomato").
- Culinary's rule agrees: "Fruit species are kinds, cultivars variants" (FRAMEWORK_v1.md, s13; checked by the domain review, section 6). In J a cultivar is a non-maker named form, so the two frameworks use compatible kind rules.

Why cultivar is not a kind: BRIEF and S2 (kinds are generic types; models and designs are named forms). Buyers do compare apple cultivars with each other (the Orange Pippin directory and a "Top 10 varieties" list, S10), but the cultivar set is open, partly licensed (S13) and mostly seasonal; this is the "model" level of produce.

**Where the choice level differs inside J4:**
- *Citrus* goes one step finer than "citrus": UNECE and shops list orange, blood orange, clementine, satsuma, other mandarins, lemon, Persian lime, West Indian lime, grapefruit and pummelo as separate headings (S36, S7, S3). Navel, Valencia and Red Navel are groups of orange cultivars (named forms, not maker-bound; S36).
- *Chillies and sweet peppers* are two kinds: the EU applies a standard to sweet peppers and excludes chillies (S33); Woolworths files them in different groups (S18); heat is measured, not a type (S24).
- *Mushrooms* go by market type, not species only: the PLU list separates regular button small and large, cremini, portabella, shiitake, oyster, enoki and others (S23); REWE separates Champignons from other fresh fungi (S2).
- *Potato and tomato "commercial types"* (new potato, baking potato; cherry vine, Roma, cherry tomato) are non-maker named forms of their kind (J-9, C2(a)): the fetched pages name them only as product names (S20, S34), and one such page admits each (C2(f)).
- *Frozen:* state is not a level (section 5 explains the grade scheme). The template's own example makes frozen vegetables one kind; here the species stays the kind because Waitrose, Kroger and FairPrice organise frozen by species (S29, S25, S31). Decided J-10; JC4(a) agrees: storage state is always an attribute.
- *Mixed products* stop at a final kind "mixed <class>" at the end of the lowest level that holds all the parts, when no kind makes up half or more of the weight, since no single species names them (S12, S28, S20; K-46, JC5(c)); where that level is the leaf, the sub-family or J4 itself, section 3 (R3) gives the home.
- *Regional-word kinds* (nashi under Asian pear, satsuma with its alias mikan, edamame): they keep their level as provisional until attested (R-19, CV-27); their generic label with the regional name as kind name (C11) is set at stage-3 harvest.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: spec-ranked by published standard; no consensus tier on kinds.** Grade ladders exist and are measured on the object, so under C4(b) they are attributes with the standard named, stored as values of `standard` rows (JC1, CV-40), not tier entries. A tier never sits on a kind (C4a); none is entered for J4.
- EU/UK: Extra Class, Class I, Class II (S5, S33); the UNECE standards are the model for these classes (S4).
- USA: voluntary U.S. grade standards per commodity (S6, S17); frozen vegetables U.S. Grade A (90 points), B (80), C (70), Substandard (S12).
- Japan: 秀・優・良 for fruit (with 特秀 above) and A/B for vegetables and mushrooms, set by prefecture since the national standard ended in 2002 (S14, S15); size classes 2L/L/M and count per box (S15).
- China: 特级 / 一级 / 二级 for sweet cherry, size by diameter (S16).
- Trade attributes: IFPS size (S22); GPC "Quality (UNECE Standard)" attribute (S8). Chilli heat is stored as the measured Scoville value; the five band names Mild to Super-Hot come from one enthusiast site (S24) and are a generated view of that value, not a tier scheme (a measured spec, C4(c) class (i)). One fetched source is enough for such a view or a scheme to enter (C4(e), CV-10), so no second region is needed.
- *Brand programmes and quality marks* are not tiers. Pink Lady® is a trade-mark alias of the cultivar Cripps Pink, of which about 10 percent of the crop is sold under the name (S13); Label Rouge, IGP, AOP and Bio on Carrefour (S3) are quality marks and designations (below); organic is PLU prefix 9 (S22). A retailer range label such as "The Odd Bunch" or "Perfectly Ripe" (S18, S26) is a dated `platform_tag` (C4c), not a grade.
- Disputed: none found in fetched pages. The Specialty Produce site carries a second page (Pink Lady apples) whose search excerpt gives a different pass share; I did not fetch it, so the figure is not used (†).

**Data form (decided CV-40, JC1, CV-42).** Grading schemes are rows of the `standard` table (`standard_id`, `name`, `authority`, `steps`, `sources`): the EU and UNECE produce classes (Extra Class, Class I, Class II, and the General Marketing Standard without classes), the USDA fresh grades per commodity, the USDA frozen-vegetable grades A, B, C and Substandard, the Japanese prefecture grades (Fukushima, S15) and GB/T 26906-2024. A value attaches as (`subject_id`, `standard_id`, `value`) to a kind or named form only where the standard defines classes for it; the class of one lot or consignment is never stored (C16, CV-33). Size class is an attribute with its standard (C6; IFPS, Fukushima). Protected names are rows of the `designation` table (`designation_id`, `name`, `scheme`, `register_id`, `authority`, `sources`; the authority is a consortium or body, never a maker): the IGP and AOP names seen in S3. A designation that binds one kind (Clémentine de Corse) is a non-maker named form of that kind; one that spans several kinds (Label Rouge) is a tag (CV-40).

**Price band (decided CV-15, JC2).** J4 carries the grocery `unit_price_band`: USD per kilogram, from a dated, sourced list price, on the kind or named form; a seasonal price is still a dated list price. Retail pages show per-unit prices (S26, S29), which are converted to a per-kilogram price where the pack weight is stated. The bands are set at the first data load. The C4(d) item band is not used for J4, and the earlier recommendation of no band is dropped.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: incidental, with identity at the named-form level for club cultivars and trade marks.** Shop trees put the species first and the brand last (origin and quality labels appear as filters in S3, brand facets in S21). Brand matters in three places only: trade marks and club cultivars (Pink Lady® and Cripps Pink: S13; Zespri SunGold: S20), growers' and retailers' own labels (Waitrose Essential, Duchy Organic and Cooks' Ingredients: S29; BigBasket fresho!: S21; Kroger own brand: S25), and frozen-food processors seen in the shop facets (Bonduelle on Carrefour Belgium is in a search excerpt only, so it is not cited).

**Maker ledger (CV-2, CV-5, CV-41, JC3).** The one domain-wide C1 ledger holds J4 makers: growers, growers' marketing bodies, nurseries, breeders, packers and processors, importers and retail own-labels. It is uncapped, and one fetched page admits a maker. `maker_class` takes C1 values only: retail own-label for the own labels above, independent or supplier for processors, and breeder or registrant for the holder of a variety. The supply roles sit in the multi-valued J column `supply_role` beside it, from the JC3 list: for J4 "grower or marketing body" (Zespri; Apple & Pear Australia for Pink Lady®: S13), "licensor" (varieties and trade marks), "processor or packer" and "retail own-label"; the rest of the JC3 list (farm or ranch, importer or trader, cooperative) applies as makers are harvested. `licensor_id` links a maker or named form to its licensor (CV-3). Consortia and bodies that guard a protected name are the `authority` of a `designation` row, not maker rows (CV-40). Scene tags are empty (section 7). `known_for_kinds` derives from the named-form ledger.

**Lines (C1, CV-46, R-18, R-35).** A range that spans several kinds or products (an own-label range of frozen vegetables such as Duchy Organic, S29) is a lines row of its maker; a single product inside one kind (SunGold kiwifruit) is a maker-bound named form (C2(d)).

**Named-form fields.** `maker_bound` (yes or no); `variety_id`, the link to the shared variety table where the `holder` (breeder or registrant) sits, empty for Cripps Pink until a source is fetched (†); `external_codes` (IFPS PLU and GS1 GPC codes, S22, S8, held on the kind or named form); `designation_id` where a designation binds the form; and `trade_mark_alias` through the C9 table (Pink Lady®). **The variant ledger (C3) stays empty for J4 for now:** no fetched source compares variants of a produce named form (CV-8). The PLU code is not a `variant_code` (it is a trade code for commodity, variety and size, and size is an attribute under C6), and a brand name is not a C3 `variant_type`; the ledger is filled if a harvested source compares variants.

**Variety table (decided JC7, CV-44, J-10).** Each cultivar row (Gala, Alphonso, Habanero) points to one row of the shared `variety` table (`variety_id`, `species`, `name`, `native_name`, `holder`, `sources`), so a chilli sold fresh (J4) and dried (J5), or a grape sold to eat (J4) and as a wine varietal (J7), is one variety row. The full cultivar registers live there, uncapped; the named form here remains the product-facing row.

No **compatibility ledger** (C13) is needed: no platform or standard sets compatibility.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Kinds (C7, CV-20): the **commodity that names the leaf comes first** (apple in pome, citrus group head orange), then **alphabetical by English name**, because the sources give no spec order for kinds (GPC and UNECE list alphabetically: S8, S4). A final kind "mixed <class>" sits last in the level that holds it: last in its leaf, last in its sub-family after the leaves, and "mixed fruit and vegetables" last in J4 (R3). Named forms (CV-21): non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetical; then maker-bound ones, by maker (ledger name order), then first-introduction year, then alphabetical. Ladder or benchmark orders are generated views only. The variant ledger is empty in J4 (section 6).

## 9. Boundaries

Each sentence below is a precedence sentence for the key; all are decided (the owner marks of the earlier draft are closed, section 12).

1. **J5 Pantry Staples.** *Closest objects:* dried shiitake, dried chilli, raisins, olives, tinned tomatoes, sauerkraut and kimchi, dried wakame or nori, sprouting seed, chestnuts, fresh coconut. *Precedence:* **A fruit, vegetable, mushroom or seaweed sold fresh, chilled, cut, washed or frozen without sauce or coating is J4; the same plant sold dried, tinned, jarred, pickled or fermented, and every nut, seed, pulse and spice, is J5.** Every nut, chestnut and coconut, tender coconut included, is J5; fresh olives are J4 and cured table olives are J5 (K-54, J4-O1). Fresh seaweed is J4 and dried seaweed J5, with storage state an attribute (J-5). Sprouting seed sold to eat is J5 and sold for growing is C3 (K-75, J4-O1); sprouts ready to eat are J4. *Note:* shops put dried fruit and nuts inside Fresh Fruit (S26) and dried mushrooms beside fresh (S2); the framework key wins. The trade guide describes the named seaweeds mostly in dried or processed forms (S30), so the seaweed part of the fungi and other edible organisms sub-family is small.
2. **C3 Plants & Living Cultures.** *Closest:* potted basil, living microgreens, mushroom growing kit, fruit trees and bushes. *Precedence:* **A plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is C3 even when sold to eat; the same plant cut and sold to eat is J4.** Fruit trees and bushes sold for planting are C3, and a living plant in any container is C3 whatever the container's value (K-73).
3. **Step 1 prepared foods (left to Culinary).** *Closest:* coleslaw and dressed salads, stir-fry kits with sauce sachets, fruit salad in syrup, par-fried frozen chips, battered vegetables, vegetable soup. *Precedence:* **Produce that is washed, trimmed, cut, peeled or frozen plain stays J4; once it is dressed, sauced, fried, coated or cooked into a dish it is out. Par-fried or coated frozen vegetables (chips, hash browns, battered vegetables) are prepared (OUT) (K-23); "coated" means breaded or battered (K-45). Vegetables frozen in a sauce are out as sauced (J4-O1).** Fruit in syrup is J5 if tinned or jarred, out as a prepared dessert if a salad.
4. **J6 Coffee, Tea & Cocoa.** *Closest:* fresh mint or ginger for infusions, fresh coffee cherries. *Precedence:* **Fresh herbs and roots sold to cook with are J4; leaves, flowers, fruit and roots dried and sold to brew as infusions are J6 (K-51).** (J4, J5 and J6 agree on herbs: fresh J4, dried for cooking J5, dried to brew J6.)
5. **H2 Health.** *Closest:* spirulina or chlorella tablets, greens powders, essence-of-vegetable tonics. *Precedence:* **Plain seaweed and fungi as food are J4; tablets, capsules and powders sold as supplements are H2.**
6. **B4 Decoration (cut flowers) and F6.** *Closest:* edible flowers, ornamental fruits and gourds, gourd and fruit specimens. *Precedence:* **Flowers and gourds sold to eat are J4; cut flowers and dried or decorative gourds are B4; fruit or seed kept as a natural specimen is F6.**
7. **Mixed sets.** *Closest:* a fruit-and-vegetable tray with dip, a gift hamper with fruit, a produce box, a frozen berry mix. *Precedence:* **A mix of kinds is filed under the kind that makes up half or more of its weight; otherwise it is the final kind "mixed <class>" at the end of the lowest level that holds all its parts (R3); a set that crosses categories is filed by its item of greatest value (step 3 set rule), and is OUT if that item is a prepared food; the basket kept afterwards is B1 (K-46).**
8. **Fruit against vegetable.** *Closest:* avocado, tomato, cucumber, pepper, sweetcorn, plantain, rhubarb, olive. *Precedence:* **Inside J4 the shop rule applies: sweet fruit as sold is Fruit, savoury botanical fruit as sold is Vegetables > Fruiting vegetables; cherry is Stone fruit and plantain is tropical fruit (J-9).**
9. **B9 Tobacco and psychoactive products.** *Closest:* fresh khat or kratom leaves, fresh psychoactive mushrooms, areca nut. *Precedence:* **A plant or fungus product taken for a psychoactive effect is B9 whatever its legal status, and the products step 27 names (areca nut, khat, kratom) are B9; kava is J6; a food mushroom or herb is J4 (K-168).**

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure (Candidate A; J-9, J-10).** Category J4 > sub-family (class of produce as sold: fruit; vegetables; herbs; fungi and other edible organisms, which holds edible fungi and sea vegetables) > leaf (crop group, only where a sub-family has more than 12 kinds: 8 fruit leaves and 7 vegetable leaves; herbs and the fourth sub-family carry `leaf = null` until they pass 12) > kind (species or commodity, with a few finer kinds in citrus, peppers and mushrooms; the final kinds "mixed <class>" sit at the end of the lowest level that holds all their parts, K-46) > named form (cultivar, commercial type, or a designation that binds one kind; `maker_bound` yes or no; Cripps Pink is no). Maximum depth four levels below J4. Fruit against vegetable is decided by sweetness as sold (avocado, tomato, cucumber in Fruiting vegetables; cherry in Stone fruit). Storage state (fresh, chilled-cut, frozen), form, size, organic, origin, ripeness and season are attributes or generated views. Grades are values of JC1 `standard` rows, protected names are `designation` rows (a named form when they bind one kind, a tag when they span several), and the grocery `unit_price_band` (JC2) is in USD per kilogram. Cultivars and commercial types enter on one fetched page each, uncapped, with the registers in the shared variety table (CV-44). Data: the one domain-wide maker ledger with C1 classes and `supply_role` (CV-41), the lines table for own-label ranges (CV-46), `external_codes` for PLU and GPC codes, the C9 alias table (trade mark included), `culinary_link` rows (CV-43) and the variety table. The variant ledger stays empty, there is no parts sub-family (CV-17) and no C13 row.

**Fruit leaves recommended** (align with Culinary: pome SW-A1-2, stone SW-A1-3, citrus SW-A1-1, berries SW-A1-5, grapes SW-A1-6, melons SW-A1-7, tropical SW-A2-x, other dryland SW-A1-4 and SW-A1-8). Leaves differ in size: the tropical leaf is the one most likely to need a later split, which Culinary does by botanical family (SW-A2-1 to SW-A2-17).

The fold of sea vegetables is provisional until attested: the only sea-vegetable page is a trade guide that describes mostly dried products (S30), and no fetched page shows fresh or frozen sea vegetables sold.
- Kinds with a rule-placed home but no fetched J4 product page (†): rocket, lemongrass, basil, mint, plantain, rhubarb, okra, pumpkin, squash, fresh olive; the bean sprout and microgreen split.
- Regional-word kinds (Asian pear or nashi, satsuma or mikan, edamame) keep their level provisionally (R-19, CV-27).
- The kind "mixed fruit and vegetables" sits directly under J4 (`sub_family = null`, `leaf = null`); C5 has no wording for a kind above the sub-family level, so the home follows the J5.4 Mixed nuts precedent (section 12).
- The cultivar load is very large (apples about 800, citrus about 1,000, chillies thousands: S35, S9, S11); harvest depends on existing catalogues, and directory-only cultivars with no page showing them sold are provisional until attested.
- Definition sources for J `tradition` values and for any scene tag are not yet fetched (†); the holder of Cripps Pink and the licensor of Pink Lady® are not on the fetched pages (†).
