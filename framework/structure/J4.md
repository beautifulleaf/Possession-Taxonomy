## 0. Category

**Membership test (placement key step 3, fourth branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** "**fruit, vegetables, herbs, edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen**, whole, cut or washed, but not dressed, sauced, fried or coated". Category line: "fruit, vegetables, herbs, edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen, by species and cultivar."

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch J4:**
- Step 3 head: groceries are filed once, as the product as bought, "divided by product and by the quality of the ingredient"; Culinary describes the same foods as eaten and links to it. Nuts, seeds, dried fruit, dried vegetables, dried mushrooms and dried seaweed are J5; pickles, preserves, tinned and jarred fruit, vegetables and tomatoes are J5; sauces and condiments are J5 "whatever they are made from"; pulses are J5.
- Step 6 note: "cut vegetables, herbs, sprouts and microgreens sold to eat are step 3; a plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is [C3] even when sold to eat, such as potted basil or living microgreens."

- Brief (owner, 2026-10-17): grocery products that Culinary also holds are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind; groceries are judged by the quality of the ingredient.

**Enthusiast communities and wikis.** Orange Pippin (S10, S35) lists tree-fruit species (apples, crab-apples, apricots, cherries, peaches, plums, pears) with named varieties inside each; its apple directory holds about 800 entries (sum of the A to Z counts), each described as dessert, culinary or cider, with synonyms (Albermarle Pippin is a synonym for Newtown Pippin). Know the Pepper (S24) sorts 215+ chillies into five Scoville tiers. Wikipedia (S11) groups Capsicum cultivars under five species and notes that British English calls sweet ones "peppers" and hot ones "chillies" while Australian and Indian English use "capsicum" for bell peppers.

**Collectors, resale and trade encyclopedias.** There is no collector market. The Specialty Produce encyclopedia of a wholesaler (S13) catalogues thousands of articles by produce family and shows the licensing pattern: Cripps Pink is the varietal name and "only around 10 percent" of the crop is sold under the trademark Pink Lady, whose name is owned and managed by a state department and an Australian industry body.

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- UNECE (S4): one standard per commodity or commodity group (Aubergines, Berry fruits, Certain small fruits, Ceps, Truffles, Leafy vegetables, Root and tubercle vegetables, Shallots, Strawberries...), plus an informal citrus variety list (S36).
- EU/UK marketing standards (S5, S33): Extra Class, Class I, Class II for ten specifically listed products (apples, citrus, kiwi, lettuces, peaches/nectarines, pears, strawberries, sweet peppers, table grapes, tomatoes); a General Marketing Standard (no classes) for most other fresh fruit, vegetables, nuts, herbs and cultivated mushrooms; excluded: potatoes, chilli peppers, sweetcorn, olives, wild mushrooms, sweet potatoes, ripened bananas, ginger.
- USDA AMS (S6, S17, S12): voluntary U.S. grade standards for each vegetable and fruit, fresh and for processing; frozen vegetables have their own grades A, B, C and Substandard, scored 90, 80 and 70 points.
- Japan (S14, S15): the national standard shipping specifications were abolished in 2002; each prefecture now sets its own. Fukushima grades fruit and fruit-like vegetables Tokushu/Shu/Yu/Ryo (特秀・秀・優・良), vegetables and mushrooms A/B, with size classes by count per box or weight and colour tables per cultivar (Fuji, Tsugaru, Jonagold...).
- China (S16): GB/T 26906-2024 grades sweet cherry special, first and second class, with size by diameter and soluble solids of at least 14 percent.
- IFPS PLU (S22, S23): over 1,500 codes identifying commodity, variety, growing method (prefix 9 for organic) and size; mushrooms alone have 21 codes (button small/large, cremini, portabella, shiitake, oyster, enoki, chanterelle, morel, cep, wood ear...).
- GS1 GPC (S8): Family Fruits Unprepared/Unprocessed (Fresh) with classes such as Pome Fruits, Kiwifruits and Miscellaneous Fruit, and bricks by species (Apples with an Apple Variety attribute, Strawberries, Dates, Durian, Mangos, Lychees); separate families for Vegetables (Non Leaf), Leaf Vegetables and Fresh Cut; an attribute "Quality (UNECE Standard)".

**Museums and reference works.** The UC Riverside Citrus Variety Collection (S9) holds about 1,000 citrus cultivars and relatives, browsable alphabetically and "by category or type". The CIAT guide (S32) divides vegetables into ten classes by plant part (leafy, root, bulb, stem, flower, fruit, pod, tuber, seed, fungi) and admits overlaps (potato root or tuber; pea pod or legume). A trade guide on seaweed (S30) groups it by colour (green, brown, red) with named types (nori, wakame, kombu, dulse, arame, hijiki, sea grapes).

**Where sources agree.** Every retailer with a full tree opens with fruit against vegetable (S1, S3, S20, S21, S26, S34); herbs are split from vegetables in three of them (S1, S21, S27); the commodity (apple, pear, tomato, mushroom) is the unit of standards, trade codes and shop sub-aisles (S4, S6, S8, S17, S22). **Where they split the same objects differently:** avocado (fruit aisle and vegetable aisle at Woolworths, S18/S19); tomato and cucumber (fruit botanically, vegetables on every shelf); mushrooms (inside vegetables at Woolworths and FairPrice, S1, S31; their own group at REWE, S2; UNECE has separate Ceps and Truffles standards, S4); frozen (a department apart at Waitrose, Kroger, Instacart: S25, S28, S29, S20; inside vegetables at FairPrice, S31); cherries (with berries at Woolworths, S19; stone fruit botanically, and Waitrose gives them an aisle of their own, S26); chillies (with herbs and garlic at Woolworths, S18; excluded from the EU standards, S33); nuts and dried fruit (inside Fresh Fruit at Waitrose, S26; the framework puts them in J5).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose in this order, from the shop trees above: (1) **fruit or vegetable, by culinary use** (all of S1, S3, S18, S20, S21, S26, S34); (2) **the crop or crop group** (apples and pears, citrus, berries; roots, leafy greens, alliums), which is also the unit of the standards (S4, S17, S6) and of the trade codes (S8, S22); (3) **the cultivar**, but only where it is sold by name: BigBasket mango and banana facets (S21), Japanese shops by sweet-potato cultivar (S7), Fukushima colour and size tables per apple cultivar (S15), the Orange Pippin directory (S10, S35); (4) **filters that are not types**: origin (S3), organic (S1, S3, S21, S26), quality labels (S3), season (S3, S18, S27 menu), ripeness ("Perfectly Ripe", S26), brand (S21), pack (loose, prepacked, cut: S1, S19, S26). Nobody navigates by botanical family, because tomato, pepper and potato (all Solanaceae) sit in different shop groups (S1, S18), and Woolworths says it files by sweetness, not botany (S18). Nobody navigates by plant part either, except guides (S32) and the Woolworths explainer (S18).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates keep depth at the framework maximum: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels below J4).

### Candidate A (recommended): class of produce, then crop group, then species, then cultivar; state as an attribute

- **Level 1 sub-family. Axis: class of produce as sold** (fruit; vegetable; herb; edible fungus; sea vegetable; mixed pack). Sources: S1, S18, S21, S26, S31; fungi alone in S2, S4, S23; herbs alone in S1, S21, S27.
- **Level 2 leaf. Axis: crop group** (botanical-culinary group as the shops and standards use it). Fruit leaves: pome, stone, citrus, berries and small fruit, grapes and vine fruit, melons, tropical and subtropical, other temperate and dryland. Vegetable leaves: leaf vegetables, brassicas, roots tubers and rhizomes, alliums, fruiting vegetables, pods beans and peas, stems and shoots. Herbs, fungi, sea vegetables and mixed packs have about 12 kinds or fewer in the sourced set, so they skip the leaf level (C5b), except fungi, which may need a leaf for cultivated against wild if the list grows (S2, S33).
- **Level 3 kind. Axis: species or commodity** (the unit of the grade standards, PLU and GPC; section 4b).
- **Level 4 named form. Axis: cultivar or commercial type**, with `maker_bound` yes for licensed or club cultivars (Pink Lady® / Cripps Pink: licensor on file, S13; SunGold kiwifruit with its marketer, S20) and no for traditional cultivars (Gala, Fuji, Alphonso, Habanero, Naruto Kintoki).
- **State, form and grade are attributes**: fresh, chilled-cut or frozen (S25, S29, S12); whole, cut, washed, prepacked (S1, S19, S26); grade class under the named standard (S5, S12, S15, S16); size class (S15, S22); organic, origin and quality label (S3, S22); ripeness (S26).

### Candidate B: state first (fresh, frozen, cut), then crop

- Levels: sub-family = state (fresh; frozen; fresh-cut), leaf = crop group, kind = species, named form = cultivar.
- Basis: Waitrose, Kroger, Instacart and Carrefour (S25, S28, S29, S20, S3) put frozen in another department, and GPC has a separate Fresh Cut family (S8).
- Breaks: the same kinds (pea, spinach, blueberry) appear in two or three sub-families, which only works if the owner treats "frozen peas" as a different kind from "peas"; Appendix A names condition as a modifier, and FairPrice files frozen inside vegetables (S31). The USDA frozen grades A/B/C (S12) differ from the fresh grades, which is a reason to record the grade scheme with the state, not a reason to split the tree. Edge objects: a frozen mix of fruit (smoothie mix, S28), a chilled washed salad (S18), thawed-and-sold-fresh berries.

### Candidate C: botanical family or plant part first

- Levels: sub-family = plant part eaten (S32 ten classes) or botanical group (Codex-style groups, shown in search results only, so not relied on here).
- Breaks: potato is root or tuber, pea is pod or legume (S32); tomato, pepper, aubergine and potato (one botanical family) would sit together while shops sell them apart (S1, S18); apple, pear and strawberry would split from the "fruit" aisle every shop shows. Tomato and cucumber count as fruit under this view, against all of S1, S3, S18, S20, S26. Edge objects: rhubarb, avocado, sweetcorn, mushrooms (not plants).

## 4. Depth and size

These counts are my estimates from the sourced lists, not counts of a complete database.

- **Generic kinds (species or commodity):** about 250 to 350. Basis: over 1,500 PLU codes for fresh produce, each a commodity, variety and size (S22); mushrooms alone have 21 PLU codes, of which about 15 are distinct species or market types (S23); UNECE has a separate standard per commodity or group (at least ten titles seen: S4); GPC lists fresh-fruit bricks by species (at least 15 seen in the partial view: S8).
- **Named forms (cultivars and commercial types):** very large if everything is loaded: about 800 apple entries (S35), about 1,000 citrus cultivars and relatives (S9), an estimated 5,000 Capsicum cultivars (S11), plus potatoes, tomatoes, grapes, mangoes and bananas. Recommended starting load: only cultivars attested as sold (retailer listing, PLU, grade standard or national tables), about 400 to 800.
- **Where depth concentrates:** apples, citrus, chillies, tomatoes, potatoes, grapes, mangoes and bananas; Japanese fruit (cultivar tables per fruit, S15) and sweet potato (S7); mushrooms by market type (S23).
- **Broad leaves (more than 12 kinds):** tropical and subtropical fruit, fruiting vegetables, roots and tubers. **Medium (5 to 12):** pome, stone, citrus, berries, leaf vegetables, brassicas, alliums, pods, stems; herbs; edible fungi. **Narrow (fewer than 5):** melons, grapes and vine fruit, other temperate fruit, sea vegetables, mixed packs.
- **Much of the tree is shallow.** In the fetched shop trees, leaf vegetables, brassicas, stems and herbs show no cultivar names; the named-form level is mostly empty there.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers. For produce that level is **the species or commodity**, which is where grade standards (S4, S6, S17, S5), trade codes (S22, S8), shop sub-aisles (S1, S19, S26, S34) and brand/maker comparison (S20, S25, S31) sit. Cultivars are the named-form level, where a licence or breeder can be attached.

Evidence for species as the kind:
- Grade standards are written per commodity: UNECE (S4), USDA fruit list of apples, apricots, bananas, berries, cherries, citrus, grapes, kiwifruit, mangos, melons, peaches, pears, pineapple, plums (S17), USDA vegetable list (S6), EU specific standards for ten commodities (S33), Fukushima per fruit (S15), China's sweet-cherry standard (S16).
- GPC bricks are species; cultivar is an attribute ("Apple Variety", S8). PLU codes the variety and size under the commodity (S22).
- Shops name the species in sub-aisles (S1, S19, S26, S34, S7) and use cultivar as a filter or a product name (S21 mango and banana facets; S20 "Gala Apple", "Roma Tomato").
- Culinary's rule agrees: "Fruit species are kinds, cultivars variants" (FRAMEWORK_v1.md, s13).

Why cultivar is not a kind: owner rule S2 (kinds are generic types; models and designs are named forms). Buyers do compare apple cultivars with each other (the Orange Pippin directory and a "Top 10 varieties" list, S10), but the cultivar set is open, partly licensed (S13) and mostly seasonal; this is the "model" level of produce.

**Where the choice level differs inside J4:**
- *Citrus* goes one step finer than "citrus": UNECE and shops list orange, blood orange, clementine, satsuma, other mandarins, lemon, Persian lime, West Indian lime, grapefruit and pummelo as separate headings (S36, S7, S3). Navel, Valencia and Red Navel are groups of orange cultivars (named forms, not maker-bound; S36).
- *Chillies and sweet peppers* are two kinds: the EU applies a standard to sweet peppers and excludes chillies (S33); Woolworths files them in different groups (S18); heat is measured, not a type (S24).
- *Mushrooms* go by market type, not species only: the PLU list separates regular button small and large, cremini, portabella, shiitake, oyster, enoki and others (S23); REWE separates Champignons from other fresh fungi (S2).
- *Potato and tomato "commercial types"* (new potato, baking potato; cherry vine, Roma) are named forms for now: the fetched pages name them only as product names (S20, S34), so there is no source showing them compared as separate categories. Owner question 8.
- *Frozen:* state is not a level (section 5 explains the grade scheme). The template's own example makes frozen vegetables one kind; here the species stays the kind because Waitrose, Kroger and FairPrice organise frozen by species (S29, S25, S31).
- *Mixed packs* stop at the pack type (frozen mixed vegetables, forest fruit mix, party tray), since no single species names them (S12, S28, S20).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: spec-ranked by published standard; no consensus tier on kinds.** Grade ladders exist and are measured on the object, so under convention C4(b) they are attributes with the standard named, not tier entries.
- EU/UK: Extra Class, Class I, Class II (S5, S33); the UNECE standards are the model for these classes (S4).
- USA: voluntary U.S. grade standards per commodity (S6, S17); frozen vegetables U.S. Grade A (90 points), B (80), C (70), Substandard (S12).
- Japan: 秀・優・良 for fruit (with 特秀 above) and A/B for vegetables and mushrooms, set by prefecture since the national standard ended in 2002 (S14, S15); size classes 2L/L/M and count per box (S15).
- China: 特级 / 一级 / 二级 for sweet cherry, size by diameter (S16).
- Trade attributes: IFPS size (S22); GPC "Quality (UNECE Standard)" attribute (S8); chilli heat in Scoville units with five named bands from Mild to Super-Hot (S24, spec ladder).
- *Brand programmes and quality marks* are attributes or ledger lines, not tiers: Pink Lady® is a licensed brand within the Cripps Pink crop of which about 10 percent of the crop is sold under the name (S13); Label Rouge, IGP, AOP, Bio on Carrefour (S3); organic via PLU prefix 9 (S22).
- Disputed: none found in fetched pages. The Specialty Produce site carries a second page (Pink Lady apples) whose search excerpt gives a different pass share; I did not fetch it, so the figure is not used.

**As a modifier:** `grade_scheme` (EU, USDA fresh, USDA frozen, JP prefecture, CN GB/T) and `grade_value` on the product; `size_class`; `quality_mark`. Price bands (C4d) are not proposed for loose produce: prices are per kilo, seasonal, and the retailer pages show per-unit prices only (S26, S29); owner question 7.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: incidental, with identity at the named-form level for licensed cultivars.** Shop trees put the species first and the brand last (origin and quality labels appear as filters in S3, brand facets in S21). Brand matters in three places only: club or licensed cultivars (Pink Lady® and Cripps Pink: S13; Zespri SunGold: S20), growers' and retailers' own labels (Waitrose Essential, Duchy Organic and Cooks' Ingredients: S29; BigBasket fresho!: S21; Kroger own brand: S25), and frozen-food processors seen in the shop facets (Bonduelle on Carrefour Belgium is in a search excerpt only, so it is not cited).

**Recommend a small maker ledger** using the core C1 schema, with `maker_class` values: licensor or growers' marketing body (Apple & Pear Australia for Pink Lady®, S13), marketer (Zespri), retail own-label, processor. Scene tags empty. `known_for_kinds` derives from the named-form ledger. Nothing here needs the tier table of A5.

A **variant ledger** (C3) fits produce naturally: the IFPS PLU code is a `variant_code` for commodity plus variety plus size (S22), and a licensed brand name (Pink Lady®) is a `variant_type` "brand name" of the cultivar Cripps Pink (S13). Fill it only where an owner-emphasis scene needs depth.

No **compatibility ledger** (C13) is needed: no platform or standard sets compatibility.

## 7. Scenes, cultures and views

There are no subcultures, so the C8 scene vocabulary stays empty for J4. Cross-cutting views, with how each should be built:
- *Seasonal produce* (S3 "produits de saison", S18 seasonal guides, S27 In Season menu): a **generated view** needing a `season` attribute by region; not a tree level.
- *Organic* (aisle or filter in S1, S3, S21, S26; PLU prefix in S22): an **attribute**.
- *Origin and protected names* (country filters S3, S31; Pruneaux d'Agen IGP and Corsican clementines seen in S3): **attributes**; not a region level (C11).
- *Heirloom, open-pollinated* (defined in S11 for peppers): a **tag on named forms**.
- *Asian, Indian and exotic produce* (Asian & Exotic Fruit in S19; Exotic Fruits & Veggies in S21; leafy greens such as kang kong in S31 (the Chinese greens I saw named only in an unfetched search excerpt); Indian mango and banana cultivars in S21; Japanese fruit and sweet potato cultivars in S7, S15): these are **kinds in their crop-group leaf** with a `region` or `tradition` tag, and a generated "by tradition" view (C2c, C11). The exotic aisle is a retail view, never a leaf.
- *Odd-shaped produce below grade* (MAFF 規格外 programmes, S14; Woolworths "The Odd Bunch", S18): a **grade attribute** ("below class").

## 8. Order inside a leaf

Kinds: the **commodity that names the leaf comes first** (apple in pome, citrus group head orange), then **alphabetical by English name**, because the sources give no spec order for kinds (GPC and UNECE list alphabetically: S8, S4). Named forms: not maker-bound cultivars by **first release year where attested**, otherwise alphabetical; maker-bound ones by licensor, then year (C7). Variants by PLU or variant code (S22).

## 9. Boundaries

1. **J5 Pantry Staples.** *Closest objects:* dried shiitake, dried chilli, raisins, olives, tinned tomatoes, sauerkraut and kimchi, dried wakame or nori, sprouting seed, chestnuts, fresh coconut. *Precedence:* **A fruit, vegetable, mushroom or seaweed sold fresh, chilled, cut, washed or frozen without sauce or coating is J4; the same plant sold dried, tinned, jarred, pickled or fermented, and every nut, seed, pulse and spice, is J5.** *Note:* shops put dried fruit and nuts inside Fresh Fruit (S26) and dried mushrooms beside fresh (S2); the framework key wins. The trade guide describes the named seaweeds mostly in dried or processed forms (S30), so the sea-vegetable sub-family is small.
2. **C3 Plants & Living Cultures.** *Closest:* potted basil, living microgreens, mushroom growing kit, fruit trees and bushes. *Precedence:* **A plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is C3 even when sold to eat; the same plant cut and sold to eat is J4.** Fruit trees and bushes sold for planting are C3.
3. **Step 1 prepared foods (left to Culinary).** *Closest:* coleslaw and dressed salads, stir-fry kits with sauce sachets, fruit salad in syrup, par-fried frozen chips, battered vegetables, vegetable soup. *Precedence:* **Produce that is washed, trimmed, cut, peeled or frozen plain stays J4; once it is dressed, sauced, fried, coated or cooked into a dish it is out.** Par-fried frozen chips are out under "fried" (owner question 5). Culinary links per section 10.
4. **J6 Coffee, Tea & Cocoa.** *Closest:* fresh mint or ginger for infusions, fresh coffee cherries. *Precedence:* **Fresh herbs and roots sold to cook with are J4; leaves, flowers and roots dried and sold to brew as infusions are J6.**
5. **H2 Health.** *Closest:* spirulina or chlorella tablets, greens powders, essence-of-vegetable tonics. *Precedence:* **Plain seaweed and fungi as food are J4; tablets, capsules and powders sold as supplements are H2.**
6. **B4 Decoration (cut flowers) and F6.** *Closest:* edible flowers, ornamental fruits and gourds, gourd and fruit specimens. *Precedence:* **Flowers and gourds sold to eat are J4; cut flowers and dried or decorative gourds are B4; fruit or seed kept as a natural specimen is F6.**

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A).** Category J4 > sub-family (class of produce: fruit, vegetable, herb, edible fungus, sea vegetable, mixed pack) > leaf (crop group, only where a sub-family has more than 12 kinds: 8 fruit leaves, 7 vegetable leaves) > kind (species or commodity, with a few finer kinds in citrus, peppers and mushrooms) > named form (cultivar or commercial type, `maker_bound` yes or no). **Maximum depth four levels below J4.** State (fresh, chilled-cut, frozen), form, grade (with its scheme), size, organic, origin, quality mark, ripeness and season are attributes or generated views. A small brand and licensor ledger, a variant ledger keyed on PLU codes, and an alias table complete the data.

**Fruit leaves recommended** (align with Culinary: pome SW-A1-2, stone SW-A1-3, citrus SW-A1-1, berries SW-A1-5, grapes SW-A1-6, melons SW-A1-7, tropical SW-A2-x, other dryland SW-A1-4 and SW-A1-8). Leaves differ in size: the tropical leaf is the one most likely to need a later split, which Culinary does by botanical family (SW-A2-1 to SW-A2-17).

**Alias and homonym table (C9) seeds, all from fetched pages:** capsicum (AU/IN) = bell pepper = sweet pepper (S11, S33); pepper (UK) = sweet pepper, chilli (UK) = hot pepper (S11); aubergine (UNECE, UK: S4) = eggplant (AU: S1); courgette = zucchini = baby marrow (S32, S1); coriander (UK/IN: S21, S29) = cilantro (US: S20); spring onion (S31) = salad onion (S34) = green onion, scallion (S20); satsuma = mikan (S7, S36); sweetcorn (UK: S29) = corn (US: S6); beetroot (S29) = beet (S6); spinach = palak (S21); pomelo = pummelo = shaddock (S36); persimmon = kaki (S15). Homonyms: "pepper" (sweet pepper or chilli: S11); "mushroom" (inside vegetables in S1, S31; separate in S2); "nuts" (inside Fresh Fruit in S26; J5 under the key).

**Culinary crosswalk (kind or leaf to Culinary leaf).** Citrus leaf ↔ SW-A1-1; pome ↔ SW-A1-2; stone ↔ SW-A1-3; persimmon ↔ SW-A1-4; berries ↔ SW-A1-5; grapes ↔ SW-A1-6; melons ↔ SW-A1-7; pomegranate and dryland ↔ SW-A1-8; banana, mango, pineapple, avocado... ↔ SW-A2-1 to SW-A2-17 by family; edible fungi (truffles, wild) ↔ SV-H5-5; sea vegetables ↔ SV-H5-2; chilli ↔ SV-H5-7; vegetables as dishes ↔ SV-E1 (dishes only; J4 holds the raw ingredient). Culinary's kind and variant rule (species kind, cultivar variant) matches this memo, so no conflict was found.

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. **Fruit or vegetable rule.** Use culinary use as sold (Woolworths, CIAT: tomato, cucumber, pepper, avocado in vegetables) or botany? This memo shows tomato and pepper in fruiting vegetables and avocado in tropical fruit. Is avocado to be filed once only? (S18, S19.)
2. **Cultivar depth.** Load only attested sold cultivars (about 400 to 800) or full lists (apples about 800, citrus about 1,000, chillies thousands: S35, S9, S11)? Culinary files these as variants; named forms here need a source (S6 attestation rule).
3. **Frozen.** Keep state as an attribute (recommended), or make frozen a sub-family (Candidate B)? Either way record the grade scheme (USDA frozen A/B/C, S12) with the state.
4. **Mixed packs.** Allow a sub-family whose axis is pack composition, as an exception to the crop-group axis?
5. **Boundary calls:** par-fried frozen chips and hash browns (fried, hence out under step 1); vegetables frozen in sauce; fresh coconut, chestnuts and tender coconut (nuts are J5, shops sell them fresh: S21, S26); olives; sprouting seed (flagged in `_framework_conflicts.md` as J5 for eating).
6. **Sea vegetables.** Keep a tiny sub-family (sold mostly dried, S30, so mostly J5), or fold fresh and frozen sea vegetables into the fungi sub-family as "other edible organisms"?
7. **Price bands.** Loose produce is sold per kilo and seasonally; accept no price band (recommended), or define one per-kilo band?
8. **Commercial types** (cherry, Roma, vine tomatoes; new, baking and salad potatoes; baby spinach): named forms (as here) or kinds? Only product-name evidence was seen (S20, S34).
9. **Cherries:** file with stone fruit (botanical, and Waitrose gives them their own aisle: S26) or with berries (Woolworths: S19)?
10. **Brand ledger:** is a licensor and own-label ledger wanted, or should brands stay only as aliases and variants?
