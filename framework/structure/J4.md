## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J4 Fruit & Vegetables, in domain J Groceries & Cellar.

**Membership test (placement key step 3, fourth branch, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md):** "**fruit, vegetables, herbs, edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen**, whole, cut or washed, but not dressed, sauced, fried or coated". Category line: "fruit, vegetables, herbs, edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen, by species and cultivar."

**Precedence sentences and notes in the framework that touch J4:**
- Step 3 head: groceries are filed once, as the product as bought, "divided by product and by the quality of the ingredient"; Culinary describes the same foods as eaten and links to it. Nuts, seeds, dried fruit, dried vegetables, dried mushrooms and dried seaweed are J5; pickles, preserves, tinned and jarred fruit, vegetables and tomatoes are J5; sauces and condiments are J5 "whatever they are made from"; pulses are J5.
- Step 6 note: "cut vegetables, herbs, sprouts and microgreens sold to eat are step 3; a plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is [C3] even when sold to eat, such as potted basil or living microgreens."
- Step 1: a salad (a dressed mixture), a dish, a soup or a ready meal is prepared and left to Culinary; "curing, smoking, ageing, fermenting, drying, salting, pickling, tinning, freezing or plain cooking of one main ingredient does not make a food prepared".
- Step 7 (F6): natural objects kept as specimens are F6; a natural material sold for a use is filed by that use.
- Brief (owner, 2026-10-17): grocery products that Culinary also holds are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind; groceries are judged by the quality of the ingredient.
- Culinary link plan (FRAMEWORK_v1.md, SW-A): Culinary already holds fruit species as kinds and cultivars as variants ("Fruit species are kinds, cultivars variants", rule s13), with leaves Citrus SW-A1-1, Pome SW-A1-2, Stone SW-A1-3, Persimmons SW-A1-4, Berries SW-A1-5, Grapes SW-A1-6, Melons SW-A1-7, Pomegranates and dryland fruit SW-A1-8, and the tropical families SW-A2-1 to SW-A2-17; edible seaweeds SV-H5-2, wild mushrooms and truffles SV-H5-5, chillies and peppers SV-H5-7. A crosswalk is in section 10.

**Retailers and marketplaces (eight regions).**
- *Australia, Woolworths* (S18, S19, S1). The aisle Fruit & Veg opens into Fruit, Vegetables, Salad, Prepared Vegetables, Organic, Fresh Herbs/Garlic/Chillies and Snacking. Fruit divides into Apples & Pears, Bananas, Berries & Cherries, Citrus, Grapes, Melons & Mangoes, Avocados, Pineapples/Kiwi/Tropical, Asian & Exotic, and Cut Fruit. Vegetables divide into crop-named groups (Zucchini/Eggplant/Squash, Sprouts, Mushrooms, Fresh Herbs/Garlic/Chillies, Organic, Prepacked; links on the aisle page also show Carrots/Onions/Root veg, Potatoes/Pumpkins, Lettuce/Kale/Leafy greens, Celery). The page explains its own rule: tomatoes, cucumbers, avocados and capsicums "are botanically fruits", but "because they aren't sweet, we categorise them as vegetables" (S18); it also teaches the plant-part view (flower with seeds, root, leaf, stem).
- *UK, Waitrose* (S26, S34, S27, S28, S29). Fresh Fruit (Berries, Loose Fruit, Bananas, Apples, Pears, Grapes, Oranges/Lemons/Citrus, Mangos/Melons/Exotic, Peaches & Nectarines, Plums/Apricots, Prepared Fruit, Nuts/Seeds/Dried Fruit, Cherries, Organic, Avocado, Perfectly Ripe) and Fresh Vegetables are separate aisles from Fresh Salad & Herbs (cucumber and radish sit there) and from In Season Fruit & Vegetables; frozen fruit (Cherries, Blueberry, smoothie mixes) and frozen vegetables (Peas, Sweetcorn & Carrots, Beans, Spinach/Broccoli/Cauliflower, Brussels Sprouts, Mixed Vegetables, Parsnips/Celeriac/Squash/Beetroot, Onions, Herbs) are aisles in the Frozen department.
- *Germany, REWE* (S2): mushrooms are their own group, split into Frische Champignons, Frische Pilze and Getrocknete Pilze, so fresh and dried mushrooms sit together in one shop category (the framework sends the dried ones to J5).
- *France, Carrefour* (S3): the Fruits et Légumes rayon divides into Bio, Fruits, Légumes, Prêt à consommer, Jus de fruits frais, Fruits et légumes secs and Corbeilles; seasonal lists (légumes de saison, fruits de saison) cut across it; the filters offer quality labels (Label Rouge, IGP, AOP, Bio) and country of origin.
- *Japan, Rakuten greengrocer* (S7): single fruit, single vegetable, boxed fruit by species (strawberry, mikan, grapefruit, persimmon, grape, peach, banana, apple, melon, pineapple, watermelon, mango, kiwi, lychee), and sweet potato split by named cultivar (Naruto Kintoki, Amakun, Annou).
- *India, BigBasket* (S21): Fresh Fruits, Fresh Vegetables, Exotic Fruits & Veggies, Herbs & Seasonings, Organic, Cuts & Sprouts; facets for banana varieties (Nendran, Poovan, Robusta, Yelakki, Green, Red) and mango varieties (Alphonso, Kesar, Dasheri, Langra, Himsagar, Totapuri...) as cultivar filters.
- *Singapore, FairPrice* (S31): Vegetables divide into Frozen Vegetables, Leafy Vegetables, Mushrooms, Potatoes & Roots, Garlic/Ginger/Onions, so frozen sits inside the vegetable group here.
- *United States* (S20, S25): Instacart divides Fresh Produce into Fresh Fruit, Fresh Vegetables and party trays, with Frozen Food as a separate top category; Kroger files Frozen Vegetables in its Frozen Foods department.

**Enthusiast communities and wikis.** Orange Pippin (S10, S35) lists tree-fruit species (apples, crab-apples, apricots, cherries, peaches, plums, pears) with named varieties inside each; its apple directory holds about 800 entries (sum of the A to Z counts), each described as dessert, culinary or cider, with synonyms (Albermarle Pippin is a synonym for Newtown Pippin). Know the Pepper (S24) sorts 215+ chillies into five Scoville tiers. Wikipedia (S11) groups Capsicum cultivars under five species and notes that British English calls sweet ones "peppers" and hot ones "chillies" while Australian and Indian English use "capsicum" for bell peppers.

**Collectors, resale and trade encyclopedias.** There is no collector market. The Specialty Produce encyclopedia of a wholesaler (S13) catalogues thousands of articles by produce family and shows the licensing pattern: Cripps Pink is the varietal name and "only around 10 percent" of the crop is sold under the trademark Pink Lady, whose name is owned and managed by a state department and an Australian industry body.

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- UNECE (S4): one standard per commodity or commodity group (Aubergines, Berry fruits, Certain small fruits, Ceps, Truffles, Leafy vegetables, Root and tubercle vegetables, Shallots, Strawberries...), plus an informal citrus variety list (S36).
- EU/UK marketing standards (S5, S33): Extra Class, Class I, Class II for ten specifically listed products (apples, citrus, kiwi, lettuces, peaches/nectarines, pears, strawberries, sweet peppers, table grapes, tomatoes); a General Marketing Standard (no classes) for most other fresh fruit, vegetables, nuts, herbs and cultivated mushrooms; excluded: potatoes, chilli peppers, sweetcorn, olives, wild mushrooms, sweet potatoes, ripened bananas, ginger.
- USDA AMS (S6, S17, S12): voluntary U.S. grade standards for each vegetable and fruit, fresh and for processing; frozen vegetables have their own grades A, B, C and Substandard, scored 90, 80 and 70 points.
- Japan (S14, S15): the national standard shipping specifications were abolished in 2002; each prefecture now sets its own. Fukushima grades fruit and fruit-like vegetables Tokushu/Shu/Yu/Ryo (特秀・秀・優・良), vegetables and mushrooms A/B, with size classes by count per box or weight and colour tables per cultivar (Fuji, Tsugaru, Jonagold...).
- China (S16): GB/T 26906-2024 grades sweet cherry special, first and second class, with size by diameter and soluble solids of at least 14 percent.
- IFPS PLU (S22, S23): over 1,500 codes identifying commodity, variety, growing method (prefix 9 for organic) and size; mushrooms alone have 21 codes (button small/large, cremini, portabella, shiitake, oyster, enoki, chanterelle, morel, cep, wood ear...).
- GS1 GPC (S8): Family Fruits Unprepared/Unprocessed (Fresh) with classes such as Pome Fruits, Kiwifruits and Miscellaneous Fruit, and bricks by species (Apples with an Apple Variety attribute, Strawberries, Dates, Durian, Mangos, Lychees); separate families for Vegetables (Non Leaf), Leaf Vegetables and Fresh Cut; an attribute "Quality (UNECE Standard)".

**Museums and reference works.** The UC Riverside Citrus Variety Collection (S9) holds about 1,000 citrus cultivars and relatives, browsable alphabetically and "by category or type". The CIAT guide (S32) divides vegetables into ten classes by plant part (leafy, root, bulb, stem, flower, fruit, pod, tuber, seed, fungi) and admits overlaps (potato root or tuber; pea pod or legume). A trade guide on seaweed (S30) groups it by colour (green, brown, red) with named types (nori, wakame, kombu, dulse, arame, hijiki, sea grapes).

**Where sources agree.** Every retailer with a full tree opens with fruit against vegetable (S1, S3, S20, S21, S26, S34); herbs are split from vegetables in three of them (S1, S21, S27); the commodity (apple, pear, tomato, mushroom) is the unit of standards, trade codes and shop sub-aisles (S4, S6, S8, S17, S22). **Where they split the same objects differently:** avocado (fruit aisle and vegetable aisle at Woolworths, S18/S19); tomato and cucumber (fruit botanically, vegetables on every shelf); mushrooms (inside vegetables at Woolworths and FairPrice, S1, S31; their own group at REWE, S2; UNECE has separate Ceps and Truffles standards, S4); frozen (a department apart at Waitrose, Kroger, Instacart: S25, S28, S29, S20; inside vegetables at FairPrice, S31); cherries (with berries at Woolworths, S19; stone fruit botanically, and Waitrose gives them an aisle of their own, S26); chillies (with herbs and garlic at Woolworths, S18; excluded from the EU standards, S33); nuts and dried fruit (inside Fresh Fruit at Waitrose, S26; the framework puts them in J5).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Buyers choose in this order, from the shop trees above: (1) **fruit or vegetable, by culinary use** (all of S1, S3, S18, S20, S21, S26, S34); (2) **the crop or crop group** (apples and pears, citrus, berries; roots, leafy greens, alliums), which is also the unit of the standards (S4, S17, S6) and of the trade codes (S8, S22); (3) **the cultivar**, but only where it is sold by name: BigBasket mango and banana facets (S21), Japanese shops by sweet-potato cultivar (S7), Fukushima colour and size tables per apple cultivar (S15), the Orange Pippin directory (S10, S35); (4) **filters that are not types**: origin (S3), organic (S1, S3, S21, S26), quality labels (S3), season (S3, S18, S27 menu), ripeness ("Perfectly Ripe", S26), brand (S21), pack (loose, prepacked, cut: S1, S19, S26). Nobody navigates by botanical family, because tomato, pepper and potato (all Solanaceae) sit in different shop groups (S1, S18), and Woolworths says it files by sweetness, not botany (S18). Nobody navigates by plant part either, except guides (S32) and the Woolworths explainer (S18).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates keep depth at the framework maximum: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels below J4; C5a).

### Candidate A (recommended): class of produce, then crop group, then species, then cultivar; state as an attribute

- **Level 1 sub-family. Axis: class of produce as sold** (fruit; vegetable; herb; edible fungus; sea vegetable). Sources: S1, S18, S21, S26, S31; fungi alone in S2, S4, S23; herbs alone in S1, S21, S27. There is no sub-family for mixed packs (J4-1, C5c): a mix is a final kind inside the sub-family that holds its parts (rule R3 below).
- **Level 2 leaf. Axis: crop group** (botanical-culinary group as the shops and standards use it). Fruit leaves: pome, stone, citrus, berries and small fruit, grapes and vine fruit, melons, tropical and subtropical, other temperate and dryland, and a last leaf **Mixed** (value "several crop groups" on the same axis). Vegetable leaves: leaf vegetables, brassicas, roots tubers and rhizomes, alliums, fruiting vegetables, pods beans and peas, stems and shoots, and a last leaf **Mixed**. Herbs, fungi and sea vegetables have about 12 kinds or fewer in the sourced set, so they skip the leaf level (C5b: the data stores `leaf = null`). Fungi may need a leaf (cultivated against wild; S2, S33) if the list grows past 12; herbs likewise.
- **Level 3 kind. Axis: species or commodity** (the unit of the grade standards, PLU and GPC; section 4b).
- **Level 4 named form. Axis: cultivar or commercial type.** Almost all are **not maker-bound** (`maker_bound = no`): traditional and open cultivars (Gala, Fuji, Alphonso, Habanero, Naruto Kintoki) **and Cripps Pink**, because only the trade mark Pink Lady® is licensed, not the cultivar (S13). Cripps Pink carries a `breeder_or_holder` field (value not in the fetched pages: †). **Pink Lady® is a C9 alias of Cripps Pink with relation `trade mark`**, and the licensor is a row in the maker ledger. A club cultivar sold under one marketer only stays **maker-bound** (SunGold kiwifruit with Zespri: S20; the exclusivity itself rests on one retailer page: †).
- **State, form and grade are attributes**: fresh, chilled-cut or frozen (S25, S29, S12); whole, cut, washed, prepacked (S1, S19, S26); grade class under the named standard (S5, S12, S15, S16; C4b); size class (S15, S22; C6); organic, origin and quality label (S3, S22); ripeness (S26). PLU and GPC codes are an `external_codes` field on the kind or named form, not variants (J4-2).

*Rules inside J4 (all from the review, J4-1 to J4-4; items that rest on a pending convention carry OWNER).*
- **R1 Fruit or vegetable: sweetness as sold.** Tomato, avocado, cucumber, pepper, chilli, aubergine, courgette and squash, sweetcorn: Vegetables > Fruiting vegetables, because the one retailer that states its rule files them by sweetness, not botany (S18), and the memo's own tree already filed tomato and pepper this way. This gives avocado one home (it was in tropical fruit in the earlier draft, which contradicted that rule). Cherry: Stone fruit (botanical; Waitrose gives it its own aisle, S26; Woolworths files it with berries, S19). Plantain: Tropical and subtropical fruit, with the green-cooking use as an attribute (plantain kind †: no fetched page lists it as a product). OWNER: the review treats the sweetness rule, avocado and cherries as settled but lists them as owner questions 1 and 9; the memo applies them so that the tree does not contradict itself, and asks for confirmation (section 10).
- **R2 Herbs.** Herbs are "leafy or soft-stemmed plants sold in bunches or packs to flavour food" (coriander, parsley, basil, mint, lemongrass). Rocket goes to leaf vegetables; ginger to roots, tubers and rhizomes; bean sprouts and cut microgreens to stems and shoots; sweetcorn to fruiting vegetables. Kinds for rocket, lemongrass, basil, mint and plantain are not yet attested on a fetched page and are listed but not counted (†).
- **R3 Mixed products.** A mix of kinds from one sub-family is the final kind "mixed fruit" or "mixed vegetables" in that sub-family's last leaf Mixed (frozen mixed vegetables, an undressed salad-leaf mix, a forest-fruit mix). A fruit-and-vegetable tray has no sub-family that holds all its parts: it is filed by its greatest-weight item (the review's wording of the step 3 set rule; the framework text says item of greatest value). OWNER: JC5(c) is pending (question 9). It would also file a mix wholly inside one leaf (a salad-leaf mix, a berry mix) as a final kind of that leaf, and file a mix by the kind that makes up half or more of its weight; the memo keeps the review's J4-1 fix for now.
- **R4 Nuts, chestnuts, coconut, olives.** These follow the key: every nut is J5; table olives are J5 (preserved). J5's nuts leaf must list fresh chestnut and coconut (cross-memo finding X7).

### Candidate B: state first (fresh, frozen, cut), then crop

- Levels: sub-family = state (fresh; frozen; fresh-cut), leaf = crop group, kind = species, named form = cultivar.
- Basis: Waitrose, Kroger, Instacart and Carrefour (S25, S28, S29, S20, S3) put frozen in another department, and GPC has a separate Fresh Cut family (S8).
- Breaks: the same kinds (pea, spinach, blueberry) appear in two or three sub-families, which only works if the owner treats "frozen peas" as a different kind from "peas"; Appendix A names condition as a modifier, and FairPrice files frozen inside vegetables (S31). The USDA frozen grades A/B/C (S12) differ from the fresh grades, which is a reason to record the grade scheme with the state, not a reason to split the tree. Edge objects: a frozen mix of fruit (smoothie mix, S28), a chilled washed salad (S18), thawed-and-sold-fresh berries.

### Candidate C: botanical family or plant part first

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers (C2a). For produce that level is **the species or commodity**, which is where grade standards (S4, S6, S17, S5), trade codes (S22, S8), shop sub-aisles (S1, S19, S26, S34) and brand/maker comparison (S20, S25, S31) sit. Cultivars are the named-form level, where a licence or breeder can be attached.

Evidence for species as the kind:
- Grade standards are written per commodity: UNECE (S4), USDA fruit list of apples, apricots, bananas, berries, cherries, citrus, grapes, kiwifruit, mangos, melons, peaches, pears, pineapple, plums (S17), USDA vegetable list (S6), EU specific standards for ten commodities (S33), Fukushima per fruit (S15), China's sweet-cherry standard (S16).
- GPC bricks are species; cultivar is an attribute ("Apple Variety", S8). PLU codes the variety and size under the commodity (S22).
- Shops name the species in sub-aisles (S1, S19, S26, S34, S7) and use cultivar as a filter or a product name (S21 mango and banana facets; S20 "Gala Apple", "Roma Tomato").
- Culinary's rule agrees: "Fruit species are kinds, cultivars variants" (FRAMEWORK_v1.md, s13; checked by the domain review, section 6). In J a cultivar is a non-maker named form, so the two frameworks use compatible kind rules.

Why cultivar is not a kind: BRIEF and S2 (kinds are generic types; models and designs are named forms). Buyers do compare apple cultivars with each other (the Orange Pippin directory and a "Top 10 varieties" list, S10), but the cultivar set is open, partly licensed (S13) and mostly seasonal; this is the "model" level of produce.

**Where the choice level differs inside J4:**
- *Citrus* goes one step finer than "citrus": UNECE and shops list orange, blood orange, clementine, satsuma, other mandarins, lemon, Persian lime, West Indian lime, grapefruit and pummelo as separate headings (S36, S7, S3). Navel, Valencia and Red Navel are groups of orange cultivars (named forms, not maker-bound; S36).
- *Chillies and sweet peppers* are two kinds: the EU applies a standard to sweet peppers and excludes chillies (S33); Woolworths files them in different groups (S18); heat is measured, not a type (S24).
- *Mushrooms* go by market type, not species only: the PLU list separates regular button small and large, cremini, portabella, shiitake, oyster, enoki and others (S23); REWE separates Champignons from other fresh fungi (S2).
- *Potato and tomato "commercial types"* (new potato, baking potato; cherry vine, Roma) are named forms for now: the fetched pages name them only as product names (S20, S34), so there is no source showing them compared as separate categories. OWNER: question 8; a comparison source is listed under sourcing (†).
- *Frozen:* state is not a level (section 5 explains the grade scheme). The template's own example makes frozen vegetables one kind; here the species stays the kind because Waitrose, Kroger and FairPrice organise frozen by species (S29, S25, S31). JC4(a) agrees: storage state is always an attribute.
- *Mixed products* stop at the final kind "mixed fruit" or "mixed vegetables", since no single species names them (S12, S28, S20). This follows J4-1 and C5(c); the half-by-weight refinement of JC5(c) is OWNER (question 9).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Type: spec-ranked by published standard; no consensus tier on kinds.** Grade ladders exist and are measured on the object, so under convention C4(b) (and JC4(d), X8 of the review) they are attributes with the standard named, not tier entries. A tier never sits on a kind (C4a); none is entered for J4.
- EU/UK: Extra Class, Class I, Class II (S5, S33); the UNECE standards are the model for these classes (S4).
- USA: voluntary U.S. grade standards per commodity (S6, S17); frozen vegetables U.S. Grade A (90 points), B (80), C (70), Substandard (S12).
- Japan: 秀・優・良 for fruit (with 特秀 above) and A/B for vegetables and mushrooms, set by prefecture since the national standard ended in 2002 (S14, S15); size classes 2L/L/M and count per box (S15).
- China: 特级 / 一级 / 二级 for sweet cherry, size by diameter (S16).
- Trade attributes: IFPS size (S22); GPC "Quality (UNECE Standard)" attribute (S8). Chilli heat is stored as the measured Scoville value; the five band names Mild to Super-Hot come from one enthusiast site (S24) and are a view of that value, not a tier scheme, because C4(e) wants a second source from another region before a tier scheme enters the data (†).
- *Brand programmes and quality marks* are not tiers. Pink Lady® is a trade-mark alias of the cultivar Cripps Pink, of which about 10 percent of the crop is sold under the name (S13); Label Rouge, IGP, AOP and Bio on Carrefour (S3) are quality marks; organic is PLU prefix 9 (S22). A retailer range label such as "The Odd Bunch" or "Perfectly Ripe" (S18, S26) is a dated `platform_tag` (C4c), not a grade.
- Disputed: none found in fetched pages. The Specialty Produce site carries a second page (Pink Lady apples) whose search excerpt gives a different pass share; I did not fetch it, so the figure is not used (†).

**Data form.** `grade_scheme` (EU, USDA fresh, USDA frozen, JP prefecture, CN GB/T), `grade_value`, `size_class` and `quality_mark` are product attributes with the standard named, as the memo first proposed. OWNER: JC1 (question 1) would replace them with two shared tables, `standard` (values attached as `subject_id`, `standard_id`, `value`; EU and UNECE produce classes, USDA fresh and frozen grades, Japanese prefecture grades, GB/T 26906) and `designation` (Label Rouge, IGP and AOP are designations; a mark spanning several kinds is a tag, JC1c).

**Price band.** The memo recommends none for loose produce: prices are per kilo, seasonal, and the retailer pages show per-unit prices only (S26, S29). OWNER: the review (J4-5) proposes the shared `unit_price_band` (JC2, USD per kilogram from a dated, sourced list price; a seasonal price is still a dated list price) in place of "none"; JC2 is pending (question 3), so the memo keeps its own recommendation and records the proposed one beside it. If JC2 is approved the band sits on the kind or named form and is filled from the first data load.

## 6. Makers and brands

Brand matters in three places only: trade marks and club cultivars (Pink Lady® and Cripps Pink: S13; Zespri SunGold: S20), growers' and retailers' own labels (Waitrose Essential, Duchy Organic and Cooks' Ingredients: S29; BigBasket fresho!: S21; Kroger own brand: S25), and frozen-food processors seen in the shop facets (Bonduelle on Carrefour Belgium is in a search excerpt only, so it is not cited).

**Maker ledger.** The C1 core schema, one maker row per company, with `maker_class` limited to the C1 values (retail own-label for the own labels above; independent or supplier for the others). Scene tags empty. `known_for_kinds` derives from the named-form ledger. Nothing here needs the tier table of A5. The earlier draft put supply roles (licensor or growers' marketing body, marketer, processor) into `maker_class`; those are not C1 values. ## 8. Order inside a leaf

Kinds (C7): the **commodity that names the leaf comes first** (apple in pome, citrus group head orange), then **alphabetical by English name**, because the sources give no spec order for kinds (GPC and UNECE list alphabetically: S8, S4). ## 9. Boundaries

Each sentence below is a precedence sentence for the key. Those marked OWNER depend on a pending framework change or convention.

1. **J5 Pantry Staples.** *Closest objects:* dried shiitake, dried chilli, raisins, olives, tinned tomatoes, sauerkraut and kimchi, dried wakame or nori, sprouting seed, chestnuts, fresh coconut. *Precedence:* **A fruit, vegetable, mushroom or seaweed sold fresh, chilled, cut, washed or frozen without sauce or coating is J4; the same plant sold dried, tinned, jarred, pickled or fermented, and every nut, seed, pulse and spice, is J5.** Fresh chestnuts and fresh coconut follow the key ("every nut ... is J5") and are J5; table olives are J5 as preserved; J5's nuts leaf must list chestnut and coconut (finding X7). *Note:* shops put dried fruit and nuts inside Fresh Fruit (S26) and dried mushrooms beside fresh (S2); the framework key wins. The trade guide describes the named seaweeds mostly in dried or processed forms (S30), so the sea-vegetable sub-family is small. OWNER: sprouting seed sold to eat is J5 and for growing is C3 (logged in `_framework_conflicts.md`; question 27). Tender coconut remains an owner call (question 5).
2. **C3 Plants & Living Cultures.** *Closest:* potted basil, living microgreens, mushroom growing kit, fruit trees and bushes. *Precedence:* **A plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is C3 even when sold to eat; the same plant cut and sold to eat is J4.** Fruit trees and bushes sold for planting are C3.
3. **Step 1 prepared foods (left to Culinary).** *Closest:* coleslaw and dressed salads, stir-fry kits with sauce sachets, fruit salad in syrup, par-fried frozen chips, battered vegetables, vegetable soup. *Precedence:* **Produce that is washed, trimmed, cut, peeled or frozen plain stays J4; once it is dressed, sauced, fried, coated or cooked into a dish it is out.** OWNER: par-fried frozen chips, hash browns and battered vegetables are single-ingredient products that step 1 does not name, so under S1a they would run to X0. The proposed step 1 sentence is "par-fried or coated frozen vegetables are prepared (OUT)" (question 21; also in `_framework_conflicts.md`). Vegetables frozen in sauce are out as sauced. Fruit in syrup is J5 if tinned or jarred, out as a prepared dessert if a salad.
4. **J6 Coffee, Tea & Cocoa.** *Closest:* fresh mint or ginger for infusions, fresh coffee cherries. *Precedence:* **Fresh herbs and roots sold to cook with are J4; leaves, flowers and roots dried and sold to brew as infusions are J6.** (J4, J5 and J6 agree on herbs: fresh J4, dried for cooking J5, dried to brew J6.)
5. **H2 Health.** *Closest:* spirulina or chlorella tablets, greens powders, essence-of-vegetable tonics. *Precedence:* **Plain seaweed and fungi as food are J4; tablets, capsules and powders sold as supplements are H2.**
6. **B4 Decoration (cut flowers) and F6.** *Closest:* edible flowers, ornamental fruits and gourds, gourd and fruit specimens. *Precedence:* **Flowers and gourds sold to eat are J4; cut flowers and dried or decorative gourds are B4; fruit or seed kept as a natural specimen is F6.**
7. **Mixed sets across categories.** *Closest:* a fruit-and-vegetable tray with dip, a gift hamper with fruit, a produce box. *Precedence:* **A mix of kinds from one sub-family is the final "mixed" kind of that sub-family; a set that crosses categories is filed by its item of greatest value (step 3 set rule), and is OUT if that item is a prepared food; the basket kept afterwards is B1.** OWNER: JC5(c) is pending (question 9).
8. **Fruit against vegetable.** *Closest:* avocado, tomato, cucumber, pepper, sweetcorn, plantain, rhubarb. *Precedence:* **Inside J4 the shop rule applies: sweet fruit as sold is Fruit, savoury botanical fruit as sold is Vegetables > Fruiting vegetables; plantain is tropical fruit.** OWNER: confirmation of R1 (question 1).
