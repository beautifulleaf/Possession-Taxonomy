**One-line result.** Divide D2 by the role the device plays for its owner (eight sub-families: phones; two-way radios and pagers; tablets; e-readers and pocket information devices; wearables; location, navigation and safety devices; power banks; and parts and accessories made for one phone, tablet or wearable), then, where a sub-family needs it, by device class as retailers shelve it (leaf), then by the build or form class buyers compare inside (kind), then by maker line and model (named form). Operating system, store, finding network, radio service, subscription and charging standard are attributes that point into one compatibility ledger (C13). Market segment is never a level and never on a kind: price-defined labels become the domain price band (C4(d)), a shop's own labels become platform tags (C4(c)), benchmark ranks become dated tier entries on named forms (C4(a)), and maker ladders (iPad base, mini, Air, Pro; Garmin Entry, Advanced, Premium) become `line_rank` in the lines table (C1).

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, second branch).** "A **phone, two-way radio, pager or other device for personal communication**; a **tablet, e-reader, electronic organiser or translator**; a **smartwatch, fitness tracker, smart ring or other smart jewellery, smart glasses, GPS unit, satellite messenger or tracker**; power banks? **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices** (Children's tablets are here.)" Domain line: "phones and two-way radios, tablets, e-readers, smartwatches and wearables, GPS units and trackers, power banks."

**Rules and notes that already touch D2.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole), tried at step 8 before step 24: a phone case, screen protector, watch strap or charger made for one device follows that device. Cases and covers are parts only when made to hold or protect one object at a time. Sub-family D2.8 receives them (C5(d), extended to domain D by the review, X-10; OWNER: O-6).
- Step 1 note: physical access cards (SIM cards among them) are objects filed at step 19 (F5), reached before step 8 (S1b). See section 9 item 9 and OWNER: O-27.
- Step 2 note (H2): devices that count steps or heart rate for fitness are D2; medical devices and monitors, hearing aids and medical alert bracelets are H2.
- Step 13 (B10), tried before step 24: equipment made for keeping, feeding, carrying or caring for animals, kept or wild, including animal identification microchips. A tracker made to attach to an animal is reached here first (section 9 item 6; OWNER: O-30).
- Step 16 note (A5): smartwatches and hybrid smartwatches are D2; smart displays and clock radios are D3.
- Step 17 note (worn on the body) and its A3 branch note: a device whose main function is computing, communication, sensing or media, such as a smartphone, smartwatch, fitness tracker, earbuds or e-reader, goes to step 24; smart rings are D2. Step 24 comes before step 26 (E2), so a GPS unit is reached as D2 before E2's "navigation gear".
- Step 24 first branch (D1): a device whose screen and keyboard can separate is D2 if sold as a tablet with an optional keyboard and D1 if the keyboard comes with it; cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices are D1 whatever device they serve (but see section 9 item 1 on step 8); drawing tablets are D1.
- Step 24 connectivity note: a connected version of a household object is filed by its task at step 30; connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 24 branches D3, D4, D5: headphones and smart speakers are D3; cameras and drones made for filming are D4; consoles, handhelds and VR headsets are D5.
- BRIEF.md: tiers by brand or specification must be capturable (here: price band, benchmark entries, maker lines, spec attributes); condition (used, refurbished) and user (child, senior) are modifiers, not levels; individual items are out (C16).

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D2** (never levels): brand and maker, price band and quality tier, era (feature phone, PDA, retro), user (child, senior, adapted), setting (outdoor, marine, aviation), market (consumer, enterprise, carrier-locked), connectivity (Wi-Fi only, cellular, satellite), condition (new, refurbished, used), power source and battery, region. Two further D2-specific attributes behave like modifiers and are carried in the compatibility ledger (C13): operating system and store (iOS, Android, HarmonyOS, watchOS, Wear OS, Kindle, Kobo) and finding or service network (Find My, Find Hub, inReach subscription); a third, the radio service (FRS, GMRS, PMR446, Part 90, amateur, CB), joins them.

### Collectors and the resale market

- Resale sites keep device-type sections and grade condition, not kind (S24, S25). Swappa sells by exact model with listing conditions; Iosys uses six ranks from S (new or unused) to J (junk). Condition is a modifier; the Iosys scale is recorded only as a row of a condition-scale reference table (section 5; OWNER: O-4).
- The PDA Museum (S33) is a collector site organised as guided tours "from old to new" and "from large to small" (chronology and size). The PalmPilot exhibit at the Computer History Museum (S34) tells the history by lineage (Psion, HP LX, Newton, EO, Zoomer, Palm).

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS/CN heading 8517 (S20).** Telephone sets "including smartphones and other telephones for cellular networks or for other wireless networks" are split into line telephone sets with cordless handsets (8517.11), smartphones (8517.13), other cellular or wireless telephones (8517.14) and other; network and data apparatus sit in 8517.6. Smartphone is its own subheading. A customs heading is evidence only, never a level.
- **IEC 60529 IP code (S22).** Two numerals, solids 0 to 6 and liquids 0 to 9; the recognised durability grade for phones, watches and trackers (also a GSMArena and Kimovil filter, S04, S17). An attribute with the standard named (C4(b)).
- **FAA PackSafe (S21).** Power banks and phone battery cases are "spare lithium batteries": carry-on only, 100 Wh per battery (101 to 160 Wh with airline approval). Watt-hours is the regulatory band for power banks; recorded as an attribute and, if the owner wants it, in the jurisdiction ledger (C17).
- **IETF DULT (S23).** A working group standardising how location-tracking accessories and nearby devices handle unwanted tracking; it treats trackers as one accessory class across makers.
- **FCC radio services (S31, S29).** FRS, GMRS and Part 90 business radio are the legal classes of handheld two-way radio in the US; the UK and EU use PMR446. They are rows of the compatibility ledger and the jurisdiction ledger (C13, C17), never kinds or leaves (section 3, 4b and 7).

### Museums and reference works

- Wikipedia "Form factor (mobile phones)" (S05) divides phones into forms with one non-movable section (bar, keyboard bar, brick, slate, phablet, multi-screen, taco), forms with movable sections (clamshell or flip, foldable, slider, swivel, mixed) and wearables (smartwatch). It defines a phablet as a screen over 5.3 in (a size label, not a kind).
- Wikipedia "Electronic organizer" (S35) separates the calculator-sized organiser from the PDA, palmtop and smartphone, and groups handheld devices as organiser, e-reader, handheld game console, handheld PC, mobile phone (camera, feature, smartphone, foldable) and tablet (detachable, phablet).
- Wikipedia "Smartwatch" (S38) groups wearables as fitness tracker (smart band), digital wristwatch (calculator watch, smartwatch, sportwatch), smart glasses and smart ring, and lists watch operating systems (watchOS, Wear OS, Fitbit OS, Zepp OS, HarmonyOS).

### Where sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

- **Agree:** phones, tablets and wearables are separate shelves in every retailer tree (S01, S07, S11, S36, S24); e-readers are their own category where a shop has enough of them (S02, S03, S26); power banks are a power accessory that sits with chargers (S12, S13, S15); satellite messengers, handheld GPS and radios are outdoor-specialist devices that retailers separate from phones (S09, S10, S28, S29, S30).
- **Split: where power banks live.** With phone accessories (Best Buy S01; idealo S13), with mobile chargers inside Mobiltelefone (Geizhals S12), with chargers and cables beside phones in a maker catalogue (Xiaomi S15), or in the household-appliance tree away from phones (Kakaku S36). The placement key settles it for this taxonomy (D2).
- **Split: smartwatch against sport watch against fitness tracker.** Geizhals merges smartwatches and sport watches (S12); Kakaku merges smartwatch and smart band as one category with a type spec (S37); Best Buy separates Smartwatches from Fitness Trackers (S07); idealo has a Sportuhr chip (S13); Garmin separates by activity (S08).
- **Split: where tablets live.** Swappa shelves Tablets and iPads beside Phones and Watches (S24), while Kakaku files tablets and e-readers under PC (S02). The placement key puts both in D2.
- **Split: tablet against e-reader against e-ink tablet.** Croma treats tablet, e-reader and digital pad as three types (S03); MobileRead has e-readers only as reading devices but notes tablets and phones as alternative readers (S27); an Android e-ink tablet (Boox) is both (S39). This memo resolves it by what the device is sold for (read, or take notes with a stylus), with size, panel and platform as attributes (section 4b).
- **Split: tier.** Retailers and rankers use "flagship, mid-range, budget" or "high-end, mid-range, low-end" but disagree on what sets the band: price (Alibaba S06, Kimovil price bands S17), benchmark score within a market segment (AnTuTu S18), or a maker's own ladder (iPad base, mini, Air, Pro S40; Garmin Entry, Advanced, Premium S09). Section 5 stores each in the place C4 gives it.
- **Split: where the objects belong.** Retail trees hold objects that the placement key sends elsewhere: Kakaku's PC-peripheral list holds e-readers beside digital pens, USB hubs and card readers (S02); Geizhals holds radio scanners and hobby radios with phones (S11); Best Buy holds wristwatches beside smartwatches (S07). These are filed by the key, and the mismatches are listed in section 9.

**Reading.** The only axis every retail tree shares at the top is the device type, which follows the role the device plays (talk, read, wear, find, power). Platform, finding network, subscription and radio service decide the purchase but vary within a role, and a platform split would cut across every role (an iPhone, an iPad, an Apple Watch and an AirTag are four roles in one ecosystem). So the structure divides by role and carries platform, finding network and radio service as attributes that point into the compatibility ledger.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): role, then device class where needed, then build or form class, then maker line and model

- **Sub-family** (axis: the role the device plays for its owner): D2.1 Phones; D2.2 Two-way radios and pagers; D2.3 Tablets; D2.4 E-readers and pocket information devices; D2.5 Wearables; D2.6 Location, navigation and safety devices; D2.7 Power banks; D2.8 Parts and accessories made for one phone, tablet or wearable (the only sub-family exempt from the role axis, C5(d)).
- **Leaf** (axis, stated per sub-family under C5(c)): device class as retailers shelve it. D2.1 Phones: smartphones, feature phones, satellite phones, landline phones (Best Buy, Geizhals presets, GSMArena OS facet: S01, S12, S04). D2.4: reading devices, organisers and PDAs, language devices (Croma, Wikipedia: S03, S35). D2.6: GPS units, satellite communicators, trackers (Garmin and REI articles separate them: S09, S10, S28, S30, S32). **D2.2, D2.3, D2.5, D2.7 and D2.8 hold twelve kinds or fewer and store their kinds with `leaf = null` (C5(b)).** The earlier leaves D2.2.1 "Licence-free" and D2.2.2 "Licensed" handheld radios (regional legal class, C11, C13), D2.5.1 "Wrist devices" and D2.5.2 "Finger and face devices" (carry position, the axis Candidate B rejects) and D2.6.4 "Sport navigation devices" (activity) are removed.
- **Kind** (axis: the build or form class buyers compare inside): bar, flip and book smartphones; smart band against smartwatch; satellite messenger against beacon; in D2.2 the form and mounting of the radio (C12).
- **Named form** (axis: maker line and model; `maker_bound: yes` for every D2 named form): iPhone 17 Pro, Galaxy Z Flip, Kindle Paperwhite, fēnix 8, AirTag.

Example kinds (15): bar smartphone, flip foldable smartphone, book foldable smartphone, bar feature phone, slate tablet, e-reader, e-paper writing tablet, PDA, smartwatch, smart band, smart ring, handheld hiking GPS, satellite messenger, Bluetooth item tracker, power bank. The full worked tree follows below.

**Where it breaks (after conformance).**

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds.** The worked tree holds 51 kinds; 14 of them have no fetched attestation yet and two more need a second source. After the gaps are closed and the owner has ruled on the provisional kinds (foldables, historic phone forms, sport watch, screenless band, writing tablet, bike computer, landline phone, phone-only accessories), the likely range is **about 45 to 65 kinds** (my estimate). This is small beside D1 because many D2 distinctions (storage size, colour, carrier lock, OS version, panel, screen size, rugged rating, radio service) are attributes of one model.

**Named forms (rough, my estimate).**
- Phones: the largest block. GSMArena's brand filter lists well over a hundred makers (S04), and a phone is announced every few days by a dozen groups; the full historic count is in the tens of thousands, the current catalogue several thousand model names. Geizhals separates the model (Apple iPhone 17 Pro) from its colour and storage variants (256GB tiefblau) (S11), which is the grain recommended here (S12 shows the same split): model and series are named forms; storage, colour and regional models are variant rows (C3; variant types `configuration` and `regional model` are the review's proposal, X-5, OWNER: O-56).

- E-readers: 11 current products on Kakaku (S02), 336 devices ever on MobileRead (S27); estimate 300 to 500.
- GPS, radios, trackers, satellite devices, power banks, parts: tens to a few hundred each.
- Overall estimate: 8,000 to 20,000 named forms if history is included, 2,000 to 4,000 if only devices sold in the last five years.

**Where depth concentrates.** Smartphones (maker line, then model, then variant), then wrist devices (Garmin alone lists about ten series entries in its own filter, S08), then tablets. ## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.** For D2 that is the build or form class inside a device class, and the model line (not the storage, colour, panel or radio-service variant) is the named form.

**Tests applied to every candidate kind.** (a) A kind defined by a measured spec, certification, size, panel or display technology, operating system, radio service, price tier, user, setting, scene, era or maker is an attribute, tag or view, not a kind (C4(b), C6, C8, C10, C11, Appendix A). (b) Technology or connectivity defines a kind only when the key names it or when it changes the physical form or installation and a fetched source ranks the types apart (C10). (c) A regional name or standard is never a leaf or kind label; it is a compatibility-ledger row (C11, C13). (d) Mounting defines a kind only when the body differs (C12). ## 5. Quality and tier

**Is there a recognised tier ordering? Yes, in three different forms, and C4 gives each its own place.** No `market_tier` field exists; no tier sits on a kind (C4(a)).

1. **Market segment (phones, tablets): three sources, three rules.** Flagship, mid-range and budget are not defined by a standard. Each source's scale is stored where its type belongs:
   - **Price-defined labels are the price band, not a tier.** Alibaba's guide defines budget under 200 USD, mid-range 300 to 500, premium over 800, leaving gaps [S06]. The price band is the one domain-wide USD scheme of C4(d), computed from a dated, sourced list price on the named form. Labels such as "budget" or "premium" may appear in a generated view over the band. Kimovil's price bands [S17] are the same type.
   - **A shop's own label is a platform tag (C4(c)).** Kimovil's Range (Low, Mid, High-end) [S17] is stored as a dated `platform_tag` with the platform named, not as a tier.
   - **A third-party measured score is a dated tier entry on the named form (C4(a)).** AnTuTu's monthly charts for flagship, mid-range and tablets [S18] and its ranking page [S19] give an average benchmark score. Entry: `subject_id` (named form), `scheme` (AnTuTu 12 average score, chart segment), `value` (score), `source_url`, `date`; extra columns proposed by the review (X-3): `scheme_type = measurement`, `test_version` (AnTuTu 12), `region = CN` for the monthly charts (S18 states China data), `stale_after` one month. The chart segment (flagship, mid-range, tablet) is the publisher's assignment and is recorded as part of the entry, never as a level. The benchmark changes every month, so scores are dated measurements, not permanent order. C4(e) asks for a preferably second source from another region: Kimovil shows an AnTuTu score filter [S17] but reproduces the same benchmark, so a truly independent second source is still needed †. OWNER: O-1 and O-23 (adopt the six-class scale rule of X-3; third-party lists may be stored as dated, attributed tier entries); section 10 item 3.
2. **Maker line ladders (everywhere).** The maker orders its own range: iPad base, iPad mini, iPad Air, iPad Pro, with chip, RAM, display and Pencil support differing [S40]; Garmin handhelds Entry, Advanced, Premium [S09]; Garmin watches fēnix, Forerunner, Venu, vívoactive, vívosmart, Instinct [S08]. Stored as rows of the C1 lines table (`line_id`, `maker_id`, kinds spanned, years, sources) with a `line_rank`, true only inside one maker. A named form does not inherit a maker's tier unless an entry says so (C4(a)). An optional maker-ladder tier entry is possible only under the review's proposal (X-3 class iv; OWNER: O-1).
3. **Standards and regulatory grades (attributes with the standard named, C4(b)).** `ip_rating` (IEC 60529) and `mil_std_810` for durability [S22, S04]; `capacity_wh` and `output_w` for power banks, with the 100 Wh air-travel limit as a fact about the standard [S21]; `radio_service` and its power cap (FRS 2 W) [S29, S31], pointing to the compatibility ledger; `update_years` (the maker's software-update promise, dated; Apple 6 to 7 years, Samsung 4 OS updates for Galaxy S and Z, S06). Legal classes of radio service and battery shipping limits may also go to the jurisdiction ledger (C17).

**Where there is no ordering (preference).** E-readers (store and ecosystem decide, S39), item trackers (network decides, S32), smart rings and glasses (too new, no ladder in fetched sources), PDAs and organisers (collector view only, S33).

**How it appears as a modifier or ledger entry.** `price_band` (C4(d), USD, dated); tier entries (AnTuTu); `platform_tag` (Kimovil Range); `line` and `line_rank` (lines table); attributes `ip_rating`, `mil_std_810`, `capacity_wh`, `output_w`, `update_years` (dated). **Condition and carrier lock are modifiers (tags on a listing view), never ledger rows of items (C16).** The resale scales (Iosys S to J, S25; Swappa grades, S24) are recorded only as rows of a condition-scale reference table that holds scales and never items. OWNER: O-4.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: platform.** The maker sets compatibility and lock-in: a tracker joins one finding network (S32), an e-reader joins one bookstore (S39), a smartwatch pairs with iOS or Android (S14, S37), a satellite messenger needs a maker plan (S28, S10), a tablet accessory depends on the line (Pencil and keyboard support differ by iPad tier, S40). Within a platform the maker is also **identity** for named forms (iPhone, Galaxy Z, fēnix, Kindle Paperwhite are sought by name). Brands are never incidental here.

**Recommendation: three tables, no maker level.**

1. **Maker ledger (C1 core schema).** Core columns only: `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds` (kind ids), `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`. `maker_class` uses the C1 list: house or manufacture (Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi, Garmin, Amazon Kindle, Boox/ONYX); independent; microbrand; retail own-label; licensee or diffusion; supplier (Condor, Jinke, Sony, Hon Hai Precision behind e-reader brand names, S26); plus the review's proposed D classes (platform holder; OEM or ODM builder), OWNER: O-3. Examples seen in fetched pages: Apple, Samsung, Google, Motorola (S01); Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO (S15); Garmin, Fitbit, Amazfit, Oura (S07); Suunto, Polar (S13); Amazon Kindle, Kobo (Rakuten), Boox (ONYX), PocketBook, Bookeen, Hanvon (S26, S39, S02); Anker, Ugreen, INIU (S12); Garmin and SPOT (S28); Tile, Chipolo, Pebblebee, Samsung SmartTag (S32). **Lines and their ladders are not maker columns:** they sit in the lines table (C1) with `line_rank`. The "known-for named forms" are derived from the named-form ledger, not stored. Operating system and store leave the maker ledger for the compatibility ledger.
2. **Maker relations table (OEM-to-brand map).** The e-reader wiki records one device sold worldwide under several names (Papyre 6.1 SE, Astak EZ Reader Plus, ECO Reader, S26) and lists a separate manufacturer row behind the brand names (S26). Stored as relations (`maker_id`, `other_id`, `relation` = builds for / rebrand of, `scope`, `years`, `sources`), not as a column of the ledger. This is the review's proposal (X-1); OWNER: O-3.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds: canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states.** Slate before foldable before keyboard-bar in smartphones; e-reader before e-paper writing tablet; power bank before magnetic power bank; handheld radio before vehicle-mounted before base station. This puts the form most buyers mean first, and every source shelves the commonest form as the default (S01, S03, S39).

**Named forms: stored order follows C7: by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, ascending, then alphabetically where no year is attested.** (The earlier recommendation "by maker line, newest first" contradicted C7 and is withdrawn.) Newest-first, benchmark order and price order are generated views: retail trees sort by popularity or price (S04, S12, S17), which changes weekly, and benchmark ladders change monthly (S18). The PDA Museum offers chronology and size as its two stable orders (S33), and chronology is the one every maker line shares (a model has a release year). A `line_rank` step between maker and year (maker, line in rank order, year ascending) is the review's addition to C7 (X-6); OWNER: O-5. Tier and benchmark score are sortable fields in the tier entries, not stored order. Variants go by `variant_code` or year (C7).
