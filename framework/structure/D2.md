**One-line result.** Divide D2 by the role the device plays for its owner (eight sub-families: phones; two-way radios and pagers; tablets; e-readers and pocket information devices; wearables; location, navigation and safety devices; power banks; and parts and accessories made for one phone, tablet or wearable), then, where a sub-family needs it, by device class as retailers shelve it (leaf), then by the build or form class buyers compare inside (kind), then by maker line and model (named form). Operating system, store, finding network, radio service, subscription and charging standard are attributes that point into one compatibility ledger (C13). Market segment is never a level and never on a kind: price-defined labels become the domain price band (C4(d)), a shop's own labels become platform tags (C4(c)), benchmark ranks become dated tier entries on named forms (C4(a)), and maker ladders (iPad base, mini, Air, Pro; Garmin Entry, Advanced, Premium) become `line_rank` in the lines table (C1).

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, second branch).** "A **phone (mobile, landline, cordless or DECT), two-way radio, pager or other device for personal communication**; a **tablet, e-reader, electronic organiser or translator**; a **smartwatch, fitness tracker, smart ring or other smart jewellery, smart glasses, GPS unit (golf GPS units included), satellite messenger or tracker**; power banks? **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices** (Children's tablets are here.)" (v0.7; the closing note adds that an electronic writing tablet is D2, K-158.) Domain line: "phones and two-way radios, tablets, e-readers, smartwatches and wearables, GPS units and trackers, power banks."

**Rules and notes that already touch D2.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole), tried at step 8 before step 24: a phone case, screen protector, watch strap or charger made for one device follows that device. Cases and covers are parts only when made to hold or protect one object at a time. Sub-family D2.8 receives them (C5(d), CV-17).
- Step 1 note: physical access cards (SIM cards among them) are objects filed at step 19 (F5), reached before step 8 (S1b). K-28: a physical SIM card is F5 and an eSIM profile is part of its I3 plan; see section 9 item 9.
- Step 2 note (H2): devices that count steps or heart rate for fitness are D2; medical devices and monitors, hearing aids and medical alert bracelets are H2.
- Step 13 (B10), tried before step 24: equipment made for keeping, feeding, carrying or caring for animals, kept or wild, including animal identification microchips. A tracker made to attach to an animal is reached here first (section 9 item 6; K-103: a GPS pet tracker is B10).
- Step 16 note (A5): smartwatches and hybrid smartwatches are D2; smart displays and clock radios are D3. A watch whose main display is the time is A5 whatever sensors it has; a device whose main function is sensing or navigation is D2 (K-124).
- Step 17 note (worn on the body) and its A3 branch note: a device whose main function is computing, communication, sensing or media, such as a smartphone, smartwatch, fitness tracker, earbuds or e-reader, goes to step 24; smart rings are D2. Step 24 comes before step 26 (E2), so a GPS unit is reached as D2 before E2's "navigation gear".
- Step 24 first branch (D1): a device whose screen and keyboard can separate is D2 if sold as a tablet with an optional keyboard and D1 if the keyboard comes with it; cables with standard connectors, adapters and generic chargers (USB, GaN, Qi) are D1, while a cable with a proprietary connector or a charger made for one device follows that device (S3b, K-2, K-3; section 9 item 1); drawing tablets are D1.
- Step 24 connectivity note: a connected version of a household object is filed by its task at step 30; connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 24 branches D3, D4, D5: headphones and smart speakers are D3; cameras and drones made for filming are D4; consoles, handhelds and VR headsets are D5. A headset that encloses the eyes is D5; glasses with a see-through display, camera or speakers are D2 (K-153).
- BRIEF.md: tiers by brand or specification must be capturable (here: price band, benchmark entries, maker lines, spec attributes); condition (used, refurbished) and user (child, adapted) are modifiers, not levels; individual items are out (C16).

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D2** (never levels): brand and maker, price band and quality tier, era (feature phone, PDA, retro), user (child, adapted; C6 has no "senior" value, a large-button phone is `adapted`), setting (outdoor, marine, aviation), market (consumer, enterprise, carrier-locked), connectivity (Wi-Fi only, cellular, satellite), condition (new, refurbished, used), power source and battery, region. Two further D2-specific attributes behave like modifiers and are carried in the compatibility ledger (C13): operating system and store (iOS, Android, HarmonyOS, watchOS, Wear OS, Kindle, Kobo) and finding or service network (Find My, Find Hub, inReach subscription); a third, the radio service (FRS, GMRS, PMR446, Part 90, amateur, CB), joins them.

### Collectors and the resale market

- Resale sites keep device-type sections and grade condition, not kind (S24, S25). Swappa sells by exact model with listing conditions; Iosys uses six ranks from S (new or unused) to J (junk). Condition is a modifier; the Iosys scale is recorded only as a row of the C16 grading-scale registry (section 5).
- The PDA Museum (S33) is a collector site organised as guided tours "from old to new" and "from large to small" (chronology and size). The PalmPilot exhibit at the Computer History Museum (S34) tells the history by lineage (Psion, HP LX, Newton, EO, Zoomer, Palm).

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS/CN heading 8517 (S20).** Telephone sets "including smartphones and other telephones for cellular networks or for other wireless networks" are split into line telephone sets with cordless handsets (8517.11), smartphones (8517.13), other cellular or wireless telephones (8517.14) and other; network and data apparatus sit in 8517.6. Smartphone is its own subheading. A customs heading is evidence only, never a level.
- **IEC 60529 IP code (S22).** Two numerals, solids 0 to 6 and liquids 0 to 9; the recognised durability grade for phones, watches and trackers (also a GSMArena and Kimovil filter, S04, S17). An attribute with the standard named (C4(b)).
- **FAA PackSafe (S21).** Power banks and phone battery cases are "spare lithium batteries": carry-on only, 100 Wh per battery (101 to 160 Wh with airline approval). Watt-hours is the regulatory band for power banks; recorded as an attribute and as a C17 jurisdiction row (US, FAA PackSafe, 100 Wh carry-on limit).
- **IETF DULT (S23).** A working group standardising how location-tracking accessories and nearby devices handle unwanted tracking; it treats trackers as one accessory class across makers.
- **FCC radio services (S31, S29).** FRS, GMRS and Part 90 business radio are the legal classes of handheld two-way radio in the US; the UK and EU use PMR446. They are rows of the compatibility ledger and the jurisdiction ledger (C13, C17), never kinds or leaves (section 3, 4b and 7).

### Museums and reference works

- Wikipedia "Form factor (mobile phones)" (S05) divides phones into forms with one non-movable section (bar, keyboard bar, brick, slate, phablet, multi-screen, taco), forms with movable sections (clamshell or flip, foldable, slider, swivel, mixed) and wearables (smartwatch). It defines a phablet as a screen over 5.3 in (a size label, not a kind).
- Wikipedia "Electronic organizer" (S35) separates the calculator-sized organiser from the PDA, palmtop and smartphone, and groups handheld devices as organiser, e-reader, handheld game console, handheld PC, mobile phone (camera, feature, smartphone, foldable) and tablet (detachable, phablet).

### Where sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

- **Agree:** phones, tablets and wearables are separate shelves in every retailer tree (S01, S07, S11, S36, S24); e-readers are their own category where a shop has enough of them (S02, S03, S26); power banks are a power accessory that sits with chargers (S12, S13, S15); satellite messengers, handheld GPS and radios are outdoor-specialist devices that retailers separate from phones (S09, S10, S28, S29, S30).
- **Split: where power banks live.** With phone accessories (Best Buy S01; idealo S13), with mobile chargers inside Mobiltelefone (Geizhals S12), with chargers and cables beside phones in a maker catalogue (Xiaomi S15), or in the household-appliance tree away from phones (Kakaku S36). The placement key settles it for this taxonomy (D2).
- **Split: smartwatch against sport watch against fitness tracker.** Geizhals merges smartwatches and sport watches (S12); Kakaku merges smartwatch and smart band as one category with a type spec (S37); Best Buy separates Smartwatches from Fitness Trackers (S07); idealo has a Sportuhr chip (S13); Garmin separates by activity (S08).
- **Split: where tablets live.** Swappa shelves Tablets and iPads beside Phones and Watches (S24), while Kakaku files tablets and e-readers under PC (S02). The placement key puts both in D2.
- **Split: tablet against e-reader against e-ink tablet.** Croma treats tablet, e-reader and digital pad as three types (S03); MobileRead has e-readers only as reading devices but notes tablets and phones as alternative readers (S27); an Android e-ink tablet (Boox) is both (S39). This memo resolves it by what the device is sold for (read, or take notes with a stylus), with size, panel and platform as attributes (section 4b).
- **Split: tier.** Retailers and rankers use "flagship, mid-range, budget" or "high-end, mid-range, low-end" but disagree on what sets the band: price (Alibaba S06, Kimovil price bands S17), benchmark score within a market segment (AnTuTu S18), or a maker's own ladder (iPad base, mini, Air, Pro S40; Garmin Entry, Advanced, Premium S09). Section 5 stores each in the place C4 gives it.
- **Split: where the objects belong.** Retail trees hold objects that the placement key sends elsewhere: Kakaku's PC-peripheral list holds e-readers beside digital pens, USB hubs and card readers (S02); Geizhals holds radio scanners and hobby radios with phones (S11); Best Buy holds wristwatches beside smartwatches (S07). These are filed by the key, and the mismatches are listed in section 9.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

D2 has no single first axis; what a buyer decides first changes with the kind of device. The evidence, by sub-family:

| Device | What buyers decide first | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Smartphones | The platform (iOS or Android) and how the phone is bought (unlocked, prepaid, on a plan, refurbished), then size, then price | Best Buy opens with Unlocked, Apple iPhone, Samsung Galaxy, Google Pixel, Motorola, with plans, prepaid, refurbished [S01] and its guide says first decide prepaid or contract, then brand [S01]; Kakaku offers "search by installed OS" [S36]; Geizhals presets are Android 17, Android 16, Featurephones, then maker [S12]; GSMArena opens with brand, year, availability and price [S04]; Kimovil with Range (low, mid, high) and price bands [S17] |
| Tablets | Platform and maker line, then screen size | Croma sorts by size bands [S03]; the Apple lineup is a maker ladder (base, mini, Air, Pro) [S40]; GSMArena and AnTuTu treat tablets as a separate chart [S04, S18] |
| E-readers | The bookstore and its library (Kindle, Kobo, any Android store), then screen size, colour, buttons, stylus, waterproofing | "The real decision is about which bookstore you want to live in" [S39]; Kakaku filters by screen size, light, waterproofing [S02]; MobileRead matrices run by screen size and screen technology [S26] |
| Smartwatches and bands | Which phone it must pair with (iOS, Android), standalone or second screen, then purpose (running, golf, diving) and battery | idealo's guide: standalone or second screen, then operating system, then functions [S14]; Geizhals and Kakaku filter by iOS/Android compatibility [S12, S37]; Garmin splits by activity and by case size, battery days, GPS, display and connectivity [S08] |

| Satellite messengers and beacons | Subscription or none (beacon versus messenger), then global coverage | REI: "choose a PLB if you want to avoid subscription fees ... choose a satellite messenger if you also want to send messages" [S28] |

| Item trackers | The finding network the tracker joins, which must match the owner's phone | "The tracker you pick locks you into one of several separate networks" [S32]; Xiaomi's "Smart Tags" shelf sits in Wearables [S15] |

**Reading.** The only axis every retail tree shares at the top is the device type, which follows the role the device plays (talk, read, wear, find, power). Platform, finding network, subscription and radio service decide the purchase but vary within a role, and a platform split would cut across every role (an iPhone, an iPad, an Apple Watch and an AirTag are four roles in one ecosystem). So the structure divides by role and carries platform, finding network and radio service as attributes that point into the compatibility ledger.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): role, then device class where needed, then build or form class, then maker line and model

- **Sub-family** (axis: the role the device plays for its owner): D2.1 Phones; D2.2 Two-way radios and pagers; D2.3 Tablets; D2.4 E-readers and pocket information devices; D2.5 Wearables; D2.6 Location, navigation and safety devices; D2.7 Power banks; D2.8 Parts and accessories made for one phone, tablet or wearable (the only sub-family exempt from the role axis, C5(d)).
- **Leaf** (axis, stated per sub-family under C5(c)): device class as retailers shelve it. ## 5. Quality and tier

**Is there a recognised tier ordering? Yes, in three different forms, and C4 gives each its own place.** No `market_tier` field exists; no tier sits on a kind (C4(a)).

1. **Market segment (phones, tablets): three sources, three rules.** Flagship, mid-range and budget are not defined by a standard. Each source's scale is stored where its type belongs:
   - **Price-defined labels are the price band, not a tier.** Alibaba's guide defines budget under 200 USD, mid-range 300 to 500, premium over 800, leaving gaps [S06]. The price band is the one domain-wide USD scheme of C4(d), computed from a dated, sourced list price on the named form. Labels such as "budget" or "premium" may appear in a generated view over the band. Kimovil's price bands [S17] are the same type.
   - **A shop's own label is a platform tag (C4(c)).** Kimovil's Range (Low, Mid, High-end) [S17] is stored as a dated `platform_tag` with the platform named, not as a tier.
   - **A third-party measured score is a dated tier entry on the named form (C4(a); class ii of C4(c)).** AnTuTu's monthly charts for flagship, mid-range and tablets [S18] and its ranking page [S19] give an average benchmark score. Scheme registry row: `scheme_id`, name "AnTuTu 12 average score, China monthly chart", `owner` AnTuTu, `scale_class` (ii) third-party measured score, `steps` numeric, `direction` higher is better, `test_version` AnTuTu 12, `stale_after` one month, `kinds_served` the smartphone and tablet kinds, `sources`. Entry: `subject_id` and `subject_type` (named form), `scheme_id`, `value` (score), `rank`, `direction`, `scope` (the publisher's chart segment, flagship, mid-range or tablet, and the China data of S18; C4(a) has no region column, so the region is carried in the scheme name and `scope`), `test_version`, `stale_after`, `source_url`, `date`, and the computed `disputed` flag. The chart segment is the publisher's assignment and is recorded as part of the entry, never as a level. The benchmark changes every month, so scores are dated measurements, not permanent order. One fetched source admits the scheme (C4(e), CV-10); Kimovil shows an AnTuTu score filter [S17] but reproduces the same benchmark, and an independent second source from another region would only add weight (it feeds `disputed` and the scheme's weight in views), never decide entry. Other third-party lists and reviewer grades enter the same way, one source each (CV-12).
2. **Maker line ladders (everywhere).** The maker orders its own range: iPad base, iPad mini, iPad Air, iPad Pro, with chip, RAM, display and Pencil support differing [S40]; Garmin handhelds Entry, Advanced, Premium [S09]; Garmin watches fēnix, Forerunner, Venu, vívoactive, vívosmart, Instinct [S08]. Stored as rows of the C1 lines table (`line_id`, `maker_id`, `name`, `kinds` spanned, `line_rank`, `years`, `sources`), with `line_rank` true only inside one maker; the ladder order is a generated view (C7). A maker's own range level may also be stored as tier entries with `scheme` = the maker's name (C4(c) class iv, CV-12), and a ladder covering the variants of one named form as a tier entry on a variant (C4(b)(i), CV-11). A named form does not inherit a maker's tier unless an entry says so (C4(a)).
3. **Standards and regulatory grades (attributes with the standard named, C4(b)).** `ip_rating` (IEC 60529) and `standard_met` (MIL-STD-810) for durability [S22, S04]; `capacity_wh` and `output_w` for power banks, with the 100 Wh air-travel limit as a fact about the standard [S21]; `radio_service` and its power cap (FRS 2 W) [S29, S31], pointing to the compatibility ledger; `update_years` (the maker's software-update promise, dated; Apple 6 to 7 years, Samsung 4 OS updates for Galaxy S and Z, S06). Legal classes of radio service and battery shipping limits go to the jurisdiction ledger (C17, CV-35); the operating system's dated `update_promise` sits on its C13 row.

**Where there is no ordering (preference).** E-readers (store and ecosystem decide, S39), item trackers (network decides, S32), smart rings and glasses (too new, no ladder in fetched sources), PDAs and organisers (collector view only, S33).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: platform.** The maker sets compatibility and lock-in: a tracker joins one finding network (S32), an e-reader joins one bookstore (S39), a smartwatch pairs with iOS or Android (S14, S37), a satellite messenger needs a maker plan (S28, S10), a tablet accessory depends on the line (Pencil and keyboard support differ by iPad tier, S40). Within a platform the maker is also **identity** for named forms (iPhone, Galaxy Z, fēnix, Kindle Paperwhite are sought by name). Brands are never incidental here.

**Recommendation: three tables, no maker level.**

1. **Maker ledger (C1).** One row per maker in the domain-wide ledger. Columns: `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class` (multi-valued), `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `licensor_id`, `authority_id`, `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`. `known_for_kinds` is not stored: it is derived from the named-form ledger (CV-1). `maker_class` takes the C1 values: house or manufacture (Apple, Samsung, Xiaomi, Garmin, Amazon Kindle, Boox/ONYX); independent; microbrand; retail own-label; licensee or diffusion; supplier (Condor, Jinke, Sony, Hon Hai Precision behind e-reader brand names, S26; chip, panel and contract builders); platform holder (CV-2; the platforms themselves are C13 rows that link to their owner, never a maker column). A contract or original-design builder is the class supplier plus an OEM relation in the relations table (CV-3), not a class of its own. Examples seen in fetched pages: Apple, Samsung, Google, Motorola (S01); Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO (S15); Garmin, Fitbit, Amazfit, Oura (S07); Suunto, Polar (S13); Amazon Kindle, Kobo (Rakuten), Boox (ONYX), PocketBook, Bookeen, Hanvon (S26, S39, S02); Anker, Ugreen, INIU (S12); Garmin and SPOT (S28); Tile, Chipolo, Pebblebee, Samsung SmartTag (S32). One fetched page admits a maker and the ledger has no cap: it is seeded from existing catalogues first (the GSMArena brand list, well over a hundred makers, S04; the MobileRead manufacturer rows, S26) and grows with the attested evidence (CV-5); identity and platform makers come first only as a harvest order. **Lines and their ladders are not maker columns:** they sit in the lines table (C1) with `line_rank`. Operating system and store leave the maker ledger for the compatibility ledger.
2. **Maker-relations table (C1).** The e-reader wiki records one device sold worldwide under several names (Papyre 6.1 SE, Astak EZ Reader Plus, ECO Reader, S26) and lists a separate manufacturer row behind the brand names (S26). Stored as relations (`relation_id`, `maker_id`, `other_id`, `relation`, `scope`, `years`, `sources`) with the C1 relation values: OEM (builds for), supplies chip or panel to, board partner of, licensed by, rebrand of (CV-3). The standing school table (C1) holds traditions of makers; no D2 tradition is attested yet, and a row is added when a fetched page names one.
3. **Compatibility ledger (C13, one ledger for the domain).** Table `platform`: `platform_id`, `name`, `owner` (maker or standards body), `platform_type`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `region`, `introduced`, `status`, `sources`, with the dated `update_promise` and `subscription` extensions for operating systems and services; table `compatibility_relation` (`from_id`, `relation`, `to_id`, `sources`). `platform_type` values used in D2: operating system, app or book store, finding network, satellite service, radio service, charging standard. Rows seen in D2: iOS, Android, HarmonyOS, KaiOS, watchOS, Wear OS, Fitbit OS, Zepp OS (operating systems; S04, S38), Kindle store, Kobo store (app or book store; S39), Find My, Find Hub, SmartThings Find, Tile network (finding networks; S32), inReach plan (satellite service; S10, S28), FRS, GMRS, PMR446, Part 90 business (radio services; S29, S31; `standard_ref` citation of the FCC rule number †), MagSafe (charging standard; S13; a Qi2 row †). An app ecosystem that controls devices is a row with `kinds_served`; the app itself (a watch companion app, a finding app) is I1 (CV-30, R-14). Cross-network rules are being standardised (IETF DULT, S23). Device `platform` is an attribute on named forms pointing into this ledger. A platform is never a level (Appendix A, C13).

**Alias and homonym table (C9).** English labels with a native-script label where one is attested. Rows: walkie-talkie = handheld two-way radio (alias); smart band = fitness tracker = activity tracker = 活動量計 (S37, S36) → kind 25; sport watch = GPS sports watch → kind 22 (alias; D-10); Handy (de, mobile phone) → two targets, bar smartphone (kind 1) and bar feature phone (kind 5); スマホ = smartphone; 電子辞書 = electronic dictionary → kind 20; モバイルバッテリー = Powerbank = power bank → kind 37 (S36, S13); Pad = tablet (alias; S18, S19); organizer = organiser (spelling); phablet = a size label, no target (screen_size attribute; S05); **slate** = bar smartphone (S05, form) and slate tablet (kind 15) — homonym; **tracker** = fitness tracker (kind 25), Bluetooth item tracker (kind 35), GPS or cellular tracker (kind 36) — homonym; Funkgerät (de) → kinds 11 to 13 (S11, a retail umbrella). ## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds: canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states.** Slate before foldable before keyboard-bar in smartphones; e-reader before e-paper writing tablet; power bank before magnetic power bank; handheld radio before vehicle-mounted before base station. This puts the form most buyers mean first, and every source shelves the commonest form as the default (S01, S03, S39).

**Named forms: stored order follows C7 and CV-21: non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, ascending, then alphabetically.** Nearly every D2 named form is maker-bound. Newest-first, the `line_rank` ladder, benchmark order and price order are generated views, not stored order: retail trees sort by popularity or price (S04, S12, S17), which changes weekly, and benchmark ladders change monthly (S18). The PDA Museum offers chronology and size as its two stable orders (S33), and chronology is the one every maker line shares (a model has a release year). Tier and benchmark score are sortable fields in the tier entries, not stored order. Variants go by `variant_code` or year (C7).
