# D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md. Owner emphasis for D2 (batch pointers): phones, tablets, e-readers, wearables, GPS units and trackers; ecosystems as platform makers; spec tiers (flagship, mid, budget) and their sources. Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_D2_sources.tsv; the short keys S01 to S40 below point to them (full URLs in the key table of section 1). Call count: 12 searches, 40 fetches (cap reached). One host (www.zol.com.cn) returned news headlines but no category tree and is logged in verify/BLOCKED_HOSTS.txt; JD.com and Taobao were not retried because earlier batches logged them as JavaScript-only, so Chinese evidence comes from a Chinese maker catalogue (S15), a Chinese benchmark ladder (S18, S19) and the Chinese-market wholesale view (S06), not from a Chinese retailer tree.*

**One-line result.** Divide D2 by the role the device plays for its owner (seven sub-families: phones; two-way radios and pagers; tablets; e-readers and pocket information devices; wearables; location, navigation and safety devices; power banks), then by device class as retailers shelve it (leaf), then by the build or form class buyers compare inside (kind), then by maker line and model (named form). Carry operating system, store, finding network and subscription as platform attributes in an ecosystem ledger; carry market tier (flagship, mid-range, budget) as a dated, sourced modifier with its scale named; carry makers in a maker ledger that records each maker's line ladder (iPad base, mini, Air, Pro; Garmin Entry, Advanced, Premium).

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, second branch).** "A **phone, two-way radio, pager or other device for personal communication**; a **tablet, e-reader, electronic organiser or translator**; a **smartwatch, fitness tracker, smart ring or other smart jewellery, smart glasses, GPS unit, satellite messenger or tracker**; power banks? **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices** (Children's tablets are here.)" Domain line: "phones and two-way radios, tablets, e-readers, smartwatches and wearables, GPS units and trackers, power banks."

**Rules and notes that already touch D2.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): a phone case, screen protector or watch strap made for one device follows that device. Cases and covers are parts only when made to hold or protect one object at a time.
- Step 2 note (H2): devices that count steps or heart rate for fitness are D2; medical devices and monitors, hearing aids and medical alert bracelets are H2.
- Step 16 note (A5): smartwatches and hybrid smartwatches are D2; smart displays and clock radios are D3.
- Step 17 note (worn on the body) and its A3 branch note: a device whose main function is computing, communication, sensing or media, such as a smartphone, smartwatch, fitness tracker, earbuds or e-reader, goes to step 24; smart rings are D2. Step 24 comes before step 26 (E2), so a GPS unit is reached as D2 before E2's "navigation gear".
- Step 24 first branch (D1): a device whose screen and keyboard can separate is D2 if sold as a tablet with an optional keyboard and D1 if the keyboard comes with it; cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices are D1 whatever device they serve; drawing tablets are D1.
- Step 24 connectivity note: a connected version of a household object is filed by its task at step 30; connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 24 branches D3, D4, D5: headphones and smart speakers are D3; cameras and drones made for filming are D4; consoles, handhelds and VR headsets are D5.
- BRIEF.md: tiers by brand or specification must be capturable (here: phone market tiers, maker lines); condition (used, refurbished) and user (child, senior) are modifiers, not levels.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D2** (never levels): brand and maker, price band and quality tier, era (feature phone, PDA, retro), user (child, senior, adapted), setting (outdoor, marine, aviation), market (consumer, enterprise, carrier-locked), connectivity (Wi-Fi only, cellular, satellite), condition (new, refurbished, used), power source and battery. Two further D2-specific attributes behave like modifiers: operating system and store (iOS, Android, HarmonyOS, watchOS, Wear OS, Kindle, Kobo) and finding or service network (Find My, Find Hub, inReach subscription).

### Collectors and the resale market

- Resale sites keep device-type sections and grade condition, not kind (S24, S25). Swappa sells by exact model with listing conditions; Iosys uses six ranks from S (new or unused) to J (junk).
- The PDA Museum (S33) is a collector site organised as guided tours "from old to new" and "from large to small" (chronology and size). The PalmPilot exhibit at the Computer History Museum (S34) tells the history by lineage (Psion, HP LX, Newton, EO, Zoomer, Palm).

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS/CN heading 8517 (S20).** Telephone sets "including smartphones and other telephones for cellular networks or for other wireless networks" are split into line telephone sets with cordless handsets (8517.11), smartphones (8517.13), other cellular or wireless telephones (8517.14) and other; network and data apparatus sit in 8517.6. Smartphone is its own subheading.
- **IEC 60529 IP code (S22).** Two numerals, solids 0 to 6 and liquids 0 to 9; the recognised durability grade for phones, watches and trackers (also a GSMArena and Kimovil filter, S04, S17).
- **FAA PackSafe (S21).** Power banks and phone battery cases are "spare lithium batteries": carry-on only, 100 Wh per battery (101 to 160 Wh with airline approval). Watt-hours is the regulatory band for power banks.
- **IETF DULT (S23).** A working group standardising how location-tracking accessories and nearby devices handle unwanted tracking; it treats trackers as one accessory class across makers.
- **FCC radio services (S31, S29).** FRS, GMRS and Part 90 business radio are the legal classes of handheld two-way radio in the US; the UK and EU use PMR446.

### Museums and reference works

- Wikipedia "Form factor (mobile phones)" (S05) divides phones into forms with one non-movable section (bar, keyboard bar, brick, slate, phablet, multi-screen, taco), forms with movable sections (clamshell or flip, foldable, slider, swivel, mixed) and wearables (smartwatch). It defines a phablet as a screen over 5.3 in.
- Wikipedia "Electronic organizer" (S35) separates the calculator-sized organiser from the PDA, palmtop and smartphone, and groups handheld devices as organiser, e-reader, handheld game console, handheld PC, mobile phone (camera, feature, smartphone, foldable) and tablet (detachable, phablet).
- Wikipedia "Smartwatch" (S38) groups wearables as fitness tracker (smart band), digital wristwatch (calculator watch, smartwatch, sportwatch), smart glasses and smart ring, and lists watch operating systems (watchOS, Wear OS, Fitbit OS, Zepp OS, HarmonyOS).

### Where sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

- **Agree:** phones, tablets and wearables are separate shelves in every retailer tree (S01, S07, S11, S36, S24); e-readers are their own category where a shop has enough of them (S02, S03, S26); power banks are a power accessory that sits with chargers (S12, S13, S15); satellite messengers, handheld GPS and radios are outdoor-specialist devices that retailers separate from phones (S09, S10, S28, S29, S30).
- **Split: where power banks live.** With phone accessories (Best Buy S01; idealo S13), with mobile chargers inside Mobiltelefone (Geizhals S12), with chargers and cables beside phones in a maker catalogue (Xiaomi S15), or in the household-appliance tree away from phones (Kakaku S36). The placement key settles it for this taxonomy (D2).
- **Split: smartwatch against sport watch against fitness tracker.** Geizhals merges smartwatches and sport watches (S12); Kakaku merges smartwatch and smart band as one category with a type spec (S37); Best Buy separates Smartwatches from Fitness Trackers (S07); idealo has a Sportuhr chip (S13); Garmin separates by activity (S08).
- **Split: where tablets live.** Swappa shelves Tablets and iPads beside Phones and Watches (S24), while Kakaku files tablets and e-readers under PC (S02). The placement key puts both in D2.
- **Split: tablet against e-reader against e-ink tablet.** Croma treats tablet, e-reader and digital pad as three types (S03); MobileRead has e-readers only as reading devices but notes tablets and phones as alternative readers (S27); an Android e-ink tablet (Boox) is both (S39).
- **Split: tier.** Retailers and rankers use "flagship, mid-range, budget" or "high-end, mid-range, low-end" but disagree on what sets the band: price (Alibaba S06, Kimovil price bands S17), benchmark score within a market segment (AnTuTu S18), or a maker's own ladder (iPad base, mini, Air, Pro S40; Garmin Entry, Advanced, Premium S09).
- **Split: where the objects belong.** Retail trees hold objects that the placement key sends elsewhere: Kakaku's PC-peripheral list holds e-readers beside digital pens, USB hubs and card readers (S02); Geizhals holds radio scanners and hobby radios with phones (S11); Best Buy holds wristwatches beside smartwatches (S07). These are filed by the key, and the mismatches are listed in section 9.

**Reading.** The only axis every retail tree shares at the top is the device type, which follows the role the device plays (talk, read, wear, find, power). Platform, finding network, subscription and radio service decide the purchase but vary within a role, and a platform split would cut across every role (an iPhone, an iPad, an Apple Watch and an AirTag are four roles in one ecosystem). So the structure divides by role and carries platform as an attribute and an ecosystem ledger.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): role, then device class, then build or form class, then maker line and model

- **Sub-family** (axis: the role the device plays for its owner): D2.1 Phones; D2.2 Two-way radios and pagers; D2.3 Tablets; D2.4 E-readers and pocket information devices; D2.5 Wearables; D2.6 Location, navigation and safety devices; D2.7 Power banks.
- **Leaf** (axis: device class as retailers shelve it): for example D2.1.1 Smartphones, D2.1.2 Feature phones; D2.5.1 Wrist devices, D2.5.2 Finger and face devices.
- **Kind** (axis: the build or form class buyers compare inside): bar, flip and book smartphones; smart band against smartwatch; satellite messenger against beacon.
- **Named form** (axis: maker line and model): iPhone 17 Pro, Galaxy Z Flip, Kindle Paperwhite, fēnix 8, AirTag.

Example kinds (15): bar smartphone, flip foldable smartphone, book foldable smartphone, rugged smartphone, bar feature phone, slate tablet, e-paper e-reader, e-ink Android tablet, PDA, smartwatch, smart band, smart ring, handheld hiking GPS, satellite messenger, Bluetooth item tracker. The full worked tree follows below.

**Where it breaks.**
1. **Boox-type e-ink Android tablet** [S39]: a tablet by operating system, an e-reader by screen. Placed in D2.4 by screen and purpose; cross-listed as a view.
2. **Apple Watch Ultra 3 with cellular and satellite emergency SOS** [S37] and **Garmin D2 Mach 2 Pro, "aviator smartwatch with satellite and LTE connectivity"** [S10]: wrist devices that perform a satellite-messenger job. They stay smartwatches (connectivity is a modifier, step 24 note); satellite capability becomes an attribute.
3. **Garmin Rino 700/750**, a two-way radio and GPS handheld in one case [S09]: D2.2 or D2.6. It needs a one-line rule (section 9 and open question 5).
4. **GPS sports watch against smartwatch**: Garmin, Wikipedia and idealo separate them [S08, S38, S13]; Geizhals and Kakaku merge them [S12, S37]. Provisional kind, owner to confirm.

### Candidate B: where on the body or in the hand the device is carried

- **Sub-family** (axis: carry position): handheld, wrist, finger, face, pocket or bag (trackers, power banks), vehicle.
- **Leaf and kind**: by device class then form.
- Example kinds (12): bar smartphone, slate tablet, e-reader, handheld GPS, handheld radio (handheld); smartwatch, smart band (wrist); smart ring (finger); smart glasses (face); Bluetooth tracker, power bank, satellite messenger (pocket or bag).

**Where it breaks.** The Wikipedia pages give wearables their own heading (S05, S38), but no retailer shelves goods by carry position (S01, S07, S11, S36). "Handheld" would hold phones, tablets, e-readers, GPS and radios, nearly the whole category. Power banks, trackers and satellite messengers have no stable position. Rejected for navigation; usable as a generated view ("everything worn").

### Candidate C: connectivity and network class first

- **Sub-family** (axis: the network the device depends on): cellular handsets; licensed or licence-free radio; satellite; short-range (Bluetooth, Wi-Fi) companions; no network (power banks).
- Example kinds (12): smartphone, feature phone, satellite phone, FRS radio, GMRS radio, PMR446 radio, satellite messenger, personal locator beacon, Bluetooth item tracker, standalone eSIM smartwatch, Wi-Fi e-reader, power bank.

**Where it breaks.** This is how the tariff and the radio regulator see D2 (HS 8517.13, 8517.14 [S20]; FRS, GMRS, Part 90 [S31]; the idealo standalone-or-second-screen question [S14]), but one product spans classes: the same tablet or e-reader is sold in Wi-Fi and cellular versions (Kakaku has a network-type spec, S02); a Garmin handheld may carry radio, GPS and satellite (S09); a smartwatch may or may not hold an eSIM (S14). Connectivity is a modifier in Appendix A, so using it as a level conflicts with the framework. Rejected; keep as an attribute and a generated view.

## 4. Depth and size

**Generic kinds.** The worked tree holds 48 kinds; 6 of them have no fetched attestation yet. After the gaps are closed and the owner has ruled on the provisional kinds (foldables, rugged, sport watch, hobby radio, bike and golf computers), the likely range is **55 to 75 kinds**. This is small beside D1 because many D2 distinctions (storage size, colour, carrier lock, OS version) are attributes of one model.

**Named forms (rough, my estimate).**
- Phones: the largest block. GSMArena's brand filter lists well over a hundred makers (S04), and a phone is announced every few days by a dozen groups; the full historic count is in the tens of thousands, the current catalogue several thousand model names. Geizhals separates the model (Apple iPhone 17 Pro) from its colour and storage variants (256GB tiefblau) (S11), which is the grain recommended here (S12 shows the same split): model and series are named forms, storage and colour variants are ledger rows.
- Wearables: Kakaku's wearable list shows several hundred current products across smartwatches and smart bands (S37), plus the historic lines on Wikipedia (S38). Estimate 1,000 to 2,000 named forms.
- Tablets: a few hundred; the iPad lineup is four lines (S40); AnTuTu's tablet chart ranks the top ten each month (S18).
- E-readers: 11 current products on Kakaku (S02), 336 devices ever on MobileRead (S27); estimate 300 to 500.
- GPS, radios, trackers, satellite devices, power banks: tens to a few hundred each.
- Overall estimate: 8,000 to 20,000 named forms if history is included, 2,000 to 4,000 if only devices sold in the last five years.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Is there a recognised tier ordering? Yes, in three different forms.**

1. **Market tier, price-based (phones, tablets).** Flagship, mid-range and budget are not defined by a standard. Three sources use different rules: Alibaba's guide uses price (budget under 200 USD, mid-range 300 to 500, premium over 800, leaving gaps) [S06]; Kimovil assigns a Range of Low, Mid or High-end and offers price bands [S17]; AnTuTu publishes separate monthly charts for flagship, mid-range and tablets on average benchmark scores (China data) [S18] and a global ranking [S19]. Type: **spec-ranked** for performance order within a band (benchmark score), with the **band edges set by price and by publisher**. Recommendation: store `market_tier` (flagship, upper mid, mid, entry) as a modifier on named forms, with the scale name (price band, AnTuTu segment, Kimovil range), the source URL and the date, never as a level.
2. **Maker line ladders (everywhere).** The maker orders its own range: iPad base, iPad mini, iPad Air, iPad Pro, with chip, RAM, display and Pencil support differing [S40]; Garmin handhelds Entry, Advanced, Premium [S09]; Garmin watches fēnix, Forerunner, Venu, vívoactive, vívosmart, Instinct [S08]. Type: **maker-ranked**, true only inside one maker. Store as `line` and `line_rank` on the named form.
3. **Standards and regulatory grades (attributes).** IP code (IEC 60529) and MIL-STD-810 for durability [S22, S04]; watt-hours for power banks (100 Wh air-travel limit) [S21]; radio service class and power cap for radios (FRS 2 W) [S29, S31]; software-update promises as a tier signal in guides (Apple 6 to 7 years, Samsung 4 OS updates for Galaxy S and Z, S06). Type: **spec-ranked** or **regulatory**; stored as attributes with the standard named.

**Where there is no ordering (preference).** E-readers (store and ecosystem decide, S39), item trackers (network decides, S32), smart rings and glasses (too new, no ladder in fetched sources), PDAs and organisers (collector view only, S33).

**How it appears as a modifier.** `market_tier` with scale, source and date; `line` and `line_rank`; `ip_rating`; `capacity_wh` and `output_w`; `update_years` (dated). Condition grades from resale (S to J at Iosys, S25) are a separate `condition` modifier. The benchmark itself changes every month, so scores are stored as dated measurements, not as permanent order.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: platform.** The maker sets compatibility and lock-in: a tracker joins one finding network (S32), an e-reader joins one bookstore (S39), a smartwatch pairs with iOS or Android (S14, S37), a satellite messenger needs a maker plan (S28, S10), a tablet accessory depends on the line (Pencil and keyboard support differ by iPad tier, S40). Within a platform the maker is also **identity** for named forms (iPhone, Galaxy Z, fēnix, Kindle Paperwhite are sought by name). Brands are never incidental here.

**Recommendation: two ledgers, no maker level.**
1. **Maker ledger.** Rows: maker, country, device classes made, lines with their tier ladder (S09, S40), OS or store, known-for kinds, price band, era (founded, exits), attestation. Examples seen in fetched pages: Apple, Samsung, Google, Motorola (S01); Xiaomi, Redmi, POCO (S15); Garmin, Fitbit, Amazfit, Oura (S07); Suunto, Polar (S13); Amazon Kindle, Kobo (Rakuten), Boox (ONYX), PocketBook, Bookeen, Hanvon (S26, S39, S02); Anker, Ugreen, INIU (S12); Garmin and SPOT (S28); Tile, Chipolo, Pebblebee, Samsung SmartTag (S32). Add an **OEM-to-brand map** because the e-reader wiki records one device sold worldwide under several names (Papyre 6.1 SE, Astak EZ Reader Plus, ECO Reader, S26) and lists a separate manufacturer row (Condor, Jinke, Sony, Hon Hai Precision) behind the brand names, S26.
2. **Ecosystem ledger.** Rows: platform (iOS, Android, HarmonyOS, KaiOS, watchOS, Wear OS, Fitbit OS, Zepp OS, Kindle store, Kobo store, Find My, Find Hub, SmartThings Find, Tile network, inReach plan), owning maker, device classes that join it, compatibility rules (watch to phone, tracker to phone, accessory to line), service or subscription cost, software-update promise (dated, S06), source. OS lists are in S04 and S38; tracker networks in S32; the DULT work shows cross-network rules are being standardised (S23).

Device `platform` is an attribute on named forms that points into the ecosystem ledger. A platform split is not a level (Appendix A: brand and maker never divide inner levels).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds: canonical form first, then variants.** Slate before foldable before keyboard-bar; e-paper reader before large document reader before e-ink tablet before LCD reader; slab power bank before magnetic. This puts the form most buyers mean first, and every source shelves the commonest form as the default (S01, S03, S39).

**Named forms: by maker line, then chronology within the line (newest first for current lines).** Reason: the alternatives are unstable or lose information. Retail trees sort by popularity or price (S04, S12, S17), which changes weekly; benchmark ladders change monthly (S18); price tier is a dated modifier. The PDA Museum offers chronology and size as its two stable orders (S33), and chronology is the one every maker line shares (a model has a release year). Tier and benchmark score become sortable fields in the tier ledger, not stored order.

## 9. Boundaries

Closest cross-boundary objects, with the precedence sentence for each neighbour. Items marked (S3) follow the parts-follow-the-whole rule.

1. **D1 Computers & Components.** *A tablet sold with an optional keyboard is D2 and a device whose keyboard comes with it is D1; drawing tablets, USB, HDMI and charging cables, adapters, laptop batteries and laptop AC adapters are D1; a phone case, phone screen protector, tablet case and watch strap follow their device to D2 (S3).* The wall charger and the USB-C GaN charger are not settled (open question 7). 2. **A5 Clocks & Watches and A3 Jewellery.** *A smartwatch, hybrid smartwatch or fitness tracker is D2 whatever it looks like; a watch that only syncs its time (G-Shock GBD, GW-B5600) stays A5; a smart ring is D2 and a plain ring is A3; a watch strap follows the watch (S3).* Best Buy lists Wristwatches beside smartwatches (S07). Sensor-led outdoor watches with altimeter, barometer and compass need a ruling (A5 memo, border case 2).
3. **D3 Audio & Video.** *Headphones, earbuds (including translating earbuds), speakers and smart speakers are D3 even when sold by a phone maker or listed in a phone tree; a two-way radio is D2 and a broadcast receiver or radio is D3; a radio scanner is D3.* Xiaomi lists earbuds in its Wearables group and speakers elsewhere (S15); Geizhals lists Funkscanner with hobby radio under Telefon (S11). ## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** Seven sub-families by the role the device plays (D2.1 Phones; D2.2 Two-way radios and pagers; D2.3 Tablets; D2.4 E-readers and pocket information devices; D2.5 Wearables; D2.6 Location, navigation and safety devices; D2.7 Power banks); leaves by device class as retailers shelve it; kinds by the build or form class buyers compare inside; named forms by maker line and model, with storage, colour and regional variants as ledger rows. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Radios, trackers and power banks may stop at kind. Platform, finding network, subscription, radio service, IP rating, battery size and connectivity live as attributes; market tier lives in a tier ledger with the scale named; makers and ecosystems live in two ledgers.

**Summary answers.** Choice level: kind at build or form class; named form at maker line and model. Tier type: spec-ranked for phones and tablets (benchmark within a price-set band) and power banks, maker-ranked inside lines, preference for e-readers and trackers. Maker centrality: platform, with identity at named-form level; two ledgers.

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. **Sub-family axis.** Accept role (recommended) against carry position (Candidate B) or connectivity (Candidate C).
2. **Smartphone kinds.** Foldables as two kinds (flip, book) or one attribute; rugged as a kind or a tag; keyboard-bar and historic slider and swivel forms as kinds or as era tags.
3. **Market tier.** Which scale or scales to store (price band, AnTuTu segment, Kimovil range), how to date them, and whether third-party benchmark charts (AnTuTu, China-only for the monthly tier charts) may be recorded as attributed, dated measurements.
4. **Wrist devices.** GPS sports watch as a kind or an attribute; hybrid smartwatch and screenless band as kinds; smart band against fitness tracker as one kind with two names.
5. **Hybrid objects.** Garmin Rino (radio plus GPS), smartwatches with satellite SOS, e-ink Android tablets, the Nokia N-Gage: confirm the rules in section 9 (primary job decides, connectivity is a modifier).
6. **Landline phones.** The key says "phone ... for personal communication"; cordless landline sets (HS 8517.11, Festnetz at Geizhals) are not named. In D2, or left unfiled?
7. **Chargers.** Phone wall chargers and GaN USB-C chargers: D1 ("adapters made to connect electronic devices") or D2 under S3. The D1 memo asks the same question.
8. **Sport computers and trackers.** Golf GPS, bike computers, sport GPS watches, pedometers, pet, child and vehicle trackers: D2 by key order (step 24 before step 26) as recommended, or E2, B10 or G1.
9. **Ledgers.** Approve the maker ledger (with OEM-to-brand map) and the ecosystem ledger, and the rule that platform and tier are never levels.
10. **Named-form grain.** Model and series (recommended), with storage, colour and regional variants as ledger rows, against model-and-variant.
11. **Attestation gaps (S6).** No fetched page names satellite phones, pagers, CB radios, amateur handhelds, electronic dictionaries (named only in a "see also"), pocket translators, laptop-class power banks, GPS or cellular trackers for vehicles, children or pets, kids' smartwatches as a kind, or in-car sat-navs; these kinds (numbers 10, 18, 27, 28, 41, 48 in the tree) need one sourcing batch before names are added. Rugged smartphone named forms are also unattested.
12. **Chinese retail evidence.** No Chinese retailer category tree was fetched (ZOL gave headlines only; JD and Taobao are logged as JavaScript-only earlier). Approve Chinese evidence from the maker catalogue and benchmark ladders, or a further retail batch.
13. **Framework conflicts for the next revision (not applied).** (a) The step 24 D1 branch "cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices" and S3 both reach chargers; (b) the D2 branch says "GPS unit, satellite messenger or tracker" without naming bike computers, golf devices or pet trackers; (c) "phone" does not say whether landline sets are included.
