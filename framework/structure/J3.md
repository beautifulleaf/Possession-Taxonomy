## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J3 Dairy, Eggs & Alternatives (domain J, Groceries & Cellar).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 3, branch 3): *milk, cream, butter and ghee, yoghurt and kefir, cheese and eggs, milk powders and infant formula, and plant-based alternatives to them (oat, soy, almond and other plant milks, plant yoghurts and plant cheeses).* Category list line: "milk, cream, butter, yoghurt, cheese, eggs, milk powders and infant formula, and plant-based alternatives to them."

**Notes and precedence sentences that touch J3:**
- Step 3 lead note: groceries are filed once, "as the product as bought, divided by product and by the quality of the ingredient", and Culinary links to it. A product is filed by what it is, not by what it contains: "sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes and cooking fats are J5 whatever they are made from, except butter and ghee (J3)". Dairy desserts, ice cream and cheese dishes are prepared foods (step 1, Culinary). Cheese, yoghurt and butter are named in step 1 as groceries ("curing, smoking, ageing, fermenting ... of one main ingredient does not make a food prepared").
- Step 1: eggs for hatching are a living animal (OUT). Baby-food meals are prepared food (OUT). A non-alcoholic drink "made by cooking or combining ingredients into something eaten or drunk as it is" is prepared (OUT).
- Step 2 note (H2): "Infant formula and follow-on milks are J3, except formulas made for a medical condition (hypoallergenic, amino-acid and metabolic formulas), which are H2." Sports-nutrition and meal-replacement products, including protein powders, are H2. Breast pumps are H2.
- Step 3 notes: coconut milk and cream are J5; tofu, tempeh and seitan are J5; wines and spirits made for cooking are J5; a set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value (cheese selection boxes are J3 if cheese is the greatest-value item); live cultures kept to grow are C3 (step 6); pet milk and animal feed are B10.
- BRIEF.md: groceries are judged by the quality of the ingredient; "Grocery products that the Culinary taxonomy also holds (cheese, cured meats, fruit, tea, coffee, wine) are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind." Baby and child goods have no category of their own: "user" is a tag.
- Appendix A modifiers that bind inner levels: region and culture, price and quality tier, brand and maker, user (baby, child), setting, market (domestic, commercial), occasion, motive, material, condition, edition; origin and how acquired.
- S2 (kinds are generic types), S5 (test the object, not the motive), S6 (attestation); stage 2 conventions C1–C17. C5(d) (a "Parts and accessories" sub-family) does not apply to groceries (J review X12); tools and containers for dairy and eggs go to B5 or B1 (section 9).

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

No forum, community wiki or glossary page was fetched (gap). The nearest sources are guides:
- **Cheese.** Wikipedia says cheeses "can be grouped or classified according to criteria such as: length of fermentation, texture, production method, fat content, animal source of the milk, and country or region of origin ... with no method used universally"; "the most common traditional categorization is based on moisture content, which is then further narrowed down by fat content and curing or ripening methods". Its sections are source of milk; moisture (soft, semi-soft, semi-hard, hard); mould (soft-ripened, washed-rind, smear-ripened, blue); fresh and whey cheeses; cooked pressed (Alpine) cheeses; processed [S25]. Roughly 500 varieties are recognised by the International Dairy Federation [S26].
- **Infant formula.** The American Academy of Pediatrics guide gives cow's-milk based; goat's-milk based; extensively hydrolysed; soy; specialised formulas for specific disorders and premature babies; toddler "formulas"; probiotic and prebiotic supplemented; lactose-free [S34]. The Irish health service guide gives first infant formula (large number 1, whey-based, powder or ready-to-feed); follow-on formula (number 2, "no benefits"); goat milk formula; hungry baby milk (more casein); comfort milk [S33]. A third-party guide describes the EU labels: Stage 1 or PRE (0–6 months), Stage 2 and 3 (follow-on), and a Stage 4 or "growing-up milk" outside the formula regulation [S35].
- **Plant alternatives.** Divided by what they replace and by plant source [S36].

None fetched (gap). Fresh dairy and eggs have no resale market. The nearest equivalent is the cheese maturer, where the quality unit is a matured batch from a named maker [S12], and the protected-name register, where a name is a registered property [S23][S27][S28].

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Codex cheese standard CXS 283** [S13]: cheese is "ripened or unripened soft, semi-hard, hard, or extra-hard". A cheese sold only as "cheese" may carry two descriptive terms: firmness from moisture on a fat-free basis (extra hard under 51; hard 49–56; firm or semi-hard 54–69; soft over 67 per cent) and principal ripening (ripened; mould ripened; unripened or fresh; in brine). Rind and smear are defined.
- **German Käseverordnung** [S15]: six cheese groups by water in the fat-free mass: Hartkäse 56 per cent or less; Schnittkäse over 54 to 63; halbfester Schnittkäse over 61 to 69; Sauermilchkäse over 60 to 73; Weichkäse over 67; Frischkäse over 73. Whey cheese, cheese sold in liquid (brine, oil) and pasta filata are outside the grouping. A second axis, fat in dry matter, has eight tiers from Doppelrahmstufe (60–87 per cent) to Magerstufe (under 10). A national quality mark "Markenkäse" requires at least 40 per cent fat in dry matter and a scored assessment. Processed cheese (Schmelzkäse) sits in a separate section "Erzeugnisse aus Käse".
- **Codex fermented milks CXS 243** [S14]: yoghurt, alternate culture yoghurt, acidophilus milk, kefir and kumys are defined by starter culture; concentrated fermented milk (protein over 5.6 per cent; strained yoghurt, labneh, ymer, ylette); flavoured fermented milks (up to 50 per cent non-dairy); drinks based on fermented milk (at least 40 per cent fermented milk).
- **Eggs.** EU marketing standards: Class A and Class B; Class A weight grades S, M, L, XL; packs carry the farming method; labels "free range", "barn", "eggs from caged hens" [S16]. The egg code printed on the shell has a first digit 0 organic, 1 free range, 2 barn (deep litter), 3 cage, then a country code and an establishment number [S24]. USDA consumer grades AA, A, B [S17], with cage-free shield variants of each grade [S18]. The two axes (quality grade, housing) are separate in the US and in the EU.
- **USDA dairy grade standards** are written per product: butter (AA, A, B scored on flavour, body, colour, salt) [S20], whipped butter, Cheddar (AA, A, B, C; Cheddar defined as at most 39 per cent moisture and at least 50 per cent milkfat in the dry matter) [S21], Colby, Monterey Jack, Swiss and Emmentaler, bulk American cheese, dry milks, whey, dry buttermilk [S19].
- **Cream.** Fat tiers are named per jurisdiction: Australia and New Zealand extra light 12–12.5, light 18–20, cream at least 35, thickened 35–36.5, double 48–60; Russia legally has two classes, normal 10–34 and heavy 35–58 [S31].
- **Infant formula.** Codex CXS 72 has two sections: infant formula, and formula for special medical purposes intended for infants; the label must avoid confusion between infant formula, follow-up formula and medical formula [S22]. EU Regulation 2016/127 has two regulated categories, infant formula (0–6 months) and follow-on formula (6 months on) [S35]. The US has one 0–12 month category (21 CFR 107) and treats toddler formula as ordinary food [S35].
- **Protected names and trade classification.** The EU register eAmbrosia records each protected name with type (PDO), product type, combined nomenclature code (Stilton: 0406 Cheese and curd, under chapter 04 "Dairy produce; birds' eggs; natural honey"), country, file number (PDO-GB-0277) and registration date [S23]. The Wikipedia lists give PDO or PGI and the registration year per country [S27][S28]. The PDO list also holds a butter, Beurre d'Ardenne [S27].
- **Dairy names for plant products.** The US standard of identity reserves "yogurt" for milk-derived product; an EU court ruling reserves "milk, cream, butter, cheese and yogurt" for products of animal origin [S32]. Waitrose therefore sells "Soya Drink", "Oat Drink" and "Dairy Free Yogurt Alternative" [S3][S4].

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:**
1. The first split is the product family, in the same order everywhere: milk, cream, butter, yoghurt, cheese, eggs (Waitrose [S1][S3], Aldi [S9], Aeon [S11], USDA [S19], Wikipedia's dairy navbox [S26]).
2. Inside cheese the main axis is texture family, meaning moisture and ripening: Codex [S13], the German statute [S15], Wikipedia [S25], and the cheese specialist's Hard / Soft / Blue [S12].
3. Fat level, salt, organic status, lactose-free status, size and housing are labels on a product type, not types.
4. Fermented milks are defined by culture [S14]; eggs by class, size and farming method [S15][S16][S24].

**Split:**
- **Plant alternatives.** Retailers file them by function, inside Milk, Yogurts and Cheese [S3][S4][S7][S9], with a diet facet "Dietary & Lifestyle" [S1]. Trade guides file them as one family divided by what they replace [S36]. Law reserves the dairy names, so plant drinks are not "milk" [S32]. The framework puts them in J3 and names them as a branch.
- **Lactose-free cow's milk.** Waitrose files Arla Lactofree with plant drinks as an "alternative" [S4]; Aldi and Woolworths list lactose-free as a line of Milk or Yoghurt [S8][S10]. It is cow's milk treated with lactase.
- **Cheese band numbers.** Codex hard 49–56, firm 54–69, soft over 67 [S13]; German Hartkäse up to 56, Schnittkäse 54–63, Weichkäse over 67 [S15]; the Wikipedia article quotes other bands (hard 25–45, semi-hard 45–50, semi-soft 42–55, as a share of dry weight) [S25]. The bands overlap in every source, and the same variety moves up as it ages (US Cheddar is defined by its moisture ceiling and graded on curing [S21]).
- **What is outside the cheese grouping.** The German statute excludes pasta filata, whey cheese and brined cheese from the water bands [S15]; Codex counts brine as a ripening term, not a firmness [S13]; Wikipedia treats fresh and whey cheeses and processed cheese as separate groups [S25].
- **Skyr, quark, labneh.** Waitrose puts skyr in Yogurts and quark in Cheese [S2][S3]; the German statute puts Speisequark in the cheese groups [S15]; Codex puts labneh in concentrated fermented milk [S14]. The skyr-against-quark call rests on Waitrose alone (one retailer; gap).
- **Washed rind against texture.** Wikipedia notes washed-rind cheeses "can be soft (Limburger), semi-hard or hard (Appenzeller)" [S25]: rind and firmness are two axes. The leaf key in section 3 therefore tests them in a fixed order.
- **Where infant formula lives.** Retail: the baby department [S3]; framework: J3; regulation: its own food class [S22][S35].
- **Quality ladders.** Statutory grades exist for eggs [S16][S17], US butter and US Cheddar [S20][S21], and a German cheese quality mark [S15]; none exists for milk, yoghurt or plant drinks. Cheese is graded in the US and not in the EU/UK, where origin (PDO) and maturer's selection carry quality [S12][S23].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Product family first (what the thing is), then, inside the family, a family-specific axis.**
- *Navigation.* Every retailer opens with the family: Waitrose Milk, Butter & Eggs / Cheese / Yogurts [S1][S3]; Aldi Butter & Margarine / Cheese / Creams / Eggs / Milk / Yogurt [S9]; Aeon 牛乳 / ヨーグルト / たまご [S11]; USDA product by product [S19].
- *Inside cheese:* texture family (Hard, Soft, Blue at a specialist [S12]; Codex firmness and ripening [S13]; German groups [S15]), then variety (Aldi and Waitrose titles are by variety [S2][S9]), then age or maker (Waitrose "Strength 1–6" and "30 Months" on Parmigiano Reggiano [S2]; maker and batch at Neal's Yard [S12]).
- *Inside eggs:* farming method (legal marking on the pack [S16]; Waitrose sells only free range [S6]), then size and breed line (Blacktail, Burford Buff, double yolk [S1][S6]).
- *Inside milk:* fat level and treatment (low-fat and non-fat at Aeon [S11]; full cream, skim, A2, lactose-free at Aldi [S10]).
- *Inside infant formula:* the stage number printed on the pack (1, 2 and 3 [S33][S35]), then protein base (cow, goat, soy, hydrolysed [S34]).
- *Inside plant alternatives:* the product they replace (drink, yoghurt, cheese, cream and creamer, egg), then the plant base (soy, oat, almond, coconut [S4][S36]).
- *Filters.* The only filter groups visible on Waitrose pages are Dietary & Lifestyle (vegan, free-from, organic and similar) and Waitrose Brands [S1][S2][S4][S5][S6][S7]. Diet and own-label brand are the facets, which supports treating plant-based and lactose-free as tags and views, not levels. No other retailer showed facets in the fetched text (gap).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the fixed level names of C5: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (at most four levels below J3).

### Candidate A (recommended): sub-family by product family; leaf by a family-specific axis; kind by product type; named form by protected name or line

- **Level 1, sub-family: the dairy or egg product family the object is, or replaces.** Seven sub-families:
  1. Milk (liquid and preserved milks)
  2. Cream and butter
  3. Yoghurt and fermented milks
  4. Cheese
  5. Eggs
  6. Milk powders and infant formula
  7. Plant-based alternatives
- **Level 2, leaf: one axis per sub-family (C5c); a sub-family of about 12 kinds or fewer skips the leaf (C5b).**

- **Cheese leaf key (C5c: first yes wins; the axis is "family of the curd and its maturing", read in this order).** (1) Processed (melted, emulsified)? Processed cheese. (2) Blue-veined? Blue cheeses. (3) White-mould rind? Bloomy-rind soft cheeses. (4) Matured in brine? Brined cheeses. (5) Stretched curd (pasta filata)? Stretched-curd cheeses. (6) Unripened, or a whey cheese? Fresh and whey cheeses. (7) Soft or semi-soft with a washed or smear rind (pungent orange rind: Culinary SV-H1-5's own definition)? Washed-rind cheeses. (8) Otherwise by Codex firmness [S13]: firm or semi-hard to Semi-hard pressed cheeses; hard or extra-hard to Hard and long-aged cheeses. A hard smear-rind cheese (Appenzeller, Comté) therefore goes by firmness, a soft blue (Cambozola, Gorgonzola dolce) is blue, and smoked scamorza is stretched-curd. Where the Codex and statute bands overlap [S13][S15], and for a cheese that changes band as it ages, the variety is filed once by its ordinary retail form and age is an attribute (Break 4). Each cheese kind carries its `leaf` result from this key, so two builders reach the same leaf.
- **Level 3, kind:** the generic type where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). **Level 4, named form:** a protected-name or regional specification of a kind (West Country farmhouse Cheddar PDO), or a maker's line (Cathedral City Cheddar, Lurpak butter, Clarence Court Burford Buff), with `maker_bound` yes or no (C2). Age, saltedness and size are attributes, never part of a named form's name (C2b): "Cathedral City Mature" is the line Cathedral City with age mature; "Lurpak Unsalted" is the line Lurpak with unsalted.
- **Attributes, never levels:** milk species for every product except the kinds in Milk and Eggs where the species names the kind (section 4b: goat's milk, duck egg; species is an attribute of cheese, yoghurt, butter and formula, and a generated view `milk_species` reads both); fat level; salt (salted, unsalted); treatment (fresh, filtered, UHT or long life, raw, homogenised or not); lactose-free; A2; organic; farming code and class and size for eggs; stage and protein base for formula; plant base for plant products; flavour; format (sliced, grated, block, ready-to-feed); age or maturity; protected-name status; pack size and price per unit (derived).
- **Rule P (protected names), revised to follow C11.** (1) A cheese **kind** is a variety made under that name by unrelated makers in at least one fetched market, labelled with the generic name (Cheddar, Gouda, Emmental, brie, camembert, feta, mozzarella, Pecorino, and "grana-type hard cheese (parmesan)": Waitrose files "Parmesan & Pecorino" as one sub-category [S2], Aldi sells Grana Padano [S9]; the term "grana" for the shared form is †). (2) A protected name whose rules bind one kind is a **non-maker named form** under that kind: West Country farmhouse Cheddar PDO under Cheddar; Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP under mozzarella; Feta PDO under feta; Parmigiano Reggiano DOP and Grana Padano DOP under grana-type hard cheese [S2][S9][S28]. (3) A protected cheese with no generic sibling sold under its name (Stilton, Gorgonzola [S23][S28]; Roquefort †) is a **kind** under C11's "no generic kind covers its form" case; its label is the generic description with the name as synonym ("blue cheese (Stilton)", "blue cheese (Gorgonzola)") and the reason is recorded on the row; that no generic sibling is sold under these names needs a fetched page (†). (4) PDO, PGI or TSG status, the register file number (PDO-GB-0277 for Stilton [S23]) and the country are fields in the designation table (section 6); a region never names a sub-family or leaf (C11). OWNER: question 2 of the J review (placement of designated names): (a) rules (1)–(4) with no exception; (b) the same with a written exception for wine and spirit names (not relevant to J3 cheeses); (c) the memo's earlier Rule P test, "a kind when the product is sold and compared only under that name (Roquefort, Gorgonzola, Stilton, Parmigiano Reggiano, Grana Padano), a named form when a broader generic kind is sold and compared", which makes Parmigiano Reggiano and Grana Padano two kinds. Rules (1)–(4) are applied here because the earlier test is subjective and, for cheese, conflicts with C11; option (c) remains the owner's to choose.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: preference (price band per unit) overall, with statutory grades and class codes held as attributes with the standard named. Closest to spec-ranked where a statutory ladder exists (eggs, US butter, US Cheddar). No tier on any kind (C4a).**

- **Origin certification is not a ranking:** PDO, PGI and TSG record place and method, not quality grade. Store as designation rows (section 6) and an attribute on the kind or named form [S23][S27][S28]. ## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states (C7). Every key is reproducible from a source or from the alphabet.**
- *Milk:* cow's milk first, then the other animal sources alphabetically (buffalo, goat, sheep), then buttermilk, then evaporated and condensed milk.
- *Cream:* by fat content, ascending (single, whipping, thickened, double), then cultured creams (soured cream, crème fraîche). Reason: the fat ladder is the buyers' ordering and the law's [S31].
- *Butter and ghee:* butter first, then blends, then clarified butter and ghee.
- *Yoghurt and fermented milks:* yoghurt first, then the culture types in the order Codex CXS 243 lists them (kefir, kumys), then the concentrated types (strained yoghurt, skyr, other concentrated fermented milk) [S14].
- *Cheese leaves:* the leaves run in the order of the Codex and statute ladders, wet to dry (fresh, brined, stretched-curd, bloomy-rind, washed-rind, semi-hard, hard; blue and processed last). Inside a leaf, kinds go alphabetically by label (the fetched standards name no canonical variety for a leaf; where one does, it comes first). Named forms go by maker (non-maker protected names have none), then first-registration year as attested [S23][S28], then alphabetically.
- *Eggs:* hen's egg first, then other species alphabetically (duck, quail), then preserved egg.
- *Milk powders and infant formula:* milk powders first (skimmed, whole), then formula by age of the infant ascending (infant formula, follow-on, growing-up milk) [S33][S35].
- *Plant:* leaves in the order of the key's list (drinks, yoghurts, cheeses), then creams and creamers, then egg alternatives; drinks alphabetically by base (almond, coconut, oat, soy); cheeses firm style before soft.
