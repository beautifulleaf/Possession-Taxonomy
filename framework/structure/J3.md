## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J3 Dairy, Eggs & Alternatives (domain J, Groceries & Cellar).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 3, branch 3): *milk, cream, butter and ghee, yoghurt and kefir, cheese and eggs, milk powders and infant formula, and plant-based alternatives to them (oat, soy, almond and other plant milks, plant yoghurts and plant cheeses).* Category list line: "milk, cream, butter, yoghurt, cheese, eggs, milk powders and infant formula, and plant-based alternatives to them."

**Notes and precedence sentences that touch J3:**
- Step 3 lead note: groceries are filed once, "as the product as bought, divided by product and by the quality of the ingredient", and Culinary links to it. A product is filed by what it is, not by what it contains: "sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes and cooking fats are J5 whatever they are made from, except butter and ghee (J3)". Dairy desserts, ice cream and cheese dishes are prepared foods (step 1, Culinary). Cheese, yoghurt and butter are named in step 1 as groceries ("curing, smoking, ageing, fermenting ... of one main ingredient does not make a food prepared").
- Step 1: eggs for hatching are a living animal (OUT). Baby-food meals are prepared food (OUT).
- Step 2 note (H2): "Infant formula and follow-on milks are J3, except formulas made for a medical condition (hypoallergenic, amino-acid and metabolic formulas), which are H2." Sports-nutrition and meal-replacement products, including protein powders, are H2. Breast pumps are H2.
- Step 3 notes: coconut milk and cream are J5; tofu, tempeh and seitan are J5; wines and spirits made for cooking are J5; a set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value (cheese selection boxes are J3 if cheese is the greatest-value item); live cultures kept to grow are C3 (step 6); pet milk and animal feed are B10.
- BRIEF.md: groceries are judged by the quality of the ingredient; "Grocery products that the Culinary taxonomy also holds (cheese, cured meats, fruit, tea, coffee, wine) are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind." Baby and child goods have no category of their own: "user" is a tag.
- Appendix A modifiers that bind inner levels: region and culture, price and quality tier, brand and maker, user (baby, child), setting, market (domestic, commercial), occasion, motive, material, condition, edition; origin and how acquired.
- S2 (kinds are generic types), S5 (test the object, not the motive), S6 (attestation); stage 2 conventions C1–C13.

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, guides and wikis

No forum, community wiki or glossary page was fetched (gap). The nearest sources are guides:
- **Cheese.** Wikipedia says cheeses "can be grouped or classified according to criteria such as: length of fermentation, texture, production method, fat content, animal source of the milk, and country or region of origin ... with no method used universally"; "the most common traditional categorization is based on moisture content, which is then further narrowed down by fat content and curing or ripening methods". Its sections are source of milk; moisture (soft, semi-soft, semi-hard, hard); mould (soft-ripened, washed-rind, smear-ripened, blue); fresh and whey cheeses; cooked pressed (Alpine) cheeses; processed [S25]. Roughly 500 varieties are recognised by the International Dairy Federation [S26].
- **Infant formula.** The American Academy of Pediatrics guide gives cow's-milk based; goat's-milk based; extensively hydrolysed; soy; specialised formulas for specific disorders and premature babies; toddler "formulas"; probiotic and prebiotic supplemented; lactose-free [S34]. The Irish health service guide gives first infant formula (large number 1, whey-based, powder or ready-to-feed); follow-on formula (number 2, "no benefits"); goat milk formula; hungry baby milk (more casein); comfort milk [S33]. A third-party guide describes the EU labels: Stage 1 or PRE (0–6 months), Stage 2 and 3 (follow-on), and a Stage 4 or "growing-up milk" outside the formula regulation [S35].
- **Plant alternatives.** Divided by what they replace and by plant source [S36].

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Codex cheese standard CXS 283** [S13]: cheese is "ripened or unripened soft, semi-hard, hard, or extra-hard". A cheese sold only as "cheese" may carry two descriptive terms: firmness from moisture on a fat-free basis (extra hard under 51; hard 49–56; firm or semi-hard 54–69; soft over 67 per cent) and principal ripening (ripened; mould ripened; unripened or fresh; in brine). Rind and smear are defined.
- **German Käseverordnung** [S15]: six cheese groups by water in the fat-free mass: Hartkäse 56 per cent or less; Schnittkäse over 54 to 63; halbfester Schnittkäse over 61 to 69; Sauermilchkäse over 60 to 73; Weichkäse over 67; Frischkäse over 73. Whey cheese, cheese sold in liquid (brine, oil) and pasta filata are outside the grouping. A second axis, fat in dry matter, has eight tiers from Doppelrahmstufe (60–87 per cent) to Magerstufe (under 10). A national quality mark "Markenkäse" requires at least 40 per cent fat in dry matter and a scored assessment. Processed cheese (Schmelzkäse) sits in a separate section "Erzeugnisse aus Käse".
- **Codex fermented milks CXS 243** [S14]: yoghurt, alternate culture yoghurt, acidophilus milk, kefir and kumys are defined by starter culture; concentrated fermented milk (protein over 5.6 per cent; strained yoghurt, labneh, ymer, ylette); flavoured fermented milks (up to 50 per cent non-dairy); drinks based on fermented milk (at least 40 per cent fermented milk).
- **Eggs.** EU marketing standards: Class A and Class B; Class A weight grades S, M, L, XL; packs carry the farming method; labels "free range", "barn", "eggs from caged hens" [S16]. The egg code printed on the shell has a first digit 0 organic, 1 free range, 2 barn (deep litter), 3 cage, then a country code and an establishment number [S24]. USDA consumer grades AA, A, B [S17], with cage-free shield variants of each grade [S18]. The two axes (quality grade, housing) are separate in the US and in the EU.
- **USDA dairy grade standards** are written per product: butter (AA, A, B scored on flavour, body, colour, salt) [S20], whipped butter, Cheddar (AA, A, B, C; Cheddar defined as at most 39 per cent moisture and at least 50 per cent milkfat in the dry matter) [S21], Colby, Monterey Jack, Swiss and Emmentaler, bulk American cheese, dry milks, whey, dry buttermilk [S19].
- **Cream.** Fat tiers are named per jurisdiction: Australia and New Zealand extra light 12–12.5, light 18–20, cream at least 35, thickened 35–36.5, double 48–60; Russia legally has two classes, normal 10–34 and heavy 35–58 [S31].
- **Infant formula.** Codex CXS 72 has two sections: infant formula, and formula for special medical purposes intended for infants; the label must avoid confusion between infant formula, follow-up formula and medical formula [S22]. EU Regulation 2016/127 has two regulated categories, infant formula (0–6 months) and follow-on formula (6 months on) [S35]. The US has one 0–12 month category (21 CFR 107) and treats toddler formula as ordinary food [S35].
- **Protected names and trade classification.** The EU register eAmbrosia records each protected name with type (PDO), product type, combined nomenclature code (Stilton: 0406 Cheese and curd, under chapter 04 "Dairy produce; birds' eggs; natural honey"), country, file number (PDO-GB-0277) and registration date [S23]. The Wikipedia lists give PDO or PGI and the registration year per country [S27][S28]. The PDO list also holds a butter, Beurre d'Ardenne [S27].
- **Dairy names for plant products.** The US standard of identity reserves "yogurt" for milk-derived product; an EU court ruling reserves "milk, cream, butter, cheese and yogurt" for products of animal origin [S32]. Waitrose therefore sells "Soya Drink", "Oat Drink" and "Dairy Free Yogurt Alternative" [S3][S4].

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:**
1. The first split is the product family, in the same order everywhere: milk, cream, butter, yoghurt, cheese, eggs (Waitrose [S1][S3], Aldi [S9], Aeon [S11], USDA [S19], Wikipedia's dairy navbox [S26]).
2. Inside cheese the main axis is texture family, meaning moisture and ripening: Codex [S13], the German statute [S15], Wikipedia [S25], and the cheese specialist's Hard / Soft / Blue [S12].
3. Fat level, salt, organic status, lactose-free status, size and housing are labels on a product type, not types.
4. Fermented milks are defined by culture [S14]; eggs by class, size and farming method [S15][S16][S24].

**Split:**
- **Plant alternatives.** Retailers file them by function, inside Milk, Yogurts and Cheese [S3][S4][S7][S9], with a diet facet "Dietary & Lifestyle" [S1]. Trade guides file them as one family divided by what they replace [S36]. Law reserves the dairy names, so plant drinks are not "milk" [S32]. The framework puts them in J3 and names them as a branch.
- **Lactose-free cow's milk.** Waitrose files Arla Lactofree with plant drinks as an "alternative" [S4]; Aldi and Woolworths list lactose-free as a line of Milk or Yoghurt [S8][S10]. It is cow's milk treated with lactase.
- **Cheese band numbers.** Codex hard 49–56, firm 54–69, soft over 67 [S13]; German Hartkäse up to 56, Schnittkäse 54–63, Weichkäse over 67 [S15]; the Wikipedia article quotes other bands (hard 25–45, semi-hard 45–50, semi-soft 42–55, as a share of dry weight) [S25]. The bands overlap in every source, and the same variety moves up as it ages (US Cheddar is defined by its moisture ceiling and graded on curing [S21]).
- **What is outside the cheese grouping.** The German statute excludes pasta filata, whey cheese and brined cheese from the water bands [S15]; Codex counts brine as a ripening term, not a firmness [S13]; Wikipedia treats fresh and whey cheeses and processed cheese as separate groups [S25].
- **Skyr, quark, labneh.** Waitrose puts skyr in Yogurts and quark in Cheese [S2][S3]; the German statute puts Speisequark in the cheese groups [S15]; Codex puts labneh in concentrated fermented milk [S14].
- **Washed rind against texture.** Wikipedia notes washed-rind cheeses "can be soft (Limburger), semi-hard or hard (Appenzeller)" [S25]: rind and firmness are two axes.
- **Where infant formula lives.** Retail: the baby department [S3]; framework: J3; regulation: its own food class [S22][S35].
- **Quality ladders.** Statutory grades exist for eggs [S16][S17], US butter and US Cheddar [S20][S21], and a German cheese quality mark [S15]; none exists for milk, yoghurt or plant drinks. Cheese is graded in the US and not in the EU/UK, where origin (PDO) and maturer's selection carry quality [S12][S23].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Product family first (what the thing is), then, inside the family, a family-specific axis.**
- *Navigation.* Every retailer opens with the family: Waitrose Milk, Butter & Eggs / Cheese / Yogurts [S1][S3]; Aldi Butter & Margarine / Cheese / Creams / Eggs / Milk / Yogurt [S9]; Aeon 牛乳 / ヨーグルト / たまご [S11]; USDA product by product [S19].
- *Inside cheese:* texture family (Hard, Soft, Blue at a specialist [S12]; Codex firmness and ripening [S13]; German groups [S15]), then variety (Aldi and Waitrose titles are by variety [S2][S9]), then age or maker (Waitrose "Strength 1–6" and "30 Months" on Parmigiano Reggiano [S2]; maker and batch at Neal's Yard [S12]).
- *Inside eggs:* farming method (legal marking on the pack [S16]; Waitrose sells only free range [S6]), then size and breed line (Blacktail, Burford Buff, double yolk [S1][S6]).
- *Inside milk:* fat level and treatment (low-fat and non-fat at Aeon [S11]; full cream, skim, A2, lactose-free at Aldi [S10]).
- *Inside infant formula:* the stage number printed on the pack (1, 2 and 3 [S33][S35]), then protein base (cow, goat, soy, hydrolysed [S34]).
- *Inside plant alternatives:* the product they replace (drink, yoghurt, cheese), then the plant base (soy, oat, almond, coconut [S4][S36]).
- *Filters.* The only filter groups visible on Waitrose pages are Dietary & Lifestyle (vegan, free-from, organic and similar) and Waitrose Brands [S1][S2][S4][S5][S6][S7]. Diet and own-label brand are the facets, which supports treating plant-based and lactose-free as tags and views, not levels. No other retailer showed facets in the fetched text (gap).

## 3. Candidate structures

All three use the fixed level names of C5: category, sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (at most four levels below J3).

### Candidate A (recommended): sub-family by product family; leaf by a family-specific axis; kind by product type; named form by protected name or line

- **Level 1, sub-family: the dairy or egg product family the object is, or replaces.** Seven sub-families:
  1. Milk (liquid and preserved milks)
  2. Cream and butter
  3. Yoghurt and fermented milks
  4. Cheese
  5. Eggs
  6. Milk powders and infant formula
  7. Plant-based alternatives
- **Level 2, leaf: one axis per sub-family (C5c); a sub-family of about 12 kinds or fewer skips the leaf (C5b).**

| Sub-family | Leaf axis | Leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Milk | none (7 kinds or fewer; leaf = null) | |
| Cream and butter | the milk-fat product | Cream; Butter and ghee |
| Yoghurt and fermented milks | none (about 6 kinds; leaf = null) | |
| Cheese | texture and ripening family: how the curd is made and matured | Fresh and whey cheeses; Brined cheeses; Stretched-curd cheeses; Bloomy-rind soft cheeses; Washed-rind cheeses; Semi-hard pressed cheeses; Hard and long-aged cheeses; Blue cheeses; Processed cheese; (Dried and preserved dairy: see section 10) |
| Eggs | none (about 4 kinds; leaf = null) | |
| Milk powders and infant formula | none (about 5 kinds; leaf = null) | |
| Plant-based alternatives | the dairy product replaced | Plant drinks; Plant yoghurts; Plant cheeses; (Plant cream and plant egg alternatives: open questions) |

- **Level 3, kind:** the generic type where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). **Level 4, named form:** a protected-name or regional specification of a kind (West Country farmhouse Cheddar PDO), or a maker's line (Cathedral City Mature Cheddar, Lurpak Unsalted Butter, Clarence Court Burford Buff), with `maker_bound` yes or no (C2).
- **Attributes, never levels:** milk species (cow, goat, sheep, buffalo, camel); fat level; salt (salted, unsalted); treatment (fresh, filtered, UHT or long life, raw, homogenised or not); lactose-free; A2; organic; farming code and class and size for eggs; stage and protein base for formula; plant base for plant products; flavour; format (sliced, grated, block, spreadable, ready-to-feed); age or maturity; protected-name status; pack size and price per unit (derived).
- **Rule P (protected names).** A protected name is a **kind** when the product is sold and compared only under that name (Roquefort, Gorgonzola, Stilton, Parmigiano Reggiano, Grana Padano). It is a **named form** when a broader generic kind is sold and compared (West Country farmhouse Cheddar PDO under Cheddar; Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP under Mozzarella; Feta PDO under Feta). In both cases PDO, PGI or TSG status, the register file number (for example PDO-GB-0277 for Stilton [S23]) and the country are fields in a protected-name table (section 6), and a region never names a sub-family or leaf (C11).

**Where Candidate A breaks (edge objects):**
1. *Lactose-free cow's milk and goat's milk.* Retailers file them with plant drinks as "alternatives" [S4]; A files them under Milk with an attribute. A buyer browsing "alternatives" finds them through the lactose-free view.
2. *Skyr, quark, labneh, and drinking yoghurt.* The sources disagree (section 1.6). A follows Waitrose for skyr (yoghurt) and the German statute for quark (cheese) and flags both.
3. *Butter with oil, plant butters, margarine.* Waitrose sells butter, butter-rapeseed blends, goat's butter and plant butters on one page [S5]; the key sends cooking fats to J5. A holds blends with butter (they contain butter) and sends plant-only butters and margarine to J5.
4. *Cheese that changes group with age.* A young Cheddar and a vintage Cheddar fall in different Codex firmness bands [S13][S21]. A files the variety once, by its ordinary retail form, and records age as an attribute.
5. *Soy infant formula.* It is a plant-protein product [S34] but an infant formula under the key; the formula sub-family wins (the product is an infant formula, not an alternative to milk).

### Candidate B: no plant sub-family; plant products are kinds inside each family with a plant-based flag

- Same sub-families 1–6; the oat drink goes under Milk, plant yoghurt under Yoghurt, plant cheese under the cheese leaves.
- For: matches the retailers' aisles (Waitrose, Aldi, Woolworths) [S3][S4][S7][S9][S8] and the diet facet [S1].
- Against: mixes two comparison sets in one leaf (an oat drink is compared with soy and almond drinks, not with goat's milk [S4][S36]); law reserves the dairy names to animal products [S32], so a plant drink cannot be filed as a milk without contradicting the label; plant cheese has no curd family, so it fits none of the nine cheese leaves; the key names plant alternatives as a branch of J3. ### Candidate C: milk species or animal source first (cow, goat, sheep, buffalo, hen, duck, plant)

- Axis: the animal or plant the product comes from.
- For: a real cut in the cheese and yoghurt trade (Waitrose "Goat's & Sheep's Cheese" [S2]; Wikipedia "source of milk" [S25]; Wikipedia's navigation by animal milk [S26]).
- Against: it cuts across every family (a goat's milk, goat butter [S5], goat cheese, goat formula [S33] would sit together but away from cow's equivalents); buyers choose the product first. Breaks on: butter, formula, cream, eggs (hen only). Milk species is kept as an attribute and a generated view.

## 4. Depth and size

Counts are estimates; no complete register was counted.

- **Generic kinds (rough):** Milk about 8; Cream and butter about 14; Yoghurt and fermented milks about 8; Eggs about 6; Milk powders and infant formula about 8; Plant-based alternatives about 20 (drinks by base, plus yoghurt, cheese, cream and egg alternatives); **Cheese about 120–250** at variety level (the International Dairy Federation recognises about 500 varieties [S26], many are regional variants of one type; the Wikipedia protected-cheese lists give per-country tables that were not counted [S28]). Total about 180–320.
- **Named forms (rough):** protected cheese names narrower than a generic kind (West Country farmhouse Cheddar PDO, Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP, Feta PDO) and maker lines for cheese, butter, eggs, formula and plant drinks: several hundred, dominated by cheese.
- **Where depth concentrates:** Cheese. Four levels are used only here (sub-family > leaf > kind > named form). Milk, Yoghurt, Eggs and Milk powders skip the leaf (C5b). Cream and butter, and Plant-based alternatives, have two leaves each.
- **Breadth:** broad leaves: semi-hard pressed cheeses, hard and long-aged cheeses, blue cheeses, plant drinks. Medium: fresh and whey cheeses, washed-rind cheeses, cream, butter and ghee, plant yoghurts. Narrow: stretched-curd cheeses, brined cheeses, bloomy-rind cheeses, processed cheese, and every skip-leaf sub-family.
- **Maximum depth recommended: four levels below J3** (cheese only); three elsewhere.

Lines and protected specifications are named forms.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: preference (price band per unit) overall, with statutory grades and class codes held as attributes with the standard named. Closest to spec-ranked where a statutory ladder exists (eggs, US butter, US Cheddar). No tier on any kind (C4a).**

- **Statutory grade and class ladders (attributes, C4b), each with its standard:** EU egg Class A and B and weight grades S to XL [S16]; EU egg farming code 0–3 [S24]; USDA egg grades AA, A, B [S17] and shields [S18]; USDA butter AA, A, B (flavour, body, colour, salt scored) [S20]; USDA Cheddar AA, A, B, C [S21]; German cheese groups and eight fat-in-dry-matter tiers [S15]; Codex firmness and ripening terms [S13]; cream fat tiers by jurisdiction [S31]. These sit in an `attribute` table keyed to a `standard_id`, not in tier entries.
- **Origin certification is not a ranking:** PDO, PGI and TSG record place and method, not quality grade. Store as `protected_name` rows (section 6) and an attribute on the kind or named form [S23][S27][S28]. The German "Markenkäse" quality mark is a national mark with a scored test [S15]; keep as a certification attribute.
- **Retailer scales are platform tags (C4c):** Waitrose "Strength 1–6" on cheese and "Award winning" flags [S2], Waitrose's No.1 range [S1], Aeon's "high-end eggs" [S11]. Store as dated `platform_tag` on named forms; do not compute a tier.
- **Maturer's selection is a quality signal without a scale:** Neal's Yard sells only the batches that "meet our standards" [S12]. Record the maturer as a maker (affineur) and attach the batch claim to the named form; no scheme.
- **Where price does not mean quality:** the Irish health service states that all first infant formula meets the same nutritional standard "the cheapest brand is just as good as the most expensive" [S33]. Do not build a formula tier.
- **Price band (C4d):** one USD scheme from dated list prices, plus a derived unit price (per kg, per litre, per egg), because every retailer prints one and pack size is an attribute [S1][S9].
- **Modifier route:** `tier` entries only on makers, lines or named forms, with scheme, value, source, date; none exist in the fetched evidence except platform tags. Grades go to attributes.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity for cheese, butter, eggs by breed line and formula; incidental for plain milk, cream and generic eggs. No platform dependence.**
- *Identity (cheese):* the specialist sells the cheesemaker's batch [S12]; Waitrose has a separate Artisanal Cheese page [S2]; protected names make the producer group part of the product [S23].
- *Identity (butter and eggs):* Lurpak, Kerrygold, Isigny Ste Mère unpasteurised and goat's butter are named lines on one page [S5]; egg breed lines (Clarence Court Burford Buff, Blacktail, Longstock Gold) are named lines [S1][S6]. The EU egg code ties a shell to a laying establishment, a traceable producer id [S24].
- *Identity (formula):* stage ranges are brand ranges (HiPP HA has its own stage numbering) [S35], although health authorities say brand does not change nutrition [S33].
- *Retail own-label:* Waitrose Essential, No.1, Duchy Organic and Waitrose Blacktail [S1][S2]; Aldi Emporium Selection and Farmdale [S9][S10].
- *Incidental:* generic milk types, double cream, hen's eggs, feta, mozzarella (outside protected names).

**Maker ledger: yes (C1).** Core columns as in C1. Extensions for J3:
- `maker_role` (cheesemaker, creamery or dairy, affineur or maturer, bottler, egg packing centre, plant-drink company, formula manufacturer). Neal's Yard is an affineur and retailer, not a maker [S12]; the schema's `maker_class` (artisan, retail own-label, supplier) does not cover it, so a role field is needed.
- `certifications` (PDO member, organic, free range): attested by the register or the label [S23][S24].
- `parent_id` for group (own-label lines to retailer).
- **Protected-name table (new, separate, like the compatibility ledger C13):** `protected_name_id`, `name`, `scheme` (PDO, PGI, TSG; AOC, DOP as national forms), `country`, `register_file_number`, `cn_code`, `registration_date`, `kind_id`, `milk_species`, `sources`. The eAmbrosia entry supplies scheme, country, file number, CN code and date [S23]; the Wikipedia lists supply scheme and year in bulk but need a register page for each row before entry [S27][S28]. Covers cheese, butter and any egg or dairy name.
- **Compatibility ledger (C13):** none needed in J3.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states (C7).**
- *Milk:* cow's milk first, then other species by market share (goat, sheep, buffalo), then buttermilk and preserved milks.
- *Cream:* by fat content, ascending (single, whipping, thickened, double), then cultured creams (soured cream, crème fraîche). Reason: the fat ladder is the buyers' ordering [S31].
- *Butter and ghee:* butter first, then blends, then clarified butter and ghee.
- *Yoghurt:* plain yoghurt first, then strained, skyr, kefir, concentrated.
- *Cheese leaves:* the families run in the order of the Codex and statute ladders, wet to dry (fresh, brined, stretched, bloomy, washed, semi-hard, hard; blue and processed last). Inside a leaf, the most widely sold variety first, then alphabetical (no attested ordering beyond this). Named forms by maker, then first-registration year as attested, then alphabetical.
- *Eggs:* hen's egg first, then other species, then preserved.
- *Formula:* by age of the infant ascending (infant formula, follow-on, growing-up milk).
- *Plant:* drinks by market prominence (soy, oat, almond, coconut), then the replaced products.

## 9. Boundaries

Closest cross-boundary objects and the precedence sentence for each. These are proposals for the owner; they restate and extend the key and do not change it.

1. **J3 and J5 (pantry).** *Butter and ghee are J3; margarine, plant-only butter alternatives, lard and other cooking fats are J5; a spread that contains butter (butter and rapeseed oil blend) stays in J3.* Coconut milk and coconut cream are J5, and the coconut drink sold as a milk alternative is J3. Tofu, tempeh and seitan are J5 even when sold as "cheese-style"; a plant product made to replace cheese, yoghurt, milk or cream is J3 (open question 3). Cheese sauces and dairy-based cooking sauces are J5; cream cheese and processed cheese spread are J3.
2. **J3 and H2 (health).** *An infant formula that is a normal food is J3; a formula made for a named medical condition (hypoallergenic, amino-acid, metabolic) is H2; sports-nutrition whey and casein powders and meal replacements are H2 in every form.* Hungry-baby and stage-numbered formula are J3 [S33][S35]; HA, AR and Comfort ranges [S35] are open question 6. Lactase tablets and breast pumps are H2.
3. **J3 and prepared food (step 1, Culinary).** *A cheese, yoghurt, butter, egg or milk as bought is J3; custard, rice pudding, dairy desserts, ice cream, cheesecake, omelettes, cheese dishes, milkshakes, and cooked or filled egg products are prepared food and OUT.* Hard-boiled and pickled eggs, flavoured milks and drinking yoghurts are open question 4. Preserved eggs (salted, century) follow the key (curing, salting, pickling a single ingredient does not make it prepared) and stay J3.
4. **J3 and C3 (living cultures).** *Kefir grains, yoghurt and sourdough starters and other living cultures kept to grow are C3; the kefir, yoghurt and cheese made from them are J3.* Eggs for hatching are OUT (step 1).
5. **J3 and B10 (animal food) and J7 (alcohol).** *Milk, milk replacer, eggs and dairy-based treats made for animals are B10; milk-based alcoholic drinks in sealed containers (cream liqueurs, egg liqueurs) are J7.* Kumys, a Codex fermented milk that may contain 0.5 per cent or more ethanol [S14], stays J3 unless sold as an alcoholic drink (open question 4).
6. **J3 and B5 (kitchen and dining).** *A cheese board, egg cup, cheese knife, butter dish, egg boiler or cheese-making equipment is B5 (made to prepare, store or serve food); the empty egg box or cheese crate kept afterwards is B1; a gift set of cheeses is filed by its item of greatest value, which is J3 if the cheese is the greatest-value item.*
7. **J3 and J6 (coffee).** *Coffee creamer and whitener are J3 if made of milk or plant ingredients as a cream replacer; powdered milk-tea and hot-chocolate mixes are J6.* (Creamers: open question 3.)

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** J3 > 7 sub-families by product family (Milk; Cream and butter; Yoghurt and fermented milks; Cheese; Eggs; Milk powders and infant formula; Plant-based alternatives) > leaves only where a sub-family exceeds about 12 kinds (Cream and butter 2 leaves; Cheese 9 leaves by texture and ripening family; Plant-based alternatives 3 leaves by the replaced product) > kinds at product-type level (variety for cheese) > named forms for protected specifications (Rule P) and maker lines. **Maximum depth: four levels below J3 (cheese), three elsewhere.** Fat level, salt, treatment, milk species, lactose-free, organic, farming code, class, size, grade, stage, protein base, plant base, age and format are attributes with standards named. Tier entries sit on makers, lines and named forms only; the protected-name table and the maker ledger hold origin and maker data.

**Links to Culinary (SV-H1, read from `/home/user/culinary-taxonomy/culinary/FRAMEWORK_v1.md`):** Fresh cheeses H1-1 to Fresh and whey cheeses; Brined H1-2 to Brined; Stretched-curd H1-3 to Stretched-curd; Bloomy H1-4; Washed H1-5; Semi-hard H1-6 (Culinary also files processed and sliced cheese here; J3 has its own Processed cheese leaf); Hard and long-aged H1-7; Blue H1-8. Culinary's H1-9 Dried and Preserved Dairy has no J3 home (open question 7). H1-10 Cultured Creams and Savory Yogurts maps to two J3 homes: soured cream and crème fraîche (Cream) and labneh (Yoghurt, as concentrated fermented milk). Egg dishes (SV-E4) are prepared and stay in Culinary; preserved eggs (E4-6) link to the J3 preserved-egg kind.

**Owner decisions needed:**
1. **Plant alternatives:** a separate sub-family (recommended, Candidate A) or folded into each family with a plant flag (Candidate B, the retail aisle practice)?
2. **Butter-oil blends, plant butters and margarine:** the key sends cooking fats to J5 and keeps butter in J3. Recommended: blends containing butter stay J3; plant-only butter alternatives and margarine go to J5. The owner may prefer one retail "spreads" home.
3. **Plant cream, creamers and plant egg alternatives** (OGGS aquafaba [S6], Waitrose lists it with eggs): J3 plant sub-family (recommended, because the key says "plant-based alternatives to them") or J5 beside tofu and plant proteins?
4. **Flavoured milk, milkshakes, drinking yoghurt, ayran, lassi, kumys, hard-boiled eggs:** grocery (J3) or prepared drink and food (OUT)? Waitrose files flavoured milk with milk alternatives [S4]; Codex defines drinks based on fermented milk [S14].
5. **Evaporated and condensed milk, UHT cream, dulce de leche:** the key names milk powders but not preserved milks. Recommended: Milk sub-family. Alternative: J5 tinned goods.
6. **Growing-up and toddler milk (12 months and over) and ranges sold for reflux, colic or allergy (HA, AR, Comfort [S35]):** the key keeps "infant formula and follow-on milks" in J3 and sends formulas "made for a medical condition" to H2. Recommended: growing-up milk J3; a range labelled for a named condition H2; partially hydrolysed ranges without a medical claim J3. Needs the owner's line.
7. **Dried and preserved dairy** (aaruul, qurut, chhurpi, kashk, jameed in Culinary H1-9): add a tenth Cheese leaf (recommended once a retail or reference page is fetched; none was in this batch), or leave unfiled.
8. **Skyr, quark, labneh:** yoghurt or cheese? Recommended: skyr and labneh in Yoghurt and fermented milks (Waitrose [S3], Codex [S14]); quark in Fresh and whey cheeses (Waitrose [S2], German statute [S15]).
9. **Rule P** (protected name as kind when no broader generic kind is compared, as named form otherwise) and the protected-name table as a separate ledger.
10. **Egg grade, class, size and farming code** as attributes with named standards (recommended) and the preserved or processed egg kinds.
11. **Salted and unsalted butter** as an attribute (recommended) or as kinds (Waitrose sub-categories [S5]).
12. **Cheese leaf axis:** texture and ripening family as one axis (recommended) acknowledges that Codex and the German statute use two or one numeric axes with overlapping bands [S13][S15]; the alternative is a pure moisture ladder with rind as an attribute (it loses the pasta filata and brined groups, which both standards treat as special [S13][S15]).

**Data sources still needed before data entry (S6):** a fetched retail or reference page for every † kind (evaporated milk, crème fraîche, duck and quail eggs, preserved eggs, whole milk powder); one register page per protected name; filter-facet lists from retailers beyond Waitrose; a forum or community page; a retailer in China, India or Korea; a resale or auction page for matured cheese if the owner wants collector depth.
