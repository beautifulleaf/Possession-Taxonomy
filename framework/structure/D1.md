# D1 Computers & Components — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md. Owner emphasis for D1: many PC components have clear tiers of quality set by brand or specification (GPU and CPU classes, PSU efficiency ratings and tier lists, keyboard switches, SSD types). This memo shows where the spec-ranked ordering lives and where makers set compatibility (sockets, form factors) without becoming levels. Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_D1_sources.tsv; the short keys S01 to S37 below point to them (full URLs in the key table of section 1). Call count: 16 searches, 40 fetches (cap reached). Two Chinese retail hosts (JD.com, Taobao) returned no category tree and are logged in verify/BLOCKED_HOSTS.txt; Chinese evidence therefore comes from two Chinese enthusiast ladders (S20, S21), not from a Chinese category tree.*

**One-line result.** Divide D1 by role in a computing setup (eight sub-families), then by part function (leaf, the shop category), then by the physical class buyers actually compare inside (kind), then by series or model (named form). Carry sockets, chipsets, DDR and PCIe generations, M.2 lengths and board, case and PSU form factors as compatibility attributes in a platform ledger; carry quality as a tier ledger with a stated scale and source; carry makers in a maker ledger with an OEM-to-brand map.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D1 Computers & Components (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, first branch).** "A desktop, laptop or convertible computer, its components, peripherals (keyboards, mice, monitors, printers, scanners and multifunction printers, webcams, speech microphones, drawing tablets, 3D printers, barcode scanners; computer speakers are D3), storage, networking, uninterruptible power supplies, point-of-sale terminals, card readers and cash registers, hardware wallets and security keys, cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices (USB, HDMI, audio and charging cables, whatever device they serve), loose electronic components and maker boards." Note on the branch: a device whose screen and keyboard can separate is D2 if sold as a tablet with an optional keyboard, and D1 if the keyboard comes with it.

**Rules and notes that already touch D1.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): a graphics card goes to Computers, and printer ink goes to Computers. Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts only when made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time; furniture, racks and shelves are never parts.
- Step 22 note (E3): microphones made for speech, calls or streaming are D1; headphones, turntables and speakers are D3 unless sold as studio, stage or DJ gear.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object (smart lock, camera, robot vacuum) is filed by its task at step 30; connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 5 note: software that comes built into a device is part of the device; a separately paid licence or unlock is I1.
- Step 31 (C5): printers, scanners and multifunction printers are D1; copiers, shredders, laminators and label makers are C5.
- Step 32 (C8): mains cables, extension leads, plugs, fuses and household batteries are C8.
- Step 21 note (F1): blank recording media are F1. Step 19 (F5): payment, access and membership cards are F5.
- Step 24 branch D5: games consoles, handhelds, controllers and VR headsets are D5.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D1** (never levels): brand and maker, price band and quality tier, era (retro), scene and subculture (gaming, overclocking, mechanical keyboards), market (consumer, server, professional), connectivity, condition (new, refurbished, used), edition. The owner's emphasis adds one more instruction: platform compatibility codes (socket, chipset, form factor) are attributes, not levels.

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **80 PLUS (CLEAResult) [S15]**: seven ordered certifications (Standard, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Titanium, Ruby) with efficiency thresholds at 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100% load, separate tables for 115 V internal non-redundant, 230 V EU, redundant data-centre and industrial applications. - **Cybenetics [S16]** runs voluntary certification: ETA (efficiency), LAMBDA (noise, graded A++, A+, A, A-, Standard++, Standard+, Standard by dB(A) band), SAFE, and for cooling DELTA (chassis soundproofing) and PHI (fan airflow and static pressure).

These bodies set **compatibility and performance classes**, not quality tiers.
- **ATX and motherboard form factors [S29, S30]**: Wikipedia lists form factors with originator and date (ATX, Intel 1995, 305 x 244 mm; microATX, Intel 1996; Mini-ITX, VIA 2001, 170 x 170 mm; SSI CEB/EEB/MEB from the SSI forum; HPTX, EVGA; XL-ATX, MSI), grouped by board size; the ATX page adds PSU derivatives (SFX, TFX, WTX) that keep the connectors and shrink the case.

### Where sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

- **Agree:** the core part list is the same everywhere (CPU, motherboard, memory, graphics card, power supply, case, cooling, SSD/HDD): S01, S02, S04, S05, S06, S09.
- **Split on storage:** PCPartPicker keeps SSD and HDD in one "Storage" category [S01]; Tsukumo, Dospara and Geizhals separate SSD, HDD and (Tsukumo, Geizhals) external storage [S02, S04, S05]; Tom's Hardware ranks SSDs of every interface in one list [S19].
- **Split on memory:** Tsukumo separates desktop, notebook and Mac memory [S02]; Geizhals separates system-specific memory [S05]; PCPartPicker has one Memory category [S01].
- **Split on maker and platform:** Japanese shops and Kakaku put the chip maker or platform in the navigation (Intel/AMD for CPUs and boards, NVIDIA/AMD for cards) [S02, S03]; PCPartPicker and Geizhals use function first [S01, S05]. Kakaku splits chip maker from board maker, which are two different maker roles [S03].
- **Split on cooling and power:** customs classification files PSUs and CPU fans outside the "computer parts" heading [S31], while every shop treats them as core parts.
- **Boundary leaks:** S01 holds operating systems, headphones and speakers; S02 holds gamepads, joysticks and racing controllers among input devices; S05 holds tablets, surveillance cameras and RFID media. The placement key sends these to I1, D3, D5, D2 and B8.
- **Platform versus tier:** a chipset, socket or generation label can be both a compatibility code and a ranked class (PCIe Gen4 requires Ryzen 3000 or newer or Intel 11th generation or newer [S26]), so a ranked scale is sometimes also a compatibility test.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Part function (what slot or job in the setup), then compatibility, then tier, then looks.** The evidence, in the order a buyer meets it:

1. **Function first.** Every shop and price tool opens on the part or machine type: PCPartPicker's 25 categories [S01], Tsukumo's 15 top groups [S02], Dospara's 19 [S04], Geizhals' 20-plus groups [S05], Overclockers' 11 [S06], eBay's Computer Components & Parts [S09]. The same list recurs: CPU, motherboard, memory, graphics card, storage, power supply, case, cooling.
2. **Compatibility second, as filters that exclude, not rank.** Kakaku's motherboard facets run chip maker, chipset, socket, form factor, M.2 slot count [S03]. Dospara prints socket, chipset and form factor on every board and "supported motherboard sizes" on every case [S04]. Overclockers explains PSU form factors as a case-fit question [S07]. The PCIe 5.0 Gen5 SSD needs an Intel 12th-generation or Ryzen 7000 platform [S26]. The buyer's first hard question is "will it fit and run on what I have", and the answer is a code (LGA1851, AM5, DDR5, ATX, 2280, PCIe Gen4).
3. **Tier or price third.** Once compatible, buyers sort by performance or rating: CPU filters by cores, TDP and packaging [S06]; GPU and CPU hierarchies rank "solely by performance" [S17, S18]; Chinese ladders put rank on one axis and vendor generation on the other [S21]; PSU buyers read the Cultists tier [S14] and the 80 PLUS level [S15].
4. **Looks and scene last.** White-model and rear-routed-cable flags [S03], "decoration and lighting parts" as a 34-item category [S04], 750 of 1,230 keyboards labelled gaming at Tsukumo [S02].
5. **For systems and boards, the first axis is form.** Used-computer shops divide by laptop, desktop PC, mini PC and all-in-one [S10]; maker boards divide by whether they run an operating system (Raspberry Pi) or one program (Arduino, ESP32, Pico) [S37]; keyboards by size class (full-size to 40%) [S25].

**Questions buyers ask (shop FAQs).** "What is the difference between Intel Core i3, i5, i7 and i9?" and "Does my CPU have graphics?" [S06]; "What wattage power supply do I need?" and "What are PSU form factors?" [S07]; "Which is better, NVMe or M.2?" (M.2 is the slot, NVMe the protocol) [S26]; which switch for gaming, typing or an open office [S22].

**Implication.** The tree axis is function (what the object does in a computing setup). Compatibility codes sit beside it as attributes, because they filter every leaf rather than defining a branch: a Ryzen CPU, an ATX board and a DDR5 kit are in three different leaves yet share one platform. Tier is a ranking column, not a branch.

## 4. Depth and size

*All counts are estimates unless a fetched page states them. The 40 fetches did not count distinct models.*

- **Generic kinds.** Tsukumo's parts tree has 241 second- and third-level nodes [S02]; most repeat compatibility codes (DDR5 DIMM, DDR5 SODIMM, DDR4 DIMM ...; USB; 2.5-inch/3.5-inch), and after merging them there are roughly 100 distinct physical classes. Adding what that tree omits (monitors, printers and scanners, routers, switches, access points, UPS, cables, hubs, complete computers, point-of-sale and security devices, maker boards) gives roughly **200 to 250 kinds for computers, parts and peripherals proper**. Loose electronic components could add 150 to 250 more if carried to Mouser's class level [S08] (capacitors, resistors, inductors, relays, sensors, MOSFETs, connectors and so on), for a total of about **350 to 500**.
- **Named forms (models, series, iconic designs).** Shop level is far larger than series level: Tsukumo's parts tree alone lists about 21,700 items [S02], Dospara 2,339 [S04], Kakaku registers 841 motherboards [S03]. At the level this memo recommends (chip model for CPU and GPU, series for PSU, SSD and cooler, switch model, board model), a rough count is **4,000 to 8,000** (CPU about 1,500; GPU about 600; PSU series about 800; SSD series about 600; keyboards, mice and switches about 1,000; boards and systems the rest). A full SKU ledger would be tens of thousands and is not recommended.
- **Where depth concentrates.** (1) CPU and GPU lines, where each generation adds a ladder of 7 to 10 models (eight desktop RTX 50 models alone [S28]); (2) PSU series, which carry the most tier information [S14, S15, S16]; (3) SSD and memory, where specification classes multiply (NAND type, PCIe generation, M.2 length, DDR generation) [S26, S27]; (4) keyboards and switches, where hundreds of switch models sit in one tabulated list [S24]; (5) breadth rather than depth in loose components (9.5 million parts at Mouser [S08]).
- **Broad leaves** (hundreds of named forms, active comparison): graphics cards, processors, motherboards, internal SSDs, memory, PC power supplies, PC cases, CPU coolers and fans, keyboards, mice. **Medium leaves:** internal HDDs, monitors, laptops, routers, UPS, webcams, NAS, cables. **Narrow leaves** (under about 50 named forms): sound cards, video capture cards, floppy and magneto-optical drives, trackballs, KVM switches, fan controllers, thermal paste.

Two cautions from the evidence: tiers expire (the fetched PSU list was last updated 2023-04-11 [S14]; GPU street prices ran "well above MSRP" at launch [S28]), so every row needs an as-of date; and a scale can double as a compatibility test, so the platform ledger and the tier ledger must stay separate tables joined on the named form.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Verdict: platform.** Makers set what fits what, and that is the first thing a buyer checks. Identity pockets exist (enthusiast keyboards, vintage machines); incidental brands cover cables, thermal paste and fittings. A **maker ledger is needed**, with a role column and an OEM-to-brand map, plus a separate **platform ledger** for the compatibility codes.

**Evidence that makers set platforms.**
- Intel and AMD define the CPU socket and chipset families a board must match (LGA1851, LGA1700, AM5, AM4) [S03]; NVIDIA, AMD and Intel (Arc) define the GPU families [S02, S21].
- Form factors have named originators: ATX (Intel, 1995), microATX (Intel, 1996), Mini-ITX (VIA, 2001), SSI CEB/EEB/MEB (the SSI forum), HPTX (EVGA), XL-ATX (MSI) [S29]; the SFX and TFX PSU forms keep the ATX connectors and change the box [S30]. Consortia (PCI-SIG, JEDEC, USB-IF) set bus, memory and port standards [S32, S33, S34]. The RTX 50 series mandates the 16-pin 12V-2x6 connector [S28].
- The MX-style stem that Cherry introduced is the compatibility standard for most keyboard switches; clones are mapped to a "Cherry MX equivalent" [S24]; Topre switches exist only in HHKB and Realforce keyboards [S22].

**Evidence that makers come in layers (why the ledger needs roles).**
- *Chip maker versus board maker:* Kakaku has a maker facet (ASUS, MSI, ASRock, GIGABYTE, Supermicro) and a separate chip-maker facet (AMD, Intel) [S03]; a card such as the ASUS ROG Astral RTX 5090 OC combines an NVIDIA GPU with an ASUS board [S05].
- *Brand versus factory:* Cultists lists "Seasonic / Haiyun", "Super Flower / Zhenhua", "Segotep / Inaza / Colorful" and footnotes platform builders (FSP, CWT) behind retail brands [S14]. Wikipedia's switch table has separate Brand and OEM columns (Razer Green is Kailh/Greetech; Logitech GX Blue is Kailh; Romer-G is Omron) [S24].
- *House brands:* Dospara Select is a maker facet with 72 items [S04].
- *Distributor view:* Mouser's manufacturer facet runs from Raspberry Pi (441) to Microchip (106,995) [S08].

**Maker ledger columns** (each row attested by a fetched page): maker, role (chip designer, board partner, PSU platform builder, switch OEM, house brand, distributor), brand-of (OEM map), known-for kinds, scene tags (gaming, silent, SFF, retro), maker-level tier if any (none found at maker level: tiers attach to series), price band, country, founding era. **Platform ledger columns:** code (LGA1851, AM5, DDR5, PCIe 5.0, ATX, SFX, M.2 2280, ATX 3.1), type (socket, chipset, memory generation, bus generation, form factor, connector, port), originator, introduced, edges (board supports socket; case supports board sizes; PSU form factor fits case; chipset requires socket). The platform ledger stays off the tree and drives a generated "what fits" view.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Recommendation: spec order where a ladder exists (class high to low, generation newest first), canonical form first elsewhere.**
- *Kinds inside a leaf:* the commonest or canonical form first, then variants: internal before external, DIMM before SODIMM, tower before mini PC, keyboard before numeric keypad. This is the order Tsukumo and Geizhals follow (desktop memory 620 before notebook 220 [S02]; SSD before HDD before external in Geizhals [S05]).
- *Named forms in a ladder leaf (CPU, GPU):* maker family, then generation newest first, then class high to low. Tsukumo lists Core Ultra 9, Core i9, Core Ultra 7, Core i7 ... Core i3 and RTX 50 down to GT 700 [S02]; Tom's Hardware ranks fastest to slowest [S17]; the mydrivers ladder fixes performance as rows and generation as columns [S21].
- *Named forms in a tier-ranked leaf (PSU, SSD):* spec order (efficiency level, then wattage or capacity), not tier letter, because tiers are revised and expire [S14].
- *Leaves without a ladder (cases, cables, keyboards, switches):* canonical form, then maker alphabetical.
- *Retro machines:* chronology, shown as a separate browse axis exactly as the museum does [S35].

**Reason:** a stored order must be maker-neutral and stable. Tier and benchmark rows change with each revision; spec class and generation do not. The tier ledger can sort any view on demand.

## 9. Boundaries

Closest cross-boundary objects, with the precedence sentence for each neighbour. Items marked (S3) follow the parts-follow-the-whole rule.

1. **D3 Audio & Video.** *A display sold for use with a computer is D1, a TV, projector or smart display is D3, and headphones, headsets and speakers are D3 even when a PC-parts site lists them as peripherals; a TV-tuner card made to fit a computer is D1 (S3).* PCPartPicker lists headphones and speakers among its peripherals [S01]; Kakaku lists a PC TV tuner under パソコン [S11]. A microphone made for speech, calls or streaming is D1; one made for music is E3 (step 22 note). An internal or USB sound card is D1; an audio interface sold for recording is E3.
2. **D5 Video Games.** *Game controllers, joysticks, arcade and racing controllers, VR headsets, consoles and handheld game machines are D5 even when a PC shop lists them with input devices; a gaming keyboard, mouse, laptop or desktop is D1 because gaming is a scene modifier.* Tsukumo files joypads, joysticks, arcade and racing controllers inside input devices [S02]; Kakaku lists head-mounted displays under PC [S11].
3. **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices.** *A tablet sold with an optional keyboard is D2, a computer whose keyboard comes with it is D1, and power banks and smartwatches are D2; a laptop's own AC adapter or battery is D1 (S3), a phone charger or power bank is D2.* Geizhals puts laptop power adapters and chargers under Notebooks and keeps tablets in their own group [S05]; Kakaku lists laptop batteries [S11].
4. **C5 Office & Stationery.** *A printer, scanner or multifunction printer for use with a computer is D1, and ink, toner and 3D-printer filament made for one printer type are D1 (S3); copiers, shredders, laminators and label makers whose main function is not printing from or scanning to a computer are C5, as is paper.* Kakaku lists copy and printer paper beside PC supplies [S11].

A memory card (SD, CompactFlash, CFexpress) is D1 storage whatever device it is sold for, because it is not made for one kind of object [S02].

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure: Candidate A.** Eight sub-families by role in a computing setup (D1.1 Complete computers; D1.2 Internal components; D1.3 Storage and media; D1.4 Input, display and imaging; D1.5 Network and power; D1.6 Cables, adapters and hubs; D1.7 Business and security hardware; D1.8 Embedded, maker and loose components); leaves by part function; kinds by the physical class buyers compare inside; named forms by series or model. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Cables, cases, cooling and loose components stop at kind (no named forms by default). Sockets, chipsets, memory and bus generations, M.2 lengths and form factors live in a **platform ledger**; quality lives in a dated **tier ledger** with named scales; makers in a **maker ledger** with roles and an OEM map; resale condition in a **condition ledger**; gaming, SFF, silent, homelab, maker and retro are **generated views**.

**Summary answers.** Choice level: kind at part-function level for core parts and at physical class where buyers never compare across (memory form, internal against external storage, switch feel type); named form at chip model, series or switch model. Tier type: spec-ranked first, consensus-ranked for PSU quality and switches, preference elsewhere. Maker centrality: platform, with a role-based maker ledger.

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. **Compatibility codes as attributes.** Confirm that sockets, chipsets, DDR and PCIe generations, M.2 lengths and board, case and PSU form factors are attributes. Two calls are close: (a) memory split into DIMM and SODIMM kinds (recommended, because buyers never compare across them and three shops separate them) against one memory-module kind; (b) PC case size classes (mid-tower, Mini-ITX ...) as an attribute (recommended) against kinds, where Tsukumo makes them branches [S02].
2. **Named-form grain.** GPU model against partner card (recommended: GPU model, partner cards as ledger variants); PSU series scoped by wattage against SKU; whether any SKU-level ledger is wanted (not recommended).
3. **Keyboard switches and sizes.** Three switch kinds by feel type against one kind with a feel attribute; keyboard size classes as attribute (recommended, the percentages are rough) against kinds.
4. **Loose electronic components.** Carry to Mouser's class level (150 to 250 more kinds), cap at a few examples, or give them their own stage-2 memo.
5. **Tier ledger scope and licensing.** Which scales to store (maker class, standard grade, benchmark rank, community tier), how often to refresh, and whether third-party community lists (Cultists) may be recorded as dated, attributed opinion.
6. **Complete computers and retro.** Whether historic machine classes (home computer, trainer kit, pocket computer) belong in D1.1; pocket computers and organisers may belong to D2. Whether handheld gaming PCs go to D5 (the key's D5 branch says handhelds do).
7. **Boundary rulings** in section 9: PC game controllers to D5; headsets with a boom microphone to D3; USB-C chargers and GaN wall chargers (D1 as laptop adapters or D2 as phone chargers); RFID tags and cards; NAS and network cameras; 3D-printer filament to D1.
8. **Attestation gap.** No fetched page in this batch attests POS terminals, card readers, cash registers, hardware wallets, security keys, printers, scanners, multifunction printers, 3D printers, barcode scanners or speech microphones; D1.7 and part of D1.4 need one sourcing batch before any name is added (S6).
9. **Chinese retail evidence.** JD.com and Taobao returned no category tree through the allowed tools; approve a different Chinese retailer or accept the two Chinese ladders as the Chinese evidence.
10. **Bundles.** CPU and board combos, upgrade bundles and parts sets are sets of separate items; confirm that they are filed per item (no D1 rule exists for sets).
