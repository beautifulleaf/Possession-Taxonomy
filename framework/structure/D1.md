## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D1 Computers & Components (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key v0.7, step 24, first branch).** "A desktop, laptop or convertible computer, its components, peripherals (keyboards, mice, monitors, printers, scanners and multifunction printers, webcams, speech microphones, drawing tablets, 3D printers, barcode scanners; computer speakers are D3), storage, networking, uninterruptible power supplies, point-of-sale terminals, card readers and cash registers, hardware wallets and security keys, cables with standard connectors and adapters made to connect electronic devices (USB, HDMI, audio, instrument, microphone, patch, MIDI and charging cables; a cable with a proprietary connector made for one device follows it, S3b), generic chargers (USB, GaN, Qi), loose electronic components and maker boards." Note on the branch: a device whose screen and keyboard can separate is D2 if sold as a tablet with an optional keyboard, and D1 if the keyboard comes with it.

**Rules and notes that already touch D1.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): a graphics card goes to Computers, and printer ink goes to Computers (sub-family D1.9). Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts only when made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time (a laptop sleeve); furniture, racks and shelves are never parts.
- Step 22 note (E3): microphones made for speech, calls or streaming are D1; headphones, turntables and speakers are D3 unless sold as studio, stage or DJ gear.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object (smart lock, camera, robot vacuum) is filed by its task at step 30; connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 5 note: software that comes built into a device is part of the device; a separately paid licence or unlock is I1.
- Step 31 (C5): printers, scanners and multifunction printers are D1; copiers, shredders, laminators, label makers and label printers of any connection are C5 (K-158).
- Step 32 (C8): mains cables, extension leads, plugs, fuses and household batteries are C8.
- Step 21 note (F1): blank recording media are F1. Step 19 (F5): payment, access and membership cards are F5.
- S3b (K-2, K-3, K-4, K-14): a cable with a proprietary connector made for one device follows it, a cable with standard connectors is D1; a generic charger is D1; a battery made for one device follows it; a recorder sold only to run security cameras is B8, a general network drive is D1.
- Step 24 branch D5: games consoles, handhelds (handheld gaming PCs included, K-152), controllers and VR headsets are D5.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D1** (never levels): brand and maker, price band and quality tier, era (retro), scene and subculture (gaming, overclocking, mechanical keyboards), market (consumer, server, professional), connectivity, condition (new, refurbished, used; scales only, never item data), edition. The owner's emphasis adds one more instruction: platform compatibility codes (socket, chipset, form factor) are attributes, not levels.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Part function (what slot or job in the setup), then compatibility, then tier, then looks.** The evidence, in the order a buyer meets it:

1. **Function first.** Every shop and price tool opens on the part or machine type: PCPartPicker's 25 categories [S01], Tsukumo's 15 top groups [S02], Dospara's 19 [S04], Geizhals' 20-plus groups [S05], Overclockers' 11 [S06], eBay's Computer Components & Parts [S09]. The same list recurs: CPU, motherboard, memory, graphics card, storage, power supply, case, cooling.
2. **Compatibility second, as filters that exclude, not rank.** Kakaku's motherboard facets run chip maker, chipset, socket, form factor, M.2 slot count [S03]. Dospara prints socket, chipset and form factor on every board and "supported motherboard sizes" on every case [S04]. Overclockers explains PSU form factors as a case-fit question [S07]. The PCIe 5.0 Gen5 SSD needs an Intel 12th-generation or Ryzen 7000 platform [S26]. The buyer's first hard question is "will it fit and run on what I have", and the answer is a code (LGA1851, AM5, DDR5, ATX, 2280, PCIe Gen4).
3. **Tier or price third.** Once compatible, buyers sort by performance or rating: CPU filters by cores, TDP and packaging [S06]; GPU and CPU hierarchies rank "solely by performance" [S17, S18]; Chinese ladders put rank on one axis and vendor generation on the other [S21]; PSU buyers read the Cultists tier [S14] and the 80 PLUS level [S15].
4. **Looks and scene last.** White-model and rear-routed-cable flags [S03], "decoration and lighting parts" as a 34-item category [S04], 750 of 1,230 keyboards labelled gaming at Tsukumo [S02].
5. **For systems and boards, the first axis is form.** Used-computer shops divide by laptop, desktop PC, mini PC and all-in-one [S10]; maker boards divide by whether they run an operating system (Raspberry Pi) or one program (Arduino, ESP32, Pico) [S37]; keyboards by size class (full-size to 40%) [S25].

**Questions buyers ask (shop FAQs).** "What is the difference between Intel Core i3, i5, i7 and i9?" and "Does my CPU have graphics?" [S06]; "What wattage power supply do I need?" and "What are PSU form factors?" [S07]; "Which is better, NVMe or M.2?" (M.2 is the slot, NVMe the protocol) [S26]; which switch for gaming, typing or an open office [S22].

**Implication.** The tree axis is function (what the object does in a computing setup). Compatibility codes sit beside it as attributes, because they filter every leaf rather than defining a branch: a Ryzen CPU, an ATX board and a DDR5 kit are in three different leaves yet share one platform. Tier is a ranking entry on a named form, line or maker, never a branch and never on a kind.

## 4. Depth and size

*All counts are estimates unless a fetched page states them. The 40 fetches did not count distinct models.*

- **Generic kinds.** Tsukumo's parts tree has 241 second- and third-level nodes [S02]; most repeat compatibility codes (DDR5 DIMM, DDR5 SODIMM, DDR4 DIMM ...; USB; 2.5-inch/3.5-inch), and after merging them there are roughly 100 distinct physical classes. Adding what that tree omits (monitors, printers and scanners, routers, switches, access points, UPS, cables, hubs, complete computers, point-of-sale and security devices, maker boards) gives roughly **200 to 250 kinds for computers, parts and peripherals proper**. Loose electronic components are carried to Mouser's class level (D-9) and add 150 to 250 more kinds [S08] (capacitors, resistors, inductors, relays, sensors, MOSFETs, connectors and so on), harvested from that catalogue in stage 3, for a total of about **350 to 500**.
- **Named forms (models, series, iconic designs).** Shop level is far larger than series level: Tsukumo's parts tree alone lists about 21,700 items [S02], Dospara 2,339 [S04], Kakaku registers 841 motherboards [S03]. At the level this memo recommends (chip model for CPU and GPU, series for PSU, SSD and cooler, switch model, board model), a rough count is **4,000 to 8,000** (CPU about 1,500; GPU about 600; PSU series about 800; SSD series about 600; keyboards, mice and switches about 1,000; boards and systems the rest). There is no cap (CV-7): named forms are seeded from the existing catalogues and grow with the attested evidence; SKUs, partner builds and capacities go to the C3 variant ledger wherever a community compares them (CV-8), not to named forms.
- **Where depth concentrates.** (1) CPU and GPU lines, where each generation adds a ladder of 7 to 10 models (eight desktop RTX 50 models alone [S28]); (2) PSU series, which carry the most tier information [S14, S15, S16]; (3) SSD and memory, where specification classes multiply (NAND type, PCIe generation, M.2 length, DDR generation) [S26, S27]; (4) keyboards and switches, where hundreds of switch models sit in one tabulated list [S24]; (5) breadth rather than depth in loose components (9.5 million parts at Mouser [S08]).
- **Broad leaves** (hundreds of named forms, active comparison): graphics cards, processors, motherboards, internal SSDs, memory, PC power supplies, PC cases, CPU coolers and fans, keyboards, mice. **Medium leaves:** internal HDDs, monitors, laptops, routers, UPS, webcams, NAS, cables. **Narrow leaves** (under about 50 named forms): sound cards, video capture cards, floppy and magneto-optical drives, trackballs, KVM switches, fan controllers, thermal paste.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied.** A kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. Where buyers compare across a distinction, that distinction is an attribute; where they never compare across it, it is a kind boundary. Compatibility codes (socket, chipset, DDR and PCIe generation, M.2 length, board, case and PSU form factor) are attributes held in the compatibility ledger in every row below, as the owner asked.

**One form-factor rule (review D1-6).** A form factor is a kind boundary where buyers never compare across it and one cannot replace the other in the same slot (DIMM and SODIMM). It is an attribute where buyers compare across it inside one kind (ATX and SFX PSUs on one tier list [S14]; ATX and Mini-ITX boards in one category [S03]; case size classes, D-6).

**Technology splits under C10(b).** Internal SSD and internal HDD are separate kinds: most SSDs are M.2 cards with no HDD equivalent [S26], and Tom's Hardware ranks SSDs in their own list [S19]. Air and all-in-one liquid coolers are separate kinds: radiator, pump and mounting differ [S02, S05]. NAND type, PCIe generation, DDR generation, display and switch technology otherwise stay attributes. Memory split into DIMM and SODIMM kinds is decided (D-5).

**Named forms** carry `maker_bound` (C2(a)): every D1 named form is maker-bound (chip model, PSU series, SSD series, board model, switch model; Holy Panda is maker-bound, Drop). A maker's class names (Core Ultra 5, Core i7, Ryzen 7, GeForce RTX 50 series) are lines-table rows, not named forms. Configurations (VRAM size, PSU wattage within a series, capacity) are C3 variants of type `configuration`, a board maker's build of a GPU is a variant of type `partner build` (both variant types are in the C3 list, CV-9; the PLAYBACK test, D-24, decides named form against variant; GPU model and PSU series are the named forms, D-8).

**How tier appears as a modifier, never a level.** A tier entry is (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) per C4(a), where the subject is a named form, line or maker. Tier entries carry `direction` (higher is better), `scope` (for example "850 to 1000 W"), `test_version`, `stale_after` and the computed `disputed` flag (C4(a)); each scheme is registered once with its `scale_class` (C4(c)). A tier scheme enters the data on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10); a second source or region only adds weight and feeds `disputed`: the GPU hierarchies have two (US and CN), the Cultists list one, and all enter. Two cautions from the evidence: tiers expire (the fetched PSU list was last updated 2023-04-11 [S14]; GPU street prices ran "well above MSRP" at launch [S28]), so every row needs an as-of date; and a scale can double as a compatibility test, so the compatibility ledger and the tier entries stay separate tables joined on the named form. Price bands follow C4(d), computed from a dated, sourced list price in USD.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Verdict: platform.** Makers set what fits what, and that is the first thing a buyer checks. Identity pockets exist (enthusiast keyboards, vintage machines); incidental brands cover cables, thermal paste and fittings. A **maker ledger is needed** (C1 core schema), plus a **compatibility ledger** (C13) for the compatibility codes.

**Evidence that makers set platforms.**
- Intel and AMD define the CPU socket and chipset families a board must match (LGA1851, LGA1700, AM5, AM4) [S03]; NVIDIA, AMD and Intel (Arc) define the GPU families [S02, S21].
- Form factors have named originators: ATX (Intel, 1995), microATX (Intel, 1996), Mini-ITX (VIA, 2001), SSI CEB/EEB/MEB (the SSI forum), HPTX (EVGA), XL-ATX (MSI) [S29]; the SFX and TFX PSU forms keep the ATX connectors and change the box [S30]. Consortia (PCI-SIG, JEDEC, USB-IF) set bus, memory and port standards [S32, S33, S34]. The RTX 50 series mandates the 16-pin 12V-2x6 connector [S28].
- The MX-style stem that Cherry introduced is the compatibility standard for most keyboard switches; clones are mapped to a "Cherry MX equivalent" [S24]; Topre switches exist only in HHKB and Realforce keyboards [S22].

**Evidence that makers come in layers (why the ledger needs relations).**
- *Chip maker versus board maker:* Kakaku has a maker facet (ASUS, MSI, ASRock, GIGABYTE, Supermicro) and a separate chip-maker facet (AMD, Intel) [S03]; a card such as the ASUS ROG Astral RTX 5090 OC combines an NVIDIA GPU with an ASUS board [S05].
- *Brand versus factory:* Cultists lists "Seasonic / Haiyun", "Super Flower / Zhenhua", "Segotep / Inaza / Colorful" and footnotes platform builders (FSP, CWT) behind retail brands [S14]. Wikipedia's switch table has separate Brand and OEM columns (Razer Green is Kailh/Greetech; Logitech GX Blue is Kailh; Romer-G is Omron) [S24].
- *House brands:* Dospara Select is a maker facet with 72 items [S04].
- *Distributor view:* Mouser's manufacturer facet runs from Raspberry Pi (441) to Microchip (106,995) [S08]. Mouser is a source, not a maker, so no `distributor` class exists.

**Maker ledger (C1).** Core columns: `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class`; `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `licensor_id`; `authority_id`; `scene_tags` (C8, with `core` or `adjacent` strength); `price_band` (C4(d)); `sources`. Each row is attested by one fetched page, which admits the maker (CV-5); `known_for_kinds` is not stored but derived from the named-form ledger (CV-1). Ledgers are seeded from the existing catalogues first (PCPartPicker, Kakaku, Geizhals and the Mouser manufacturer facet [S01, S03, S05, S08]) and grow without a cap; artisan keyboard, keycap and cable makers, custom case builders and similar hobby makers are rows of class `artisan or atelier` or `microbrand`, never kinds. Mapping from this memo's earlier roles to C1 classes: chip designer (Intel, AMD, NVIDIA) and PSU platform builder (FSP, CWT) and switch OEM (Kailh) are `supplier`; board partner and PSU or SSD brand are `house or manufacture`; Dospara Select is `retail own-label`. A maker has no tier column: ladders are lines (C1) and measured or editorial tiers are tier entries on lines or named forms (C4(a)). Lines and collections, tier entries, named forms and variants are separate tables (C1).

**Maker relations (OEM-to-brand map, chip and board partners).** The brand-of map, "board partner of", "supplies chip to" and "licensed by" belong in a maker-relations table (`relation_id`, `maker_id`, `other_id`, `relation`, `scope`, `years`, `sources`) (C1, CV-3), with relation values OEM (builds for), supplies chip or panel to, board partner of, licensed by and rebrand of. The domain-wide `maker_class` list (CV-2) serves D1; the OEM map is held in that table, with `built_by_maker_id` and `licensor_id` on lines.

**Compatibility ledger (C13).** Columns: `platform_id`, `name`, `owner` (maker or standards body), `platform_type`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, `region`, `introduced`, `status`, `sources` (C13; CV-29, CV-30), and a compatibility-relations table (`from_id`, `relation`, `to_id`, `sources`) that holds this memo's earlier "edges" (board supports socket; case supports board sizes; PSU form factor fits case; chipset requires socket). `platform_type` values used: CPU socket, chipset, memory standard, bus, form factor, connector or port, charging standard; the switch mounts take the nearest value (form factor). Rows include LGA1851, AM5, DDR5, PCIe 5.0, ATX, SFX, M.2 2280, ATX 3.1 and the switch mounts MX-style, Alps and Topre. The ledger stays off the tree and drives a generated "what fits" view. Platforms are never levels.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Order (decided: C7, CV-20, CV-21).** The memo's earlier stored maker-neutral spec order is dropped. Stored order follows C7; ladder, generation and benchmark orders are generated views.

- *Kinds inside a leaf (C7):* the canonical form first, then the sub-family's secondary key: internal before external, DIMM before SODIMM, tower before mini PC, keyboard before numeric keypad. This is the order Tsukumo and Geizhals follow (desktop memory 620 before notebook 220 [S02]; SSD before HDD before external in Geizhals [S05]).
- *Named forms (C7, CV-21):* every D1 named form is maker-bound, so the stored order is by maker (ledger `name` order), then first-introduction year, then alphabetically. Non-maker named forms, if harvest finds any, come first by first-attested year.
- *Variants (C7):* by `variant_code` or year.
- *Generated views, not stored order:* maker family, then generation newest first, then class high to low for CPU and GPU (Tsukumo lists Core Ultra 9, Core i9, Core Ultra 7, Core i7 ... Core i3 and RTX 50 down to GT 700 [S02]; Tom's Hardware ranks fastest to slowest [S17]; the mydrivers ladder fixes performance as rows and generation as columns [S21]); spec order (efficiency level, then wattage or capacity) for PSU and SSD, not tier letter, because tiers are revised and expire [S14]; benchmark and tier orders; `line_rank` ladders (C1).
- *Retro machines:* chronology, shown as a generated browse axis exactly as the museum does [S35], from the `era` tag.

**Reason:** a stored order should be stable and the same across the domain. Tier and benchmark rows change with each revision, and spec class and generation are attributes that sort any view on demand.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure (Candidate A).** Nine sub-families by role in a computing setup: D1.1 Complete computers (including the historic classes keyboard computer, pocket computer and trainer kit, each with an `era` tag); D1.2 Internal components; D1.3 Storage and media; D1.4 Input, display and imaging (leaves Keyboards, Keyboard switches, Pointing, Display, Printing and scanning, Capture and switching); D1.5 Network and power; D1.6 Cables, adapters and hubs (standard-connector cables and generic chargers included); D1.7 Transaction and authentication hardware; D1.8 Embedded, maker and loose components (leaves by component family, at the Mouser class level); D1.9 Parts and accessories made for one computer or peripheral. Leaves follow the axis each sub-family states (part function in D1.2, component family in D1.8) and are skipped where a sub-family holds about 12 kinds or fewer (D1.5, D1.6, D1.7, D1.9). Kinds are the physical class buyers compare inside (DIMM and SODIMM; internal SSD and HDD; air and all-in-one liquid cooling; three keyboard-switch kinds by feel). Named forms are chip model, series or switch model, all maker-bound and uncapped; partner builds and wattage or capacity models are C3 variants. **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Cables, cases, cooling and loose components stop at kind by default. Sockets, chipsets, memory and bus generations, M.2 lengths, case, board and PSU form factors, case and keyboard size classes are attributes, with the compatibility codes in the **C13 compatibility ledger**; spec ladders and certifications are **attributes with their standard named**; maker ladders are **lines**; measured and editorial rankings are dated **tier entries** on named forms, lines or makers (never on a kind), each entering on one fetched source; makers are in the **maker ledger** with relations and the OEM map; resale condition scales sit in the **grading-scale registry** with no item data; a CPU-and-board combo is filed by its item of greatest value with a `bundle` variant; handheld gaming PCs are D5; gaming, homelab, maker, retro and similar are scene tags or generated views, each tag held back until it has a definition source.

**Summary answers.** Choice level: kind at part-function level for core parts and at physical class where buyers never compare across (memory form, internal against external storage, switch feel type); named form at chip model, series or switch model. Tier type: spec-ranked first (attributes), consensus-ranked for PSU quality and switches (tier entries), preference elsewhere. Maker centrality: platform, with a maker ledger, maker relations and a compatibility ledger.
