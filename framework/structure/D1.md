# D1 Computers & Components — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research for the owner. Research, not a decision. Prepared under framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, BRIEF.md and framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md. Owner emphasis for D1: many PC components have clear tiers of quality set by brand or specification (GPU and CPU classes, PSU efficiency ratings and tier lists, keyboard switches, SSD types). This memo shows where the spec-ranked ordering lives and where makers set compatibility (sockets, form factors) without becoming levels. Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_D1_sources.tsv; the short keys S01 to S37 below point to them (full URLs in the key table of section 1). Call count: 16 searches, 40 fetches (cap reached). Two Chinese retail hosts (JD.com, Taobao) returned no category tree and are logged in verify/BLOCKED_HOSTS.txt; Chinese evidence therefore comes from two Chinese enthusiast ladders (S20, S21), not from a Chinese category tree.*

**One-line result.** Divide D1 by role in a computing setup (eight sub-families), then by part function (leaf, the shop category), then by the physical class buyers actually compare inside (kind), then by series or model (named form). Carry sockets, chipsets, DDR and PCIe generations, M.2 lengths and board, case and PSU form factors as compatibility attributes in a platform ledger; carry quality as a tier ledger with a stated scale and source; carry makers in a maker ledger with an OEM-to-brand map.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D1 Computers & Components (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, first branch).** "A desktop, laptop or convertible computer, its components, peripherals (keyboards, mice, monitors, printers, scanners and multifunction printers, webcams, speech microphones, drawing tablets, 3D printers, barcode scanners; computer speakers are D3), storage, networking, uninterruptible power supplies, point-of-sale terminals, card readers and cash registers, hardware wallets and security keys, cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices (USB, HDMI, audio and charging cables, whatever device they serve), loose electronic components and maker boards." Note on the branch: a device whose screen and keyboard can separate is D2 if sold as a tablet with an optional keyboard, and D1 if the keyboard comes with it.

**Rules and notes that already touch D1.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): a graphics card goes to Computers, and printer ink goes to Computers. Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts only when made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time; furniture, racks and shelves are never parts.
- Step 22 note (E3): microphones made for speech, calls or streaming are D1; headphones, turntables and speakers are D3 unless sold as studio, stage or DJ gear.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object (smart lock, camera, robot vacuum) is filed by its task at step 30; connectivity is a modifier.
- Step 5 note: software that comes built into a device is part of the device; a separately paid licence or unlock is I1.
- Step 31 (C5): printers, scanners and multifunction printers are D1; copiers, shredders, laminators and label makers are C5.
- Step 32 (C8): mains cables, extension leads, plugs, fuses and household batteries are C8.
- Step 21 note (F1): blank recording media are F1. Step 19 (F5): payment, access and membership cards are F5.
- Step 24 branch D5: games consoles, handhelds, controllers and VR headsets are D5.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D1** (never levels): brand and maker, price band and quality tier, era (retro), scene and subculture (gaming, overclocking, mechanical keyboards), market (consumer, server, professional), connectivity, condition (new, refurbished, used), edition. The owner's emphasis adds one more instruction: platform compatibility codes (socket, chipset, form factor) are attributes, not levels.

### Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **80 PLUS (CLEAResult) [S15]**: seven ordered certifications (Standard, Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, Titanium, Ruby) with efficiency thresholds at 5, 10, 20, 50 and 100% load, separate tables for 115 V internal non-redundant, 230 V EU, redundant data-centre and industrial applications. - **Cybenetics [S16]** runs voluntary certification: ETA (efficiency), LAMBDA (noise, graded A++, A+, A, A-, Standard++, Standard+, Standard by dB(A) band), SAFE, and for cooling DELTA (chassis soundproofing) and PHI (fan airflow and static pressure).

These bodies set **compatibility and performance classes**, not quality tiers.
- **ATX and motherboard form factors [S29, S30]**: Wikipedia lists form factors with originator and date (ATX, Intel 1995, 305 x 244 mm; microATX, Intel 1996; Mini-ITX, VIA 2001, 170 x 170 mm; SSI CEB/EEB/MEB from the SSI forum; HPTX, EVGA; XL-ATX, MSI), grouped by board size; the ATX page adds PSU derivatives (SFX, TFX, WTX) that keep the connectors and shrink the case.

### Where sources agree and where they split the same objects differently

- **Agree:** the core part list is the same everywhere (CPU, motherboard, memory, graphics card, power supply, case, cooling, SSD/HDD): S01, S02, S04, S05, S06, S09.
- **Split on storage:** PCPartPicker keeps SSD and HDD in one "Storage" category [S01]; Tsukumo, Dospara and Geizhals separate SSD, HDD and (Tsukumo, Geizhals) external storage [S02, S04, S05]; Tom's Hardware ranks SSDs of every interface in one list [S19].
- **Split on memory:** Tsukumo separates desktop, notebook and Mac memory [S02]; Geizhals separates system-specific memory [S05]; PCPartPicker has one Memory category [S01].
- **Split on maker and platform:** Japanese shops and Kakaku put the chip maker or platform in the navigation (Intel/AMD for CPUs and boards, NVIDIA/AMD for cards) [S02, S03]; PCPartPicker and Geizhals use function first [S01, S05]. Kakaku splits chip maker from board maker, which are two different maker roles [S03].
- **Split on cooling and power:** customs classification files PSUs and CPU fans outside the "computer parts" heading [S31], while every shop treats them as core parts.
- **Boundary leaks:** S01 holds operating systems, headphones and speakers; S02 holds gamepads, joysticks and racing controllers among input devices; S05 holds tablets, surveillance cameras and RFID media. The placement key sends these to I1, D3, D5, D2 and B8.
- **Platform versus tier:** a chipset, socket or generation label can be both a compatibility code and a ranked class (PCIe Gen4 requires Ryzen 3000 or newer or Intel 11th generation or newer [S26]), so a ranked scale is sometimes also a compatibility test.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Part function (what slot or job in the setup), then compatibility, then tier, then looks.** The evidence, in the order a buyer meets it:

1. **Function first.** Every shop and price tool opens on the part or machine type: PCPartPicker's 25 categories [S01], Tsukumo's 15 top groups [S02], Dospara's 19 [S04], Geizhals' 20-plus groups [S05], Overclockers' 11 [S06], eBay's Computer Components & Parts [S09]. The same list recurs: CPU, motherboard, memory, graphics card, storage, power supply, case, cooling.
2. **Compatibility second, as filters that exclude, not rank.** Kakaku's motherboard facets run chip maker, chipset, socket, form factor, M.2 slot count [S03]. Dospara prints socket, chipset and form factor on every board and "supported motherboard sizes" on every case [S04]. Overclockers explains PSU form factors as a case-fit question [S07]. The PCIe 5.0 Gen5 SSD needs an Intel 12th-generation or Ryzen 7000 platform [S26]. The buyer's first hard question is "will it fit and run on what I have", and the answer is a code (LGA1851, AM5, DDR5, ATX, 2280, PCIe Gen4).
3. **Tier or price third.** Once compatible, buyers sort by performance or rating: CPU filters by cores, TDP and packaging [S06]; GPU and CPU hierarchies rank "solely by performance" [S17, S18]; Chinese ladders put rank on one axis and vendor generation on the other [S21]; PSU buyers read the Cultists tier [S14] and the 80 PLUS level [S15].
4. **Looks and scene last.** White-model and rear-routed-cable flags [S03], "decoration and lighting parts" as a 34-item category [S04], 750 of 1,230 keyboards labelled gaming at Tsukumo [S02].
5. **For systems and boards, the first axis is form.** Used-computer shops divide by laptop, desktop PC, mini PC and all-in-one [S10]; maker boards divide by whether they run an operating system (Raspberry Pi) or one program (Arduino, ESP32, Pico) [S37]; keyboards by size class (full-size to 40%) [S25].

**Questions buyers ask (shop FAQs).** "What is the difference between Intel Core i3, i5, i7 and i9?" and "Does my CPU have graphics?" [S06]; "What wattage power supply do I need?" and "What are PSU form factors?" [S07]; "Which is better, NVMe or M.2?" (M.2 is the slot, NVMe the protocol) [S26]; which switch for gaming, typing or an open office [S22].

**Implication.** The tree axis is function (what the object does in a computing setup). Compatibility codes sit beside it as attributes, because they filter every leaf rather than defining a branch: a Ryzen CPU, an ATX board and a DDR5 kit are in three different leaves yet share one platform. Tier is a ranking column, not a branch.

**Where Candidate A breaks (edge objects).**
1. *Barebone kit*: the EU files it as a part (8473 30 80) [S31]; Geizhals shelves it under Systems [S05]. Recommended home: Complete computers > Kits, because it is sold as the machine's body.
2. *Board with the CPU soldered or fitted*: Tsukumo lists five boards "with low-power CPU fitted" [S02], Geizhals has "Mainboards with CPU" [S05]; the EU treats a board complete with CPU and RAM as a complete base unit (8471 50) [S31]. It sits between motherboard and complete computer.
3. *NAS*: storage by function, network device by connection; Tsukumo files it under HDD [S02], Geizhals gives it its own top group [S05].
4. *Compute accelerator card with no display output* (an eBay listing for a Tesla V100 accelerator [S09]; Mouser "Accelerator Cards" [S08]): graphics card, expansion card or neither.
5. *Arduino UNO Q*, a Linux processor plus a microcontroller on one board [S37]: single-board computer or microcontroller board.

### Candidate B: platform first (how the Japanese shops navigate CPUs, boards and cards)

| Level | Axis | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | chip maker or platform | Intel LGA1851 / AMD AM5 / NVIDIA / AMD Radeon |
| Leaf | part function | CPU, motherboard, video card |
| Kind | series or generation | Core Ultra 5, Ryzen 7, Intel 800 series boards, GeForce RTX 50 |
| Named form | model | Ryzen 7 9800X3D |

Evidence for this shape: Tsukumo splits CPUs, boards and cards by chip maker and series [S02]; Kakaku filters by chip maker, chipset and socket [S03]. Example kinds (10): Core Ultra 9, Core i9, Core Ultra 7, Core i7, Ryzen 9, Ryzen 7, Ryzen Threadripper, Intel 800 Series M/B, AMD B850 M/B, GeForce RTX 50 Series GPU [S02].

**Where B breaks.**
1. *PSU, case, SSD, keyboard, monitor* have no platform; the tree needs a second axis for them.
2. *A GeForce card* runs on an Intel or an AMD board, so "platform" for a card means the chip maker, which is brand: Appendix A forbids brand as a level.
3. *Chipset naming churns* (Tsukumo needs 100, 200, 300 ... 800 series nodes for Intel) and one chipset spans two CPU generations.
4. *Cross-platform comparison is the buyer's actual task* (Ryzen 7 against Core i7 in one hierarchy [S18]); B puts the two in different branches.

### Candidate C: system class first (desktop, laptop, server, embedded), parts nested under each

| Level | Axis | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | system class | Desktop; Laptop; Server and workstation; Embedded |
| Leaf | part function | Memory; Storage; Processor |
| Kind | physical class | desktop memory module; laptop memory module |
| Named form | model | (kit or board model) |

Evidence: Tsukumo separates desktop, notebook, Mac desktop and Mac notebook memory [S02]; Geizhals has "system-specific memory" [S05]; Kakaku and Swappa separate laptops from desktops [S10, S11].

**Where C breaks.**
1. *An M.2 SSD* goes into a laptop, a mini PC, a handheld and a desktop (the form-factor codes say nothing about the machine [S26]).
2. *A SODIMM in a mini PC*: desktop-class machine, notebook-class part.
3. *Keyboards, mice, monitors, cables, routers* belong to no system class.
4. *Server vs desktop* is the market modifier of Appendix A, so it cannot be a level; Tsukumo keeps Xeon and Threadripper inside the ordinary CPU tree [S02], Kakaku's board list carries 305 Supermicro and 44 multi-CPU boards beside desktop boards [S03], and only Geizhals splits out "HPC processors" [S05].
