## 0. Category

**Code and name.** E3 Musical Instruments & Music Gear (domain E, Play, Sport & Music). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md`, category list and placement key step 22.

**Membership test (step 22).** "A **musical instrument**, acoustic or electronic, or equipment for making, amplifying, performing or recording music (amplifiers, effects pedals, mixers, microphones for music, audio interfaces, stage, studio and PA speakers, studio headphones, DJ turntables and controllers, metronomes, music stands), and music boxes?" Notes bound to the step: toy instruments are E1; headphones, turntables and speakers are D3 unless sold as studio, stage or DJ equipment; microphones made for speech, calls or streaming are D1; karaoke machines are D3; wind chimes are B4. Studio or listening gear is filed by the maker's own product category, and a stage in-ear kit with a transmitter pack is E3 (K-143). A microphone sold for both music recording and speech is filed by the maker's stated first use (K-144). Stage lighting is B3; piano benches and racks are B1; fog and haze machines are B4 (K-145). An audio card fitted inside a computer is D1 (S3); any external audio converter or headphone amplifier, USB-C dongles included, is D3; an audio interface sold for recording music is E3 (K-150).

**Rules that touch E3.**
- Step 22 is tried after step 8 (parts follow the whole, S3) and step 21 (F1 media), and before steps 23 to 33. So strings, picks, reeds, drum heads and a case made for one instrument are E3 under S3 (the S3 text names "guitar strings to Music", "a guitar case or stand"), sheet music is F1, and software instruments are I1 (step 5 comes first).
- S3 "never parts": furniture, racks, shelves, hooks, hangers and storage that hold several objects (B1). A piano bench, a studio desk and a 19-inch equipment rack therefore leave E3 for B1.
- Step 24, D1 branch and S3b (K-2): "cables with standard connectors and adapters made to connect electronic devices (USB, HDMI, audio, instrument, microphone, patch, MIDI and charging cables; a cable with a proprietary connector made for one device follows it, S3b)". Instrument, microphone, patch and MIDI cables are therefore D1 (see section 9).
- Step 14 note: instruments also used outside worship, such as an organ or harmonium, are E3; instruments made for a rite are F4; singing bowls and meditation instruments are E3 unless made for a rite (K-110).
- Step 25 note: toy instruments are E1. Appendix A and C6: user (child, teen, adult; R-20) is a modifier, so a child-size real violin or guitar stays E3; left-handed is the attribute `handedness`, not a user value (CV-19 list). C15 (toy or real): a play object that imitates an instrument is E1 when it cannot do the real instrument's job to that instrument's own standard (a tuned instrument); the real instrument in a child's size stays E3.
- S2: a model or iconic design is a named form under a kind. S4 and Appendix A: region and culture, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, market, power source and technology, material (unless material is the form), condition and edition are modifiers and never levels.
- Conventions used: C1 (maker ledger, lines table, `built_by_maker_id`), C2 (kind against named form, `maker_bound`), C3 (variants), C4 (tier entries on lines; measured specs are attributes), C5 (levels, leaf axes, parts sub-family), C6 (size and user), C7 (order), C8 (scene tags), C9 (aliases), C10 (technology), C11 (regional names), C12 (mounting), C13 (compatibility ledger), C16 (no individual-item facts).

### 1.2 Enthusiast communities, makers' guides and technicians

- Sweetwater's amp guide (https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/guitar-amp-buying-guide/) divides amps by technology (tube, solid-state, digital) and by form (head vs combo, cabinets, speaker size, FRFR cabs) and tells buyers to start from where they play, wattage and style.
- Roland's effects guide (https://www.roland.com/uk/blog/guitar-effects-pedals-guide) divides pedals first into four functions (dynamic, time-based, frequency-based, modelling) and then lists effect types A to Z (chorus, compressor, delay, distortion, fuzz, flanger, overdrive, phaser, reverb, tremolo, wah, vibrato, looper).
- The Gear Page thread (https://www.thegearpage.net/board/index.php?threads%2Fhow-does-gibson-epiphone-quality-difference-compare-to-fender-squire.2567159%2F) shows buyers ranking by maker line and country of build (Squier and Mexican Fender "in the same space as Epiphone"; Gibson brands non-US guitars Epiphone).
- A1Guitar (https://a1guitar.com/fender-lineup-guide) lays out eleven Fender tiers, from Squier Sonic to Custom Shop, and says the ladder answers "how much guitar", not "what does it sound like".
- Piano technicians and review sites (https://www.pianobrands.org/piano-brands-ranking/, https://www.pianoanswers.com/post/best-piano-brands-of-2026-an-expert-technician-s-guide-to-quality-value) rank makers into five and three tiers respectively.
- Band-instrument dealers (https://www.brassandwinds.com/blogs/news/instrument-buying-guide-student-vs-professional) use student / step-up / professional tiers for trumpet, trombone, saxophone, clarinet and flute.
- Drum review sites (https://vintagevinylnews.com/best-drum-sets-for-professionals/) list each maker's own line ladder (Pearl Decade / Reference / Masters; TAMA Imperialstar / Superstar Classic / Starclassic / Star; Gretsch Catalina / Brooklyn / USA Custom; Sonor AQ2 / SQ2 / Delite; DW Design / Performance / Collector's).
- Luthiers have their own guild (https://luth.org/): the Guild of American Luthiers, a nonprofit since 1972 for makers, repairers and restorers of stringed instruments.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- Reverb's Vintage Guitar FAQ (https://reverb.com/page/vintage-guitar-faq): two guitars of the same make, model, year and finish differ greatly by originality and condition; "player grade" vs "collector grade"; vintage usually means pre-1980; the most valued acoustics are pre-war Martin dreadnoughts and Gibson jumbos.
- Reverb Price Guide (https://reverb.com/price-guide): values 240,000+ products by model and condition from sold listings. Value attaches to the model, not to a generic kind.
- Official Vintage Guitar Price Guide (https://www.vintageguitar.com/product/the-official-vintage-guitar-price-guide-2026/): sections electric guitars, acoustics, basses, amplifiers, effects, lap steels, steels, ukuleles; inside each, alphabetical by brand and then by model, with years of availability; 34 dealers supply values.
- Reverb desktop-synth listing (https://applink.reverb.com/c/keyboards-and-synths/desktop-synths) shows used, B-stock and new listings by condition for named products (for example the Suzuki OM-84 Omnichord).

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- EU Combined Nomenclature 2026 / HS Chapter 92 (https://wisepim.com/hs-codes/92): 9201 pianos (upright, grand, other), 9202 other string instruments (bowed, other), 9205 wind instruments (brass, other; accordions, harmoniums and mouth organs are here), 9206 percussion, 9207 electrically produced or amplified instruments (keyboard, other), 9208 music boxes and others, 9209 parts and accessories (strings, metronomes, tuning forks). So customs divides by sound source and by electric vs acoustic; none of the seven headings names microphones, amplifiers or loudspeakers.
- MIMO revision of Hornbostel-Sachs (http://www.mimo-international.com/documents/Hornbostel%20Sachs.pdf): five classes (1 idiophones, 2 membranophones, 3 chordophones, 4 aerophones, 5 electrophones). Class 5 has 51 electro-acoustic (electric guitar 513, electric piano 511), 52 electromechanical (Hammond tone-wheel organ, Mellotron), 53 electronic analogue modules, 54 digital (samplers 544). Microphones, pick-ups and loudspeakers are 515 "transducers". Instruments whose signal goes to an amplifier and loudspeaker are classed as electrophones "even if they have some capability of sounding acoustically".

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- Horniman Museum (https://www.horniman.ac.uk/event/music-gallery): a "Listening to order" display groups instruments from around the world by how they produce sound, while other displays group by keyboards in the home, life events, and makers and inventors (Boosey & Hawkes, Wheatstone).
- Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_instrument_classification): lists Western orchestral families (strings, woodwinds, brass, percussion, sometimes keyboards or electronic), Hornbostel-Sachs, the Indian Natyashastra four classes (tata, avanaddha, sushira, ghana), the Chinese bayin eight materials, and notes that "modern catalogues may record several attributes rather than force every instrument into one exclusive family" (the piano is a keyboard by interface, a string instrument in orchestral use and a chordophone in H-S).
- Baidu Baike (https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E5%85%AB%E9%9F%B3%E5%88%86%E7%B1%BB%E6%B3%95/7940873): the eight-sound system sorts by material (metal, stone, silk, bamboo, gourd, clay, leather, wood) and was in use from the Zhou dynasty to the early Qing.
- Wikipedia on wagakki (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Japanese_musical_instruments): Japanese traditional instruments grouped as string (plucked: koto, biwa, shamisen; bowed: kokyū), wind (flutes, hichiriki, shō) and percussion.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects

- **Agree.** Every retailer in six regions opens with "which instrument do you play": guitars and basses, keyboards, drums and percussion, winds, strings. The H-S classes, the Natyashastra four classes and the orchestral families all rest on the sound source. Music gear is always treated as a separate zone: amplification, effects, studio, PA, DJ.
- **Split on amps and effects.** Thomann, Woodbrass and Muztorg file guitar amps and pedals inside Guitars; Sweetwater, Reverb and Digimart give amps and effects their own top-level.
- **Split on keyless sound generators.** Reverb puts drum machines and samplers in Keyboards and Synths; Sweetwater puts Drum Machines & Samplers in Electronic Drums; Thomann puts grooveboxes under DJ-Equipment.
- **Split on accordions.** Thomann files them under Tasteninstrumente and again under Traditionelle Instrumente; Muztorg gives them their own top-level; Reverb puts them in Folk Instruments; Digimart in "other instruments"; HS Chapter 92 files them as wind instruments.
- **Split on world instruments.** Thomann (Traditionelle Instrumente), Muztorg (Народные и этнические), Digimart (和楽器・民族楽器) and Reverb (Folk Instruments) give them their own top-level or branch; H-S, Natyashastra and the bayin system place them in the same sound-source families as Western instruments.
- **Split on headphones, turntables, studio speakers.** Sweetwater makes Headphones a top-level with studio, audiophile, DJ and gaming types, which is the boundary the framework note has to cut.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Which instrument or which job (form and function).** Thomann's page asks "acoustic or electric?" first (https://www.thomann.de/de/gitarren_baesse.html); Digimart's form asks product category, then detail category (https://www.digimart.net/category/); Sweetwater's menu opens with the instrument families, then gear jobs (https://www.sweetwater.com/shop/by-category). Gear guides start from the player's situation: where they play, how loud, which style (https://www.sweetwater.com/insync/guitar-amp-buying-guide/).
2. **Level or tier (performance tier, set by maker line).** Guides in every instrument family write for "beginner, intermediate, professional" (https://www.sharmusic.com/blogs/all/violin-buying-guide-professional-vs-student-instruments-comparison, https://www.brassandwinds.com/blogs/news/instrument-buying-guide-student-vs-professional, https://a1guitar.com/fender-lineup-guide). Price appears as a band on Digimart's search form, after category and brand.
3. **Maker.** Every store has a manufacturer A to Z; forum questions are maker-line questions (Squier vs Fender, Epiphone vs Gibson).
4. **Condition and era.** New / used / vintage is a facet (Digimart) and a section (Woodbrass "Second Life"); on the collector side originality and condition decide value (https://reverb.com/page/vintage-guitar-faq).
5. **Mechanism and construction** (solid vs hollow body, tube vs solid-state, condenser vs dynamic) matter inside a family and come after the instrument is chosen.

So the evidence supports: form or function first, then tier within a maker line, then maker, then condition and era.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: category E3, then up to four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form).

### 3A. Shop-floor departments (the retail pattern)

- **Sub-family** axis: the department a shop files it in (mixed: instrument families such as Guitars & Basses, Keyboards, Drums & Percussion, Winds, Strings, Traditional, and gear zones such as Amplification, Effects, Studio, PA, DJ, Accessories).
- **Leaf** axis: type group inside the department. **Kind**: generic type. **Named form**: model or iconic design.
- Example sub-families and leaves (Thomann, Reverb, Digimart, Muztorg all converge on these): Guitars & basses (electric guitar, acoustic guitar, classical guitar, bass guitar, ukulele); Keyboards (piano, digital piano, synthesizer, organ); Drums & percussion (drum kit, cymbal, hand drum, mallet percussion); Winds (saxophone, trumpet, flute, clarinet); Strings (violin, cello); Amplification (combo amp, amp head, cabinet); Effects (distortion, delay, reverb); Studio (audio interface, microphone, monitor); PA & DJ.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) Guitar amp: Guitars at Thomann, Amps at Sweetwater. (2) Synth module, drum machine or groovebox: Keyboards (Reverb), Electronic Drums (Sweetwater) or DJ (Thomann). (3) Accordion: Keyboards, Traditional and Wind in one retailer and in customs. (4) A department like "Traditional Instruments" is a region, which Appendix A bars as a level. (5) The sub-family axis is mixed, so a reader cannot predict the department from a stated rule.

### 3B. Role in the music path, then family or job (decided, E-3)

- **Sub-family** axis: role of the object in making music: *Instruments* (sound sources, acoustic or electronic), *Amplification & effects* (amplify and shape the signal), *Sound capture, mixing & reproduction* (capture, mix, control and reproduce the sound, in any setting), *Parts, accessories & music aids* (serve a host instrument, or aid practice and performance). The four sub-families are one axis (role); a fifth role-based sub-family for live sound or DJ is not used, because studio, stage and DJ are settings (Appendix A) and are recorded as `use` tags (X8 of the review), never as a level.
- **Leaf** axis, stated for each sub-family (C5c):
  - Instruments: the playing family, by how the sound is produced and how the instrument is played (keyboards; synthesizers and electronic instruments; guitars and basses; lutes and other plucked strings; zithers and harps; bowed strings; woodwinds; brass; free-reed instruments; drums and percussion; mechanical instruments; other idiophones).
  - Amplification & effects: the job (amplify; modify the signal).
  - Sound capture, mixing & reproduction: the job (capture with microphones; mix and interface; reproduce through loudspeakers; reproduce through headphones and in-ear monitors; process the signal; perform with DJ equipment; control other gear).
  - Parts, accessories & music aids (CV-17, C5(d)): the object served; the last leaf holds aids not made for one instrument.
- **Kind**: generic type at the choice level (section 4b). **Named form**: any design, model or line inside one kind that one fetched page shows made or sold, uncapped (`maker_bound: yes | no`, C2(a); CV-7).
- Example: Instruments > Guitars & basses > solid-body electric guitar > S-type double-cutaway solid body (not maker-bound) and Fender Stratocaster (maker-bound); Instruments > Drums & percussion > hi-hat cymbal (the Zildjian K† line spans the cymbal kinds, so it is a lines row, C2(d)); Amplification & effects > Effects > distortion effect > Boss DS-1†; Sound capture, mixing & reproduction > Microphones & wireless systems > large-diaphragm condenser microphone > Neumann U87; Sound capture, mixing & reproduction > DJ equipment > DJ controller.
- Hornbostel-Sachs, Natyashastra and bayin classes are stored as attributes of each instrument kind, so a museum view or a "by material" view can be generated without changing the tree.
- **Where it breaks (and how it is closed).** (1) DJ turntable and MIDI keyboard controller are both sound sources and control gear: a DJ turntable and a DJ controller sit in the DJ equipment leaf because the key names them as music equipment; a MIDI keyboard controller and a pad controller sit in the Controllers leaf; neither is an Instruments kind (E-20). (2) Studio monitor and PA speaker were split by setting in the first draft. They are now two kinds in one leaf (Loudspeakers & monitors) because the bodies differ (near-field cabinet against pole-mount or flown cabinet, C12a); the use (studio, stage) is a `use` tag. (3) An amp modeller pedal is both effect and amp: a floor unit that carries amp models is an "amp modeller" (Amplification); a unit that offers only effects is a "multi-effects processor" (Effects). Decided (E-20). (4) A "guitar with built-in amp" (Ishibashi category mini/アンプ内蔵) is an instrument that includes gear and is filed as the guitar; the built-in amp is an attribute. (5) Parts and accessories form a sub-family only because S3 sends them here (C5d).

### 3C. Sound-source classes (Hornbostel-Sachs)

- **Sub-family** axis: how the sound is first produced (idiophone, membranophone, chordophone, aerophone, electrophone). **Leaf**: the H-S subdivision (struck, plucked, bowed; flutes, reeds, trumpets...). **Kind**, **named form** as above.
- Example: chordophones > plucked > acoustic guitar; aerophones > reed > saxophone; electrophones > electro-acoustic > electric guitar; electrophones > electronic > synthesizer.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) The electric guitar is class 513 (electrophone) while the acoustic guitar is class 3, so the guitar family splits (MIMO). (2) A drum kit mixes membranophones and idiophones (cymbals). (3) Piano, harpsichord and accordion are bought as keyboards yet fall in chordophones or aerophones. (4) Amplifiers, effects, mixers, monitors and DJ gear are outside the scheme (microphones and speakers are only "transducers"). (5) The Horniman shows the scheme as one museum display ("Listening to order") beside displays by keyboards in the home, life events and makers (https://www.horniman.ac.uk/event/music-gallery), and none of the retailers fetched uses it as their navigation.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds.** Reverb lists 321 sub-entries across 14 top categories, including 37 home-audio (D3) and 29 accessory entries (https://reverb.com/gear-categories); removing home audio (37) and accessories (29) leaves about 255 entries, with some duplicates (chorus, tremolo, cymbals listed twice). Thomann's 22 top categories contain roughly 300 sub-category links. Estimate for E3 generic kinds under the recommended structure: **250 to 400** (instruments ~160 including world and folk kinds, voice members such as the four saxophones, and cymbal types; gear ~90; parts and accessories ~100). The world and folk long tail is the uncertain part: Digimart groups it as one branch and Reverb lists 13 folk kinds, but museum classification (MIMO) runs to thousands of types.
- **Named forms.** Reverb's price guide covers 240,000+ products and the Vintage Guitar guide lists "thousands of models" from 34 dealers (https://reverb.com/price-guide, https://www.vintageguitar.com/product/the-official-vintage-guitar-price-guide-2026/). If every model entered, named forms would number tens of thousands. Recommendation: admit only attested iconic designs (Stratocaster, Les Paul, Steinway Model D, U87, DX7) and attested design families that many makers build (S-type, T-type, dreadnought), with model lines in the maker lines table (section 6). **OWNER:** the named-form policy (decision 9).
- **Where depth concentrates.** Guitars and basses (Thomann 34,013 items; Digimart further splits electric guitars by named design), drums and cymbals (Reverb 49 entries; Drum Center splits cymbals by maker and type), pedals (30 effect types on Reverb), microphones (Thomann splits by construction and use), winds (Thomann 18,664 items split by instrument) and modular synths.
- **Broad, medium, narrow leaves.** Broad: electric guitar, acoustic guitar, digital piano, microphones, effects pedals, drum kits and cymbals. Medium: bass guitar, saxophone, violin, synthesizers, amplifiers, audio interfaces. Narrow: theremin, shruti box, glass harmonica, vihuela, hichiriki, musical saw.
- **Leaf skipping (C5b).** A sub-family or leaf holding about 12 kinds or fewer may store its kinds with `leaf = null`. Amplification & effects keeps its two leaves because their axes (amplify; modify) differ; Mechanical instruments and Other idiophones are small leaves that stay because the Instruments leaf axis is the playing family.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recognised ordering: consensus-ranked, set by maker line, with a shared generic scale.** No measurable spec orders E3 as a whole. Where a spec exists (amp wattage, interface dynamic range, key count) it describes capability and does not rank quality; such specs are **attributes with the standard or unit named** (C4b), not tier entries.

Evidence:
- Fender sells eleven ladder steps: Squier Sonic, Affinity, Classic Vibe, Paranormal; Fender Standard, Player II, Player Plus, Vintera III, American Professional II, American Ultra II, American Vintage II, Custom Shop. The guide says the ladder "answers how much guitar, not what does it sound like" (https://a1guitar.com/fender-lineup-guide).
- Drum makers each publish their own line ladder (Pearl Decade / Reference / Masters; TAMA Imperialstar to Star; DW Design / Performance / Collector's) and reviewers map them onto entry-pro, mid-pro, premium, flagship, boutique (https://vintagevinylnews.com/best-drum-sets-for-professionals/).
- Band instruments use student / step-up / professional with price ranges ($400 to $2,200; $1,500 to $3,000; $3,000+) and different materials and hand-finishing (https://www.brassandwinds.com/blogs/news/instrument-buying-guide-student-vs-professional); violins use Beginning / Intermediate / Advanced / Fine & Timeless (https://www.sharmusic.com/blogs/all/violin-buying-guide-professional-vs-student-instruments-comparison).
- Pianos have two competing ladders: five tiers (Concert Hall, Professional Elite, Serious Home, Value, Budget) at https://www.pianobrands.org/piano-brands-ranking/ and three tiers (luxury, performance-grade, consumer-grade) at https://www.pianoanswers.com/post/best-piano-brands-of-2026-an-expert-technician-s-guide-to-quality-value. The pianobrands.org text itself warns that one maker can sit in two tiers by model and country of build.
- Forum users disagree about whether Squier is closer to Fender than Epiphone is to Gibson (https://www.thegearpage.net/board/index.php?threads%2Fhow-does-gibson-epiphone-quality-difference-compare-to-fender-squire.2567159%2F), so even the cross-maker order is contested.

**How it enters the data (C4).**
- A tier never sits on a kind. It is a **tier entry** (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) on a maker line (Fender Player II, Pearl Masters) or, where an entry says so, a named form. A subject may carry several entries from different sources, with a computed `disputed` flag (the two piano ladders; the Squier question). A named form does not inherit its maker's tier.
- Schemes: the maker's own ladder (`scheme` = the maker's name: Fender, Pearl, TAMA, DW); each reviewer's ladder under its own name (pianobrands.org five-tier; pianoanswers.com three-tier); and the domain-E `player_level` scheme (starter, intermediate, advanced, professional), the generic scale to which student / step-up / professional and Beginning / Intermediate / Advanced / Fine & Timeless are mapped, each entry with its source.
- C4(e): a scheme enters the data only with one fetched source and preferably a second from another region. Every tier source fetched here is US-based, so the generic ladder and the reviewer ladders wait for a second-region source (a Japanese or German retailer's beginner / advanced / professional labels): **†**.
- Price band: the single domain-wide USD scheme (C4d), derived from a dated list price. The brassandwinds and Shar Music prices are source figures for that derivation and are not tier values.
- Condition grades and "player grade vs collector grade" (https://reverb.com/page/vintage-guitar-faq) are facts about one particular object and are not stored (C16); era and vintage are tags; model years and reissues are variants (C3).
- Country of build (Squier vs Mexican vs Japanese vs US Fender) is an attribute of the line, because it changes the tier in some makers' ladders and not in others.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought: Steinway, Fender, Gibson, Yamaha, Neumann, Zildjian. With small pockets of **platform** (Eurorack modular format; a speaker cabinet and head from one amp line), which are compatibility-ledger rows (C13), not a reason to change the choice.

Evidence:
- Every store carries a manufacturer A to Z and brand pages (Thomann "Herstellerübersicht A–Z", https://www.thomann.de/de/gitarren_baesse.html; Digimart "ブランド一覧", https://www.digimart.net/category/).
- The collector guide orders each section alphabetically by brand and then by model (https://www.vintageguitar.com/product/the-official-vintage-guitar-price-guide-2026/); Reverb Price Guide values by model and condition (https://reverb.com/price-guide).
- Brand, factory and country are separate facts: Fender Japan was a 1982 joint venture whose guitars were built by FujiGen and later Dyna Gakki, with "Made in Japan" and "Crafted in Japan" labels marking the factory (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fender_Japan); Japanese OEM factories built guitars for Ibanez, Fender, Greco and others (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Japanese_OEM_guitar_manufacturers).
- Second brands and designed-by lines: Squier under Fender, Epiphone under Gibson (A1Guitar, Gear Page), Boston is designed by Steinway and built by Kawai, and Essex is Steinway's entry-level line (https://www.pianoanswers.com/post/best-piano-brands-of-2026-an-expert-technician-s-guide-to-quality-value); "stencil" brands are flagged as a risk by the same technician.
- Craft makers are a separate class: the Wikipedia manufacturer list "excludes most custom-only shops" and points to a separate list of luthiers (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_guitar_manufacturers); the Guild of American Luthiers publishes plans and a journal for individual makers (https://luth.org/); Shar Music says professional violins get "individual maker attention" (Shar page above).

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger on the C1 schema.**
- *Core columns* (C1): `maker_id`, name, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, country, place, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags`, `price_band` (C4d), `sources`. The first draft's columns map as follows: brand owner = house or manufacture; OEM factory = supplier; second brand or house brand = licensee or diffusion, with `parent_id` (Squier, Epiphone); individual luthier = artisan or atelier; independent and microbrand as C1 lists them. "Parent or designer brand" is `parent_id`. The "maker lines with their tier_band" column is gone: see the lines table.
- *Lines table* (C1): `line_id`, `maker_id`, name, the kinds it spans, `tier` entries (C4a), years, `country_of_build`, sources, and `built_by_maker_id`, so that designer and builder are both recorded (Boston designed by Steinway, built by Kawai; Fender Japan built by FujiGen then Dyna Gakki). A line spanning several kinds is a lines row; a line or design inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (domain F rule).
- *Variants* (C3): reissues, model years and colourways, for example the Vintage Guitar "years of availability", only where an owner-emphasis scene needs depth.
- *Optional `school` table* (C1 additions): luthier schools or traditions, only if a fetched source attests them (**†**); a school is not a maker.
- *Compatibility ledger* (C13): `platform_id`, name, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`, sources. Candidate rows: Eurorack (Reverb has it as a category; the standard reference needs a page†), MIDI†, API 500-series†, Buchla 4U†, Moog 5U†. Platforms are never levels.
- *Size estimate*: 300 to 600 rows for brands and factories; luthiers are open-ended and are admitted only when attested.
- This follows the owner's wish to capture brand depth and tiers "where they live (named forms, a maker ledger, a tier modifier)".

A scene becomes a **named form** only when it names a distinct form: for example the dreadnought (Reverb acoustic list), the S-type (Ishibashi), a cajon or doumbek. Region, scene and era never become levels. Use tags (studio, stage, DJ, classroom) are one `use_tags` list per category, each with a source, never a level.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical (prototype) form first (C7), then the secondary key the sub-family states: size or range where the family has a size ladder.**
- Kinds: the most widely bought or defining form first (solid-body electric before hollow-body; violin before viola, cello, double bass; trumpet before trombone, tuba; soprano before alto, tenor, baritone saxophone), then others in the family's own sequence. Orchestral families already have a size or pitch ladder (Thomann's brass list runs trumpet through tuba; Sweetwater's orchestral list runs violin to double bass). Cymbals go crash, ride, hi-hat, splash, China (the order Reverb lists them).
- Named forms (C7): by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetical where no year is attested. Not-maker-bound design families (S-type, dreadnought) go by first attested year, then alphabetically. Reason for the year key: the collector trade keys on years of availability and design changes (the Vintage Guitar guide lists "each model's years of availability and any design changes that affect values", https://www.vintageguitar.com/product/the-official-vintage-guitar-price-guide-2026/) and on dating by year and maker (https://reverb.com/page/vintage-guitar-faq: vintage usually means pre-1980).
- Variants go by `variant_code` or year.
- Not spec order, because specs do not rank across kinds (wattage, bit depth, key count do not compare a fuzz pedal with a synthesizer).

## 9. Boundaries

1. **D3 Audio & Video.** Nearest objects: studio headphones, DJ turntable, studio monitor, hi-fi turntable, karaoke machine. *Precedence: step 22 is tried before step 24, so headphones, turntables and speakers sold as studio, stage or DJ equipment are E3 and the same objects sold for listening are D3; a karaoke machine is D3 in every case.* Sweetwater files studio, audiophile, DJ and gaming headphones in one Headphones branch, which is why the note is needed.
2. **D1 Computers & Components.** Nearest objects: USB microphone, audio interface, MIDI cable, instrument cable, DAW computer. *Precedence: a microphone made for speech, calls or streaming is D1, a microphone sold for recording music is E3 (USB output is a connectivity attribute, C10, not a kind); an audio interface is E3 because step 22 names it; instrument, microphone, patch and MIDI cables are D1, because the key files "cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices (USB, HDMI, audio and charging cables, whatever device they serve)" there.* **OWNER:** the memo's first draft proposed an E3 exception for a cable made for one instrument; the key has no such test, so D1 stands unless the owner adds the exception (decision 7). Thomann and Sweetwater sell instrument and mic cables as music gear.
3. **I1 Software and I2 Digital Media.** Nearest objects: virtual instrument, plugin, DAW, sample library, sound pack. *Precedence: step 5 is tried before step 22, so virtual instruments, plugins and DAWs are I1 and a sample library held as files is I2, while the hardware that runs them is E3.* Thomann and Sweetwater both list software beside hardware.
4. **E1 Toys & Games.** Nearest objects: toy guitar, kids' electric guitar, classroom Orff instruments, play xylophone. *Precedence (C15): a toy instrument is E1 when it cannot do the real instrument's job to that instrument's own standard (a tuned instrument); a real instrument sold in a child's size is E3 because user age is a modifier; classroom Orff instruments are E3 when tuned and made to be played as instruments, and E1 when sold as toys.* Sweetwater lists "Kids Electric Guitars" and "Classroom Instruments" beside adult ones, and Digimart has a separate トイ楽器/音楽玩具 (toy instruments and music toys) category (https://www.digimart.net/category/).
5. **F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects.** Nearest objects: church organ (kind: pipe organ†), singing bowl, temple bell, meditation and music-therapy drum. *Precedence: an instrument also played outside worship (organ, harmonium) is E3; an instrument made for a rite, such as a temple bell, is F4.* Thomann and Sweetwater both list "meditation and music therapy" instruments and Drum Center lists singing bowls, which sit on this line. **OWNER:** singing bowls and meditation instruments: E3 unless made for a rite (decision 8).
6. **B1 Furniture & Storage.** Nearest objects: piano bench, studio desk, equipment rack, instrument shelf. *Precedence: under S3 furniture and storage that holds several objects is B1, so a piano bench, studio furniture and a 19-inch rack are B1 while a stand or case made for one instrument stays E3.* Reverb lists keyboard and piano benches and studio desks and racks in music categories.
7. **F1 Media & Publications.** *Precedence: sheet music, method books and fake books are F1; the music stand that holds them is E3.*
8. **B3 Lighting.** Nearest objects: stage lighting, fog machines. *Precedence: lamps and fittings made to give light are B3 even when sold in a music shop, because step 22 lists equipment for making, amplifying, performing or recording music and not lighting.* Fog and haze machines have no home in the key (the review proposes B4 as decorative effects, or a new note). **OWNER:** fog machines (decision 7). Thomann and Sweetwater both sell lighting beside PA.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions
