Pages fetched in this batch are listed in verify/out/S2_D4_sources.tsv (37 rows); the keys S01 to S37 below point to them (full URLs in the key table of section 1). Call count: 10 searches, 40 fetches (cap reached). Three fetches returned nothing usable and are not cited: George Eastman Museum camera list (cookie and opening-hours text only), the Kitamura film camera category (fetch_error, logged in verify/BLOCKED_HOSTS.txt), and a B&H flashes URL that served a paper-roll page. Chinese evidence is thinner than the Japanese, German and US evidence: one maker store (S15), one buyer post (S16) and one weak affiliate shop (S17); JD and Taobao were already logged as blocked by earlier batches, so no Chinese category tree was attempted.*

**One-line result.** Divide D4 by role in the picture-making chain (seven sub-families: cameras, lenses and optical attachments, light, support, film and sensitised media, darkroom, camera accessories), then by device class inside the role (leaf), then by the generic type buyers compare (kind), then by model, series or stock (named form). Carry lens mount, flash and lighting mounts, sensor format and film format as compatibility attributes in a platform ledger (the maker sets them); carry quality as a spec-ranked tier (sensor format class, lens speed, film speed) with a maker-line tier beside it; never use maker, medium era or scene as a level.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D4 Cameras & Photography (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, fourth branch).** "A **camera** or equipment for taking and making pictures, powered or not: lenses, flashes and photo lighting, ring lights, tripods, film, darkroom equipment, drones made for filming." The category line adds: "cameras, lenses, photo lighting, tripods, film and darkroom equipment."

**Rules and notes that already touch D4.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): "a lens to Cameras" is the framework's own example. Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts "only when they are made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time and travel or stand with it": a camera bag is named. Never parts: furniture, racks, shelves, storage that holds several objects (so a studio shelving unit or a general equipment cabinet is B1).
- Step 8 (a part, attachment, accessory, case or consumable made for one kind of object goes to that object's category) is tried before step 24, so camera batteries, camera straps, lens filters, lens hoods, mount adapters and camera cages reach D4 through S3, not through step 24.
- Step 24, first branch (D1): webcams, scanners, printers, storage, card readers and "cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices, whatever device they serve" are D1. The D1 branch is tried first, so memory cards, card readers and camera USB cables stay D1.
- Step 24, second branch (D2): a smartphone with a camera is D2. Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object, such as a camera, is filed by its task at step 30 (security camera, doorbell: B8).
- Step 23 (C6): binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, weather instruments and loupes are C6. Its note files a measuring tool "made for a task the key files elsewhere" with that task, which sends a photographic light meter to D4.
- Step 30 branch B3 (lighting): a lamp for lighting a room. Step 24 is tried before step 30, so a ring light, flash or studio light made for photography goes to D4.
- Step 19 (F5) and step 20 (F2): photographic prints and slides "of the owner's own life" are F5; photographs made as art are F2. Step 5, I2: the owner's photographs held as files are I2. Step 28 note (C4): paints, inks and papers made for picture-making are C4.
- Step 25 (E1): toy cameras made for play are E1.
- Step 11 (G1): a part "made to be fitted to or used up in one kind of object" goes to that object (see the dashcam question in section 10).

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D4** (never levels): brand and maker (the strongest, because it sets compatibility), era (vintage and retro film gear), price band and quality tier, user (beginner, professional, child), market (consumer, studio, cinema), setting (underwater, travel, studio), scene (film revival, street, vlogging), condition (new, used, refurbished: the resale market grades gear), edition (limited, Leica special editions), connectivity and power source.

### Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree.** Cameras, lenses and film are three separate shelves everywhere (S01, S09, S11, S18). Lens mount is the first lens filter or sits within the first two (S02, S10, S12, S18, S20, S30). Film divides by format and by emulsion (S03, S21, S29).
- **Split on the first cut among cameras.** US and Japanese shops cut digital from film first (S01, S09); the German price site cuts digital stills, system cameras, camcorders, action cams and analogue as peers (S11); the enthusiast wiki cuts by viewing system (S25).
- **Split on lens grouping.** Retailers group lenses by camera system class (mirrorless, SLR, rangefinder, medium format, large format, cine; S02, S09, S18, S20); Digitec groups by optical type and mount at once (S12); Map Camera adds focal class inside each system (S09).
- **Split on what belongs with cameras.** Kakaku files security cameras and astronomical telescopes in the camera tree (S07); Wikipedia lists thermal, security and traffic cameras as camera types (S24); the framework sends them to B8 and C6.
- **Split on darkroom and lighting.** US shops keep lighting and darkroom as a side shelf of Photography (S01); Freestyle makes film, paper and darkroom three peers (S21, S22).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

The buyer's first decision is **the system**, then the device class. Evidence:
- B&H and Map Camera split lenses by camera system before any optical type (S02, S10); Digitec's "popular filters" and Geizhals' preset list are mounts (Sony E, Canon RF, Nikon Z; S11, S12); KEH advertises "Shop by Top Mounts" (S18).
- Chinese buyer posts frame the choice as 卡口 (mount) and the cost of switching (S16); the English guide says "every camera system uses a specific lens mount" (S30).
- For film the first decision is the format (35 mm, 120, 4x5, Instax Mini or Wide): B&H film, Freestyle film size and The Darkroom lab all open with it (S03, S21, S32); DPReview's first advice is to start with 35 mm (S31); Ted's rule is to match the film format to the camera before anything else (S23).
- For lighting the first decision is the source (strobe, continuous, speedlight) and then the mount of the modifier system (S33).
- For cameras the second decision, after the system, is body class (mirrorless, DSLR, rangefinder, compact, medium format, camcorder, action; S08, S09, S11) and the third is sensor format (full-frame, APS-C, Micro Four Thirds; S08, S09, S11, S36).

Implication: the first axis is **platform compatibility** (maker-set), which the framework forbids as a level (brand and maker are modifiers). So the tree cannot follow the buyer's first choice; it follows the **role of the object** and records platform as an attribute that generated views and a platform ledger can sort on. Section 6 handles this.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): role in the picture-making chain

Levels, one axis each:
1. Sub-family: **role in making a picture** (capture, optics, light, support, sensitised media, processing, accessories).
2. Leaf: **device class within the role** (the shop shelf).
3. Kind: **the generic type buyers compare** (the choice level of section 4b).
4. Named form: **model, series, stock or iconic design**.

Sub-families and leaves:
- **D4.1 Cameras** (the capture device). Leaves by what the camera is built to record onto: digital still cameras; video and cinema cameras; action, 360 and pocket cameras (built to be worn, mounted or pocketed); film cameras; instant cameras; special and historic cameras (pinhole, stereo, panoramic, plate; S25, S37).
- **D4.2 Lenses & optical attachments.** Leaves by the format class the lens is built to cover: lenses for small-format interchangeable cameras (35 mm film and digital sensors up to full frame, including mirrorless, SLR and rangefinder mounts); medium-format lenses; large-format lenses; cine lenses; special-effect lenses (S02); and optical attachments (teleconverters, filters, close-up and wide converters, mount adapters; S10).
- **D4.3 Light.** Leaves by source: speedlights and on-camera flashes; studio flash heads (monolights, pack-and-head); continuous lights (LED panels, COB lights, fresnels, ring lights); modifiers and light-shaping accessories; stands and mounting for lights (S05, S33).
- **D4.4 Support & stabilisation.** Tripods and legs; tripod heads; quick-release systems; monopods and poles; gimbals and handheld stabilisers; rigs, cages and mounts; camera stands (S06).
- **D4.5 Film & sensitised media.** Leaves by emulsion and process: black-and-white negative; colour negative (C-41); colour reversal or slide (E-6); instant film; cine film; sheet film for large format is a format attribute of those leaves (S03, S21, S29).
- **D4.6 Darkroom & processing.** Enlargers and enlarging lenses; film developing equipment (tanks, reels, changing bags); print processing equipment (trays, timers, easels, safelights, dryers); chemistry; photographic paper (S04, S22).
- **D4.7 Camera accessories.** Bags and cases; batteries, chargers and grips; straps; viewfinders and eyepieces; remote releases and controllers; exposure and flash meters; cleaning kits; underwater housings (S01, S06, S37).

Example sub-families, leaves and 10 to 15 real kinds are in the worked tree below.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. A **hybrid vlog mirrorless** (for example Sony ZV-E10) records stills and video: placed by the shop class it ships in (mirrorless camera, S08), not by the video bias; an edge rule is needed.
2. A **speed booster** or **focal reducer adapter** is both a mount adapter and an optical converter (S30): placed with optical attachments, one home, mount recorded as two attributes.
3. A **camera cage with handles** is a rig (D4.4) or an accessory (D4.7); the stated axis (supports the camera for shooting) puts it in D4.4.
4. An **instant-film camera** such as Instax Mini is both a camera and bound to one film format; the camera is D4.1 instant and the film is D4.5 instant, linked by the format attribute.

### Candidate B: medium first (digital | film | studio | darkroom)

Levels: sub-family by **medium era** (digital, analogue film), leaf by device role, kind by type. This mirrors Map Camera (digital camera vs film camera, S09), B&H (Digital Cameras vs Film Cameras, S01) and Geizhals (Digitalkameras vs Analogkameras, S11).
Break points: (1) lighting, tripods, bags and most lenses serve both media, so they need either duplication (breaking one home) or a third "both" bucket; (2) adapted vintage glass on digital bodies (Leica M and M42 lenses on Sony E; S30) is a lens that is "film-era" by origin but "digital" by use; (3) the darkroom would sit under analogue while the film scanner (D1) and digital negative workflows cross the line; (4) era is a modifier in Appendix A, so it must not be a level.

### Candidate C: platform first (mount system)

Levels: sub-family by **camera system** (Canon RF, Nikon Z, Sony E, Leica M, Micro Four Thirds, ...), leaf by device role, kind by type. This mirrors the buyer's first decision (section 2) and B&H, KEH and Digitec lens navigation.
Break points: (1) maker is an Appendix A modifier and may not be a level; (2) third-party lenses (Sigma, Tamron) exist in several mounts, so the same kind appears many times (S30, S12); (3) objects with no system (tripods, film, darkroom, lights) have no home; (4) one lens with an adapter belongs to two systems. Retained only as a **generated view** (see sections 6 and 7).

## 4. Depth and size

**Rough count of generic kinds** (judgement, not a sourced figure): about 180 to 220 under D4: cameras 40 to 45, lenses and optical attachments 25 to 30, light 25 to 30, support 15 to 20, film 12 to 15, darkroom 20 to 25, accessories 25 to 30.

**Rough count of named forms** (models, series, stocks): film stocks listed on Wikipedia run to roughly 200 (S29); camera and lens models run to tens of thousands over time. A practical attested seed of 600 to 1,000 is sufficient for stage 3 (series-level camera forms such as Sony α7 or Leica M, famous lens lines such as Canon L or Sigma Art, all listed film stocks, the film format series 110, 120, 135 and instant formats).

**Where depth concentrates.**
- Lenses: breadth in optical type crossed with mount, so most of the size is the mount attribute (S27 lists dozens of mounts). The kinds stay few because the mount is not a kind.
- Cameras: breadth in film camera types (S09, S25) and in mirrorless series (S08, S15).
- Film: stock names (hundreds) under five emulsion kinds (S29, S21).
- Light: modifiers, which have many shapes (S33, S34).

**Broad, medium and narrow leaves.**
- Broad: digital still cameras, lenses of the small-format class, film cameras, camera accessories.
- Medium: light (speedlights, studio flash, continuous), support, film stocks, darkroom equipment.
- Narrow: medium-format lenses, large-format lenses, cine lenses, special cameras (pinhole, stereo), instant cameras, cine film.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers.

- **Cameras.** Buyers compare within a body class and a sensor format: Kakaku's top filters are sensor type and mount (S08); Geizhals' camera filters are maker, sensor format and type (mirrorless vs DSLR vs rangefinder; S11); DXOMARK ranks within type and sensor format (S36). Recommendation: **kind = body class** (mirrorless interchangeable-lens, DSLR, compact and so on); **sensor format is a spec tier attribute** (section 5), and series (α7, EOS R, X-T) is the named form. The alternative, making "full-frame mirrorless" and "APS-C mirrorless" separate kinds, is supported by Kakaku's popular filters (S08) and by Sony's own tiering (S15); this is an owner decision (section 10). Medium-format digital is kept as its own kind because its format is a different body design, not just a bigger sensor (S28).
- **Lenses.** Buyers compare within focal class and prime or zoom (Map Camera focal classes, Digitec lens types; S09, S12), and within a maker line (Canon L, Sony G Master, Sigma Art named in S12). Recommendation: **kind = focal class by prime or zoom plus the special optical designs** (macro, fisheye, tilt-shift, anamorphic), with the mount as a platform attribute and the maker line as a tier tag. This keeps the same Tamron design in E, Z and RF versions as one kind (S30).
- **Light.** Buyers compare by source (strobe, speedlight, LED) and by modifier shape (S33, S34). Recommendation: **kind = source type for lights, shape for modifiers**; power and mount (Bowens, Profoto) are attributes.
- **Film.** Buyers choose a format, then emulsion type, then stock (S21, S31). Recommendation: **kind = emulsion and process** (black-and-white negative, C-41 colour negative, E-6 slide, instant, cine); film format (135, 120, 4x5, Instax Mini) is a platform attribute because the camera sets it, and the stock (Portra 400, HP5 Plus) is the named form. Instant film is an exception worth flagging: the format is as binding as a lens mount (S23), so a format code stays an attribute, not a kind.
- **Support.** Buyers compare tripods by material and load (attributes), heads by type (ball, fluid, gimbal; S06). Kind = tripod, tripod head, monopod, gimbal, cage, stand; head type is a named-form-level distinction unless the owner wants it split.
- **Darkroom.** Enlarger type (condenser, diffusion, colour dichroic; S22) is where buyers compare. Kind = enlarger; type is an attribute.

Depth differs by sub-family: cameras and lenses need the full four levels; film and darkroom stop at kind with stock and model as named forms; light and support stop at kind.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended tier type: spec-ranked, with a maker-line tier beside it.**
- **Sensor format** is a measured ladder (1/2.3 inch to medium format; S28, S36) and sets the order for cameras: DXOMARK even uses it as a facet and ranks by spec scores for colour depth, dynamic range and low-light ISO (S36). ISO speed itself is standardised (S35) and film speed is a number on the box (S29).
- **Lens speed** (maximum aperture) is a spec the retailers lead with (S02, S12).
- **Maker-line tiers** are consensus or maker-declared: Sony's own series ladder (S15), Canon L, Sony G Master and Sigma Art named in the Digitec lens text (S12), Kodak consumer and professional film lines (S29), "entry-level DSLR, semi-pro DSLR, professional" body classes (S36). These are not measurable, so they sit in the ledger as a tier tag with the source.
- **Condition** in the resale market is a separate ledger (KEH grading scale; S18) and stays a modifier.
- **Placement.** A tier appears as a modifier on named forms (for example `tier=pro-line`, `sensor=full-frame`), never as a level. A generated view "by sensor format" and "by maker line" gives the owner the PC-component-style tier reading.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended centrality: platform, with identity for a small set.**
- **Platform.** The maker sets compatibility for lenses (Canon RF, Nikon Z, Sony E; S27, S30), flashes (hot-shoe protocol), lighting modifiers (Bowens, Profoto, Elinchrom mounts; S33), instant film (Instax, Polaroid; S23) and batteries. Retailers lead with mount (S02, S10, S12, S18). Mounts are shared across makers in places (Leica L alliance, Micro Four Thirds shared by Olympus and Panasonic; S10, S27) and one third-party design ships in several mounts (S30).
- **Identity.** A few makers are sought as such: Leica (digital rangefinder, Leica M glass adapted to other bodies; S28, S30), Hasselblad (V, H, X mounts; S10), Polaroid, Kodak and Ilford as film names (S29, S21), Lomography and Holga as film-camera scenes (S13).
- **Ledger recommendation: yes, two ledgers.**
  1. A **maker ledger**: maker, known-for kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country and era, each row attested.
  2. A **platform ledger** (mounts and formats): name, owner maker, flange distance, coverage or frame size, bayonet or screw, type (mirrorless, SLR, rangefinder, medium format, cine), era and status (S27, S28), adapters known. Film formats (110, 126, 127, 135, 120, sheet sizes, Instax Mini, Wide, Square; S26, S23) and flash and lighting mounts belong in the same platform ledger.
  Neither ledger is a level.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then spec order, then chronology only for formats.**
- Cameras: the mainstream body class first (mirrorless before DSLR before rangefinder before compact), then sensor format descending (medium format, full frame, APS-C, Micro Four Thirds, 1 inch). Reason: Map Camera and Geizhals order digital system cameras by sensor size (S09, S11) and the ladder is a measured quantity (S28).
- Lenses: wide to telephoto by focal length, prime then zoom inside each focal class, special designs last. Reason: Map Camera orders focal class from wide to telephoto (S09), and focal length is a number.
- Film: format size ascending (110, 135, 120, 4x5, 8x10; S26) is the order for formats; stocks inside an emulsion kind by ISO ascending (S29). Reason: film format and ISO are numbers.
- Light: source order by portability (speedlight, monolight, pack-and-head, continuous), modifiers by soft to hard. Reason: S33 and S34 order them this way.
- Chronology is for the museum or collector view only (S37), not the default order.

## 9. Boundaries

1. **D1 Computers & Components.** Webcams, scanners (including film scanners), printers (including photo printers), memory cards, card readers and camera USB and HDMI cables are D1. *Precedence sentence:* a webcam, film scanner, photo printer, memory card or card reader goes to D1, not D4, because the D1 branch of step 24 is tried first and names them; a camera strap, battery or lens filter goes to D4 because step 8 sends a part made for one kind of object to that object, and memory cards and cables fit many devices.
2. **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices.** Camera phones, phone gimbals and clip-on phone lenses. *Precedence sentence:* a smartphone, even one sold for its camera, goes to D2 because step 24's second branch names phones; a clip-on phone lens goes to D2 as a part made for phones under S3, while a lens made for interchangeable cameras goes to D4.
3. **B3 Lighting and B4 Decor.** Ring lights, LED panels, softboxes versus room lamps. *Precedence sentence:* a flash, studio light or ring light made for photography goes to D4, not B3, because step 24 is tried before step 30; a lamp made to light a room goes to B3, and a fairy-light or neon sign made as decoration is B4.
4. **B8 Home Safety & Security.** Security cameras, doorbell cameras, baby monitors, trail cameras. *Precedence sentence:* a camera made to watch a home or doorway goes to B8 under the step 24 note on connected household cameras, not D4; a camera made to take pictures or video for the owner (camcorder, action camera, 360 camera) goes to D4.
5. **C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments.** Binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, spotting scopes, digiscoping gear. *Precedence sentence:* binoculars and telescopes go to C6, not D4, because step 23 names them and is tried before step 24; a camera adapter or T-ring made to fit a camera to a telescope goes to D4 as a part of the camera.
6. **F5 Documents & Personal Records, F2 Art, I2 Digital Media & Items.** Prints, negatives, slides and photo files. *Precedence sentence:* unexposed film and unused photographic paper go to D4, but exposed or developed film, prints and slides of the owner's own life go to F5, photographs made as art go to F2, and the owner's photo files go to I2, because the carrier and the use decide the home, not the fact that a photograph is on it.
7. **E1 Toys & Games.** Toy cameras, children's play cameras. *Precedence sentence:* a camera made for play (a toy with a fake shutter) goes to E1, not D4, while a plastic film camera that takes real pictures (Holga, Lomo) goes to D4.
8. **D3 Audio & Video.** Slide and film projectors. *Precedence sentence:* a projector goes to D3 because step 24's third branch names projectors, while an enlarger, which projects a negative onto paper in a darkroom, goes to D4 as darkroom equipment.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure.** Candidate A: D4 → seven sub-families by role in the picture-making chain → leaf by device class within the role → kind at the buyer's comparison level → named form (model, series or stock). Maximum depth below the category: four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Mount, film format, flash and lighting mounts, sensor format, tier and condition are attributes held in a platform ledger, a tier field and a maker ledger; scenes are tags and generated views. Choice level: body class for cameras, focal class and optical design for lenses, source type for lights, emulsion type for film. Tier type: spec-ranked (sensor format, lens speed, film speed) with a maker-line tag. Maker centrality: platform, with identity for a few makers.

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. Should "full-frame mirrorless" and "APS-C mirrorless" be separate kinds (as Kakaku's popular filters suggest, S08) or one kind with sensor format as a tier attribute (recommended)?
2. Should lenses be grouped by format class (recommended: small format, medium, large, cine) or by camera-system class as every shop does (mirrorless, SLR, rangefinder; S02, S09, S18, S20)? The shop grouping is by mount era, which is a platform attribute.
3. Dashcams and other cameras made to fit one vehicle: D4 or G1 under S3 (step 8 sends a part made for one kind of object to that object)? Wildlife trail cameras: D4, B8 or E2?
4. Phone and camera tripods, gimbals and selfie sticks: one D4.4 home regardless of whether they take a phone (recommended), or follow the phone to D2?
5. Photographic paper: D4.6 (recommended, since the darkroom and film shelves hold it; S22) versus C5 paper goods or C4 for inkjet fine-art papers; inkjet paper may belong with printers and ink in D1.
6. Drones: the key puts "drones made for filming" in D4, but a drone is also a transport in G1 (powered aircraft) or a toy in E1; confirm D4 for camera drones and say how camera-less toy drones are filed.
7. Studio backdrops and photo-studio furniture (B1 or B2) and photo-booth equipment: confirm B1 and B2 under S3's furniture rule.
8. Is a camera maker ledger (maker, kinds, scene tags, tier, price band, country, era) plus a platform ledger the right home for compatibility, or should mounts become named forms?
9. Do cine and video cameras belong inside D4.1 as leaves (recommended, following the key's wording) or in a separate video category if the owner later creates one?

**Framework observations (not decisions).** The key's step 24 branch text does not name camcorders, action cameras or 360 cameras; they are read in here from "a camera ... for taking and making pictures" and from S11 and S09, where they sit with cameras. A one-line note in the next CATEGORIES version would remove the doubt.
