**One-line result.** Divide D4 by role in the picture-making chain (seven sub-families: cameras, lenses and optical attachments, light, support, film and sensitised media, darkroom, and the parts-and-accessories sub-family of C5(d)), then by a leaf axis that each sub-family states (recording technology for cameras, function for light, process stage for the darkroom; lenses, support and film skip the leaf level under C5(b)), then by the generic type buyers compare (kind), then by model, series or stock (named form). Lens mounts, flash shoes, light-modifier mounts, film formats and instant film formats go into the one compatibility ledger of C13 (the maker or a standards body sets them). Sensor format, format coverage, maximum aperture, focal class and ISO speed are attributes measured on the object (C4(b)). Maker lines carry a line rank; third-party scores and classes are tier entries on named forms, makers or lines, never on kinds (C4(a)). Maker, medium era and scene are never levels.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D4 Cameras & Photography (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key, step 24, fourth branch).** "A **camera** or equipment for taking and making pictures, powered or not: lenses, flashes and photo lighting, ring lights, tripods, film, darkroom equipment, drones made for filming." The category line adds: "cameras, lenses, photo lighting, tripods, film and darkroom equipment."

**Rules and notes that already touch D4.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): "a lens to Cameras" is the framework's own example. Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts "only when they are made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time and travel or stand with it": a camera bag is named. Never parts: furniture, racks, shelves, storage that holds several objects (so a studio shelving unit or a general equipment cabinet is B1).
- Step 8 (a part, attachment, accessory, case or consumable made for one kind of object goes to that object's category) is tried before step 24. So camera batteries, camera straps, lens filters, lens hoods, mount adapters and camera cages reach D4 through S3, not through step 24. By the same rule, an accessory made only for phones (a phone gimbal, phone tripod clamp, selfie stick or clip-on phone lens) goes to D2 before step 24 is read (review X-11(a)).
- Step 24, first branch (D1): webcams, scanners, printers, storage, card readers and "cables and adapters made to connect electronic devices, whatever device they serve" are D1. The D1 branch is tried first among the step-24 branches, so memory cards, card readers and standard-connector camera cables (USB, HDMI) stay D1. A cable with a proprietary connector made for one kind of camera (a camera-specific remote cable) is a part under step 8 and stays D4 (review X-11(b)).
- Step 24, second branch (D2): a smartphone with a camera is D2. Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object, such as a camera, is filed by its task at step 30 (security camera, doorbell: B8).
- Step 23 (C6): binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, weather instruments and loupes are C6. Its note files a measuring tool "made for a task the key files elsewhere" with that task, which sends a photographic light meter to D4.
- Step 30 branch B3 (lighting): a lamp for lighting a room. Step 24 is tried before step 30, so a ring light, flash or studio light made for photography goes to D4.
- Step 19 (F5) and step 20 (F2): photographic prints and slides "of the owner's own life" are F5; photographs made as art are F2. Step 5, I2: the owner's photographs held as files are I2. Step 28 note (C4): paints, inks and papers made for picture-making are C4.
- Step 25 (E1) and C15: toy cameras made for play that cannot take a picture are E1.
- Step 8 with step 11 (G1): a part "made to be fitted to or used up in one kind of object" goes to that object, which raises the dashcam question in section 9 and section 10 (owner question O-10).

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D4** (never levels): brand and maker (the strongest, because it sets compatibility), era (vintage and retro film gear), price band and quality tier, user (beginner, professional, child), market (consumer, studio, cinema), setting (underwater, travel, studio), scene (film revival, street, vlogging), condition (new, used, refurbished: the resale market grades gear, but no item's grade is stored, C16), edition (limited, Leica special editions: variants under C3), connectivity and power source (C10).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

The buyer's first decision is **the system**, then the device class. Evidence:
- B&H and Map Camera split lenses by camera system before any optical type (S04, S25); Digitec's "popular filters" and Geizhals' preset list are mounts (Sony E, Canon RF, Nikon Z; S32, S33); KEH advertises "Shop by Top Mounts" (S15).
- Chinese buyer posts frame the choice as 卡口 (mount) and the cost of switching (S27); the English guide says "every camera system uses a specific lens mount" (S18).
- For film the first decision is the format (35 mm, 120, 4x5, Instax Mini or Wide): B&H film, Freestyle film size and The Darkroom lab all open with it (S05, S21, S20); DPReview's first advice is to start with 35 mm (S19); Ted's rule is to match the film format to the camera before anything else (S34).
- For lighting the first decision is the source (strobe, continuous, speedlight) and then the mount of the modifier system (S35).
- For cameras the second decision, after the system, is body class (mirrorless, DSLR, rangefinder, compact, medium format, camcorder, action; S03, S24, S32) and the third is sensor format (full-frame, APS-C, Micro Four Thirds; S03, S24, S32, S29).

Implication: the first axis is **platform compatibility** (maker-set), which the framework forbids as a level (brand and maker are modifiers; C13: platforms are never levels). So the tree cannot follow the buyer's first choice; it follows the **role of the object** and records the platform (mount, film format, instant format, flash shoe, modifier mount) in the compatibility ledger, which generated views can sort on. Section 6 handles this.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): role in the picture-making chain

Levels, one axis each (C5(c): the leaf axis is stated per sub-family):
1. Sub-family: **role in making a picture** (capture, optics, light, support, sensitised media, processing; plus the C5(d) parts-and-accessories sub-family, which is exempt from this axis).
2. Leaf: **the axis each sub-family states** (below). Sub-families with about 12 kinds or fewer skip the leaf level and store `leaf = null` (C5(b)).
3. Kind: **the generic type buyers compare** (the choice level of section 4b).
4. Named form: **model, series, stock or iconic design** (`maker_bound: yes | no`, C2).

Sub-families and their leaf axes:
- **D4.1 Cameras** (the capture device). Leaf axis: **recording technology**, admitted as a kind-level split by C10(b): film needs film and processing, the body and the workflow differ, and shops and the resale market shelve the two apart (S01, S24, S32, S16). Leaves: **Digital cameras**; **Film cameras**; **Instant cameras** (instant film is its own medium with its own camera bodies: integral and pack film, S34, S10). Kinds inside each leaf are ordered by body and viewing system. OWNER: O-49 asks whether cine and video cameras stay inside D4.1; this memo keeps them here (as kinds in Digital cameras), as before.
- **D4.2 Lenses & optical attachments.** `leaf = null` (13 kinds). Kinds by **optical design**: general-purpose (prime, zoom), special-purpose (macro, fisheye, tilt-shift, anamorphic, special effect), cine lens, then optical attachments and adapters (S04, S24, S25, S33). Format coverage (full frame, APS-C, medium format, large format) and focal class are attributes; the mount is a compatibility-ledger entry.
- **D4.3 Light.** Leaf axis: **what the object does with light**: makes a flash; gives continuous light; shapes light; holds the source. Leaves: **Flash**; **Continuous light**; **Light modifiers**; **Light stands and mounts** (S07, S35, S36).
- **D4.4 Support & stabilisation.** `leaf = null` (9 kinds): tripods, heads, quick-release systems, monopods, handheld gimbals, rigs, cages and mounts (S30).
- **D4.5 Film & sensitised media.** `leaf = null` (6 kinds). Kinds by **process** (the chemistry the film is made for): black-and-white negative; colour negative (C-41); colour negative (ECN-2, motion picture); colour reversal (E-6); integral instant film; peel-apart instant film (S21, S34, S10, S31). Film format (135, 120, 4x5, Super 8 and other gauges, instant formats) is a compatibility-ledger attribute.
- **D4.6 Darkroom & processing.** Leaf axis: **the stage of the darkroom process the object serves**, with consumable materials used across stages in a last leaf (C14(c)). Leaves: **Film processing**; **Enlarging and exposure**; **Print processing**; **Darkroom materials** (S06, S22).
- **D4.7 Parts and accessories made for one camera or lens** (C5(d), review X-10; exempt from the leaf axis). Bags and cases, straps, batteries, chargers and grips, viewfinders and eyepieces, remote releases (including camera-specific cables), light meters, cleaning kits, underwater housings, lens hoods and caps, telescope adapters (S01, S30, S37). OWNER: O-6 confirms the C5(d) naming for domain D.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. A **hybrid vlog mirrorless** (for example Sony ZV-E10) records stills and video: placed by its body class (mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera, S03), not by the video bias; "vlogging" is a tag.
2. A **speed booster** or **focal reducer adapter** is both a mount adapter and an optical converter (S18): one home as the kind "focal reducer" in D4.2, with both mounts recorded as compatibility relations (adapts to through an adapter).
3. A **camera cage with handles** is a rig (D4.4) or an accessory (D4.7); a cage supports the camera for shooting, so it is the D4.4 kind "camera cage".
4. An **instant-film camera** such as Instax Mini is both a camera and bound to one film format; the camera is the D4.1 kind "integral-film instant camera", the film is the D4.5 kind "integral instant film", and both point to the same `instant film format` entry in the compatibility ledger.
5. A **phone gimbal** or **selfie stick made for phones** looks like D4.4 support, but step 8 sends an accessory made for one kind of object (phones) to D2 before step 24 is read. A tripod or gimbal made for cameras that also takes a phone stays in D4.4 (review X-11(a)).

### Candidate B: medium first (digital | film | studio | darkroom)

Levels: sub-family by **medium era** (digital, analogue film), leaf by device role, kind by type. This mirrors Map Camera (digital camera vs film camera, S24), B&H (Digital Cameras vs Film Cameras, S01) and Geizhals (Digitalkameras vs Analogkameras, S32).
Break points: (1) lighting, tripods, bags and most lenses serve both media, so they need either duplication (breaking one home) or a third "both" bucket; (2) adapted vintage glass on digital bodies (Leica M and M42 lenses on Sony E; S18) is a lens that is "film-era" by origin but "digital" by use; (3) the darkroom would sit under analogue while the film scanner (D1) and digital negative workflows cross the line. Candidate A also cuts digital from film, but only inside D4.1 and only for the capture body, where C10(b) admits the split (recording technology changes the body and needs film and processing); Candidate B fails on break points 1 to 3, because it applies the split to gear that serves both media, not on the word "era" alone.

### Candidate C: platform first (mount system)

Levels: sub-family by **camera system** (Canon RF, Nikon Z, Sony E, Leica M, Micro Four Thirds, ...), leaf by device role, kind by type. This mirrors the buyer's first decision (section 2) and B&H, KEH and Digitec lens navigation.
Break points: (1) maker is an Appendix A modifier and platforms are never levels (C13); (2) third-party lenses (Sigma, Tamron) exist in several mounts, so the same kind appears many times (S18, S33); (3) objects with no system (tripods, film, darkroom, lights) have no home; (4) one lens with an adapter belongs to two systems. Retained only as a **generated view** (see sections 6 and 7).

## 4. Depth and size

**Rough count of generic kinds** (judgement, not a sourced figure): about 100 to 130 under D4, of which 96 are in the worked tree: cameras 22 to 28, lenses and optical attachments 13 to 15, light 19 to 25, support 9 to 12, film 6 to 8, darkroom 14 to 20, parts and accessories 13 to 20. The count fell from the research draft's 180 to 220 because format coverage, focal class, sensor format and film format are now attributes, not kinds (D4-1 to D4-6).

**Rough count of named forms** (models, series, stocks): film stocks listed on Wikipedia run to roughly 200 (S31); camera and lens models run to tens of thousands over time. A practical attested seed of 600 to 1,000 is sufficient for stage 3 (series-level camera forms such as Sony α7 or Leica M, all listed film stocks). Famous maker lines (Sigma Art; Canon L † and Sony G Master †) are rows in the lines table (C1), not named forms. The film format series (110, 120, 135, sheet sizes) and the instant formats are compatibility-ledger entries (C13), not named forms. Colourways, special editions (Leica special editions) and reference numbers are variants (C3).

**Where depth concentrates.**
- Lenses: breadth in optical design crossed with mount and format coverage, so most of the size is in the compatibility ledger (S11 lists dozens of mounts) and in named forms. The kinds stay few because neither the mount nor the format coverage is a kind.
- Cameras: breadth in film camera types (S24, S09) and in mirrorless series (S03, S26).
- Film: stock names (hundreds) under six process kinds (S31, S21).
- Light: modifiers, which have many shapes (S35, S36).

**Broad, medium and narrow leaves and sub-families.**
- Broad: Digital cameras, D4.2 lenses (as a sub-family), Light modifiers, D4.7 parts and accessories.
- Medium: Film cameras, Flash, Continuous light, D4.4 support, D4.5 film stocks, Enlarging and exposure.
- Narrow: Instant cameras, Light stands and mounts, Film processing, Print processing, Darkroom materials.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers.

- **Cameras.** Buyers compare within a body class and a sensor format: Kakaku's top filters are sensor type and mount (S03); Geizhals' camera filters are maker, sensor format and type (mirrorless vs DSLR vs rangefinder; S32); DXOMARK ranks within type and sensor format (S29). Recommendation: **kind = body and viewing system within the recording technology** (mirrorless interchangeable-lens, DSLR, compact and so on for digital; SLR, rangefinder, viewfinder, TLR, view camera for film); **sensor format is an attribute** measured on the object (C4(b), S12), and series (α7, EOS R, X-T) is the named form. Medium-format digital is not a separate kind: GFX and Hasselblad X are mirrorless bodies, and Kakaku lists GFX among the mirrorless series (S03); they carry `sensor_format = medium format`, and a "medium format" view lists them (review D4-5). The alternative, making "full-frame mirrorless" and "APS-C mirrorless" separate kinds, is supported by Kakaku's popular filters (S03) and by Sony's own ladder (S26). OWNER: owner question 1 (O-40) decides this; the memo keeps its recommendation (sensor format as an attribute).
- **Film cameras.** Buyers compare by viewing and body system (SLR, rangefinder, TLR, view camera; S24, S16, S09) and pick a film format; the format is the camera's compatibility, not its kind (review D4-3). Holga and Lomo cameras are named forms under viewfinder cameras.
- **Instant cameras.** The film type sets the body (integral or peel-apart; S34, S10); the maker formats (Instax Mini, Wide, Square; Polaroid i-Type, 600, SX-70, Go) are `instant film format` entries in the compatibility ledger, not kinds (review D4-4, Appendix A brand, C13).
- **Lenses.** Buyers compare within prime or zoom and focal class (Map Camera focal classes, Digitec lens types; S24, S33), and within a maker line (Sigma Art is named in S33; Canon L and Sony G Master are general knowledge, not fetched evidence †). Recommendation: **kind = prime lens, zoom lens and the special optical designs** (macro, fisheye, tilt-shift, anamorphic, special effect), plus the cine lens, whose body differs (T-stops, geared rings, cine mounts; S04, S17, S11). `focal_class` is an attribute derived from the full-frame-equivalent focal length, with band edges taken from Map Camera's classes (wide, standard or medium-telephoto, telephoto; S24); the millimetre thresholds are not in the fetched text and need a fetched attestation † before the attribute is filled (review D4-2). `format_coverage` (full frame, APS-C, medium format, large format) is an attribute, so a Hasselblad XCD 80 mm and a 4x5 view-camera lens are both prime lenses (review D4-1). The mount is a compatibility entry, which keeps the same Tamron design in E, Z and RF versions as one named form (S18). A generated view lists lenses by focal class.
- **Light.** Buyers compare by source (strobe, speedlight, LED) and by modifier shape (S35, S36). Recommendation: **kind = source type for lights, shape for modifiers**; power is an attribute; the modifier mount (Bowens, Profoto) and the flash shoe are compatibility entries.
- **Film.** Buyers choose a format, then emulsion type, then stock (S21, S19). Recommendation: **kind = process** (black-and-white negative, C-41 colour negative, ECN-2 colour negative †, E-6 reversal, integral instant, peel-apart instant); a cine stock such as Kodak Vision3 † is an ECN-2 colour negative, not a separate "cine film" kind, so the kinds no longer overlap (review D4-6). Film format (135, 120, 4x5, Super 8, 16 mm and 35 mm motion-picture gauges, Instax Mini) is a compatibility attribute because the camera sets it, and the stock (Portra 400, HP5 Plus) is the named form.
- **Support.** Buyers compare tripods by material and load (attributes), heads by type (ball, fluid, gimbal; S30). Kind = tripod, tabletop tripod, tripod head, quick-release system, monopod, handheld gimbal stabiliser, rig, cage, mount; head types are named forms with `maker_bound: no` (C2) unless the owner wants them split.
- **Darkroom.** Enlarger type (condenser, diffusion, colour dichroic; S22) is where buyers compare. Kind = enlarger; type is an attribute.

Depth differs by sub-family: D4.1, D4.3 and D4.6 use all four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); D4.2, D4.4, D4.5 and D4.7 skip the leaf (`leaf = null`); film and darkroom stop at kind with stock and model as named forms; light and support mostly stop at kind.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended centrality: platform, with identity for a small set.**

Retailers lead with mount (S04, S25, S33, S15). Mounts are shared across makers in places (Leica L alliance, Micro Four Thirds shared by Olympus and Panasonic; S25, S11) and one third-party design ships in several mounts (S18).

**Kinds: canonical form first, then the secondary key each sub-family states (C7). Named forms: maker, then year (C7). Spec orders are generated views.**
- **Kinds.** Digital cameras: mirrorless before DSLR before rangefinder before compact, then the video-first and special bodies. Film cameras: SLR, rangefinder, viewfinder, TLR, view, box or folding, single-use, pinhole. Lenses: prime, zoom, special designs, cine, attachments. Light: speedlight before monolight before pack and head (portability, S35); modifiers soft to hard (S35, S36). Film: by process as listed. Darkroom: by process stage.
- **Named forms (stored order, C7).** By maker in the maker ledger's `name` order, then first-introduction year ascending as attested, then alphabetically. OWNER: review X-6 adds "then line in `line_rank` order" between maker and year for domain D (O-5); applied provisionally.
- **Generated views, not stored order.** Sensor format descending (medium format, full frame, APS-C, Micro Four Thirds, 1 inch; Map Camera and Geizhals order system cameras this way, S24, S32, S12); lenses by focal length wide to telephoto (S24); film stocks by ISO ascending (S31); film formats by size (110, 135, 120, 4x5, 8x10; S10).
- **Variants** (C3): by `variant_code` or year.
- Chronology is for the museum or collector view only (S37).

## 9. Boundaries

1. **D1 Computers & Components.** Webcams, scanners (including film scanners), printers (including photo printers), memory cards, card readers and standard USB and HDMI cables are D1. *Precedence sentence:* a webcam, film scanner, photo printer, memory card, card reader or standard-connector cable goes to D1, not D4, because the D1 branch of step 24 names them and they serve many devices; a camera strap, battery, battery charger, lens filter or camera-specific remote cable goes to D4 because step 8 sends a part or accessory made for one kind of object to that object (review X-11(b), X-11(c)).
2. **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices.** Camera phones, phone gimbals, phone tripod clamps, selfie sticks and clip-on phone lenses. *Precedence sentence:* a smartphone, even one sold for its camera, goes to D2 because step 24's second branch names phones; a gimbal, tripod clamp, selfie stick or clip-on lens made only for phones goes to D2 (its parts sub-family) because step 8 sends an accessory made for one kind of object to that object before step 24 is read; a tripod or gimbal made for cameras that also takes a phone stays in D4.4 (review D4-8, X-11(a); owner question O-7 confirms).
3. **B3 Lighting and B4 Decor.** Ring lights, LED panels, softboxes versus room lamps. *Precedence sentence:* a flash, studio light or ring light made for photography goes to D4, not B3, because step 24 is tried before step 30; a lamp made to light a room goes to B3, and a fairy-light or neon sign made as decoration is B4.
4. **B8 Home Safety & Security.** Security cameras, doorbell cameras, baby monitors. *Precedence sentence:* a camera made to watch a home or doorway goes to B8 under the step 24 note on connected household cameras, not D4; a camera made to take pictures or video for the owner (camcorder, action camera, 360 camera) goes to D4. OWNER: wildlife trail cameras (D4, B8 or E2) are owner question O-47; this memo makes no recommendation.
5. **C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments.** Binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, spotting scopes, digiscoping gear. *Precedence sentence:* binoculars and telescopes go to C6, not D4, because step 23 names them and is tried before step 24; a camera adapter or T-ring made to fit a camera to a telescope goes to D4 (D4.7) as a part of the camera.
6. **F5 Documents & Personal Records, F2 Art, I2 Digital Media & Items.** Prints, negatives, slides and photo files. *Precedence sentence:* unexposed film and unused photographic paper go to D4, but exposed or developed film, prints and slides of the owner's own life go to F5, photographs made as art go to F2, and the owner's photo files go to I2, because the carrier and the use decide the home, not the fact that a photograph is on it.
7. **E1 Toys & Games.** Toy cameras, children's play cameras, camera-less toy drones. *Precedence sentence:* a camera made for play that cannot take a picture (a toy with a fake shutter) goes to E1, not D4 (C15), while a plastic film camera that takes real pictures (Holga, Lomo) goes to D4, and a child-sized camera that takes real pictures stays in D4 (user is an attribute, C6). OWNER: O-46 confirms camera drones as D4 (the key) and camera-less toy drones as E1.
8. **D3 Audio & Video.** Slide and film projectors. *Precedence sentence:* a projector goes to D3 because step 24's third branch names projectors, while an enlarger, which projects a negative onto paper in a darkroom, goes to D4 as darkroom equipment. (The D3 tree has no slide or cine projector kind yet; review D3-5 adds them on the D3 side.)
9. **G1 Vehicles.** Dashcams and other cameras made to be fitted to one kind of vehicle. *Precedence sentence (proposed by review X-11(d), not decided):* a camera made to be fitted to or mounted on a vehicle, such as a dashcam, goes to G1 under step 8; an action camera made for general use and only mounted on a vehicle stays in D4. OWNER: O-10 decides, with the G1 memo's agreement; until then the dashcam has no D4 kind.
10. **A4 Bags & Accessories.** Camera backpacks and general backpacks. *Precedence sentence (from _review_A):* a backpack made with fitted dividers to carry a camera and its lenses is a camera bag, D4 (D4.7) under S3, which names camera bags; a general backpack with a padded sleeve is A4.
11. **B1 Furniture & Storage, B2 Soft Furnishings.** Studio backdrops, backdrop stands, photo-studio furniture and photo-booth equipment. *Precedence sentence:* furniture and storage that hold several objects or stand in a room as furniture are never parts under S3 (B1); OWNER: O-48 confirms backdrops and studio furniture in B1 and B2.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure.** Candidate A: D4 → seven sub-families by role in the picture-making chain (the seventh is the C5(d) parts-and-accessories sub-family) → leaf by the axis each sub-family states (D4.1 recording technology, D4.3 what the object does with light, D4.6 process stage; `leaf = null` in D4.2, D4.4, D4.5 and D4.7) → kind at the buyer's comparison level → named form (model, series or stock). Maximum depth below the category: four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Mounts, flash shoes, light-modifier mounts, film formats and instant formats are compatibility-ledger entries (C13); sensor format, format coverage, focal class, maximum aperture and ISO speed are attributes (C4(b)); maker lines carry a `line_rank`; tier entries sit on makers, lines or named forms (C4(a)); condition is a scale reference only (C16).
