**One-line result.** Divide D4 by role in the picture-making chain (seven sub-families: cameras, lenses and optical attachments, light, support, film and sensitised media, darkroom, and the parts-and-accessories sub-family of C5(d)), then by a leaf axis that each sub-family states (recording technology for cameras, function for light, process stage for the darkroom; lenses, support and film skip the leaf level under C5(b)), then by the generic type buyers compare (kind), then by model, series or stock (named form). ## 0. Category

**Code and name.** D4 Cameras & Photography (domain D, Electronics).

**Membership test (placement key CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 24, fourth branch).** "A **camera** (camcorders, action and 360 cameras, and wildlife trail cameras included) or equipment for taking and making pictures, powered or not: lenses, flashes and photo lighting, ring lights, tripods, film, photographic paper, darkroom equipment, drones made for filming." (K-151 and K-156 added the bracket and photographic paper.) The category line adds: "cameras, lenses, photo lighting, tripods, film and darkroom equipment."

**Rules and notes that already touch D4.**
- S3 (parts follow the whole): "a lens to Cameras" is the framework's own example. Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts "only when they are made to hold, carry or protect one object at a time and travel or stand with it": a camera bag is named. Never parts: furniture, racks, shelves, storage that holds several objects (so a studio shelving unit or a general equipment cabinet is B1).
- Step 8 (a part, attachment, accessory, case or consumable made for one kind of object goes to that object's category) is tried before step 24. So camera batteries, camera straps, lens filters, lens hoods, mount adapters and camera cages reach D4 through S3, not through step 24. By the same rule, an accessory made only for phones (a phone gimbal, phone tripod clamp, selfie stick or clip-on phone lens) goes to D2 before step 24 is read (review X-11(a)).
- Step 24, first branch (D1): webcams, scanners, printers, storage, card readers, "cables with standard connectors" and "generic chargers (USB, GaN, Qi)" are D1. The D1 branch is tried first among the step-24 branches, so memory cards, card readers and standard-connector camera cables (USB, HDMI) stay D1. S3b (K-2, K-3, K-4): a cable with a proprietary connector made for one device follows it, so a camera-specific remote cable is D4; a charger or battery made for one device follows it, so camera batteries and their chargers are D4; a generic USB charger is D1 and a charger for standard-size cells C8.
- S3b (K-15, K-17): "A universal tripod is D4. A camera made only to fit a telescope is C6 (S3); a camera made for general photography as well is D4." "Studio backdrops are B2 and studio furniture B1; studio lights, stands and flashes are D4."
- S3b (K-6): "Electronics made to be fitted to a vehicle (car audio, dashcam, mounted sat-nav, bike computer, OBD tracker) are G1 (S3)."
- Step 24, second branch (D2): a smartphone with a camera is D2. Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object, such as a camera, is filed by its task at step 30 (security camera, doorbell: B8).
- Step 23 (C6): binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, weather instruments and loupes are C6. Its note files a measuring tool "made for a task the key files elsewhere" with that task, which sends a photographic light meter to D4.
- Step 30 branch B3 (lighting): a lamp for lighting a room. Step 24 is tried before step 30, so a ring light, flash or studio light made for photography goes to D4.
- Step 19 (F5) and step 20 (F2), as amended by K-139 and K-141: photographic prints and "developed negatives and slides" are F5; "a photographic print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2; every other photographic print, slide or negative, whoever is shown, is F5." Step 5, I2: the owner's photographs held as files are I2. Step 28 note (C4): paints, inks and papers made for picture-making are C4, but photographic paper is named in the D4 branch (K-156).
- Step 25 (E1) and C15: toy cameras made for play that cannot take a picture are E1. Step 11 note (K-97): "An RC or FPV drone sold as a hobby model is E1; one made for filming is D4; camera-less toy drones … are E1."

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in D4** (never levels): brand and maker (the strongest, because it sets compatibility), era (vintage and retro film gear), price band and quality tier, user (the C6 user list, child included; "beginner" is a level scheme under C4, not a user, CV-19), market (consumer, studio, cinema), setting (underwater, travel, studio, wildlife), scene (film revival, street, vlogging), condition (new, used, refurbished: the resale market grades gear, but no item's grade is stored, C16), edition (limited, Leica special editions: variants under C3), connectivity and power source (C10).

## 2. The first axis people choose by

The buyer's first decision is **the system**, then the device class. Evidence:
- B&H and Map Camera split lenses by camera system before any optical type (S04, S25); Digitec's "popular filters" and Geizhals' preset list are mounts (Sony E, Canon RF, Nikon Z; S32, S33); KEH advertises "Shop by Top Mounts" (S15).
- Chinese buyer posts frame the choice as 卡口 (mount) and the cost of switching (S27); the English guide says "every camera system uses a specific lens mount" (S18).
- For film the first decision is the format (35 mm, 120, 4x5, Instax Mini or Wide): B&H film, Freestyle film size and The Darkroom lab all open with it (S05, S21, S20); DPReview's first advice is to start with 35 mm (S19); Ted's rule is to match the film format to the camera before anything else (S34).
- For lighting the first decision is the source (strobe, continuous, speedlight) and then the mount of the modifier system (S35).
- For cameras the second decision, after the system, is body class (mirrorless, DSLR, rangefinder, compact, medium format, camcorder, action; S03, S24, S32) and the third is sensor format (full-frame, APS-C, Micro Four Thirds; S03, S24, S32, S29).

Implication: the first axis is **platform compatibility** (maker-set), which the framework forbids as a level (brand and maker are modifiers; C13: platforms are never levels). So the tree cannot follow the buyer's first choice; it follows the **role of the object** and records the platform (mount, film format, instant format, flash shoe, modifier mount) in the compatibility ledger, which generated views can sort on. Section 6 handles this.

## 3. Candidate structures

### Candidate A (recommended): role in the picture-making chain

Levels, one axis each (C5(c): the leaf axis is stated per sub-family):
1. Sub-family: **role in making a picture** (capture, optics, light, support, sensitised media, processing; plus the C5(d) parts-and-accessories sub-family, which is exempt from this axis).
2. Leaf: **the axis each sub-family states** (below). Sub-families with about 12 kinds or fewer skip the leaf level and store `leaf = null` (C5(b)).
3. Kind: **the generic type buyers compare** (the choice level of section 4b).
4. Named form: **model, series, stock or iconic design** (`maker_bound: yes | no`, C2).

Sub-families and their leaf axes:
- **D4.1 Cameras** (the capture device). Leaf axis: **recording technology**, admitted as a kind-level split by C10(b): film needs film and processing, the body and the workflow differ, and shops and the resale market shelve the two apart (S01, S24, S32, S16). Leaves: **Digital cameras**; **Film cameras**; **Instant cameras** (instant film is its own medium with its own camera bodies: integral and pack film, S34, S10). Kinds inside each leaf are ordered by body and viewing system. Cine and video cameras stay inside D4.1 as kinds in Digital cameras (decision D-21, with K-151 naming camcorders, action and 360 cameras in the D4 branch). The wildlife trail camera (K-156) and the photo booth (D4-O1) are kinds in Digital cameras too.
- **D4.2 Lenses & optical attachments.** `leaf = null` (13 kinds). Kinds by **optical design**: general-purpose (prime, zoom), special-purpose (macro, fisheye, tilt-shift, anamorphic, special effect), cine lens, then optical attachments and adapters (S04, S24, S25, S33). Format coverage (full frame, APS-C, medium format, large format) and focal class are attributes; the mount is a compatibility-ledger entry.
- **D4.3 Light.** Leaf axis: **what the object does with light**: makes a flash; gives continuous light; shapes light; holds the source or the studio backdrop. Leaves: **Flash**; **Continuous light**; **Light modifiers**; **Light stands and mounts** (S07, S35, S36).
- **D4.4 Support & stabilisation.** `leaf = null` (9 kinds): tripods, heads, quick-release systems, monopods, handheld gimbals, rigs, cages and mounts (S30).
- **D4.5 Film & sensitised media.** `leaf = null` (6 kinds). Kinds by **process** (the chemistry the film is made for): black-and-white negative; colour negative (C-41); colour negative (ECN-2, motion picture); colour reversal (E-6); integral instant film; peel-apart instant film (S21, S34, S10, S31). Film format (135, 120, 4x5, Super 8 and other gauges, instant formats) is a compatibility-ledger attribute.
- **D4.6 Darkroom & processing.** Leaf axis: **the stage of the darkroom process the object serves**, with consumable materials used across stages in a last leaf (C14(c)). Leaves: **Film processing**; **Enlarging and exposure**; **Print processing**; **Darkroom materials** (S06, S22).
- **D4.7 Parts and accessories made for one camera or lens** (C5(d), review X-10; exempt from the leaf axis). Bags and cases, straps, batteries, chargers and grips, viewfinders and eyepieces, remote releases (including camera-specific cables), light meters, cleaning kits, underwater housings, lens hoods and caps, telescope adapters (S01, S30, S37). Decision CV-17 makes this closing sub-family standing for every category that receives S3 parts, domain D included; its leaf axis is the object served (camera or lens), and with 13 kinds it stores `leaf = null` (C5(b)).

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. A **hybrid vlog mirrorless** (for example Sony ZV-E10) records stills and video: placed by its body class (mirrorless interchangeable-lens camera, S03), not by the video bias; "vlogging" is a tag.
2. A **speed booster** or **focal reducer adapter** is both a mount adapter and an optical converter (S18): one home as the kind "focal reducer" in D4.2, with both mounts recorded as compatibility relations (adapts to through an adapter).
3. A **camera cage with handles** is a rig (D4.4) or an accessory (D4.7); a cage supports the camera for shooting, so it is the D4.4 kind "camera cage".
4. An **instant-film camera** such as Instax Mini is both a camera and bound to one film format; the camera is the D4.1 kind "integral-film instant camera", the film is the D4.5 kind "integral instant film", and both point to the same `instant film format` entry in the compatibility ledger.
5. A **phone gimbal** or **selfie stick made for phones** looks like D4.4 support, but S3b (K-5) sends "gimbals, selfie sticks, clip-on lenses … made only for phones" to D2. A tripod or gimbal made for cameras that also takes a phone stays in D4.4, and a universal tripod is D4 (K-15).
6. A **trail camera** is a camera made to be left outdoors and triggered by motion; K-156 files it in D4 ("a camera; setting and use are modifiers"), so wildlife is a setting tag, and it is the D4.1 kind "trail camera", not a B8 security camera.

### Candidate B: medium first (digital | film | studio | darkroom)

Levels: sub-family by **medium era** (digital, analogue film), leaf by device role, kind by type. This mirrors Map Camera (digital camera vs film camera, S24), B&H (Digital Cameras vs Film Cameras, S01) and Geizhals (Digitalkameras vs Analogkameras, S32).
Break points: (1) lighting, tripods, bags and most lenses serve both media, so they need either duplication (breaking one home) or a third "both" bucket; (2) adapted vintage glass on digital bodies (Leica M and M42 lenses on Sony E; S18) is a lens that is "film-era" by origin but "digital" by use; (3) the darkroom would sit under analogue while the film scanner (D1) and digital negative workflows cross the line. Candidate A also cuts digital from film, but only inside D4.1 and only for the capture body, where C10(b) admits the split (recording technology changes the body and needs film and processing); Candidate B fails on break points 1 to 3, because it applies the split to gear that serves both media, not on the word "era" alone.

### Candidate C: platform first (mount system)

Levels: sub-family by **camera system** (Canon RF, Nikon Z, Sony E, Leica M, Micro Four Thirds, ...), leaf by device role, kind by type. This mirrors the buyer's first decision (section 2) and B&H, KEH and Digitec lens navigation.
Break points: (1) maker is an Appendix A modifier and platforms are never levels (C13); (2) third-party lenses (Sigma, Tamron) exist in several mounts, so the same kind appears many times (S18, S33); (3) objects with no system (tripods, film, darkroom, lights) have no home; (4) one lens with an adapter belongs to two systems. Retained only as a **generated view** (see sections 6 and 7).

## 4. Depth and size

**Rough count of generic kinds** (judgement, not a sourced figure): about 100 to 130 under D4, of which 98 are in the worked tree: cameras 24 to 30, lenses and optical attachments 13 to 15, light 19 to 25, support 9 to 12, film 6 to 8, darkroom 14 to 20, parts and accessories 13 to 20. The count fell from the research draft's 180 to 220 because format coverage, focal class, sensor format and film format are now attributes, not kinds (D4-1 to D4-6).

**Rough count of named forms** (models, series, stocks): film stocks listed on Wikipedia run to roughly 200 (S31); camera and lens models run to tens of thousands over time. There is no cap (owner depth-and-breadth rule; CV-5, CV-7): named forms, lines and makers are harvested in stage 3 from the catalogues that already list them (camera-wiki.org, the Wikipedia lists of film stocks and lens mounts, the DXOMARK database, maker catalogues, KEH, MPB and Map Camera listings; S09, S31, S11, S29, S15, S17, S24) and grow as far as the attested evidence goes, one fetched page per row. Famous maker lines (Sigma Art; Canon L † and Sony G Master †) are rows in the lines table (C1), not named forms. The film format series (110, 120, 135, sheet sizes) and the instant formats are compatibility-ledger entries (C13), not named forms. Colourways, special editions (Leica special editions), body-only against kit bundles and reference numbers are variants (C3), filled wherever a community compares them (CV-8).

**Where depth concentrates.**

- Cameras: breadth in film camera types (S24, S09) and in mirrorless series (S03, S26).
- Film: stock names (hundreds) under six process kinds (S31, S21).
- Light: modifiers, which have many shapes (S35, S36).

**Broad, medium and narrow leaves and sub-families.**
- Broad: Digital cameras, D4.2 lenses (as a sub-family), Light modifiers, D4.7 parts and accessories.
- Medium: Film cameras, Flash, Continuous light, D4.4 support, D4.5 film stocks, Enlarging and exposure.
- Narrow: Instant cameras, Light stands and mounts, Film processing, Print processing, Darkroom materials.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule:** a kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers.

- **Cameras.** Buyers compare within a body class and a sensor format: Kakaku's top filters are sensor type and mount (S03); Geizhals' camera filters are maker, sensor format and type (mirrorless vs DSLR vs rangefinder; S32); DXOMARK ranks within type and sensor format (S29). Recommendation: **kind = body and viewing system within the recording technology** (mirrorless interchangeable-lens, DSLR, compact and so on for digital; SLR, rangefinder, viewfinder, TLR, view camera for film); **sensor format is an attribute** measured on the object (C4(c) class (i), S12), and series (α7, EOS R, X-T) is the named form. Medium-format digital is not a separate kind: GFX and Hasselblad X are mirrorless bodies, and Kakaku lists GFX among the mirrorless series (S03); they carry `sensor_format = medium format`, and a "medium format" view lists them (review D4-5). The alternative, making "full-frame mirrorless" and "APS-C mirrorless" separate kinds (Kakaku's popular filters, S03; Sony's own ladder, S26), is not taken: decision D-19 makes sensor format, medium format included, an attribute, with generated views by sensor format.
- **Film cameras.** Buyers compare by viewing and body system (SLR, rangefinder, TLR, view camera; S24, S16, S09) and pick a film format; the format is the camera's compatibility, not its kind (review D4-3). Holga and Lomo cameras are named forms under viewfinder cameras.
- **Instant cameras.** The film type sets the body (integral or peel-apart; S34, S10); the maker formats (Instax Mini, Wide, Square; Polaroid i-Type, 600, SX-70, Go) are `instant film format` entries in the compatibility ledger, not kinds (review D4-4, Appendix A brand, C13).
- **Lenses.** Buyers compare within prime or zoom and focal class (Map Camera focal classes, Digitec lens types; S24, S33), and within a maker line (Sigma Art is named in S33; Canon L and Sony G Master are general knowledge, not fetched evidence †). Recommendation: **kind = prime lens, zoom lens and the special optical designs** (macro, fisheye, tilt-shift, anamorphic, special effect), plus the cine lens, whose body differs (T-stops, geared rings, cine mounts; S04, S17, S11). `focal_class` is an attribute derived from the full-frame-equivalent focal length, with band edges taken from Map Camera's classes (wide, standard or medium-telephoto, telephoto; S24); the millimetre thresholds are not in the fetched text and need a fetched attestation † before the attribute is filled (review D4-2). `format_coverage` (full frame, APS-C, medium format, large format) is an attribute, so a Hasselblad XCD 80 mm and a 4x5 view-camera lens are both prime lenses (review D4-1). The mount is a compatibility entry, which keeps the same Tamron design in E, Z and RF versions as one named form (S18). A generated view lists lenses by focal class.
- **Light.** Buyers compare by source (strobe, speedlight, LED) and by modifier shape (S35, S36). Recommendation: **kind = source type for lights, shape for modifiers**; power is an attribute; the modifier mount (Bowens, Profoto) and the flash shoe are compatibility entries.
- **Film.** Buyers choose a format, then emulsion type, then stock (S21, S19). Recommendation: **kind = process** (black-and-white negative, C-41 colour negative, ECN-2 colour negative †, E-6 reversal, integral instant, peel-apart instant); a cine stock such as Kodak Vision3 † is an ECN-2 colour negative, not a separate "cine film" kind, so the kinds no longer overlap (review D4-6). Film format (135, 120, 4x5, Super 8, 16 mm and 35 mm motion-picture gauges, Instax Mini) is a compatibility attribute because the camera sets it, and the stock (Portra 400, HP5 Plus) is the named form.
- **Support.** Buyers compare tripods by material and load (attributes), heads by type (ball, fluid, gimbal; S30). Kind = tripod, tabletop tripod, tripod head, quick-release system, monopod, handheld gimbal stabiliser, rig, cage, mount; head types are named forms with `maker_bound: no` (C2), not kinds.
- **Darkroom.** Enlarger type (condenser, diffusion, colour dichroic; S22) is where buyers compare. Kind = enlarger; type is an attribute. Photographic printing paper is one D4.6 kind (K-156), with `paper_process` (silver-gelatin fibre or resin-coated, chromogenic colour, inkjet photo paper) and surface and grade as attributes; copy and printer paper stay C5 (K-158).

Depth differs by sub-family: D4.1, D4.3 and D4.6 use all four levels (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); D4.2, D4.4, D4.5 and D4.7 skip the leaf (`leaf = null`); film and darkroom stop at kind with stock and model as named forms; light and support mostly stop at kind.

**Decided (D-19, D-20, D-21).** Sensor format, medium format included, is an attribute (D-19). Lenses are kinds by optical design with format coverage and focal class as attributes; film cameras are kinds by viewing system with film format in the compatibility ledger; instant cameras are kinds by film type; film is kinds by process with motion-picture gauges as format values (D-20, adopted as a ruling). Cine and video cameras stay kinds in D4.1 (D-21). Every choice above is therefore the decided structure, not a recommendation awaiting approval.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Tier type: spec-ranked by attributes, with maker-line ranks and sourced tier entries beside it.** Every scale is registered once in the C4(c) scheme registry and typed as one of the six scale classes before it enters the data (decisions CV-10, CV-12); no tier ever sits on a kind. A scheme enters on one fetched source; a second source or region adds weight (it feeds `disputed`), never entry (C4(e)).
- **Attributes measured on the object (C4(c) class (i)), not tiers.** Sensor format is a measured ladder (1/2.3 inch to medium format; S12, S29). Maximum aperture (lens speed) is a spec the retailers lead with (S04, S33). ISO speed is standardised (ISO 12232, S28) and film speed is a number on the box (S31). Each is stored with its standard named; generated views "by sensor format" and "by maximum aperture" give the owner the PC-component-style tier reading.
- **Maker ladders (C1 lines table, `line_rank`).** Sony's own series ladder (α1, α9, 7R, 7S, 7, 7C, ZV; S26) is a `line_rank` in the lines table (scale class (iv), maker's own ladder; optionally tier entries with `scheme = Sony`). The Sigma Art line named in the Digitec lens text (S33) is a line. Canon L and Sony G Master are lines once attested †. Kodak consumer and professional film lines (S31) are lines.
- **Tier entries (C4(a)) on makers, lines or named forms, never on kinds.** DXOMARK's body classes ("entry-level DSLR, semi-pro DSLR, professional"; S29) are tier entries of scale class (iii), editorial or community class; DXOMARK's colour-depth, dynamic-range and low-light scores are tier entries of scale class (ii), third-party measured score, with numeric value, `test_version` and `stale_after` (S29). Each entry carries `source_url` and `date`; entries from different sources on one subject are all kept, with `disputed` computed. Under C4(b) an external score may sit on a variant row when it rates that specific version.
- **Price band.** The domain-wide USD bands of C4(d) (scale class (v); never a tier), derived from a dated, sourced list price on the named form, variant or maker row (CV-13). A shop's own label (such as a resale platform's rank or badge) is scale class (vi), a dated `platform_tag`, never a tier.
- **Condition.** The KEH grading scale (S15) is a row of the C16 grading-scale registry (`scale_id`, `name`, `owner`, `scale_type` = condition grade, `steps`, `kinds_served`, `sources`; decisions CV-33, CV-34, which merged domain D's condition-scale table into the registry); MPB and Map Camera scales join it in stage 3 on one fetched page each. Kinds link to scales; no grade value of any item is ever stored.
- **Placement.** Nothing in this section is a level. The research draft's `tier=pro-line, sensor=full-frame` tags are replaced by the attribute `sensor_format`, the line row and its `line_rank`, and sourced tier entries.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Maker centrality: platform, with identity for a small set.**
- **Platform.** The maker sets compatibility for lenses (Canon RF, Nikon Z, Sony E; S11, S18), flashes (flash shoe protocol), lighting modifiers (Bowens, Profoto, Elinchrom mounts; S35), instant film (Instax, Polaroid; S34) and batteries. Retailers lead with mount (S04, S25, S33, S15). Mounts are shared across makers in places (Leica L alliance, Micro Four Thirds shared by Olympus and Panasonic; S25, S11) and one third-party design ships in several mounts (S18).
- **Identity.** A few makers are sought as such: Leica (digital rangefinder, Leica M glass adapted to other bodies; S12, S18), Hasselblad (V, H, X mounts; S25), Polaroid, Kodak and Ilford as film names (S31, S21), Lomography and Holga as film-camera makers with a scene (S13).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds: canonical form first, then the secondary key each sub-family states (C7). Named forms: non-maker named forms first, then maker-bound ones by maker and year (C7, CV-21). Spec orders and maker ladders are generated views.**
- **Kinds.** Digital cameras: mirrorless before DSLR before rangefinder before compact, then the video-first and special bodies. Film cameras: SLR, rangefinder, viewfinder, TLR, view, box or folding, single-use, pinhole. Lenses: prime, zoom, special designs, cine, attachments. Light: speedlight before monolight before pack and head (portability, S35); modifiers soft to hard (S35, S36). Film: by process as listed. Darkroom: by process stage.
- **Named forms (stored order, C7 as amended by CV-21).** Non-maker named forms first (C-stand, tripod-head and softbox shapes), by first-attested year, then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms, by maker in the maker ledger's `name` order, then first-introduction year ascending as attested, then alphabetically. Review X-6's "then line in `line_rank` order" is not a stored step: the maker ladder by `line_rank` is a generated ladder view (CV-21).
- **Generated views, not stored order.** Maker ladders by `line_rank` (Sony α ladder, S26); sensor format descending (medium format, full frame, APS-C, Micro Four Thirds, 1 inch; Map Camera and Geizhals order system cameras this way, S24, S32, S12); lenses by focal length wide to telephoto (S24); film stocks by ISO ascending (S31); film formats by size (110, 135, 120, 4x5, 8x10; S10).
- **Variants** (C3): by `variant_code` or year.
- Chronology is for the museum or collector view only (S37).

## 9. Boundaries

1. **D1 Computers & Components.** Webcams, scanners (including film scanners), printers (including photo printers), memory cards, card readers and standard USB and HDMI cables are D1. *Precedence sentence:* a webcam, film scanner, photo printer, memory card, card reader or standard-connector cable goes to D1, not D4, because the D1 branch of step 24 names them and they serve many devices; a camera strap, battery, battery charger, lens filter or camera-specific remote cable goes to D4 because S3 sends a part or accessory made for one device to that device; S3b states it for cables (K-2), chargers (K-3) and batteries (K-4): a generic USB, GaN or Qi charger is D1 and a charger for standard-size cells is C8.
2. **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices.** Camera phones, phone gimbals, phone tripod clamps, selfie sticks and clip-on phone lenses. *Precedence sentence:* a smartphone, even one sold for its camera, goes to D2 because step 24's second branch names phones; a gimbal, tripod clamp, selfie stick or clip-on lens made only for phones goes to D2 (its parts sub-family) under S3b (K-5): "Gimbals, selfie sticks, clip-on lenses, battery cases and clip-on game controllers made only for phones are D2 (S3); one made for cameras … that also takes a phone stays there"; so a tripod or gimbal made for cameras that also takes a phone stays in D4.4, and a universal tripod is D4 (K-15).
3. **B3 Lighting and B4 Decor.** Ring lights, LED panels, softboxes versus room lamps. *Precedence sentence:* a flash, studio light or ring light made for photography goes to D4, not B3, because step 24 is tried before step 30; a lamp made to light a room goes to B3, and a fairy-light or neon sign made as decoration is B4.
4. **B8 Home Safety & Security.** Security cameras, doorbell cameras, baby monitors. *Precedence sentence:* a camera made to watch a home or doorway goes to B8 under the step 24 note on connected household cameras, not D4; a camera made to take pictures or video for the owner (camcorder, action camera, 360 camera) goes to D4. A wildlife trail camera goes to D4, kind "trail camera" in D4.1, not B8 or E2, because the D4 branch names it (K-156: "a camera; setting and use are modifiers"); a recorder sold only to run security cameras follows them to B8 (K-14), and a camera sold only for watching pets is B10 (K-103).
5. **C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments.** Binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, spotting scopes, digiscoping gear. *Precedence sentence:* binoculars and telescopes go to C6, not D4, because step 23 names them and is tried before step 24; a camera adapter or T-ring made to fit a camera to a telescope goes to D4 (D4.7) as a part of the camera; a camera made only to fit a telescope is C6 under S3, and a camera made for general photography as well is D4 (K-15).
6. **F5 Documents & Personal Records, F2 Art, I2 Digital Media & Items.** Prints, negatives, slides and photo files. *Precedence sentence:* unexposed film and unused photographic paper go to D4, but developed negatives, slides and photographic prints go to F5 whoever is shown, a photographic print issued as an edition or signed or stamped by its maker as a work goes to F2, and the owner's photo files go to I2, because the carrier and its state decide the home, not the fact that a photograph is on it (step 19 and its note as amended by K-139 and K-141). A filled photo album is F5, an empty one B1 (K-9).
7. **E1 Toys & Games.** Toy cameras, children's play cameras, camera-less toy drones. *Precedence sentence:* a camera made for play that cannot take a picture (a toy with a fake shutter) goes to E1, not D4 (C15), while a plastic film camera that takes real pictures (Holga, Lomo) goes to D4, and a child-sized camera that takes real pictures stays in D4 (user is a tag, C6). A drone made for filming is D4 (kind "camera drone"), while an RC or FPV drone sold as a hobby model and a camera-less toy drone are E1 (K-97).
8. **D3 Audio & Video.** Slide and film projectors. *Precedence sentence:* a projector goes to D3 because step 24's third branch names projectors, while an enlarger, which projects a negative onto paper in a darkroom, goes to D4 as darkroom equipment. (Decision D-16 adds slide and cine projectors as D3 kinds, provisional until attested, so the projector has a home on the D3 side.)
9. **G1 Vehicles.** Dashcams and other cameras made to be fitted to one kind of vehicle. *Precedence sentence (K-6, S3b):* electronics made to be fitted to a vehicle, a dashcam included, go to G1 under S3, so D4 has no dashcam kind; an action camera made for general use and only mounted on a vehicle stays in D4, and its vehicle mount is a D4.4 camera mount.
10. **A4 Bags & Accessories.** Camera backpacks and general backpacks. *Precedence sentence (from _review_A):* a backpack made with fitted dividers to carry a camera and its lenses is a camera bag, D4 (D4.7) under S3, which names camera bags; a general backpack with a padded sleeve is A4.
11. **B1 Furniture & Storage, B2 Soft Furnishings.** Studio backdrops, backdrop stands, photo-studio furniture and photo-booth equipment. *Precedence sentence (K-17, S3b):* studio backdrops are B2 and studio furniture B1, because furniture and storage that hold several objects or stand in a room as furniture are never parts under S3; studio lights, stands and flashes are D4. A backdrop stand (the stand and crossbar that hold a studio backdrop) is a studio stand, so it is D4 (residual decision R-1): the D4.3 kind "backdrop stand †" in the Light stands and mounts leaf; the backdrop itself stays B2. A photo booth (camera, light and printer in one cabinet) is the D4.1 kind "photo booth" (decision D4-O1), not furniture.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure.** Candidate A, as decided (D-19, D-20 adopted as a ruling, D-21, CV-16, CV-17): D4 divides into seven sub-families by role in the picture-making chain (D4.1 Cameras, D4.2 Lenses & optical attachments, D4.3 Light, D4.4 Support & stabilisation, D4.5 Film & sensitised media, D4.6 Darkroom & processing, and the closing C5(d) sub-family D4.7 Parts and accessories made for one camera or lens); leaves follow the axis each sub-family states (D4.1 recording technology: Digital, Film and Instant cameras; D4.3 what the object does with light; D4.6 process stage), and D4.2, D4.4, D4.5 and D4.7 store `leaf = null`; kinds sit at the buyer's comparison level (body and viewing system for cameras, optical design for lenses, source type and shape for light, process for film), and named forms are models, series, stocks and iconic designs, with at most four levels below the category. The tree holds 99 kinds, cine, video, action, 360 and wildlife trail cameras, the camera drone and the photo booth among them, and photographic paper in D4.6; dashcams are G1 (K-6), phone-only accessories D2 (K-5), studio backdrops B2 and studio furniture B1 (K-17), telescope-only cameras C6 (K-15), and hobby and camera-less toy drones E1 (K-97). Lens mounts, flash shoes, light-modifier mounts, film formats, instant film formats and battery platforms are rows of the one domain-wide C13 compatibility ledger with its relations table (CV-29, CV-30); sensor format, format coverage, focal class, maximum aperture and ISO speed are class (i) attributes (C4(c)); maker ladders are `line_rank` in the lines table and generated views; third-party scores and editorial classes are per-source tier entries on makers, lines, named forms or variants, never on kinds (CV-10, CV-11, CV-12); price is the C4(d) band; condition scales are rows of the C16 grading-scale registry and no item's grade is stored (CV-33, CV-34). The maker ledger (C1 with the maker-relations and lines tables), named forms, variants and the scene vocabulary have no cap and grow from attested catalogues in stage 3 (CV-5, CV-7, CV-8, CV-22). Maker centrality: platform, with identity for a few makers. Scenes are C8 tags and generated views, each tag held back until it has a definition source. Stored named-form order is non-maker first, then maker, year, alphabet (CV-21).
