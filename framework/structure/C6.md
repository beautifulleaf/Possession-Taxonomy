## 1. How the domain already divides itself

**Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK, DE, JP, AU).**
- *US general photo/electronics retailer.* B&H "Optics" has one flat level of instrument types: Binoculars, Telescopes, Spotting Scopes, Riflescopes & Sights, Night & Thermal Vision, Field Accessories, Monoculars, Laser Rangefinders, Microscopes [S1]. Under Telescopes the children are Digital Telescopes, Refractors, Reflectors (Newtonian/Dobsonian), Catadioptrics (Cassegrains) and Solar Telescopes, with Eyepieces, Mounts & Tripods, Accessories and Astrophotography as separate branches [S2]. Its guide divides binoculars by spec class (full-size 8x42 or 10x50, midsize 7x35 or 10x32, compact 8x25 or 10x25) and names aperture "the most important specification" of a telescope [S1].
- *US education supplier.* Carolina "Microscopes & Optics" lists Binoculars & Telescopes, Compound Microscopes, Digital Cameras, Digital Microscopes, Field Microscopes, Magnifiers, Microscope Slides, Microscope Accessories, Stereomicroscopes [S6]. Its "Lab Supplies & Equipment" lists Glass & Plasticware, Lab Equipment, Measuring Equipment, Lab Safety, Dissection Supplies, Sensors, Biotech Equipment, Lab Furniture, Microscope Slide Preparation; science kits are a separate family by discipline (AP, chemistry, earth and environmental, life, physical) [S23]. Fisher Scientific shows lab devices as one flat list of device kinds (Lab Ventilation, Microscopes, Mixers, Ovens and Furnaces and so on) [S5].
- *Germany.* Teleskop-Express groups "Ferngläser, Spektive, Mikroskope, Entfernungsmesser" as one section and sells its own TS Optics line beside Swarovski, Zeiss, Leica and Optolyth [S3]. Optikshop24's top level is Teleskope, Montierungen, Sonnenbeobachtung, Ferngläser, Spektive, Mikroskope, Fotostative, Sternwarten, Gebrauchtmarkt, Zubehör; mounts are a sibling of telescopes, and solar items (filter foil, eclipse glasses, solar finders, solar telescopes) form their own branch [S26]. Astroshop splits microscopes by optical technique (upright: brightfield, darkfield, phase contrast, fluorescence; stereo, inverted and digital branches appear in the same sitemap) and has a separate "Planetarien und Sonnenuhren" branch with "Historische Instrumente" [S25]. A German binocular test sorts models into high-end class, premium top-of-range and premium mid-range (entry-level and pocket classes appear in the page's search excerpt), then names winners by use [S36].
- *Japan.* Vixen's online store is a maker-run optics tree: telescopes (alt-az mount, equatorial mount, tube, parts, smart telescope), portable equatorial mounts, binoculars and monoculars, field scopes, tripods, laser rangefinders, microscopes (biological, binocular stereo, digital, pocket), loupes (nine kinds: pocket, reading, lighted, stationery, neck/stand, spectacle/head, metal-holder, professional, observation), compasses and altimeters (oil, dry), viewing goods (star chart, eclipse glasses); a second navigation is by scene (see, shoot, learn, live) [S8].
- *Australia.* BinoCentral navigates telescopes by user purpose (For Kids, Beginners, Astrophotography, Deep Space, Serious Astronomy), binoculars by use (Astronomy/Night, Compact/Travel, Hunting, Birdwatching, Marine), microscopes by type (Biological, Stereo, Digital, Biosecurity), and has Spotting Scopes, Range Finders, Monocular, Night Vision/Thermal as siblings [S4].
- *UK.* First Light Optics navigates telescopes brand first (Celestron, Skywatcher, Takahashi expanded into named series), then solar (DayStar, Lunt H-alpha) and smart telescopes; optical tube assemblies are sold apart from mounted sets; the page shows no aperture or design facets [S37].
- *Weather.* AcuRite filters weather instruments by display type and names stations by sensor count (Iris 5-in-1); its FAQ separates indoor temperature/humidity sensors from outdoor stations that add pressure, rain and wind [S32]. Davis Instruments sells stations as sensor-suite packages (Vantage Vue, Pro2, Pro2 Plus, GroWeather), wireless or cabled, with or without a fan-aspirated shield, from $425 to $1,730 [S13].
- *Science kits.* Thames & Kosmos filters by age, price, product line and subject (Astronomy & Space, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Robotics, Electrical Science, Crystals & Materials, Engineering, Geology & Earth Science) [S21]; Kosmos in Germany frames experiment kits by Physik, Mechanik, Robotik, Chemie, Technik and an age band [S22].

**Enthusiast communities and guides.**
- Cloudy Nights (astronomy): buy the largest aperture, mounts are alt-az, equatorial or computerised, cheap binoculars beat cheap telescopes [S24]. Sky & Telescope: two binocular body styles (Porro and roof), spec printed as magnification x aperture, giant binoculars of 70 to 150 mm on a tripod, BAK-4 versus BK-7 prism glass as a quality marker [S7]. Vixen's guide classes binocular objectives (25 mm or less compact, 30–49 standard, 50+ astronomy), prism glass grades BK7 general, BaK4 high, SK15 top, and coating tiers [S9]. The Chinese Academy of Sciences observatory guide: aperture is the key spec; binoculars are marked magnification x aperture, single-tube scopes aperture/focal length; ED/APO marks low-dispersion glass; the Dobsonian is the usual step-up visual instrument [S19]. Microscope.com: first choice compound versus stereo, then monocular, binocular or trinocular, then objective quality and illumination [S20].
- BirdForum (binoculars): "alpha" means top-tier models of elite makers, usually Swarovski EL/NL, Leica Noctivid/Ultravid HD-Plus, Zeiss SF and Nikon EDG, at about $2,000 or more, though one member calls the term invented on the forum and Meopta and Kowa are disputed [S33].

**Collectors and the resale market.**
- Binoculars resell by brand and model: "alpha glass holds value", model version matters, 8x sells for less than 10x [S30]. Antique microscopes are collected by maker (Zeiss, Bausch & Lomb, Powell & Lealand, Leitz, Nachet, Watson), value turns on rarity and completeness (eyepieces, slides, box) [S27]; a private museum groups them by stand form (simple pocket, Society of Arts type, bar-limb, English-foot, continental, student/household/toy, replicas) [S28]. A UK auction house files "Cameras, Optical & Scientific Instruments" as one department and names Leica, Zeiss, Swarovski as sought brands; sample results are a brass travelling microscope at £3,000 and a decagonal refractor at £1,200 [S29].

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- UNSPSC class 41111700 "Viewing and observing instruments and accessories" lists microscopes by optical technique (stereo, binocular compound, trinocular, inverted, metallurgical, darkfield, polarizing, phase contrast, fluorescent, electron, scanning probe) together with magnifiers, loupes, telescopes and binoculars, and many microscope parts [S14]. US HTS heading 9005 is "Binoculars, monoculars, other optical telescopes ... other astronomical instruments"; compasses are 9014, surveying and meteorological instruments 9015, education demonstration models 9023, physical and chemical analysis 9027, telescopic sights for arms 9013 [S15]. WMO-No. 8 is the standard reference for meteorological instruments [S12].

**Museums and reference works.**
- Whipple Museum organises by theme (Astronomy and Empire, Acoustics, Calculating Devices, barometers under Meteorology) [S16] and types barometers by mechanism: cistern, angle or diagonal, aneroid [S17]. Wikipedia classes optical telescopes by three primary designs (refractor, reflector, catadioptric), sub-designs, construction or task [S10], and weather instruments by the quantity measured [S11]; Taiwan's weather agency teaches the same three telescope types with achromatic and apochromatic refractors, Newtonian/Cassegrain/Gregorian reflectors, and Schmidt- and Maksutov-Cassegrain catadioptrics [S18]. Binoculars are described by prism design, twilight factor and field-flattener lenses, with each maker's series listed [S35].

**Where sources agree and where they split.** All instrument sources agree that the first cut is the instrument type (binocular, telescope, microscope, spotting scope) and that optics sit together as one family [S1, S6, S8, S14, S25, S26]. They split on (a) what comes under telescopes: design (B&H [S2]), user purpose (BinoCentral [S4]), brand and series (First Light [S37]); (b) whether mounts are parts of telescopes (Optikshop24, Vixen treat them as separate products [S8, S26]); (c) where weather instruments go: trade law puts them with surveying (HTS 9015 [S15]), AcuRite sells them as a home product family [S32]; (d) science kits: divided by discipline and age, not by object form [S21, S22, S23].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Instrument type first, then performance spec, then maker.**
- *Navigation order.* Every optics store opens on the instrument type: binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, spotting scopes, monoculars [S1, S4, S6, S8, S25, S26]. The next level is design (telescopes: refractor, reflector, catadioptric [S2]; microscopes: compound, stereo, digital [S6, S20]) or use (BinoCentral [S4], Vixen "scene" [S8]). Maker comes last as a brand menu or index [S4, S25], except at First Light Optics, where the telescope tree is brand first with series beneath [S37].
- *Buyer questions in guides.* "Which design and how big an aperture": B&H calls aperture the most important telescope spec and sets a 70 mm floor [S1]; Cloudy Nights says buy the largest aperture and weighs alt-az against equatorial mounts [S24]; the Chinese observatory guide makes aperture the key index [S19]. For binoculars the first question is magnification x aperture (8x42, 10x50) and prism type [S1, S7, S9]; for microscopes it is compound versus stereo [S20]; for a home weather station it is how many sensors (5-in-1) [S32].
- *Use is the second filter, not the first.* BinoCentral lists astronomy, compact/travel, hunting, birdwatching and marine binoculars [S4]; the German test gives winners by use [S36]. These are scenes. They are not what the object is, and the framework treats them as modifiers (Appendix A).
- *Kits are the exception.* Science kits are chosen by subject and age, not by instrument type [S21, S22, S23].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework limit of four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; C5(a)). The recommended structure is Candidate 1.

### Candidate 1 (recommended): by kind of instrument, optics together
- **Levels and axes** (one axis per level within each sub-family, C5(c)).
  - *Sub-family* = what the instrument is made to do with its subject: look through it or model the sky (optical and astronomical instruments), read the air (weather instruments), hold and treat samples (laboratory apparatus), teach by experiment (science-education kits). A closing sub-family holds the S3 parts, with leaf axis = the instrument served; it is the only sub-family exempt from the level-1 axis (C5(d), CV-17).
  - *Leaf* axis, stated per sub-family: C6.1 instrument type; C6.2 quantity measured, with one leaf for stations that measure several (C14(c)); C6.3 what the item does to a sample (hold or measure it; weigh, separate, warm, cool, enclose or dissect it); C6.4 none (the sub-family holds one kind, so the data stores `leaf = null`, C5(b)); C6.5 instrument served.
  - *Kind* = optical or mechanical design principle for binoculars, telescopes and microscopes (Porro, roof, refractor, Newtonian, compound, stereo); the form in which the lens is held for magnifiers; the mechanism for barometers; the vessel or device for laboratory items; the part type for parts. Solar and smart telescopes, night-vision and thermal viewers and the digital microscope are kinds under rule T(b) (C6-O1, CV-24), provisional until attested. The Dobsonian is a non-maker named form under the Newtonian reflector, with `mount` an attribute (C-18).
  - *Named form* = a maker's series or model (`maker_bound: yes`) or a design many makers make (`maker_bound: no`, C2(a)), such as a stand form of antique microscope or the Dobsonian.
- **Why.** It matches how optics retailers [S1, S6, S8, S25, S26], UNSPSC class 41111700 [S14] and HTS heading 9005 [S15] group the same objects, and it matches the framework's own list in step 23.
- **Example sub-families and leaves (18 leaves, 63 kinds in the worked tree below).**

**Where it breaks (edge objects, decided).**
1. *Dobsonian telescope.* It is a Newtonian on a simple alt-az base. Decided (C-18): a non-maker named form under the Newtonian reflector kind, with `mount` an attribute; Orion XT8 and other maker models of the design are maker-bound named forms of the same kind. Retailers file it under Reflectors [S2]; the Chinese observatory guide treats it as its own recommended visual instrument [S19], which is why it stays a recorded named form and its makers stay in the ledger.
2. *Smart (imaging) telescope and the other technology kinds.* The smart telescope has a sensor and no eyepiece (B&H has "Digital Telescopes" as a separate child [S2]; First Light and Vixen list smart telescopes separately [S8, S37]). Decided (C6-O1, CV-24): smart telescopes, digital microscopes, solar telescopes, night-vision viewers and thermal viewers are kinds under rule T(b), provisional until attested: the decision stands once a fetched comparison source ranks each pair apart †. B&H, First Light and Vixen are catalogues, not comparison sources.
3. *Rangefinder binoculars and laser rangefinders.* B&H lists Laser Rangefinders beside Binoculars [S1]; BinoCentral has Range Finders with a "Range Finding Binoculars" child [S4]. Decided (K-148): golf and hunting laser rangefinders are E2, building laser measurers C1, and a rangefinding binocular is C6, filed by its prism design with a `rangefinder` attribute.
4. *Telescope mount versus telescope.* Optikshop24 and Vixen sell mounts as separate products [S8, S26]. Decided (K-15, S3): a mount made for a telescope is a C6 part (C6.5.1); a universal tripod is D4.

### Candidate 2: by what is observed
- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = what the instrument looks at (far things, small things, the atmosphere, laboratory samples, none/kits). Leaf = instrument type. Kind = design. Named form = series.
- **Example.** Far things: binoculars, monoculars, spotting scopes, telescopes. Small things: microscopes, magnifiers, loupes. Atmosphere: barometers, hygrometers, anemometers, rain gauges, stations. Laboratory samples: glassware, balances, centrifuges. Kits.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) It separates microscopes from telescopes although every optics source keeps them together [S1, S6, S14]. (2) A spotting scope can look at a bird 20 m away or a star; the target is a scene, not a property. (3) A magnifier used for stamps or for a watch dial sits between "small" and "far". (4) Kits have no target and need a fifth, unlike branch.

### Candidate 3: by science discipline
- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = discipline (astronomy, biology and microscopy, meteorology, chemistry and lab, physics and electricity). Leaf = instrument type. Kind = design.
- **Where it appears.** Museum themes [S16], Carolina's discipline families [S23], Thames & Kosmos subject facet [S21].
- **Where it breaks.** (1) A binocular is used for birds, stars, sport and theatre; a microscope in biology, geology and electronics. Discipline gives each instrument more than one home and fails the one-home rule (S1). (2) A barometer is meteorology to a museum [S17] but a décor item in a home. (3) Glassware belongs to chemistry and biology equally. (4) Discipline is closer to a genre of use than to the object.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (rough):** 180 to 240. Optical viewing: binoculars and monoculars 8 to 12; night and thermal 3 to 4; telescopes 12 to 18; spotting scopes 1 to 4 (an antique hand spyglass is a likely extra kind, but no fetched page named it †); microscopes 12 to 25 (UNSPSC lists about 25 microscope techniques; most private owners hold five or six [S14, S6]); magnifiers and loupes 4 to 7 once classed by form only, head-worn loupes included (Vixen sells nine loupe classes, several of them use classes [S8]); parts 30 to 45 (eyepieces, mounts, finders, filters, slides, objectives) in the closing sub-family. Weather: 20 to 30 (Wikipedia lists a dozen "typical" station instruments and many more specialist ones [S11]). Laboratory: 40 to 60 (vessels and volumetric ware 20 to 30; devices 20 to 30 [S5, S23, S31]). Kits: 1 kind (science experiment kit); the disciplines are attribute values.
- **Named forms (rough):** 400 to 800 if iconic series are the unit and 20,000 or more if individual models are listed. First Light's telescope page alone names about 40 series for three makers (Celestron, Skywatcher, Takahashi) plus some 25 other brands [S37]; a binocular maker has 4 to 8 series [S35]; the binocular alpha tier is 6 to 10 named models [S33, S34]. Named forms are harvested in stage 3, one attested row each, and grow without a cap (CV-7); large series sets may sit in registers (CV-45); no colour, size or SKU rows (C2(b)).
- **Where depth concentrates.** Telescopes (design x series x mount system), binoculars (maker series), microscopes (technique), and parts for telescopes and microscopes. Laboratory ware is wide but shallow: many kinds, few makers that matter to a private owner. Weather is moderate and flatter.
- **Broad leaves (more than 15 kinds, at full size):** telescope parts, vessels and volumetric ware, sample-handling equipment. **Medium (6 to 15):** telescopes, binoculars and monoculars, microscopes, weather instruments by quantity, kits. **Narrow (fewer than 6):** spotting scopes, night-vision and thermal, magnifiers and loupes, precipitation and radiation, multi-quantity stations.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommended type: spec-ranked (measured attributes), with a consensus-ranked overlay at the top of binoculars.** There is no single tier ladder for the whole category. Under C4 a tier never sits on a kind: it is a tier entry (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`) on a maker, a line or a named form, with a computed `disputed` flag when sources differ; a measured spec ladder is an attribute with its standard named (C4(b)); a retailer's own class labels are a dated `platform_tag` (C4(c)); a price band is a separate domain-wide USD band (C4(d)). A tier scheme enters the data on one fetched source; a second source or region only adds weight (C4(e) as amended, CV-10).

- *Binoculars, measured.* Magnification x aperture, field of view (apparent field computed under JIS B7157:2003), prism glass grade (BK7 general, BaK4 high, SK15 top [S9]; BAK-4 versus BK-7 [S7]), coating class (single, multi, phase-corrected [S9, S35]) and field-flattener lenses on high-end models [S35] are attributes with the standard or scale named.
- *Binoculars, consensus tier entries.* Scheme "alpha" (community, BirdForum [S33]): named models of elite makers, usually Swarovski EL/NL, Leica Noctivid/Ultravid HD-Plus, Zeiss SF and Nikon EDG, entered on named forms or on a maker's line, with `source_url` naming the forum thread. Scheme "high-end" (review site, Best Binocular Reviews [S34]): the named models it lists (Maven, Nikon Monarch HG, Meopta MeoStar, Minox, GPO, Vortex Razor). The price ranges these sources quote (about $2,000 and up for alpha, $700 to $1,500 for high-end) are price bands (C4(d)), not part of the tier. The edges of "alpha" are disputed in the community itself (Meopta, Kowa; "invented on the forum") [S33], and Best Binocular Reviews doubts the optical gap [S34], so the `disputed` flag is set. The German retailer's classes (premium upper class, premium top-of-range, premium mid-range, entry-level, pocket class [S36]) are that retailer's own labels, so they are a dated `platform_tag` (C4(c)), not a tier; the entry-level and pocket classes were seen in a search excerpt only †. Each scheme enters on its own one fetched source (CV-10, CV-12); no second-region source is sought. Resale follows the tier: alpha holds value, model version and 10x versus 8x matter [S30]. The review's `basis` field was not adopted (Q13a, CV-10): the scheme registry's scale class says whether a scheme is an editorial or community class (iii), a maker's own ladder (iv) or a shop label (vi) (C4(c)).
- *Telescopes.* Spec-ranked by aperture first [S1, S19, S24], then by glass class (achromat, ED, APO [S19]) and by design (Cloudy Nights weighs the simple alt-az mount against the heavier, slower-to-set-up equatorial and computerised mounts [S24]). Aperture, glass class and mount type are attributes; no tier entries.
- *Microscopes.* Spec-ranked by objective class (achromatic as the standard; plan objectives for flat fields), construction (metal, iris diaphragm, Abbe condenser), illumination (halogen, LED); microscope.com names construction (solid metal alloys, iris diaphragm, Abbe condenser) as the quality marker [S20]. These are attributes. Antique value is by maker, rarity and completeness, not by optics [S27], and is not stored (C16: no individual-item facts).
- *Weather instruments.* Spec-ranked by sensor suite and number of sensors (5-in-1) [S32] and by sensor package and shield (Vantage Vue, Pro2, Pro2 Plus; fan-aspirated shield) [S13], all attributes of the weather-station kind. The WMO Guide is the standards body for instrument performance [S12]; its tiers were not readable in the fetch †.
- *Laboratory ware.* No tier ordering was found in a fetched page. Volume and glass versus plastic are attributes [S31]; accuracy classes of volumetric glassware were not sourced in this batch †.
- *Kits.* Preference: age band and product line only [S21].

**How it appears.** Tier entries on named forms, lines and makers only (never on a kind); spec attributes on the kind (`prism_glass`, `aperture_mm`, `objective_class`, `sensor_suite`, `sensor_count`, `link`, `shield`) with the standard named where one exists. No per-leaf tier vocabulary. Price band is a separate field.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended type: identity.** The platform case for telescope systems is not attested and is deferred (below).
- *Identity.* Buyers and collectors seek the maker: BirdForum tiers are lists of named series from Swarovski, Leica, Zeiss, Nikon [S33]; resale prices follow brand and model [S30]; antique microscope collecting is by maker (Zeiss, Bausch & Lomb, Powell & Lealand, Leitz, Nachet, Watson) [S27]; the auction house lists Leica, Zeiss, Swarovski as sought names [S29]; the first level of First Light Optics' telescope tree is the maker, with series beneath [S37].

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states, then chronology only for antiques** (C7).
- *Telescopes:* refractor, reflector (Newtonian), catadioptric (Schmidt-Cassegrain, Maksutov-Cassegrain), then specialised (solar, smart). Reason: B&H, Wikipedia, the Taiwanese agency and the NAOC guide all use refractor-reflector-catadioptric [S2, S10, S18, S19].
- *Binoculars:* Porro, roof, giant, Galilean opera glass, monocular. Reason: historical order in Wikipedia and the S&T guide [S7, S35]. A generated view may sort by aperture class (Optikshop24 filters 25–31, 32–41, 42–50, over 50 mm, seen in a search excerpt †), but aperture is an attribute and does not order named forms.
- *Microscopes:* compound, stereo, digital, pocket, then specialist techniques (polarizing; later inverted, phase contrast, fluorescence). Reason: Carolina and microscope.com order [S6, S20]; Astroshop orders upright first [S25].
- *Magnifiers and loupes:* handheld, pocket (folding), stand, head-worn.
- *Weather:* by quantity measured (temperature, pressure, humidity, wind, precipitation, radiation), as the Wikipedia list and WMO chapters do [S11, S12], then the multi-quantity station; within barometers, by mechanism (cistern, angle, aneroid) [S17].
- *Vessels and volumetric ware:* by function (hold and mix, heat, culture, then measure volume) [S31]; then sample-handling equipment.
- *Parts:* by instrument served (telescope, then microscope); within telescope parts, eyepiece, mount, filter, camera; then microscope slide and microscope camera.
- *Astronomical models and sky instruments:* astrolabe, orrery, planetarium projector. *Weather, last leaf:* air-quality monitor after the multi-quantity station. *Sample handling:* balance, centrifuge, incubator, oven, freezer, fume hood, dissection set.
- *Named forms (C7 as amended by CV-21):* non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year, then alphabetically (antique stand forms by date [S28]); maker-bound named forms follow, by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then first-introduction year as attested, then alphabetically.

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure.** Candidate 1, by kind of instrument with optics together. Inside the category: 5 sub-families (optical and astronomical instruments; weather instruments; laboratory apparatus; science-education kits, one kind with `leaf = null`; and the closing "Parts and accessories made for one instrument"), 18 leaves, 63 kinds, then named forms (a maker's series or model, or a design many makers make, with `maker_bound`; uncapped, harvested in stage 3, large series sets in registers). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels.** The kind is the design principle for binoculars, telescopes and microscopes, the form in which the lens is held for magnifiers (head-worn included), the mechanism for barometers, the vessel or device for laboratory ware (laboratory freezers and fume hoods included) and the part type for parts. Solar and smart telescopes, night-vision and thermal viewers and the digital microscope are kinds under rule T(b), provisional until attested; the Dobsonian is a non-maker named form under the Newtonian reflector; binocular size class is an attribute; science kits are one kind with `discipline` an attribute; eclipse glasses, head-worn magnifiers, air-quality monitors, orreries, astrolabes and planetarium projectors are C6 kinds by K-135, R-13, K-147 and C-18. Modifiers (tier, price band, aperture class, maker, scene, era, user, market, material, connectivity, mount) are attributes, tags and views, never levels. One domain-wide maker ledger on the C1 core schema, with no cap. Tier: tier entries on named forms, lines and makers only, each scheme on one fetched source; spec attributes with standards named; no per-leaf tier vocabulary and no `basis` field. Parts follow the whole (S3, K-15) in the closing sub-family; a universal tripod is D4. Telescope and eyepiece standards go in the shared C13 ledger when a fetched page attests them.

**Remaining risks.**
- The five technology kinds rest on rule T(b) and a comparison source not yet fetched (C6-O1); the decision stands only if the harvest finds one for each pair.
