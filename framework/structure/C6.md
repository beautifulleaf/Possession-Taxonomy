## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments (domain C, Tools & Making).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 23): *made to **observe, magnify or measure for observation, study or science**: binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, magnifiers and loupes, weather instruments and windsocks, laboratory glassware and instruments, science-education kits.* Category line: "instruments to observe, magnify or measure for study or science, and science-education kits."

**Notes bound to the step:** a measuring tool made for a task the key files elsewhere goes with that task: kitchen scales and thermometers B5, rulers and postal scales C5, sewing tape measures C4, pool and soil test kits C2, building and trade measuring tools C1, bathroom and body scales H1, luggage scales A4. Rifle scopes are E4 under S3.

**Rules and precedence sentences elsewhere that touch C6:**
- Key order matters. Steps tried before step 23: step 2 (H2, spectacles and contact lenses that correct vision), step 10 (E4, rifle scopes and weapons), step 12 (C7, agricultural machines), step 13 (B10, animal equipment), step 16 (A5, timepieces), step 17 (A1/A4, worn items, "sunglasses or other non-corrective eyewear" A4, safety glasses A1), step 19 (F5), step 21 (F1, "maps, atlases and globes"), step 22 (E3). Steps tried after: step 24 (D4 "a camera or equipment for taking and making pictures ... lenses, tripods"), step 25 (E1 toys and "scale models"), step 26 (E2 "climbing, caving and navigation gear"), step 28 (C4), step 29 (C2), step 32 (C1 "tool, instrument or machine made to ... measure, test").
- Because step 23 comes before 24 and 25, a toy microscope, a children's telescope and a digital microscope with its own camera are C6, not E1 or D-category (S5: test the object).
- S3 (parts follow the whole): an eyepiece, finder, solar filter, telescope mount, microscope objective or slide made for one kind of C6 instrument goes to C6; "Never parts" includes anything worn on the body (step 17) and furniture or storage that holds several objects.
- Step 7 (F6): minerals, fossils and pinned insects are specimens, not instruments.
- Step 24 note: a connected version of a household object is filed by its task, so a connected weather station is still C6.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

**Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK, DE, JP, AU).**
- *US general photo/electronics retailer.* B&H "Optics" has one flat level of instrument types: Binoculars, Telescopes, Spotting Scopes, Riflescopes & Sights, Night & Thermal Vision, Field Accessories, Monoculars, Laser Rangefinders, Microscopes [S1]. Under Telescopes the children are Digital Telescopes, Refractors, Reflectors (Newtonian/Dobsonian), Catadioptrics (Cassegrains) and Solar Telescopes, with Eyepieces, Mounts & Tripods, Accessories and Astrophotography as separate branches [S2]. Its guide divides binoculars by spec class (full-size 8x42 or 10x50, midsize 7x35 or 10x32, compact 8x25 or 10x25) and names aperture "the most important specification" of a telescope [S1].
- *US education supplier.* Carolina "Microscopes & Optics" lists Binoculars & Telescopes, Compound Microscopes, Digital Cameras, Digital Microscopes, Field Microscopes, Magnifiers, Microscope Slides, Microscope Accessories, Stereomicroscopes [S6]. Its "Lab Supplies & Equipment" lists Glass & Plasticware, Lab Equipment, Measuring Equipment, Lab Safety, Dissection Supplies, Sensors, Biotech Equipment, Lab Furniture, Microscope Slide Preparation; science kits are a separate family by discipline (AP, chemistry, earth and environmental, life, physical) [S23]. Fisher Scientific shows lab devices as one flat list of device kinds (Lab Ventilation, Microscopes, Mixers, Ovens and Furnaces and so on) [S5].
- *Germany.* Teleskop-Express groups "Ferngläser, Spektive, Mikroskope, Entfernungsmesser" as one section and sells its own TS Optics line beside Swarovski, Zeiss, Leica and Optolyth [S3]. Optikshop24's top level is Teleskope, Montierungen, Sonnenbeobachtung, Ferngläser, Spektive, Mikroskope, Fotostative, Sternwarten, Gebrauchtmarkt, Zubehör; mounts are a sibling of telescopes, and solar items (filter foil, eclipse glasses, solar finders, solar telescopes) form their own branch [S26]. Astroshop splits microscopes by optical technique (upright: brightfield, darkfield, phase contrast, fluorescence; stereo, inverted and digital branches appear in the same sitemap) and has a separate "Planetarien und Sonnenuhren" branch with "Historische Instrumente" [S25]. A German binocular test sorts models into high-end class, premium top-of-range, premium mid-range, entry-level and pocket class, then names winners by use [S36].
- *Japan.* Vixen's online store is a maker-run optics tree: telescopes (alt-az mount, equatorial mount, tube, parts, smart telescope), portable equatorial mounts, binoculars and monoculars, field scopes, tripods, laser rangefinders, microscopes (biological, binocular stereo, digital, pocket), loupes (nine kinds: pocket, reading, lighted, stationery, neck/stand, spectacle/head, metal-holder, professional, observation), compasses and altimeters (oil, dry), viewing goods (star chart, eclipse glasses); a second navigation is by scene (see, shoot, learn, live) [S8].
- *Australia.* BinoCentral navigates telescopes by user purpose (For Kids, Beginners, Astrophotography, Deep Space, Serious Astronomy), binoculars by use (Astronomy/Night, Compact/Travel, Hunting, Birdwatching, Marine), microscopes by type (Biological, Stereo, Digital, Biosecurity), and has Spotting Scopes, Range Finders, Monocular, Night Vision/Thermal as siblings [S4].
- *UK.* First Light Optics navigates telescopes brand first (Celestron, Skywatcher, Takahashi expanded into named series), then solar (DayStar, Lunt H-alpha) and smart telescopes; optical tube assemblies are sold apart from mounted sets; the page shows no aperture or design facets [S37].
- *Weather.* AcuRite filters weather instruments by display type and names stations by sensor count (Iris 5-in-1); its FAQ separates indoor temperature/humidity sensors from outdoor stations that add pressure, rain and wind [S32]. Davis Instruments sells stations as sensor-suite packages (Vantage Vue, Pro2, Pro2 Plus, GroWeather), wireless or cabled, with or without a fan-aspirated shield, from $425 to $1,730 [S13].
- *Science kits.* Thames & Kosmos filters by age, price, product line and subject (Astronomy & Space, Biology, Chemistry, Physics, Robotics, Electrical Science, Crystals & Materials, Engineering, Geology & Earth Science) [S21]; Kosmos in Germany frames experiment kits by Physik, Mechanik, Robotik, Chemie, Technik and an age band [S22].

**Enthusiast communities and guides.**
- Cloudy Nights (astronomy): buy the largest aperture, mounts are alt-az, equatorial or computerised, cheap binoculars beat cheap telescopes [S24]. Sky & Telescope: two binocular body styles (Porro and roof), spec printed as magnification x aperture, giant binoculars of 70 to 150 mm on a tripod, BAK-4 versus BK-7 prism glass as a quality marker [S7]. Vixen's guide classes binocular objectives (25 mm or less compact, 30–49 standard, 50+ astronomy), prism glass grades BK7 general, BaK4 high, SK15 top, and coating tiers [S9]. The Chinese Academy of Sciences observatory guide: aperture is the key spec; binoculars are marked magnification x aperture, single-tube scopes aperture/focal length; ED/APO marks low-dispersion glass; the Dobsonian is the usual step-up visual instrument [S19]. Microscope.com: first choice compound versus stereo, then monocular, binocular or trinocular, then objective quality and illumination [S20].
- BirdForum (binoculars): "alpha" means top-tier models of elite makers, usually Swarovski EL/NL, Leica Noctivid/Ultravid HD-Plus, Zeiss SF and Nikon EDG, at about $2,000 or more, though one member calls the term invented on the forum and Meopta and Kowa are disputed [S33].

**Collectors and the resale market.**
- Binoculars resell by brand and model: "alpha glass holds value", model version matters, 8x sells for less than 10x [S30]. Antique microscopes are collected by maker (Zeiss, Bausch & Lomb, Powell & Lealand, Leitz, Nachet, Watson), value turns on rarity and completeness (eyepieces, slides, box) [S27]; a private museum groups them by stand form (simple pocket, Society of Arts type, bar-limb, English-foot, continental, student/household/toy, replicas) [S28]. A UK auction house files "Cameras, Optical & Scientific Instruments" as one department and names Leica, Zeiss, Swarovski as sought brands; sample results are a brass travelling microscope at £3,000 and a decagonal refractor at £1,200 [S29].

**Standards bodies and trade classifications.**
- UNSPSC class 41111700 "Viewing and observing instruments and accessories" lists microscopes by optical technique (stereo, binocular compound, trinocular, inverted, metallurgical, darkfield, polarizing, phase contrast, fluorescent, electron, scanning probe) together with magnifiers, loupes, telescopes and binoculars, and many microscope parts [S14]. US HTS heading 9005 is "Binoculars, monoculars, other optical telescopes ... other astronomical instruments"; compasses are 9014, surveying and meteorological instruments 9015, education demonstration models 9023, physical and chemical analysis 9027, telescopic sights for arms 9013 [S15]. WMO-No. 8 is the standard reference for meteorological instruments [S12].

**Museums and reference works.**
- Whipple Museum organises by theme (Astronomy and Empire, Acoustics, Calculating Devices, barometers under Meteorology) [S16] and types barometers by mechanism: cistern, angle or diagonal, aneroid [S17]. Wikipedia classes optical telescopes by three primary designs (refractor, reflector, catadioptric), sub-designs, construction or task [S10], and weather instruments by the quantity measured [S11]; Taiwan's weather agency teaches the same three telescope types with achromatic and apochromatic refractors, Newtonian/Cassegrain/Gregorian reflectors, and Schmidt- and Maksutov-Cassegrain catadioptrics [S18]. Binoculars are described by prism design, twilight factor and field-flattener lenses, with each maker's series listed [S35].

**Where sources agree and where they split.** All instrument sources agree that the first cut is the instrument type (binocular, telescope, microscope, spotting scope) and that optics sit together as one family [S1, S6, S8, S14, S25, S26]. They split on (a) what comes under telescopes: design (B&H [S2]), user purpose (BinoCentral [S4]), brand and series (First Light [S37]); (b) whether mounts are parts of telescopes (Optikshop24, Vixen treat them as separate products [S8, S26]); (c) where weather instruments go: trade law puts them with surveying (HTS 9015 [S15]), AcuRite sells them as a home product family [S32]; (d) science kits: divided by discipline and age, not by object form [S21, S22, S23].

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Instrument type first, then performance spec, then maker.**
- *Navigation order.* Every optics store opens on the instrument type: binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, spotting scopes, monoculars [S1, S4, S6, S8, S25, S26]. The next level is design (telescopes: refractor, reflector, catadioptric [S2]; microscopes: compound, stereo, digital [S6, S20]) or use (BinoCentral [S4], Vixen "scene" [S8]). Maker comes last as a brand menu or index [S4, S25], except at First Light Optics, where the telescope tree is brand first with series beneath [S37].
- *Buyer questions in guides.* "Which design and how big an aperture": B&H calls aperture the most important telescope spec and sets a 70 mm floor [S1]; Cloudy Nights says buy the largest aperture and weighs alt-az against equatorial mounts [S24]; the Chinese observatory guide makes aperture the key index [S19]. For binoculars the first question is magnification x aperture (8x42, 10x50) and prism type [S1, S7, S9]; for microscopes it is compound versus stereo [S20]; for a home weather station it is how many sensors (5-in-1) [S32].
- *Use is the second filter, not the first.* BinoCentral lists astronomy, compact/travel, hunting, birdwatching and marine binoculars [S4]; the German test gives winners by use [S36]. These are scenes. They are not what the object is, and the framework treats them as modifiers (Appendix A).
- *Kits are the exception.* Science kits are chosen by subject and age, not by instrument type [S21, S22, S23].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework limit of four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). The recommended structure is Candidate 1.

### Candidate 1 (recommended): by kind of instrument, optics together
- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = what kind of instrument it is (optical viewing instrument; weather instrument; laboratory apparatus; science-education kit). Leaf = instrument type (binoculars, telescopes, microscopes). Kind = optical or mechanical design principle (Porro, roof, refractor, Newtonian, compound, stereo, aneroid). Named form = maker series or iconic model.
- **Why.** It matches how optics retailers [S1, S6, S8, S25, S26], UNSPSC class 41111700 [S14] and HTS heading 9005 [S15] group the same objects, and it matches the framework's own list in step 23.
- **Example sub-families and leaves (15 leaves, 59 kinds in the worked tree below).**

Each named form is shown on the fetched page in brackets.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. *Dobsonian telescope.* It is a Newtonian on a simple alt-az base, so it is a design (kind) and a mount (part) at once. Retailers file it under Reflectors [S2]; the Chinese observatory guide treats it as its own recommended visual instrument [S19].
2. *Smart (imaging) telescope.* It has a sensor and no eyepiece (B&H has "Digital Telescopes" as a separate child [S2]; First Light and Vixen list smart telescopes separately [S8, S37]). Appendix A makes technology a modifier, so this needs an owner call: kind by principle, or attribute.
3. *Rangefinder binoculars and laser rangefinders.* B&H lists Laser Rangefinders beside Binoculars [S1]; BinoCentral has Range Finders with a "Range Finding Binoculars" child [S4]. The step 23 test (observe, study, science) does not decide a golf or hunting rangefinder.
4. *Telescope mount versus telescope.* Optikshop24 and Vixen sell mounts as separate products [S8, S26]; S3 sends a mount to C6 only if it is made for one kind of object, and photographic tripods are D4.

### Candidate 2: by what is observed
- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = what the instrument looks at (far things, small things, the atmosphere, laboratory samples, none/kits). Leaf = instrument type. Kind = design. Named form = series.
- **Example.** Far things: binoculars, monoculars, spotting scopes, telescopes. Small things: microscopes, magnifiers, loupes. Atmosphere: barometers, hygrometers, anemometers, rain gauges, stations. Laboratory samples: glassware, balances, centrifuges. Kits.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) It separates microscopes from telescopes although every optics source keeps them together [S1, S6, S14]. (2) A spotting scope can look at a bird 20 m away or a star; the target is a scene, not a property. (3) A magnifier used for stamps or for a watch dial sits between "small" and "far". (4) Kits have no target and need a fifth, unlike branch.

### Candidate 3: by science discipline
- **Levels and axes.** Sub-family = discipline (astronomy, biology and microscopy, meteorology, chemistry and lab, physics and electricity). Leaf = instrument type. Kind = design.
- **Where it appears.** Museum themes [S16], Carolina's discipline families [S23], Thames & Kosmos subject facet [S21].
- **Where it breaks.** (1) A binocular is used for birds, stars, sport and theatre; a microscope in biology, geology and electronics. Discipline gives each instrument more than one home and fails the one-home rule (S1). (2) A barometer is meteorology to a museum [S17] but a décor item in a home. (3) Glassware belongs to chemistry and biology equally. (4) Discipline is closer to a genre of use than to the object.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (rough):** 180 to 240. Optical viewing: binoculars and monoculars 8 to 12; night and thermal 3 to 4; telescopes 12 to 18; spotting scopes 1 to 4 (an antique hand spyglass is a likely extra kind, but no fetched page named it); microscopes 12 to 25 (UNSPSC lists about 25 microscope techniques; most private owners hold five or six [S14, S6]); magnifiers and loupes 6 to 9 (Vixen alone sells nine loupe forms [S8]); parts 30 to 45 (eyepieces, mounts, finders, filters, slides, objectives). Weather: 20 to 30 (Wikipedia lists a dozen "typical" station instruments and many more specialist ones [S11]). Laboratory: 40 to 60 (glassware 20 to 30; devices 20 to 30 [S5, S23, S31]). Kits: 8 to 12.
- **Named forms (rough):** 400 to 800 if iconic series are the unit and 20,000 or more if individual models are listed. First Light's telescope page alone names about 40 series for three makers (Celestron, Skywatcher, Takahashi) plus some 25 other brands [S37]; a binocular maker has 4 to 8 series [S35]; the binocular alpha tier is 6 to 10 named models [S33, S34].
- **Where depth concentrates.** Telescopes (design x series x mount system), binoculars (maker series), microscopes (technique), and parts for telescopes and microscopes. Laboratory glassware is wide but shallow: many kinds, few makers that matter to a private owner. Weather is moderate and flatter.
- **Broad leaves (more than 15 kinds):** parts for optical instruments, laboratory glassware, laboratory equipment. **Medium (6 to 15):** telescopes, binoculars and monoculars, microscopes, magnifiers and loupes, weather stations and pressure/wind groups, kits. **Narrow (fewer than 6):** spotting scopes, night-vision and thermal, precipitation and radiation.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule: a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers.** Finer distinctions are named forms or attributes.

| Leaf | Kind stops at | Attributes, not kinds | Named forms begin at | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Binoculars | prism design or form (Porro, roof, giant, Galilean opera glass, monocular) | magnification x aperture class, ED glass, phase coating, waterproofing, stabilisation | maker series (Swarovski NL Pure, Zeiss SF, Nikon Monarch HG) | S&T compares Porro and roof [S7]; B&H sorts by size class [S1]; Best Binocular Reviews compares alpha and high-end tiers by named brands and models [S34]; Vixen lists glass grade and coating as specs [S9] |
| Telescopes | optical design (refractor, Newtonian, Dobsonian, Schmidt-Cassegrain, Maksutov-Cassegrain, solar, smart) | aperture, achromat/ED/APO glass, GoTo or manual, mount type of a complete set | series (NexStar SE, Takahashi FC-76) | B&H, Taiwan CWA and the NAOC guide all list the three designs and then aperture [S2, S18, S19]; Cloudy Nights weighs aperture first and mount type second [S24] |
| Microscopes | principle (compound, stereo, digital, pocket, polarizing) | mono/bino/trinocular head, objective grade, illumination | model | microscope.com decides compound or stereo first, then head and optics [S20]; Carolina and Astroshop split by technique [S6, S25] |
| Magnifiers and loupes | form and use (handheld, pocket, lighted, stand) | power (2x to 10x), lens material | model | Vixen's nine loupe forms are form-and-use classes [S8]; Carolina has one Magnifiers family [S6] |
| Weather instruments | the instrument; barometer splits by mechanism (aneroid, cistern) | sensor count of a station, display type | Davis Vantage Pro2, AcuRite Iris | museum types barometers by mechanism [S17]; AcuRite and Davis sell stations by sensor suite [S13, S32] |
| Laboratory glassware | vessel type (beaker, Erlenmeyer flask, burette) | volume, glass versus plastic, tolerance class | rarely used | glassware lists are by function [S31]; Carolina has one family with sizes inside [S23] |
| Science kits | discipline kit | age band, product line | a named kit | T&K and Kosmos facets [S21, S22] |

The level differs between sub-families: binoculars and microscopes stop at principle; weather instruments go one step finer for barometers only.
