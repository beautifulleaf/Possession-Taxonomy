# A5 Clocks & Watches — structure memo (research draft)

Research for owner review. Nothing here is a decision. Every statement about how a source divides the category cites a page fetched in this batch; the full list with what each page shows is in `verify/out/S2_A5_sources.tsv` (37 fetches, 11 search calls). Prices and counts come from the page cited and are only as current as that page. Chinese retail sites (Tmall, JD) were not reached; see section 10.

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** A5 Clocks & Watches (domain A, Worn & Carried). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md`, category list and placement key step 16.

**Membership test (step 16).** "A **timepiece**: a watch or clock whose main function is to show or measure time, of any kind, including stopwatches, hourglasses and timers made as timepieces?" Notes bound to the step: smartwatches and hybrid smartwatches are D2; smart displays and clock radios are D3; sunrise lamps are B3; kitchen timers are B5; sundials made as garden ornaments are B4.

**Rules that touch A5.**
- Step 16 is tried before step 17 (worn or carried), step 23 (C6 instruments), step 22 (E3 metronomes) and step 26 (sport). A timepiece is therefore A5 even when it is worn, set with gems, made for diving or sold as a collectible.
- S3 (parts follow the whole) names "a watch strap" as a part that goes with its object. So straps, and by the same rule dials, cases, crystals and movements made for watches, belong to A5. S3 also says boxes, racks and cabinets that hold several objects are never parts (B1).
- Step 15 (F3) is tried before step 16: a clock made as an award or trophy is F3.
- S2: a model, cut or iconic design is a named form under a kind; an individual item is never a kind.
- Appendix A: region, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, market, power source and technology, connectivity, occasion, motive, origin, how acquired are modifiers and never levels.

### 1.2 Enthusiast guides and communities

- Vocabineer, https://www.vocabineer.com/types-of-watches/ : "types" grouped by display (analog, digital, hybrid), movement (quartz, mechanical, automatic), key features (smartwatch, chronograph, GMT, diving, solar, alarm, skeleton) and use or style (dress, casual, sports, field, pilot, military, luxury, fashion, pocket). Four axes in one list.
- WatchTime, https://www.watchtime.com/brands/manufacture-movement/watch-movements-explained-automatic-manual-wind-quartz-and-more : movements divide into mechanical and electronic first; then manual-wind versus automatic; quartz; manufacture (in-house) versus standard calibre from specialists (ETA, Sellita, Miyota, Time Module, Vaucher). It says "there is no universally accepted definition of a manufacture movement".
- Casio G-Shock guide, https://blog.creationwatches.com/watches/casio-g-shock-complete-guide.html : one maker's families are named series (DW-5600, GW-B5600, DW-6900, GA, GW-M5610, GBD, Master of G with Mudmaster, Rangeman, Gulfmaster, Frogman), chosen "by use case".

- Microbrand page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbrand_watches : a recognised maker class (small, outsourced production, direct to consumer, often under 2000 USD) that the enthusiast community treats as its own scene.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- COSC, https://www.cosc.swiss/cosc-certifications : two levels. "Chronometer Certified" (ISO 3159, −4/+6 s/day, 5 positions, 3 temperatures) and "Excellence Chronometer Certified" (finished watch, −2/+4 s/day, 200 Gauss). Quartz is tested on in-house specifications.
- Poinçon de Genève, https://www.poincondegeneve.ch/en : a certificate of provenance (Geneva), craftsmanship (finishing) and reliability, since 1886.
- ISO 6425:2018, https://www.iso.org/standard/66517.html : divers' watches: at least 100 m, with a secured diving-time measure visible in the dark. Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_watch) says conformant watches are marked DIVER'S, which separates them from dive-style watches.
- Swiss ordinance, https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1971/1908_1915_1915/en : defines "watch" as a device worn on the wrist, or a device whose main function is to measure time with a movement not over 60 mm in width, length or diameter or 14 mm in thickness; the part that lets it be worn is excluded; Swiss watch needs 60 percent of manufacturing costs in Switzerland.
- AHCI, https://www.ahci.ch/members/ : an association of independent watchmakers and clockmakers: current members, candidates and former members across 16 countries (for example Philippe Dufour, F.P. Journe, Kari Voutilainen, Konstantin Chaykin, Hajime Asaoka, Masahiro Kikuno, Xushu Ma; former member George Daniels).
- GPHG prizes (Wikipedia page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_d%27Horlogerie_de_Gen%C3%A8ve): the industry's own list of kinds: Chronograph, Iconic, Mechanical Exception, Ladies, Ladies Complication, Men's, Men's Complication, Calendar and Astronomy, Tourbillon, Sports, Time Only, Jewellery, Artistic Crafts, Petite Aiguille, Challenge, with special prizes (Chronometry, Audacity). Note that sex-based prizes sit next to function-based prizes. The prize list page on gphg.org returned only its header text.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- Wikipedia "Clock", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock : clocks "can be classified by the type of time display, as well as by the method of timekeeping", and the "Specific types" table is split into three columns, By mechanism, By function, By style. Watches "are usually not referred to as clocks".
- Wikipedia "Complication (horology)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complication_(horology) : complications grouped Timing, Mechanical, Astronomical, Striking, Non-horological; a grande complication has no official definition, but one criterion is one timing, one astronomical and one striking complication.
- Wikipedia "Category:Watches", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Watches : subcategories are brands, models, individual watches, smartwatches, watchmakers; pages include diving watch, pocket watch, military watches, microbrand watches, skeleton watch, solar-powered watch, Spring Drive, watch strap, watchcase.
- Musée international d'horlogerie, https://www.mih.ch/en/watch-collections/ : about 10,000 items: watches and clocks from the 16th century to now plus tools, machines, instruments and automata; labels are by form (pocket watch, astrarium, bracket clock, marine clock, jumping hours watch, astronomical regulator, automaton watch).
- Pocket watch, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_watch : hunter-case, open-face and demi-hunter styles; key-wind versus stem-wind; "adjusted" grades.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

Agree:
- Everyone separates a worn watch from a placed clock, and the pocket watch is a kept-apart form (Chrono24, Wikipedia, MIH, NAWCC).
- Nearly every watch retailer puts a movement facet (mechanical manual, automatic, quartz, solar) and a type or style facet side by side (Jomashop, Chrono24, Kakaku, bestwatch.ru).
- For clocks, collectors and dealers divide by case or placement form: longcase, bracket, mantel, carriage, wall (Collectors Weekly, Sellingantiques, FindArtInfo, Babla's, NAWCC).
- Brand and model are the identity layer: Chrono24 "most popular models", Kakaku series, Tempus iconic models, Seiko Prospex lines.

Split:
- Chronograph and GMT are a complication in Wikipedia's list, a watch type in Tempus, Jomashop and Chrono24, and a prize class in GPHG.
- Movement is the first facet at Kakaku, a mid-list facet at Jomashop, and a category link among types at Chrono24. Tempus puts function before movement.
- The Swiss ordinance excludes the strap from "watch"; Chrono24 sells straps, winders, boxes, dials and movements as categories next to watches; the framework's S3 files a strap with the watch but a box that holds several watches elsewhere (B1), which differs from Chrono24 for boxes.
- Dive watch: a standard (ISO 6425, pass or fail) versus a style that retailers apply loosely.
- Sex of the user is a top facet at Jomashop and Chrono24 and a prize split at GPHG; in the framework it is a modifier.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence for what buyers decide first.
- **Watches: function or style family, then movement, then maker and model.** Tempus states it ("une montre se choisit d'abord par sa fonction et son style", https://tempus-watches.fr/types-de-montres/). Jomashop's tiles (Automatic, Chronograph, Dive, Pilot, Luxury) mix movement, complication and use. Kakaku leads with movement (https://kakaku.com/watch_accessory/watch/), so Japanese buying starts at "how does it run". Bestwatch.ru starts with sex and brand, then mechanism.
- **Clocks: where it stands or hangs, and the case form.** Babla's (wall, bedside alarm, mantel, LCD, travel, cuckoo), Sellingantiques and Collectors Weekly all begin with placement form.
- **Maker is a first axis for collectors.** Chrono24 lists brands before categories on its home page; Bob's tier list and Soner's pyramid sort by brand (section 5).
- **No page uses one axis.** Wikipedia's own clock table has three (mechanism, function, style); Vocabineer has four. This is the main structural finding: any single-axis tree has to say clearly which axis it keeps and send the rest to tags.

## 3. Candidate structures

Depth counted from the category: sub-family (level 1), leaf (level 2), kind (level 3), named form (level 4). The category itself is A5.

### Candidate A (recommended): carry or placement, then designed function, then kind, then named form

- Level 1 sub-family axis: **how the timepiece is carried or placed in use** (on the wrist; elsewhere on the person; in a room; hand-held for timing), plus one sub-family for parts that S3 sends here.
- Level 2 leaf axis for watches: **primary designed function or design family**, which is the framework's own axis. For clocks: **placement or mounting** (floor, table or shelf, wall, portable).
- Level 3 kinds are generic types. Level 4 named forms are model families (Submariner, Speedmaster) and iconic designs.
- Kept off the tree as tags or views: movement and power (manual, automatic, quartz, solar, Spring Drive, kinetic), complications that are not the defining function, case material and metal, size, shape, strap, dial colour, price band, maker, tier, era, region, user (sex, age), condition, certification (COSC, Geneva Seal, Swiss Made), edition, collaboration.
- Precedence inside the watch leaves, so that each named form has one home (S1): a watch built for an environment (dive, pilot, field) goes there; else by the complication that defines it; else by style. Examples below.

Precedence rules that make homes unique (candidate A):
- Environment beats complication beats style. Navitimer is a pilot's watch with a chronograph tag; Royal Oak Perpetual Calendar is a perpetual-calendar watch with a sports-style tag; a Frogman with ISO 6425 is a diver's watch even though the G-Shock family sits in Sports & tough.
- A tough-built digital watch is A5.1.5, not A5.1.11. A5.1.11 is for display-led watches of ordinary build.

Where candidate A breaks (edge objects):
1. Level 2 for watches mixes use environments (dive, pilot, field) and complications (chronograph, GMT, calendar). Both are "designed function", but it is two sub-axes in one level. Section 10 asks whether to accept the precedence rule or to demote complications to tags.
2. G-Shock Rangeman and similar watches with altimeter, barometer and compass: the main function may be sensing (D2 by the step 17 note) or time (A5 by step 16). The G-Shock guide calls the GBD line "basic connected functionality" that "prioritises toughness"; those stay A5, but sensor-led models need a ruling.
3. Pocket watch versus wristwatch: a "wristlet" or trench watch is a pocket-watch case adapted to the wrist (Wikipedia Pocket watch), so the carry axis is clean for modern watches and fuzzy for 1900-1920 transition pieces.
4. Parts (A5.5) do not fit the carry axis; they are there because S3 says so.
5. Game clocks, attendance (time) clocks and public tower clocks fit no sub-family well (section 10).

### Candidate B: carry, then movement or power, then design family

- Levels: carry; movement (mechanical manual, mechanical automatic, quartz, solar, Spring Drive, auto-quartz, digital); design family; named form.
- Evidence for it: Kakaku opens with movement counts (https://kakaku.com/watch_accessory/watch/), WatchTime's article is organised by movement, Wikipedia's clock table has a "By mechanism" column.
- Examples: automatic > diver's watch > Submariner; quartz > diver's watch > a Seiko quartz diver; solar > field watch.
- Breaks: (1) Appendix A makes power source and technology a modifier, not a level; (2) the same design appears in several movements (Seiko Prospex listings carry Mechanical and Solar tags on one line), which would split a named form across leaves, against S1; (3) tiny leaves (Spring Drive 48, auto-quartz 4 at Kakaku); (4) clocks split the same way (Babla's cuckoo: 8 mechanical, 77 quartz) so the axis does not separate watches from clocks. It is better as a facet.

### Candidate C: feature or complication first, as in Chrono24 and GPHG

- Levels: carry; feature family (chronograph, GMT, calendar, tourbillon, repeater, dive, pilot...); kind; named form.
- Evidence for it: Chrono24 category list; GPHG prizes; Wikipedia Complication groups.
- Breaks: features overlap, so a dive chronograph GMT has no single home; Chrono24 mixes sex, metal, colour and tie-ins (James Bond, Ferrari, Cristiano Ronaldo, Navy Seals) into the same list; no exclusive rule exists. It works as a set of generated views.

## 4. Depth and size

These are estimates for planning, not counts from a source.

- **Generic kinds:** about 60 to 90. Wristwatches 30 to 40 (the tree above has 29 and a few more design-family kinds will appear when pilot, field and sailing watches are sourced further); pocket and carried watches 4 to 8; clocks 20 to 30 (Sellingantiques shows about 15 type categories among its antique clock list); timing instruments 5 to 8; parts 8 to 12.
- **Named forms (model families and iconic designs):** several hundred worth recording. Evidence: Chrono24's "most popular models" strip, Kakaku's series pickups per maker, Seiko's Prospex Sea, Land, Speedtimer and LX lines, the G-Shock series list, Tempus' iconic models per type. Reference numbers and colourways of a model (SPB143, SRPE05) are variants of the form, not further forms; the framework's wording ("models ... named forms") leaves this open (section 10).
- **Where depth concentrates:** wristwatches. Dive, dress and chronograph have the most model families; pilot, field and GMT are medium; striking, grande complication, moon phase and world time are narrow but valuable; clocks concentrate in antique and mid-century forms (longcase, bracket, mantel, carriage, wall).
- **Broad leaves:** dress, dive, chronograph, sports and tough, mantel and table clocks, wall clocks. **Medium:** pilot, field and military, GMT, display-led, pocket watches, parts. **Narrow:** calendar and astronomical, striking, showpiece mechanics, longcase (one kind), portable and marine, timing instruments.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. Evidence by sub-family:

- **Wristwatches stop at the design family** (diver's watch, pilot's watch, chronograph, GMT watch, dress watch). Chrono24 gives each of these its own category page (Taucheruhren, Fliegeruhren, Chronographen, Dress Watches, GMT-Uhren), Jomashop gives quick tiles for Dive, Chronograph, Pilot, Tempus gives five type pages with price guides per model, and Seiko's Prospex lineup is split Sea, Land, Speedtimer. Comparison between makers happens inside these pages ("which dive watch"). Models (Submariner, Fifty Fathoms, Aquis) are named forms below. Movement, size, metal and strap are attributes, because the facets sit beside the type, not under it.
- **High complications go one step finer** (annual calendar versus perpetual calendar, tourbillon, minute repeater, grande complication). Chrono24 has separate pages for Tourbillon, Ewiger Kalender, Minutenrepetition, Mondphase; GPHG awards Tourbillon, Calendar and Astronomy and Mechanical Exception prizes; Wikipedia defines grande complication by three complication groups. Collectors compare makers inside each of these.
- **Tough digital watches stop at the series** only as named forms (DW-5600, GA, Mudmaster). The kind is "tough digital watch"; buyers compare series (G-Shock guide: "start with use case").
- **Clocks stop at the case or placement form** (longcase, bracket, mantel, carriage, wall dial, cuckoo). Dealers price and count at that level (Sellingantiques counts per type; Collectors Weekly "Types"). Finer features (fusee, strike, regulator movement) are value attributes (FindArtInfo), and regional styles (Vienna, Black Forest, ogee, banjo) are named forms.
- **Pocket watches stop at case style** (open-face, hunter, demi-hunter), with railroad grade as a named form (Wikipedia Pocket watch).
- **Parts stop at the part type** (strap, bracelet, winder, dial, movement, crystal, case); strap material and attachment size are attributes.
- **Hourglass, stopwatch, game clock** stop at the generic instrument: no source compares makers within them.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Finding: two layers, one measurable and one consensus.**

*Layer 1: measurable and certified (spec-ranked, attached to a watch or movement, not to a maker).*
- COSC: two named levels with numeric limits: Chronometer Certified (−4 to +6 s/day) and Excellence Chronometer Certified (−2 to +4 s/day, 200 Gauss, tested on the finished watch) (https://www.cosc.swiss/cosc-certifications). The certificate is optional and belongs to the brand, not the buyer (source for the levels: the certifications page above; the optional-certificate point comes from a search excerpt of the COSC FAQ and is not a fetched claim).
- ISO 6425 diver's watch: pass or fail, minimum 100 m with a secured dive-time device (https://www.iso.org/standard/66517.html).
- Swiss Made / Swiss watch: pass or fail, 60 percent of manufacturing costs (https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1971/1908_1915_1915/en).
- Poinçon de Genève: pass or fail (provenance, finishing, reliability) (https://www.poincondegeneve.ch/en).
- Movement provenance: in-house "manufacture" versus standard calibre (ETA, Sellita, Miyota). WatchTime says there is no universal definition of "manufacture movement", so this is a labelled attribute with the source's own wording, not a rank.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Classification: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought, and often is the first thing a buyer names.
- Chrono24's home navigation carries a brand menu next to the categories (seen in a search excerpt of the home page), and its category list contains brand-defined groups (Breitling Chronograph, Cheap Rolex, Rolex tie-ins). Kakaku and Tempus put the maker's series (Portugieser, Overseas, Big Bang) at the front of the catalogue.
- Tier lists are written per brand, not per kind (section 5).
- Standards and awards identify makers: the Geneva Seal is geographically bound, GPHG prizes list maker plus model, AHCI is a maker association.
- Collectors of clocks name makers too (Tompion, Seth Thomas, Ansonia, Howard Miller, Telechron: FindArtInfo, Collectors Weekly).
- Platform effects are weak: no maker sets compatibility for whole watches, though movement supply (ETA, Sellita, Miyota, Time Module, Vaucher: WatchTime) and strap fit are cross-maker technical links to track as attributes.

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger.** One row per maker, each row attested:
- maker name and any group; country and place; founded (the Wikipedia list gives year, place and founder: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swiss_watch_manufacturers);
- house type: manufacture or large brand; independent (AHCI member, current or former); microbrand (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbrand_watches); fashion or licensed brand; clock-maker; movement supplier;
- known-for kinds and named forms (Rolex: Submariner, Datejust, Daytona, GMT-Master II; Omega: Speedmaster, Seamaster; Seiko: Prospex Sea, Land, Speedtimer; Casio: G-Shock series; Breguet: carriage clock form, Classique Souscription);
- movement policy (in-house, standard calibre, mixed) with the source's wording;
- certifications held (COSC, Geneva Seal, Swiss Made); scene tags; price band; era; tiers per source with a disputed flag.

**Size of the maker space (from fetched pages):** AHCI alone lists 36 members, 6 candidates and 18 former members from 16 countries; Bob's covers 20 brands; the Wikipedia list of Swiss manufacturers is long; Chrono24 and bestwatch.ru show Japanese, German and Russian makers (Slava, Molniya, Ouglich); Collectors Weekly lists nine American clock makers. Clock makers need their own rows, with a separate house type.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

- **Wristwatch leaves:** canonical form first (the archetype for the family: for dive, the ISO 6425 diver's watch; for chronograph, the plain chronograph), then increasing mechanical complexity (flyback, split-seconds). Within a kind, named forms in alphabetical order by maker then model, not by chronology or price. Reason: model families have no clean date (many are in production for decades), while complexity is a stable rank that Wikipedia's complication groups and GPHG's "Complication" prize pairs already use.
- **Clock leaves:** by placement size, floor to table to wall to portable, as dealers lay them out; inside a kind, canonical form first (English bracket clock before regional variants), then by regional style.
- **Pocket watches:** open-face (the plain form), hunter, demi-hunter, then railroad grade as a named form.
- **Parts:** by the part's position from the outside in (strap or bracelet, case, crystal, dial, movement), which matches Chrono24's repair-oriented categories.
- Chronology is useful only as an attribute (introduction year of a model) and for the antique clock leaves, where dealers use date ranges (FindArtInfo: longcase c. 1660, bracket 1660-1850, carriage 1820-1920).

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (each can be copied into the placement key after approval):

1. **A3 Jewellery.** A watch whose main function is to tell time is A5 whatever its case metal, gems or price (a diamond-set or "jewellery watch" is A5); an ornament with a decorative dial that cannot be read as a timepiece is A3. (Step 16 is tried before step 17.)
2. **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices.** A smartwatch, hybrid smartwatch or fitness tracker is D2; a watch that only syncs time by Bluetooth or radio (G-Shock GBD, GW-B5600) stays A5, because connectivity is a modifier.
3. **D3 Audio & Video.** A clock radio, smart display or voice-assistant clock is D3; a clock with radio-controlled time (a "radio clock" in the sense of time signal) is A5.
4. **B4 Decor and B3 Lighting.** A sundial sold as a garden ornament is B4; a sundial made to read the time as an instrument is A5; a sunrise lamp is B3.
5. **B5 Kitchen & Dining.** A kitchen timer is B5; an hourglass or sand timer made as a timepiece is A5.
6. **B1 Furniture & Storage.** A box, roll or cabinet that holds several watches is B1 (S3: never parts), although Chrono24 files "Uhrenboxen" with watches; a winder or stand that holds one watch is a part and goes to A5. A grandfather clock is a clock (A5), not furniture, even though its case is cabinetry.
7. **C1 Tools & C6 Instruments.** A watchmaker's tool is C1; a barometer or weather station is C6; a marine chronometer is a timepiece (A5, step 16 before step 23).
8. **E3 Musical Instruments.** A metronome is E3 (named in step 22), even though it counts time; a game or chess clock is a timer made for a game and is the first open question below.
9. **F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards.** A clock made as an award or trophy is F3 (step 15 precedes step 16); a commemorative clock is a clock (S5).
10. **Sport (step 26).** A diver's watch with ISO 6425 is A5, not E2, because step 16 precedes step 26; the dive computer that is sensing-led is outside A5.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (candidate A).** Category A5 > sub-family by carry or placement (wristwatches; carried watches; clocks; timing instruments; parts) > leaf by designed function for watches and by placement for clocks > kind (generic type) > named form (model family or iconic design). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category.** Movement, complications that do not define the function, material, size, brand, tier, era, region, user, condition and certification are tags or views. A maker ledger carries brand identity and per-source tier. Kinds stop at the design family for wristwatches, one step finer for high complications, and at case or placement form for clocks.

**Owner decisions needed.**
1. **Movement as a facet, not a level.** Appendix A treats power source and technology as modifiers; Kakaku and WatchTime put movement first. Confirm tag-only treatment, or approve an exception for horology.
2. **Level 2 axis for watches.** Accept "designed function" with the precedence rule (environment, then complication, then style), or demote complications to tags and keep leaves to environments only.
3. **Parts in A5.** S3 puts straps, dials, movements, cases and one-watch winders in A5. Confirm a parts sub-family, and that boxes holding several watches go to B1 although Chrono24 files them with watches.
4. **Border cases that need a home.** Game and chess clocks (A5 or E1), attendance time clocks (A5 or C5), sensor-led outdoor watches such as Rangeman (A5 or D2), turret and tower clocks fixed to a building (A5 or part of G2 as a building element), laboratory atomic clocks (A5 or C6), pulse watches and medical timers (A5 or H2).
5. **Tier handling.** Adopt "store each source's tier, flag disagreement" for brand tiers, and attach certifications as attributes. Choose which tier lists count as sources.
6. **Named form definition.** A model family (Submariner) is a named form; is a reference number (a particular Submariner Date reference) a variant attribute, or a further named form?
7. **Fashion watches and tie-ins.** Treat "fashion watch" and collaboration editions as maker-type and motive tags, not kinds.
8. **User-based categories.** Sex of the user is a facet or prize split at Jomashop, Chrono24 and GPHG; keep it as a user modifier and view.
9. **Naming synonyms.** Longcase and grandfather clock are split in one dealer's list (Sellingantiques) but are the same type; confirm a synonym rule.
