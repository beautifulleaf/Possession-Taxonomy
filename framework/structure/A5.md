## 0. Category

**Code and name.** A5 Clocks & Watches (domain A, Worn & Carried). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md`, category list and placement key step 16.

**Membership test (step 16).** "A **timepiece**: a watch or clock whose main function is to show or measure time, of any kind, including stopwatches, hourglasses and timers made as timepieces?" Notes bound to the step: smartwatches and hybrid smartwatches are D2; smart displays and clock radios are D3; sunrise lamps are B3; kitchen timers are B5; sundials made as garden ornaments are B4.

**Rules that touch A5.**
- Step 16 is tried before step 17 (worn or carried), step 23 (C6 instruments), step 22 (E3 metronomes), step 25 (E1 games), step 26 (sport) and step 31 (C5 office machines). A timepiece is therefore A5 even when it is worn, set with gems, made for diving or sold as a collectible. Step 2 (H2) is tried before step 16.
- S3 (parts follow the whole) names "a watch strap" as a part that goes with its object (step 8, tried before step 16). So straps, and by the same rule dials, cases, crystals, movements and chains made for watches, belong to A5. S3 also says boxes, racks and cabinets that hold several objects are never parts (B1).
- Step 4's note says installed machines keep their own category, so a clock fixed to a building is A5, not G2.
- Step 15 (F3) is tried before step 16: a clock made as an award or trophy is F3.
- S2 and BRIEF: a model, cut or iconic design is a named form under a kind; an individual item (a unique piece) is never a kind or a named form.
- Appendix A: region, era, price band and quality tier, brand and maker, scene, user, setting, market, power source and technology, connectivity, occasion, motive, origin, how acquired are modifiers and never levels.
- Conventions applied: C1 (maker ledger), C2 (kind against named form), C3 (variants), C4 (tiers and price bands), C5 (levels and axes), C6 (size and user), C7 (order), C8 (one tag vocabulary), C9 (alias table), C10 (technology is an attribute), C11 (regional names), C13 (compatibility ledger), C16 (no individual-item facts).

### 1.2 Enthusiast guides and communities

- Vocabineer, https://www.vocabineer.com/types-of-watches/ : "types" grouped by display (analog, digital, hybrid), movement (quartz, mechanical, automatic), key features (smartwatch, chronograph, GMT, diving, solar, alarm, skeleton) and use or style (dress, casual, sports, field, pilot, military, luxury, fashion, pocket). Four axes in one list.
- WatchTime, https://www.watchtime.com/brands/manufacture-movement/watch-movements-explained-automatic-manual-wind-quartz-and-more : movements divide into mechanical and electronic first; then manual-wind versus automatic; quartz; manufacture (in-house) versus standard calibre from specialists (ETA, Sellita, Miyota, Time Module, Vaucher). It says "there is no universally accepted definition of a manufacture movement".
- Casio G-Shock guide, https://blog.creationwatches.com/watches/casio-g-shock-complete-guide.html : one maker's families are named series (DW-5600, GW-B5600, DW-6900, GA, GW-M5610, GBD, Master of G with Mudmaster, Rangeman, Gulfmaster, Frogman), chosen "by use case".
- NAWCC Horology Index, https://theindex.nawcc.org/Types.php : Types of Timepieces lists aircraft clock, bracket, carriage, dial (schoolhouse or railway), alarm, American pocket watch, lantern, longcase; notable and public or astronomical clocks are separate sections.
- Microbrand page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbrand_watches : a recognised maker class (small, outsourced production, direct to consumer, often under 2000 USD) that the enthusiast community treats as its own scene.

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- COSC, https://www.cosc.swiss/cosc-certifications : two levels. "Chronometer Certified" (ISO 3159, −4/+6 s/day, 5 positions, 3 temperatures) and "Excellence Chronometer Certified" (finished watch, −2/+4 s/day, 200 Gauss). Quartz is tested on in-house specifications.
- Poinçon de Genève, https://www.poincondegeneve.ch/en : a certificate of provenance (Geneva), craftsmanship (finishing) and reliability, since 1886.
- ISO 6425:2018, https://www.iso.org/standard/66517.html : divers' watches: at least 100 m, with a secured diving-time measure visible in the dark. Wikipedia (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_watch) says conformant watches are marked DIVER'S, which separates them from dive-style watches.
- Swiss ordinance, https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1971/1908_1915_1915/en : defines "watch" as a device worn on the wrist, or a device whose main function is to measure time with a movement not over 60 mm in width, length or diameter or 14 mm in thickness; the part that lets it be worn is excluded; Swiss watch needs 60 percent of manufacturing costs in Switzerland.
- AHCI, https://www.ahci.ch/members/ : an association of independent watchmakers and clockmakers: current members, candidates and former members across 16 countries (for example Philippe Dufour, F.P. Journe, Kari Voutilainen, Konstantin Chaykin, Hajime Asaoka, Masahiro Kikuno, Xushu Ma; former member George Daniels).
- GPHG prizes (Wikipedia page, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_d%27Horlogerie_de_Gen%C3%A8ve): the industry's own list of kinds: Chronograph, Iconic, Mechanical Exception, Ladies, Ladies Complication, Men's, Men's Complication, Calendar and Astronomy, Tourbillon, Sports, Time Only, Jewellery, Artistic Crafts, Petite Aiguille, Challenge, with special prizes (Chronometry, Audacity). Note that sex-based prizes sit next to function-based prizes. The prize list page on gphg.org returned only its header text.

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- Wikipedia "Clock", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock : clocks "can be classified by the type of time display, as well as by the method of timekeeping", and the "Specific types" table is split into three columns, By mechanism, By function, By style. Watches "are usually not referred to as clocks".
- Wikipedia "Complication (horology)", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complication_(horology) : complications grouped Timing, Mechanical, Astronomical, Striking, Non-horological; a grande complication has no official definition, but one criterion is one timing, one astronomical and one striking complication.
- Wikipedia "Category:Watches", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category:Watches : subcategories are brands, models, individual watches, smartwatches, watchmakers; pages include diving watch, pocket watch, military watches, microbrand watches, skeleton watch, solar-powered watch, Spring Drive, watch strap, watchcase.
- Musée international d'horlogerie, https://www.mih.ch/en/watch-collections/ : about 10,000 items: watches and clocks from the 16th century to now plus tools, machines, instruments and automata; labels are by form (pocket watch, astrarium, bracket clock, marine clock, jumping hours watch, astronomical regulator, automaton watch).
- Pocket watch, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocket_watch : hunter-case, open-face and demi-hunter styles; key-wind versus stem-wind; "adjusted" grades.

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

Agree:
- Everyone separates a worn watch from a placed clock, and the pocket watch is a kept-apart form (Chrono24, Wikipedia, MIH, NAWCC).
- Nearly every watch retailer puts a movement facet (mechanical manual, automatic, quartz, solar) and a type or style facet side by side (Jomashop, Chrono24, Kakaku, bestwatch.ru).
- For clocks, collectors and dealers divide by case or placement form: longcase, bracket, mantel, carriage, wall (Collectors Weekly, Sellingantiques, FindArtInfo, Babla's, NAWCC).
- Brand and model are the identity layer: Chrono24 "most popular models", Kakaku series, Tempus iconic models, Seiko Prospex lines.

Split:
- Chronograph and GMT are a complication in Wikipedia's list, a watch type in Tempus, Jomashop and Chrono24, and a prize class in GPHG.
- Movement is the first facet at Kakaku, a mid-list facet at Jomashop, and a category link among types at Chrono24. Tempus puts function before movement.
- The Swiss ordinance excludes the strap from "watch"; Chrono24 sells straps, winders, boxes, dials and movements as categories next to watches; the framework's S3 files a strap with the watch but a box that holds several watches elsewhere (B1), which differs from Chrono24 for boxes.
- Dive watch: a standard (ISO 6425, pass or fail) versus a style that retailers apply loosely.
- Sex of the user is a top facet at Jomashop and Chrono24 and a prize split at GPHG; in the framework it is a modifier.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence for what buyers decide first.
- **Watches: function or style family, then movement, then maker and model.** Tempus states it ("une montre se choisit d'abord par sa fonction et son style", https://tempus-watches.fr/types-de-montres/). Jomashop's tiles (Automatic, Chronograph, Dive, Pilot, Luxury) mix movement, complication and use. Kakaku leads with movement (https://kakaku.com/watch_accessory/watch/), so Japanese buying starts at "how does it run". Bestwatch.ru starts with sex and brand, then mechanism. In this memo the style families (dress, pilot, field, racing, sailing) are kept as `design_family` tags with generated views, not as levels (section 3); this is owner question A5-2.
- **Clocks: where it stands or hangs, and the case form.** Babla's (wall, bedside alarm, mantel, LCD, travel, cuckoo), Sellingantiques and Collectors Weekly all begin with placement form.
- **Maker is a first axis for collectors.** Chrono24 lists brands before categories on its home page; Bob's tier list and Soner's pyramid sort by brand (section 5).
- **No page uses one axis.** Wikipedia's own clock table has three (mechanism, function, style); Vocabineer has four. This is the main structural finding: any single-axis tree has to say clearly which axis it keeps and send the rest to tags.

## 3. Candidate structures

Depth counted from the category: sub-family (level 1), leaf (level 2, skipped where a sub-family holds about 12 kinds or fewer, C5b), kind (level 3), named form (level 4). The category itself is A5. At most four levels below the category.

### Candidate A (recommended): carry or placement, then defining complication or certified function (watches) or placement (clocks), then kind, then named form

- **Level 1 sub-family axis:** how the timepiece is carried or placed in use (on the wrist; elsewhere on the person; in a room or on a site; stand-alone timing instruments), plus the last sub-family "Parts and accessories made for one watch or clock", which is exempt from the level-1 axis (C5d).
- **Level 2 leaf axis, stated per sub-family (C5c).**
  - *Wristwatches:* the **defining complication or certified function, tested on the object**. This is the review's version of owner question 2 (F33). The tests are:
    - Dive: a unidirectional timing bezel and at least 100 m water resistance, or conformity to ISO 6425.
    - Chronograph: a chronograph mechanism (start, stop and reset of an elapsed-time hand or register).
    - Multi-zone: a GMT or world-time display.
    - Calendar and astronomical: an annual or perpetual calendar, or a moon phase.
    - Striking: a repeater or an alarm mechanism.
    - Tourbillon and grande complication: a tourbillon, or one timing, one astronomical and one striking complication (Wikipedia Complication).
    - Time-only and simple-date: none of the above.
  - *Carried watches:* no leaf (4 kinds; C5b).
  - *Clocks:* **placement or mounting** (floor, table or shelf, wall, portable, building-mounted).
  - *Timing instruments:* no leaf (6 kinds; C5b).
- **Level 3 kinds** are generic types where buyers compare quality or makers (section 4b). **Level 4 named forms** are model families and iconic designs, either maker-bound (Submariner, Speedmaster) or not maker-bound (Vienna regulator, Black Forest cuckoo), recorded with `maker_bound` (C2a). Specifications, reference numbers, colourways, collaborations and editions are never named forms (C2b; variants, C3).
- **Kept off the tree as attributes, tags or views:** movement and power (manual, automatic, quartz, solar, Spring Drive, kinetic; C10), complications that do not define the object, case material and metal, case diameter and other size (C6), shape, strap, dial colour, `display` (analogue, digital, ana-digi), `skeleton`, `gem_set`, `iso6425`, `iso6425_annex_a`, `mil_spec`, `cosc`, `geneva_seal`, `swiss_made`; `design_family` tags (dress, pilot, field, military, racing, sailing, sports or tough, jewellery); price band, maker, tier, era, region, user (sex, age), condition, edition, collaboration.
- **`design_family` tags** are one controlled list on kinds and named forms, with no automatic inheritance (C8). Each value has a definition source: the Chrono24 category pages (Dress Watches, Fliegeruhren, Militäruhren, Rennfahreruhren, Segeluhren), Tempus (habillée, pilote) and Jomashop (Pilot). Each generates a view. A tag that has no source is held back.
- **Precedence inside watches, so that each named form has one home (S1).**
  1. The kind is found by the object's defining complication or certified function, never by marketing lineage, design family or occasion.
  2. If a watch has more than one defining function, it goes to the first of these that it has: Dive; Tourbillon and grande complication; Striking; Calendar and astronomical; Chronograph; Multi-zone; Time-only. **OWNER:** confirm this order (part of question A5-2); it is an editor's proposal, ordered by certified function first and then by complexity, which also matches the memo's order inside a leaf (section 8). Example: the Sky-Dweller, which Tempus lists with its GMT family, has an annual calendar and a second time zone, so this order sends it to Calendar and astronomical.
  3. A display that is the form of the watch (digital; one hand; jumping hours) beats a function. A digital watch with stopwatch and alarm modes is a digital watch, because those modes are standard on the form and are not mechanical complications. **OWNER:** confirm (part of A5-2); this is an editor's reading of F34, which keeps "digital watch" as a kind under time-only because its display is its form.
  4. A pocket watch is a pocket watch whatever its complication (the carry axis comes first).

### Where candidate A still bends (edge objects)

1. G-Shock Rangeman, Mudmaster and Gulfmaster, and similar watches with altimeter, barometer and compass: the main function may be sensing (D2 by the step 17 note) or time (A5 by step 16). The G-Shock guide calls the GBD line "basic connected functionality" that "prioritises toughness"; those stay A5. **OWNER:** the sensor-led case needs a ruling; the proposed default is in section 9 and question A5-4.
2. Pocket watch versus wristwatch: a "wristlet" or trench watch is a pocket-watch case adapted to the wrist (Wikipedia Pocket watch). The carry axis is clean for modern watches and fuzzy for 1900-1920 transition pieces. The trench watch is filed as a not maker-bound named form of the time-only watch. "Wristlet" is also an A4 handbag and wallet word: one word, two targets, kept in the alias table (C9).
3. Parts (A5.5) do not fit the carry axis; they are there because S3 says so, and C5(d) exempts them.
4. Tower and turret clocks, attendance clocks, game clocks and atomic clocks fit no room-clock leaf well. They are decided by key order (section 9) and given a leaf (A5.3.5) or the timing-instruments sub-family.

### Candidate A′ (the memo's earlier recommendation, kept for owner question A5-2): environment first

- Level 2 for wristwatches would be a use environment (dive, pilot, field, military, racing, sailing) then complication then style, with the precedence rule "environment beats complication beats style".
- It does not give one home per object: applied as written it sends the Daytona to racing, the GMT-Master II to pilot, the Yacht-Master to sailing, the Explorer to field and the Santos to pilot, because environment is judged from marketing lineage (review F33). It also names leaves by occasion (dress) and setting (sports, tough), which Appendix A treats as modifiers. This is why Candidate A above replaces it.
- Evidence for it: Chrono24, Tempus and Jomashop give pilot, dress and field their own pages. **OWNER:** A5-2 asks the owner to choose between Candidate A (recommended by the review, applied here) and Candidate A′ with a testable rule written for each environment.

### Candidate B: carry, then movement or power, then design family

- Levels: carry; movement (mechanical manual, mechanical automatic, quartz, solar, Spring Drive, auto-quartz, digital); design family; named form.
- Evidence for it: Kakaku opens with movement counts (https://kakaku.com/watch_accessory/watch/), WatchTime's article is organised by movement, Wikipedia's clock table has a "By mechanism" column.
- Examples: automatic > diver's watch > Submariner; quartz > diver's watch > a Seiko quartz diver; solar > field watch.
- Breaks: (1) Appendix A makes power source and technology a modifier, not a level, and C10 (rule T) keeps it an attribute unless the placement key names the kind or the form and fittings differ; neither holds for watches; (2) the same design appears in several movements (Seiko Prospex listings carry Mechanical and Solar tags on one line), which would split a named form across leaves, against S1; (3) tiny leaves (Spring Drive 48, auto-quartz 4 at Kakaku); (4) clocks split the same way (Babla's cuckoo: 8 mechanical, 77 quartz) so the axis does not separate watches from clocks. It is better as a facet.

### Candidate C: feature or complication first, as in Chrono24 and GPHG

- Levels: carry; feature family (chronograph, GMT, calendar, tourbillon, repeater, dive, pilot...); kind; named form.
- Evidence for it: Chrono24 category list; GPHG prizes; Wikipedia Complication groups.
- Breaks: features overlap, so a dive chronograph GMT has no single home; Chrono24 mixes sex, metal, colour and tie-ins (James Bond, Ferrari, Cristiano Ronaldo, Navy Seals) into the same list; no exclusive rule exists. Candidate A takes from C only the defining complication or certified function, and adds the precedence order that C lacks. The rest of C works as a set of generated views.

A few more will appear when pocket-watch and clock types are sourced further (Sellingantiques shows about 15 type categories among its antique clock list) and when clock parts are attested. Planning range: 50 to 80. The earlier draft's 59 kinds included 13 style, occasion, finish and specification kinds that are now tags, attributes or named forms.
- **Named forms (model families and iconic designs):** several hundred worth recording. Evidence: Chrono24's "most popular models" strip, Kakaku's series pickups per maker, Seiko's Prospex Sea, Land, Speedtimer and LX lines, the G-Shock series list, Tempus' iconic models per type. - **Where depth concentrates:** wristwatches. Time-only (with the design-family views dress, pilot, field, sports), chronograph and dive have the most model families; GMT is medium; striking, tourbillon and grande complication, moon phase and world time are narrow but valuable; clocks concentrate in antique and mid-century forms (longcase, bracket, mantel, carriage, wall).
- **Broad leaves:** time-only and simple-date, chronograph, dive, table and shelf clocks, wall clocks. **Medium:** multi-zone, calendar and astronomical, parts. **Narrow:** striking, tourbillon and grande complication, floor-standing, portable and marine, building-mounted.

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied (C2a):** a kind stops where buyers compare quality or makers. Evidence by sub-family:

- **Wristwatches stop at the defining complication or certified function** (diver's watch, chronograph, GMT watch, time-only watch). Chrono24 gives each of these its own category page (Taucheruhren, Chronographen, GMT-Uhren, Weltzeituhren), Jomashop gives quick tiles for Dive and Chronograph, Tempus gives type pages with price guides per model, and Seiko's Prospex lineup is split Sea, Land, Speedtimer. Comparison between makers happens inside these pages ("which dive watch"). Models (Submariner, Fifty Fathoms, Aquis) are named forms below. Movement, size, metal and strap are attributes, because the facets sit beside the type, not under it.
- **The pilot, dress, field, military, racing and sailing pages are also compared at that level** (Chrono24 Fliegeruhren, Dress Watches, Militäruhren, Rennfahreruhren, Segeluhren; Tempus habillée and pilote). By the choice-level test alone they could be kinds. They are held as `design_family` tags with views because no test on the object separates them, which would break "one home per object" (review F33). **OWNER:** this is question A5-2.
- **High complications go one step finer** (annual calendar versus perpetual calendar, tourbillon, minute repeater, grande complication). Chrono24 has separate pages for Tourbillon, Ewiger Kalender, Minutenrepetition, Mondphase; GPHG awards Tourbillon, Calendar and Astronomy and Mechanical Exception prizes; Wikipedia defines grande complication by three complication groups. Collectors compare makers inside each of these.
- **Digital, one-hand and jumping-hours watches are kinds because the display is the form** (Chrono24 Digitaluhren and Einzeigeruhren; MIH). Tough digital series (DW-5600, GA, Mudmaster) are named forms; the buyer compares series ("start with use case", G-Shock guide).
- **Clocks stop at the case or placement form** (longcase, bracket, mantel, carriage, wall dial, cuckoo). Dealers price and count at that level (Sellingantiques counts per type; Collectors Weekly "Types"). Finer features (fusee, strike, regulator movement) are value attributes (FindArtInfo), and regional styles (Vienna, Black Forest, ogee, banjo) are named forms with a region tag (C11). Mounting defines the clock kinds because the body differs by mount (C12a: a longcase, a mantel clock and a wall clock are made only for their placement).
- **Pocket watches stop at case style** (open-face, hunter, demi-hunter), with railroad grade as an attribute (a grade is a specification, C2b) and a model such as the Hamilton 992 as a named form †.
- **Parts stop at the part type** (strap, bracelet, winder, dial, movement, crystal, case, chain); strap material and attachment size are attributes.
- **Hourglass, stopwatch, game clock, time clock, atomic clock** stop at the generic instrument: no source compares makers within them.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Finding: two layers, one measurable and one consensus.**

*Layer 1: measurable and certified (spec-ranked, attached to a watch or movement, not to a maker). These are attributes with the standard named (C4b), never tier entries.*
- `cosc`: two named levels with numeric limits: Chronometer Certified (−4 to +6 s/day) and Excellence Chronometer Certified (−2 to +4 s/day, 200 Gauss, tested on the finished watch) (https://www.cosc.swiss/cosc-certifications). The certificate is optional and belongs to the brand, not the buyer (source for the levels: the certifications page above; the optional-certificate point comes from a search excerpt of the COSC FAQ and is not a fetched claim †).
- `iso6425`: pass or fail, minimum 100 m with a secured dive-time device (https://www.iso.org/standard/66517.html); `iso6425_annex_a` for saturation diving.
- `swiss_made`: pass or fail, 60 percent of manufacturing costs (https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/1971/1908_1915_1915/en). **OWNER:** this is a legal designation; whether it belongs in the C17 jurisdiction ledger instead of an attribute is for the owner when C17 is approved.
- `geneva_seal`: pass or fail (provenance, finishing, reliability) (https://www.poincondegeneve.ch/en).
- `mil_spec`: a military specification the watch meets (MIL-W-46374: Wikipedia Category:Watches). MIL-W-46374 watches by named makers (for example a Marathon or Hamilton model) can be named forms once sourced †.
- `movement_source` (in-house, ETA, Sellita, Miyota, other): in-house "manufacture" versus standard calibre. WatchTime says there is no universal definition of "manufacture movement", so this is a labelled attribute with the source's own wording, not a rank.

*Layer 2: brand tiers (consensus-ranked, and the consensus is soft).* Three fetched tier lists give different results for the same houses:

**Classification:** consensus-ranked for brands (the order is a community claim, not a standard), spec-ranked for certifications and for COSC accuracy classes, and preference for everything else. Clocks have no comparable ordering in the fetched sources; FindArtInfo's value factors (eight-day versus 30-hour movement, fusee, strike and repeat, signed makers such as Tompion) are attributes, not a tier.

**How it appears (C4; modifiers, never levels):**
- **Tier entries, never on a kind.** A tier sits on a maker, a line or a named form, as one entry per source: (`subject_id`, `scheme`, `value`, `source_url`, `date`), with a computed `disputed` flag. Do not pick a winner. A named form does not inherit its maker's tier unless an entry says so. The schemes are "Bob's S/A/B/C", "Watch Scanner tiers 1 to 4" and "Soner tiers 1 to 6".
- **A scheme enters the data only with at least one fetched source and preferably a second from another region (C4e).** The three schemes here are all English-language dealer or brand blogs. Before tiers enter the data, add one non-English tier or hierarchy source (for example a Japanese or German enthusiast ranking) †. Bob's Watches' S/A/B/C is a dealer's own segmentation from its sales data; if the owner treats dealer labels as platform segments, it becomes a dated `platform_tag` instead (C4c).
- **Price band** is the one domain-wide USD scheme (C4d): under 50, 50-150, 150-500, 500-1,500, 1,500-5,000, 5,000-15,000, over 15,000, derived from a dated, sourced list price. The earlier draft's Jomashop bands (under 100 up to over 5,000 USD) are replaced. Soner's price-band tiers are a tier scheme, not the price band.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Classification: identity.** The maker is part of what is sought, and often is the first thing a buyer names.
- Chrono24's home navigation carries a brand menu next to the categories (seen in a search excerpt of the home page †), and its category list contains brand-defined groups (Breitling Chronograph, Cheap Rolex, Rolex tie-ins). Kakaku and Tempus put the maker's series (Portugieser, Overseas, Big Bang) at the front of the catalogue.
- Tier lists are written per brand, not per kind (section 5).
- Standards and awards identify makers: the Geneva Seal is geographically bound, GPHG prizes list maker plus model, AHCI is a maker association.
- Collectors of clocks name makers too (Tompion, Seth Thomas, Ansonia, Howard Miller, Telechron: FindArtInfo, Collectors Weekly).
- Platform effects are weak: no maker sets compatibility for whole watches. Movement supply (ETA, Sellita, Miyota, Time Module, Vaucher: WatchTime) is recorded in the compatibility ledger (C13: `platform_id`, `name`, `owner`, `kinds_served` = watch movement, `standard_ref`, `sources`), and each line records `built_by_maker_id` where the movement maker differs from the brand. Strap fit (lug width, quick-release) is an attribute of strap and watch; no standard for it is identified in the fetched pages †.

**Recommendation: yes, a maker ledger on the C1 schema.** One row per maker, each row attested.
- *Core columns (C1):* `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class`; `country`; `place`; `founded` (the Wikipedia list gives year, place and founder: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swiss_watch_manufacturers); `ended`; `status`; `parent_id` (group or licensor); `scene_tags` (with strength; C8); `price_band` (C4d); `sources` (a fetched URL, and a second where available). The `known_for_kinds` column is derived from the named-form ledger, not typed in.
- *`maker_class` values (C1):* house or manufacture; independent (AHCI member, current or former); microbrand (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbrand_watches); retail own-label; licensee or diffusion; costume or fashion brand; artisan or atelier; supplier (movement supplier, dial or case maker). A clock-maker is a house or manufacture or an artisan, with kinds recorded in the named-form ledger.
- *Separate tables (C1):* **lines and collections** (Seiko Prospex Sea, Land, Speedtimer and LX; Casio G-Shock series; Omega Speedmaster; with the kinds they span, years, tier entries, sources and `built_by_maker_id`); **tier entries** (section 5); **named forms** (C2); **variants** (C3: reference numbers, colourways, collaborations, editions; filled only where the owner emphasises watch references).
- *A5 extension columns (C1):* `movement_policy` (in-house, standard calibre, mixed, with the source's wording) and `certifications` (COSC, Geneva Seal, Swiss Made held by the maker).
- *Known-for examples to seed from the named-form ledger:* Rolex: Submariner, Datejust, Daytona, GMT-Master II; Omega: Speedmaster, Seamaster; Seiko: Prospex Sea, Land, Speedtimer; Casio: G-Shock series; Breguet: carriage clock form, Classique Souscription.

**Size of the maker space (from fetched pages):** AHCI alone lists 36 members, 6 candidates and 18 former members from 16 countries; Bob's covers 20 brands; the Wikipedia list of Swiss manufacturers is long; Chrono24 and bestwatch.ru show Japanese, German and Russian makers (Slava, Molniya, Ouglich); Collectors Weekly lists nine American clock makers. Clock makers need their own rows, with their own `maker_class`.

## 8. Order inside a leaf (C7)

- **Kinds:** canonical form first (the archetype for the leaf), then the secondary key the sub-family states. In wristwatch leaves the secondary key is increasing mechanical complexity (for chronograph: chronograph, flyback chronograph, split-seconds chronograph; in the time-only leaf: time-only watch, digital watch, one-hand watch, jumping-hours watch). Reason: complexity is a stable rank that Wikipedia's complication groups and GPHG's "Complication" prize pairs already use. Where a leaf lists kinds that overlap, the object takes the last-listed kind that it has (a perpetual calendar with a moon phase is a perpetual-calendar watch).
- **Named forms:** by maker (in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction year as attested (the year on the maker's own page), then alphabetically where no year is attested. This replaces the earlier draft's "alphabetical by maker then model" (review F37).
- **Variants:** by `variant_code` (reference number) or year.
- **Clock leaves:** by placement size, floor to table to wall to portable to building-mounted, as dealers lay them out; inside a leaf, the general kind first (mantel clock, wall clock), then the specific forms (English bracket clock before regional variants), then by regional style.
- **Pocket watches:** open-face (the plain form), hunter, demi-hunter, then coin watch.
- **Parts:** by the part's position from the outside in (strap or bracelet, chain, case, crystal, dial, movement), which matches Chrono24's repair-oriented categories.
- Chronology is useful as an attribute (introduction year of a model) and for the antique clock leaves, where dealers use date ranges (FindArtInfo: longcase c. 1660, bracket 1660-1850, carriage 1820-1920).

## 9. Boundaries

Precedence sentences (each can be copied into the placement key after approval).

1. **A3 Jewellery.** A watch whose main function is to tell time is A5 whatever its case metal, gems or price (a diamond-set watch is A5 with the attribute `gem_set`); an ornament with a decorative dial that cannot be read as a timepiece is A3. (Step 16 is tried before step 17.)
2. **A3 Jewellery, watch chains (W1, review F21).** A watch chain, Albert chain or fob chain made to attach a pocket watch is A5 under S3; a decorative fob, seal or charm worn without a watch is A3.
3. **D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices.** A smartwatch, hybrid smartwatch or fitness tracker is D2; a watch that only syncs time by Bluetooth or radio (G-Shock GBD, GW-B5600) stays A5, because connectivity is a modifier. **Sensor-led watches (OWNER, question A5-4).** Proposed default: a watch whose main display is time and whose sensors (altimeter, barometer, compass) are secondary is A5; a device whose main function is sensing or navigation is D2 (matches the step 17 note).
4. **D3 Audio & Video.** A clock radio, smart display or voice-assistant clock is D3; a clock with radio-controlled time (a "radio clock" in the sense of time signal) is A5.
5. **B4 Decor and B3 Lighting.** A sundial sold as a garden ornament is B4; a sundial made to read the time as an instrument is A5; a sunrise lamp is B3.
6. **B5 Kitchen & Dining.** A kitchen timer is B5; an hourglass or sand timer made as a timepiece is A5.
7. **B1 Furniture & Storage.** A box, roll, watch roll or cabinet that holds several watches is B1 (S3: never parts), although Chrono24 files "Uhrenboxen" with watches; a winder or stand that holds one watch is a part and goes to A5. A grandfather clock is a clock (A5), not furniture, even though its case is cabinetry.
8. **C1 Tools & C6 Instruments.** A watchmaker's tool is C1; a barometer or weather station is C6; a marine chronometer is a timepiece (A5, step 16 before step 23). A laboratory atomic clock is A5 for the same reason.
9. **E3 Musical Instruments.** A metronome is E3 (named in step 22), even though it counts time.
10. **E1 Toys & Games.** A chess or game clock measures time and step 16 is tried before step 25, so it is A5 (game clock).
11. **C5 Office & Stationery.** An attendance clock (time clock) is A5, because step 16 is tried before step 31.
12. **G2 Property & Structures.** A tower or turret clock fixed to a building is A5: the step 4 note says installed machines keep their own category.
13. **H2 Health, Medical & Mobility.** A nurse's pulse watch made to monitor a patient is H2 (step 2 is tried before step 16).
14. **F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards.** A clock made as an award or trophy is F3 (step 15 precedes step 16); a commemorative clock is a clock (S5).
15. **Sport (step 26).** A diver's watch with ISO 6425 is A5, not E2, because step 16 precedes step 26; the dive computer that is sensing-led is outside A5.
16. **A4 Bags & Accessories.** A watch strap follows its watch (A5, S3); "wristlet" is also an A4 bag and wallet word, and the alias table (C9) gives it two targets.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure (Candidate A).** Category A5 > sub-family by carry or placement (wristwatches; carried watches; clocks; timing instruments; parts) > leaf (wristwatches by defining complication or certified function; clocks by placement or mounting; none in the other three) > kind (generic type) > named form (model family or iconic design, maker-bound or not). **Maximum depth: four levels below the category, the named form counting as one.** Movement, complications that do not define the object, design family, material, size, brand, tier, era, region, user, condition and certification are attributes, tags or views. A maker ledger on the C1 schema carries brand identity; tier entries carry per-source tier. Kinds stop at the defining complication or certified function for wristwatches, one step finer for high complications, and at case or placement form for clocks.
