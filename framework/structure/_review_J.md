# Stage 2 domain review: J Groceries & Cellar (J1 to J7)

*Batch S2R_J. Cold, offline review (no web, no git) of memos framework/structure/J1.md to J7.md and their sources files verify/out/S2_J1_sources.tsv to S2_J7_sources.tsv. They are checked against BRIEF.md, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md and the draft shared conventions C1 to C13 in framework/structure/_conventions.md. C14 to C17 came later, from the domain E review, and are cited only where they settle a point. The conventions were written after these memos. J1, J2, J5 and J6 say they applied C1 to C13, and J3, J4 and J7 cite some of them; section 4 lists the changes each memo still needs. The Culinary boundary is checked against the BRIEF rule (products as bought here, dishes there) and against /home/user/culinary-taxonomy/culinary/FRAMEWORK_v1.md (v1.1), read locally. Every Culinary code a memo cites was looked up there. This is a reviewer's judgement, not an owner decision. Severity: **high** = breaks a shared rule (BRIEF, the placement key, Appendix A or a convention), or files the same kind of object in two places across several memos; **medium** = one rule break, one contradiction or a clear split; **low** = wording, evidence or tidy-up. A point that rests on the reviewer's own knowledge, not on a fetched page, is marked "(reviewer knowledge, needs a fetched source)".*

**Mechanical checks.**
- *Sources match.* J1 cites S1 to S37, and its TSV has 37 rows. J2: S1 to S38, 38 rows. J3: S1 to S36, 36 rows. J4: S1 to S36, 36 rows. J5: S1 to S34, 34 rows. J6: S1 to S36, 36 rows. ## 2. Findings by memo

Each finding gives its severity, the evidence, the objects that would split where relevant, and an exact proposed fix. "Conforms to" names the convention the fix applies. X numbers refer to the cross-memo findings in section 3, and JC numbers to the shared conventions proposed in section 5.

### J1 Meat & Poultry

**J1-1 (medium) Plain-cooked meat and tinned meat have no leaf.**
- *Evidence.* The J1 branch of step 3 reads "fresh, frozen, cured, smoked or cooked", and the step 1 note says "plain cooking of one main ingredient does not make a food prepared". Candidate A had a "cooked" leaf. Candidate B (recommended) has J1-A "Fresh & frozen meat (raw muscle, bone, offal, mince)", J1-B "Cured, smoked & dried", J1-C "Sausages" and J1-D "Pâtés, terrines, rillettes & preserved cooked meats" with three attested kinds. Open question 6 leaves tinned plain meat unplaced, and the J5 memo (section 9) answers "Dried, cured and tinned meat and fish are J1 and J2". Culinary already lists corned beef, Spam and confit in SV-H3-5.
- *Objects that would split or go unfiled.* Sliced roast beef; cooked chicken pieces; a whole rotisserie chicken (plain cooked); tinned corned beef; luncheon meat; duck confit in a tin; pastrami.
- *Fix.* Rename J1-D "Cooked, tinned & potted meats". If it passes about 12 kinds, give it the leaves "Plain-cooked meats", "Tinned & potted meats" and "Pâtés & terrines (slicing)". Add to P1: "A meat cooked plainly, unseasoned or only seasoned, whole or sliced, is J1-D. A whole muscle cured and then cooked (cooked ham, pastrami, corned beef brisket) is J1-B Cooked-cured. Meat sealed shelf-stable in a tin, jar or pouch is J1-D Tinned & potted, unless it is a ready meal (step 1)." This answers open question 6 and matches Culinary SV-H3-5.

**J1-2 (high) Spreads and pastes made of meat.** J1-D files pâté and rillettes, and J1-C lists "liver paste" as a named form of liver sausage. The step 3 note puts "sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes ... from any source" and "sweet and savoury spreads" in J5, and the placement key outranks category text. See X2 for the shared fix: a spreadable meat paste in a jar or tub goes to J5; a pâté or terrine sold as a loaf for slicing stays in J1-D.

**J1-3 (medium) The size of a kind varies inside J1-A.**
- *Evidence.* Section 4b says the game kind is "the species with its main joint (saddle, haunch, medallions, whole bird)" and the poultry kind "species × joint". The tree has venison saddle and venison medallions (joints), but rabbit, hare, wild boar, horse meat and bison as kinds by species alone. Turkey has only "turkey breast (pending)". Suckling pig and suckling lamb are kinds by the animal's age.
- *Objects that would split.* A wild boar haunch (the kind "wild boar", or a new joint kind?); a bison ribeye (the kind "bison", or "ribeye steak" under Other meats?); rabbit legs; a horse fillet; a venison haunch.
- *Fix.* State one rule for J1-A. A kind is the animal and its retail cut or joint. A species with fewer than three attested cuts gets one kind, "<species>, whole or jointed", with the cut as an attribute until sources justify a split. Bison and other bovines in "Other meats" follow the same rule. Suckling pig and lamb stay kinds because they are sold whole, as "primal or side" kinds (open question 11).

**J1-4 (medium) There is no test for protected names, and an appellation body is not a maker.**
- *Evidence.* In the tree, "Prosciutto di Parma PDO [S33]" is a named form, while usability row 2 says "Kobe is a scheme entry". Section 6 adds "producer (farm or ranch) and appellation body" to `maker_class`, and lists the Consorzio del Prosciutto di Parma and the Kobe Beef association among the makers.
- *Fix (conforms to JC1, proposed).* A designation whose rules bind one kind is a non-maker named form of that kind and carries a `designation_id` (Prosciutto di Parma under dry-cured ham). A designation that spans several kinds is a tag, not a named form: Kobe Beef applies to every cut, and so do the Ibérico sale names. Consortia and associations become the `authority` of the designation row, not makers. Drop "appellation body" from `maker_class` and keep "producer (farm or ranch)" as a `supply_role` value (JC3).

**J1-5 (medium) Several cured kinds are missing, regional names need C11 labels, and pancetta is a homonym.**
- *Evidence.* Candidate B names a "cured fat" leaf in J1-B, but the tree has no kind for it. J1-B has no cured-beef kind, although Culinary SV-H3-1 names bresaola and pastırma. The tree puts "panceta (ES)" as a form of fresh pork belly, while Culinary's precedence sentence treats pancetta as a cured cut (SV-H3-1): Spanish *panceta* is fresh belly and Italian *pancetta* is cured belly (reviewer knowledge, needs a fetched source). The kinds "cured chorizo", "mortadella" and "suckling lamb (lechazo)" are labelled by their regional words.
- *Objects that would split.* Pancetta (bacon, or fresh belly?); guanciale (cured neck, or nothing?); bresaola, cecina and biltong; lardo and salo; 'nduja (dry-cured sausage, or a spread?).
- *Fix (conforms to C9, C11).* Once attested, add these J1-B kinds: "cured pork belly (pancetta)", "cured pork jowl (guanciale)", "cured beef (bresaola, cecina)", "cured back fat (lardo, salo)" and "dried meat strips (jerky, biltong)". Add a C9 homonym row: *panceta* (ES, fresh belly) and *pancetta* (IT, cured belly). Label the regional kinds with the generic description and the word as name: "paprika dry-cured sausage (chorizo)", "large emulsified scalded sausage (mortadella)". 'Nduja is a spreadable dry-cured sausage: J1-C under X2's rule, because it is sold in casings.

**J1-6 (low) Evidence.** The choice-level table cites Serious Eats [S23], but only its closing paragraph was returned. Korea rests on one restaurant page [S22], the Chinese beef grade on a trade-news item [S26], and the BMS bands on an exporter's page [S6]. Pork hock, trotter and jowl are attested only by one consumer guide [S10], and the sweetbreads' animal is assumed. No forum or butchery reference was fetched. *Fix:* before approval, fetch the AHDB cut list itself or a national cut chart, plus one Korean or Chinese retail tree. Mark single-source kinds in the tree.

**J1-7 (low) Scene tags and the Culinary link note.** (a) The proposed "cooking-tradition tags" (yakiniku, hotpot, pot-au-feu, US barbecue) are dish practices, and Culinary already holds them (SV-K2-1 tableside hot pots, SV-K2-2 tableside grills, SV-F2-3 barbecue). *Fix:* use the shared J tradition vocabulary only for production traditions (Spanish jamonería, Japanese wagyu grading), and generate the "by cooking style" view from Culinary links (JC6). (b) The link plan says Culinary's hot-against-cold sausage rule "must be restated as a J1 note". That is not needed, because J1-C divides by process class. *Fix:* map each J1-C kind to one or two Culinary targets in the link table: fresh sausage to SV-F1-4; mortadella, Lyoner and frankfurter for slicing to SV-H3-5; salami and chorizo to SV-H3-3.

### J2 Fish & Seafood

**J2-1 (medium) Caviar grades are not named forms.**
- *Evidence.* Kinds 53 and 54 carry "grades 000, 00, 0" and "royal, Imperial (seller grades)" as named forms, and section 5 says the caviar grade "is stored as a named form (or a dated tier entry ...)". C2(b) says specifications are attributes, not named forms. C4(b) and C4(c) make a measured ladder an attribute and a seller's label a tier entry or platform tag.
- *Fix (conforms to C2(b), C4).* Store the Beluga grain categories 000, 00 and 0 as an attribute `grain_grade`, with the scheme named (FAO, quoting US Customs 1997 [S25]). Store a producer's grade (Royal, Imperial, Grade 1) as a dated tier entry with `scheme = producer grade` on that producer's line, or as a `platform_tag` when a retailer names it. Pressed caviar (payusnaya) stays a kind, because it is a different product form. Akami, chūtoro and ōtoro are cuts, so they stay non-maker named forms, which matches J1's treatment of sub-cuts.

**J2-2 (high) Formed fish products.** Fish sausage, kamaboko and surimi sticks are OUT here (section 9 point 4 and usability row 20), but meat sausages are J1-C and J1 sends fish sausage to J2 (J1 P2). A Japanese fish sausage therefore has no home. See X3 for the shared rule and the owner question.

**J2-3 (medium) Wet-cured fish has no sub-family.**
- *Evidence.* J2.2 is "Smoked", J2.3 "Salted, dried & fermented" and J2.4 "Tinned, jarred & marinated". P4 sends "salted or dried" fish to J2.3, and Matjes is marked "cure class open". Culinary SV-G1-4 holds gravlax and soused herring.
- *Objects that would split.* Gravlax (salt-sugar cured, chilled, not dried); Matjes; anchovy fillets in salt; boquerones in vinegar; lox.
- *Fix.* Rename J2.3 "Salted, cured, dried & fermented fish & seafood". Add P4b: "Fish cured in salt, sugar or brine and sold chilled (gravlax, Matjes, salted anchovies) is J2.3; fish marinated in vinegar, oil or a sauce is J2.4. P3 (the pack decides) is applied first."

**J2-4 (medium) Snails are molluscs, and frogs, turtles and insects have no home anywhere in domain J.**
- *Evidence.* Section 9.1 reads: "Aquatic animals other than fish, crustaceans, molluscs and other marine invertebrates (frogs, turtles, snails) are J1 as game". Snails are molluscs (reviewer knowledge, basic biology). J1 has no frog, snail, turtle or insect kind. Edible insects are a Culinary leaf (SV-H5-1). In the CATEGORIES key, no J branch names insects: the J1 branch is "meat, poultry and game", and J5's list does not include them. Under S1a an insect therefore runs to X0.
- *Fix.* Rewrite P-J1: "Land and sea snails sold to eat (escargot, whelks) are J2.1 molluscs; frogs' legs and turtle are J1 'Other meats'." Log a framework change: extend the J1 branch to "meat, poultry, game and other land animals eaten (frogs, insects)" (owner question 12).

**J2-5 (low) Modifiers in labels and order, and the mixed leaf.** Kind 1 is labelled "Atlantic salmon (farmed Scottish and similar)", and section 8 orders "farmed salmon and the salmon species first". Farmed and Scottish are modifiers. *Fix:* use the label "Atlantic salmon" and the order "salmon species first". The leaf "Mixed seafood" is on no animal-group axis. Replace it with the shared mixed rule (X5, JC5(c)).

**J2-6 (low) Prawn kinds.** "King prawn" and "banana prawn" are trade names that cover several species (reviewer knowledge, needs a fetched source), and usability row 8 already needs a fallback kind, "prawn (unspecified)". *Fix:* take crustacean kinds from a fetched commercial-designation list (the EU national lists of commercial designations, or FAO ASFIS, neither of which was fetched). Make "prawn or shrimp, species not stated" the canonical first kind, and record `scientific_name` as an attribute (owner question 9).

**J2-7 (low) Evidence.** The Ocado and Waitrose child lists come partly from search excerpts (the memo says so). The Chinese classes come from a popular article [S19] and a commentary page [S20], not from the GB/T text. There is no comparison or "best X" source, and the memo says its choice-level evidence is indirect. *Fix:* fetch a fishmonger's species guide or a commercial-designation list, and one US or Spanish retail tree. Mark S28 and S29 "not usable" in the TSV.

**J2-8 (low) The Culinary link plan names leaves, not targets per kind.** *Fix:* add per-kind targets. Named fish and shellfish (hairy crab, Copper River salmon) go to SV-H5-4; live oysters to SV-G1-5; raw-eating fish to SV-G1-2; gravlax and pickled herring to SV-G1-4; smoked fish to SV-H4-2; dried fish to SV-H4-1; roe to SV-H4-4; and fish-paste products to SV-H4-5, if the owner keeps them in J2 (X3).

### J3 Dairy, Eggs & Alternatives

**J3-1 (medium) Rule P (protected name as kind or named form) is subjective and conflicts with C11.**
- *Evidence.* Rule P (section 3): "A protected name is a kind when the product is sold and compared only under that name (Roquefort, Gorgonzola, Stilton, Parmigiano Reggiano, Grana Padano). It is a named form when a broader generic kind is sold and compared (West Country farmhouse Cheddar PDO under Cheddar; Mozzarella di Bufala Campana DOP under Mozzarella; Feta PDO under Feta)." C11 makes a regional name a kind only when no generic kind in the tree covers its form, with the generic description as the label. Parmigiano Reggiano and Grana Padano share one grana form, and "parmesan" is sold generically outside the EU (reviewer knowledge, needs a fetched US retail page). Culinary's rule makes an appellation a variant.
- *Objects that would split.* Brie de Meaux (the kind brie, or a kind of its own?); Comté; Pecorino Romano (named form of Pecorino, or its own kind?); a US "parmesan"; Grana Padano; Roquefort.
- *Fix (conforms to C11 and JC1).* Replace Rule P with the shared designation rule. A cheese kind is a variety made under that name by unrelated makers in at least one fetched market (Cheddar, Gouda, Emmental, brie, camembert, feta, mozzarella, Pecorino, "grana-type hard cheese (parmesan)"), labelled by the generic name. A protected name is a named form under it (Parmigiano Reggiano and Grana Padano under grana; Brie de Meaux under brie). A protected cheese with no generic sibling sold under the name (Roquefort and Stilton, if a fetched source confirms) is a kind that carries its own name, under C11's "no generic kind covers it" case, and the reason is recorded on the row. Owner question 2.

**J3-2 (medium) The cheese leaf axis needs a first-*yes* order.**
- *Evidence.* The leaf axis is "texture and ripening family: how the curd is made and matured". The memo itself quotes "washed-rind cheeses can be soft (Limburger), semi-hard or hard (Appenzeller)" [S25] and notes that Codex treats brine as a ripening term, not a firmness [S13]. Culinary SV-H1-5 defines washed-rind by "a pungent orange rind (Époisses, Taleggio, Limburger, Munster)", all of them soft or semi-soft.
- *Objects that would split.* Appenzeller and Gruyère (washed or smear rind, but hard); Cambozola and Gorgonzola dolce (soft and blue); halloumi (brined, or something else?); smoked scamorza (stretched curd and smoked); aged Gouda (semi-hard or hard).
- *Fix (conforms to C5(c)).* Write the leaf test as a first-*yes* key. (1) Processed? Then Processed cheese. (2) Blue-veined? Then Blue. (3) White-mould rind? Then Bloomy-rind. (4) Matured in brine? Then Brined. (5) Stretched curd? Then Stretched-curd. (6) Unripened, or a whey cheese? Then Fresh and whey. (7) Soft or semi-soft with a washed or smear rind? Then Washed-rind. (8) Otherwise by the Codex firmness band [S13]: firm or semi-hard goes to Semi-hard pressed, hard or extra-hard to Hard and long-aged. Hard smear-rind cheeses such as Appenzeller and Comté then go by firmness. Age moves no cheese: it is filed by its usual retail form, as the memo already says.

**J3-3 (medium) Evidence is thin outside UK retail.** Eight of 36 rows are UK pages (7 from Waitrose). The only Japanese source is one Aeon page. The fetches for Woolworths' department page and REWE failed. Sheep's milk, buffalo milk, buttermilk and soured cream are attested only by a navigation box ("S26 nav"), which is a weak attestation. Skyr against quark is decided on Waitrose alone. *Fix:* fetch an Indian dairy tree (BigBasket), one Chinese or Korean dairy tree, a French or Italian cheese retailer and a cheesemonger's classification page. Mark the nav-only kinds with †.

**J3-4 (low) Order keys that cannot be reproduced.** Section 8 orders cheese kinds "the most widely sold variety first, then alphabetical" and milk "other species by market share", and neither has a source. *Fix:* order by the canonical form of the leaf (the Codex or Käseverordnung example, where one is named), then alphabetically, and order milk species alphabetically after cow's milk.

**J3-5 (low) Boundaries the key already settles.** (a) Plant egg and plant cream alternatives (open question 3): the J3 branch says "plant-based alternatives to them", and the list it refers to includes cream and eggs, so both are J3, and so is a non-dairy creamer. (b) Flavoured milk and milkshakes (open question 4): step 1 makes "a non-alcoholic drink made by combining ingredients" prepared, so they are OUT, and Culinary DR-D1-3 holds them. Plain drinking yoghurt, kefir and plain buttermilk are fermented single-ingredient products and stay J3. Ayran (yoghurt, water and salt) is the edge, and goes to the owner (question 17). (c) Milk-tea and hot-chocolate mixes: J3 sentence 7 disagrees with J6 (X7). (d) Butter blends: J3 keeps them, but J5's precedence 2 names only margarine. J5 should add "a spread that contains butter is J3".

**J3-6 (low) Culinary links stop at SV-H1.** *Fix:* add kefir and kumys to DR-D1-1, plant drinks to DR-D1-4, ayran and lassi (if kept) to DR-D1-2, and preserved eggs to SV-E4-6. The code SV-E4-6 exists, so the egg link is coherent.

### J4 Fruit & Vegetables

**J4-1 (medium) "Mixed packs" is a sub-family on a second axis.** The memo says so itself ("its own axis is pack composition", break 4). *Fix (conforms to C5(c), JC5(c)):* drop the sub-family. A mix of one sub-family's kinds goes to a final kind in that sub-family: "mixed vegetables" (frozen mixed vegetables, an undressed salad-leaf mix) under Vegetables, "mixed fruit" (forest fruit mix) under Fruit, stored with `leaf = null` or in a last leaf "Mixed". A fruit-and-vegetable party tray is filed by its greatest-weight item (the step 3 set rule).

**J4-2 (medium) Cripps Pink, Pink Lady and PLU are modelled wrongly.**
- *Evidence.* Section 3 says "`maker_bound` yes for licensed or club cultivars (Pink Lady® / Cripps Pink: licensor on file, S13)". Section 6 says the PLU code "is a `variant_code` for commodity plus variety plus size" and that "a licensed brand name (Pink Lady®) is a `variant_type` 'brand name' of the cultivar Cripps Pink". S13 itself separates the varietal name (Cripps Pink) from the trade mark (Pink Lady, about 10 % of the crop). C3's variant types do not include "brand name", and C6 makes size an attribute.
- *Fix (conforms to C2(a), C3, C6, C9).* Make Cripps Pink a non-maker-bound named form with a `breeder_or_holder` field. Record Pink Lady as a C9 alias with relation `trade mark`, pointing to Cripps Pink, with the licensor in the maker ledger. A club variety sold under one marketer only, such as SunGold kiwifruit with Zespri, stays maker-bound. Store PLU and GS1 GPC codes in an `external_codes` field on the kind or named form (the same field J1, J2 and J3 need for HS and CN codes), and keep size as an attribute. The C3 variant ledger stays empty for J4.

**J4-3 (medium) The fruit-or-vegetable rule and cherries can be settled now.**
- *Evidence.* Woolworths states its rule: tomatoes, cucumbers, avocados and capsicums "are botanically fruits", but "because they aren't sweet, we categorise them as vegetables" [S18]. Put cherries in Stone fruit. Plantain goes to Tropical fruit, with Culinary SW-A2-1, and the green-cooking use is an attribute. Owner questions 1 and 9 become confirmations.

**J4-4 (low) Objects that two builders would file differently.** Rocket (herb or leaf vegetable); lemongrass (herb, or stems and shoots); ginger (root, or herb, beside Woolworths' "herbs, garlic and chillies"); bean sprouts and cut microgreens (no kind); sweetcorn (fruiting vegetable, or pods and seeds); fresh chestnuts and fresh coconut (J4 or J5). *Fix:* define the herbs sub-family as "leafy or soft-stemmed plants sold in bunches or packs to flavour food" (coriander, parsley, basil, mint, lemongrass). Rocket goes to leaf vegetables, ginger to roots, bean sprouts and microgreens to stems and shoots, and sweetcorn to fruiting vegetables. Chestnuts and coconuts follow the key ("every nut ... is J5"), and J5's nuts leaf must list them (X7).

**J4-5 (low) No price band.** Open question 7 recommends none, against the unit band proposed in J1, J2, J3, J5 and J6. *Fix:* use the shared unit-price band (JC2). A seasonal price is still a dated list price.

### J5 Pantry Staples

**J5-1 (high) The level-1 axis is mixed, and one sub-family is a preservation class.**
- *Evidence.* Section 3: "No retailer or standard uses a single principle here ... so the level is stable even though its axis is mixed; the memo states this plainly." J5.10 "Tinned, jarred & pickled goods" divides by preservation. The other sub-families divide by ingredient (J5.1 to J5.7), product role (J5.8 spreads, J5.9 sauces) or function (J5.11 baking). Break 1 is "a dried chickpea is J5.3 and a tinned chickpea is J5.10". J5.9 has a leaf "Pastes & purées" and J5.10 a leaf "Tomatoes". J4 argues that state is an attribute, not a level.

- *Fix (conforms to C5(c), JC4).* State the level-1 axis as "what the product is made from or is", and replace J5.4 and J5.10 with two sub-families. "J5.4 Nuts & seeds" takes coconut milk and cream (coconut is a J5 nut). "J5.x Preserved fruit & vegetables" has the leaf axis "how it is preserved": dried; tinned or jarred in water, brine or syrup; pickled in vinegar; lacto-fermented (sauerkraut, kimchi); in oil. Its kinds are the crop, so dried apricots and tinned apricots are sister kinds in one sub-family. Tomato products (tinned tomatoes, passata, purée, paste) form one leaf there. Pulses keep tinned pulses in J5.3 as a kind or with a pack attribute, because the buyer compares chickpeas with chickpeas. Jam stays in the spreads sub-family. With this, every sub-family is an ingredient or product family, and preservation is a leaf axis inside one of them.

**J5-2 (medium) Rice kinds mix six axes.**
- *Evidence.* The Rice leaf has the kinds long-grain white, basmati, brown and wholegrain, risotto and paella, sushi and short-grain, South Indian boiled and raw, and "flattened rice (poha) and tapioca pearls (sabudana)". Milling (white, brown or wholegrain, parboiled), the "boiled" process and age in months become attributes. Sona Masoori, Ponni and Koshihikari are named forms. Poha becomes a kind "flattened rice" in the rice leaf, and sabudana and wild rice go to Other grains & starch pearls.

**J5-3 (medium) Dips, spreads and pastes have no stated rule.** The key puts "sweet and savoury spreads, sauces, condiments, stocks and pastes from any source" in J5. **J5-4 (medium) The designation ledger mixes designations with standards.** The memo's ledger holds "Aceto Balsamico Tradizionale di Modena DOP ... San Marzano DOP, the JAS soy sauce grades, ISO 3632, the EU olive oil categories". The first three are protected names. The last three are grading standards, which C4(b) makes attributes. *Fix:* split them into the two tables of JC1, `designation` and `standard`. Q8 then reads as the shared convention.

**J5-5 (low) Internal splits.** (a) Dried chillies: the memo lists both J5.4 dried vegetables and J5.6 spices. (b) Miso, doenjang and doubanjiang (J5.9 "Other Asian fermented seasonings"), against natto and tempeh (J5.12 "Tempeh & fermented soy"). *Fix:* "A dried chilli, whole, flaked or ground, sold to season food is a spice (J5.6); a dried vegetable is one sold to rehydrate and cook as a vegetable." ### J7 Wine, Beer & Spirits

**J7-1 (high) Kinds that differ only by country, and regional kinds against C11.**
- *Evidence.* The whisky leaf has the kinds "Irish whiskey [S12] · Japanese whisky [S12]", and the memo would add Canadian whisky. Appendix A makes region and culture modifiers, and C11 says a regional name is a kind only when it "names a form that no generic kind in the tree already covers". Japanese whisky names no form: Japanese malts and blends are made in the same ways as other malts and blends. The memo's own Q1 asks the same question for Champagne, Port, Sherry, Cognac and similar. The tree also has "Bordeaux red blend", a kind named for a region.
- *Objects that would split.* A Japanese single malt (the kind Japanese whisky, or a single-malt kind?); an Irish single pot still; a Scotch blended malt; a Tasmanian single malt; a crémant rosé; a pisco against an unaged grape brandy.
- *Fix (conforms to Appendix A, C11, JC1).* Make the whisky kinds production types: single malt, blended malt, single grain, blended, pot still, and the US legal types (bourbon, rye, corn whisky), each with the generic description as its label. Country and region (Scotch, Irish, Japanese, Canadian, Campbeltown) become origin tags, and "Scotch" a designation row that spans several kinds (JC1(b)). For wine and brandy the recommended rule is the same as J1, J3, J5 and J6: generic method kinds ("traditional-method sparkling wine", "tank-method sparkling wine", "aged grape brandy", "fortified wine") with Champagne, Cava, Crémant, Prosecco, Cognac, Armagnac, Port and Sherry as non-maker named forms carrying a designation. The owner may keep them as kinds (owner question 2). If so, the exception should be written into C11 for every J category, not for J7 alone.

**J7-2 (medium) Sake designations are kinds, but most of them form a spec ladder.** The NTA names step along the polishing ratio (honjozo 70 %, ginjo 60 %, daiginjo 50 %) and combine it with one composition switch, brewer's alcohol added or not [S8]. Break 4 of the memo already says "the polishing ratio goes to an attribute". J5 makes the EU olive-oil categories an attribute (Q3), and J1 does the same with the Ibérico sale names (X8). Nigori (cloudy, a separate Culinary leaf, DR-E3-2), namazake and genshu have no kind. *Fix (conforms to C4(b)):* make the kinds "junmai sake (rice, koji and water only)", "sake with brewer's alcohol", "cloudy sake (nigori)" and "non-designated sake (futsūshu)". The tokutei meisho-shu name becomes the attribute `designation` (NTA, with the polishing ratio as a value), and nama, genshu and taru become attributes. The leaf level is not needed (four kinds).

**J7-3 (medium) The wine leaf axis needs a first-*yes* order, and the sweet wine leaf is missing.** The axis is "wine class (colour for still wines; method for sparkling, fortified and sweet)". The tree's leaf is "Fortified wine", but the breadth list and usability row 19 call it "Fortified and sweet wine". Sauternes and Tokaj are named but not placed, and vermouth is unattested. *Objects that would split:* sparkling rosé; Lambrusco (sparkling red); Moscato d'Asti; vin doux naturel; orange sparkling wine. *Fix:* use this order. (1) Fortified? Then Fortified wine. (2) Botrytised, ice, dried-grape or late-harvest sweet? Then Sweet wine. (3) Sparkling? Then Sparkling wine. (4) A white made with long skin contact? Then Orange wine. (5) Pink? Then Rosé. (6) Otherwise still red or still white. Add the leaf "Aromatised wine" (vermouth, retsina) once it is sourced, to match Culinary DR-F2-4.

**J7-4 (medium) Coverage is Western only, and several drink families have no kind.** Beer has six kinds, all from BJCP [S6]; cider has BJCP styles only; wine kinds come from one US facet [S10] and Wine Folly. No East Asian retailer was fetched, nor a customs heading or the EU wine and spirit-drink rules. Culinary holds families that J7 cannot yet file: baijiu (DR-G1-3), shochu and soju (DR-G1-4), makgeolli (DR-E3-2), huangjiu (DR-E3-4), sorghum and maize beers (DR-E2-1, DR-E2-2), palm wine (DR-F3-4), fruit wines (DR-F3-2), vermouth (DR-F2-4), and anise spirits (DR-G3-1). *Fix:* approve the memo's Q10 second pass, with at least a Japanese, a Korean and a Chinese retailer and the EU spirit-drink categories. Until then, list these families as named-but-unsourced kinds, so that no Culinary drink is left with no J7 target.

**J7-5 (low) Bottlers should use the C1 addition.** The `bottler_role` extension and the class "independent bottler" model a fact that C1 already covers: the domain E addition `built_by_maker_id` on lines. *Fix:* the named form or line's maker is the bottler or brand owner, and `built_by_maker_id` is the distillery (Jura 1975 by Signatory Vintage [S30]). The maker classes winery, négociant, distillery, brewery and cooperative become `supply_role` values (JC3).

**J7-6 (low) Critic scores belong to vintages.** Critic scores and Liv-ex prices attach to a vintage, which is a C3 variant, while C4(a) lets tier entries sit only on makers, lines and named forms. *Fix:* extend C4(a) for J to allow a variant as the `subject_id` of a tier entry (J7 vintages, J6 Cup of Excellence lots), with `scheme` and `date` as usual. Q9 (`variant_type` vintage and bottler edition) is then needed and reasonable.

**J7-7 (low) Ready-to-drink and hard seltzer.** J7.7 holds one kind, "Mixed drink (blanddryck)" [S20]. Hard seltzer (fermented sugar or malt base with flavour) is unattested, and so is the canned spritzer. *Fix:* keep J7.7 with `leaf = null`, and add kinds only when they are sourced. Precedence 1 already separates a sealed ready-to-drink drink (J7) from a cocktail made to serve (Culinary DR-I).
