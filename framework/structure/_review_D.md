# Stage 2 domain review: D Electronics (D1 to D5)

*Batch S2R_D. Cold, offline review (no web, no git) of memos framework/structure/D1.md to D5.md and their sources files verify/out/S2_D1_sources.tsv to S2_D5_sources.tsv, against BRIEF.md, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md and the draft conventions C1 to C13 (framework/structure/_conventions.md). The conventions were written after these memos, so each memo is also given the list of changes it needs to conform. This is the reviewer's judgement, not an owner decision. Severity: **high** = breaks a shared rule, or would file the same object in two places, across many objects; **medium** = one rule break, one contradiction or a clear split; **low** = wording, evidence or tidy-up.*

**Mechanical checks.** Every memo's URL list matches its TSV row for row (37, 40, 35, 37 and 40 URLs). In D1, D2, D3 and D5 the key S01, S02 ... is the TSV row number. **In D4 it is not:** 31 of the 37 keys point to a different TSV row (memo S02 is B&H Lenses; TSV row 2 is kakaku.com/camera/), see D4-10. All five memos recommend four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form) and use the C5(a) level names. All five use "role of the object" as the sub-family axis. None divides by content genre, and D5 rejects the Geizhals PC-games genre split in so many words. Every memo marks the claims it took from search excerpts (B&H home page and Foto Erhardt home page in D4, the "tuner-less TV" type in D3, Canon L and Sony G Master in D4) and none of those claims carries a structural choice. The TSV notes mark six fetches as thin or title-only (D1 S18 and S19; D3 S07; D4 TSV rows 6, 14 and 23; D5 S19). I list them below only where a choice depends on them.

## 2. Findings by memo

Each finding gives severity, memo, evidence and an exact proposed fix. "Conformance" lines list the changes each memo needs for C1 to C13; the shared fixes they point to are in section 3 (X-1 to X-13).

### D1 Computers & Components

**D1-1 (medium) The tier ledger holds things C4 does not call tiers.** Section 5 groups the tier scales as "maker class (Core i-numbers, model ladders), standard grade (80 PLUS, LAMBDA, NAND type, PCIe and DDR generation), benchmark rank (hierarchies, Chinese ladders), community tier (Cultists A to F)". Under C4(b) a spec ladder measured on the object is an attribute with its standard named, not a tier entry. A maker's class ladder is a property of the maker's lines (C1 lines table). *Fix:* (a) move to attributes, each with its standard: `psu_efficiency` (80 PLUS, CLEAResult [S15]), `psu_noise_grade` (Cybenetics LAMBDA [S16]), `nand_type`, `pcie_gen` (PCI-SIG [S32]), `ddr_gen` and `speed_grade` (JEDEC [S33]); (b) record Core Ultra 5/7/9, Core i3 to i9, Ryzen 3 to 9 and RTX xx50 to xx90 as lines in the C1 lines table with a `line_rank`; (c) keep as tier entries (C4(a)) only the Tom's Hardware hierarchies [S17, S18], the two Chinese ladders [S20, S21] and the Cultists PSU tiers [S14], under the shared scheme types of X-3. D1's extra tier columns `direction` and `scope` ("850 to 1000 W") are good; X-3 proposes them for every category.

**D1-2 (medium) A per-listing condition ledger files individual items.** Section 5 and section 10 propose a "condition ledger per listing". BRIEF puts individual items out ("a particular card ... are out"), and condition is an Appendix A modifier. *Fix:* delete the condition ledger. Keep the eBay, Back Market and Overclockers scales [S09, S36, S06] only as rows of the shared condition-scale reference table (X-4), which holds scales and never items.

**D1-3 (medium) GPU partner cards have no defined place.** Section 4b: "partner card lines are variants linked in the maker ledger". C3 variant types do not include a board partner's build, and C1 keeps variants in their own table, not in the maker ledger. Objects that would split: ASUS ROG Astral RTX 5090 OC [S05] (named form, variant or maker-ledger row?). *Fix:* the named form is the GPU model (`maker_bound: yes`, maker NVIDIA, AMD or Intel); each partner card is a C3 variant row with `variant_type = partner build` and `partner = ASUS` (C3 already has the `partner` column); partner lines (ROG Astral, TUF Gaming) are rows in the C1 lines table under the partner maker. The same rule covers motherboards only where a chip maker's reference board exists; otherwise board models are the board maker's own named forms, as now.

**D1-4 (medium) No home for S3 parts and several key-named objects.** S3's own example is "printer ink to Computers"; section 9 of the memo files "a laptop's own AC adapter or battery" and "ink, toner and 3D-printer filament" in D1; S3 makes a laptop sleeve made for one laptop a part; section 4 names "fan controllers" as a narrow leaf. None has a kind in the worked tree. Objects that would split or have no home: inkjet cartridge, toner, PLA filament, laptop charger, laptop battery, laptop sleeve, monitor arm, laptop cooler (listed by Kakaku [S11]), M.2 heatsink, fan or RGB controller, docking station, external GPU enclosure, PCIe riser cable, powerline adapter, SD card reader. *Fix:* add sub-family **D1.9 Parts and accessories made for one computer or peripheral** (X-10), leaf null (C5(b)), with kinds ink cartridge, toner cartridge, 3D-printer filament, laptop AC adapter, laptop battery, laptop sleeve or case, monitor arm or stand, laptop cooler. Add to D1.2 Cooling "M.2 heatsink" and "fan or lighting controller"; to D1.2 Graphics "external GPU enclosure"; to D1.6 "docking station" (separate from "adapter or hub" because it carries power delivery and several outputs; attest it in the sourcing batch); to D1.3 "memory-card reader". The printer, scanner, multifunction printer, 3D printer, barcode scanner and speech-microphone kinds wait for the sourcing batch the memo already asks for (owner question 8).

**D1-5 (low) D1.7 is named by market.** "Business and security hardware" names a market (Appendix A: domestic, commercial, professional). *Fix:* rename **D1.7 Transaction and authentication hardware** (point-of-sale terminal, payment card reader, cash register, hardware wallet, security key). "Card reader" is a homonym (payment card reader in the key's POS list; memory-card reader in storage), so add both kinds and two C9 alias rows.

**D1-6 (medium) Form factor is an attribute in one leaf and a kind in another, and SSD vs HDD needs its C10 argument.** Section 4b makes board, case, PSU and M.2 form factors attributes "as the owner asked", but splits memory into DIMM and SODIMM kinds. Separately, internal SSD against internal HDD is a split by storage technology, and C10(b) admits it only if the technology changes the form or installation and a fetched source ranks them apart. *Fix:* state one rule in section 4b: "a form factor is a kind boundary where buyers never compare across it and one cannot replace the other in the same slot (DIMM, SODIMM); it is an attribute where buyers compare across it inside one kind (ATX and SFX PSUs on one tier list [S14]; ATX and Mini-ITX boards in one category [S03])". Keep internal SSD and internal HDD as kinds under C10(b): most SSDs are M.2 cards with no HDD equivalent [S26], and Tom's Hardware ranks SSDs in their own list [S19]. State that a 2.5-inch SATA SSD is an internal SSD.

**D1-7 (low) "Home computer" is named by era and rests on one source.** The kind is attested only by the museum [S35]. *Fix:* label it by form, "keyboard computer (computer built into its keyboard)", with an `era` tag, and look for a second source in the sourcing batch. Add one sentence for pocket computers that matches D2 section 9: a programmable pocket computer is D1; a PDA or organiser is D2.

**D1-8 (low) Usability: objects that would split.** Of 20 test objects (tower PC, mini PC, laptop, barebone, Ryzen CPU, ATX board, DDR5 kit, RTX card, SFX PSU, Mini-ITX case, AIO cooler, M.2 SSD, NAS, USB stick, TKL keyboard, loose switches, mouse, monitor, router, Raspberry Pi), two builders would agree on 17. They would split on: the NAS (D1.3 "Network storage" or D1.5 network equipment), the Arduino UNO Q (single-board computer or microcontroller board; the memo flags it [S37]) and a USB video capture dongle (the only capture kind is an expansion card). Outside the 20 they would also split on a pen display (pen tablet or monitor), a Thunderbolt dock, a PCIe riser cable (internal cable or graphics accessory, Geizhals [S05]), a compute accelerator card (flagged in the memo) and a Steam Deck (the memo's own section 9 sends handhelds to D5; say so in D1.1). *Fix:* add five sentences. NAS is storage (D1.3). A board that runs an operating system is a single-board computer, even with a microcontroller beside it. An external capture device is a "video capture device" kind next to the card, with `connection` as an attribute. A pen display is a pen tablet with `screen = yes`. An accelerator without display output is a graphics card with `display_outputs = 0`.

**D1-9 (low) Evidence.** The "one processor kind" argument leans on S18, which the TSV marks "fetched excerpt thin"; the Tsukumo and Overclockers evidence [S02, S06] carries it anyway. Switch-kind evidence is US and global only [S22 to S24]. Numeric keypad, trackball, floppy drive, video capture card and I/O card rest on Tsukumo alone [S02]; KVM switch rests on Kakaku alone [S11]. The Chinese evidence is two ladders (the memo flags this).

**D1 conformance list.** C1: map `role` to `maker_class` (chip designer and PSU platform builder are suppliers; house brand is retail own-label; drop "distributor", because Mouser is a source, not a maker); move `brand-of` to the maker-relations table (X-1); `maker-level tier` becomes C4 entries; `founding era` becomes `founded`. C2: add `maker_bound` to named forms (all D1 named forms are maker-bound; Holy Panda is maker-bound, Drop). C3: partner builds, VRAM and wattage configurations (X-5). C4: D1-1. C5(b): D1.5, D1.6, D1.7 and D1.8 hold 12 kinds or fewer, so store them with `leaf = null`. C5(d): D1.9 (X-10). C7: named-form order (X-6). C8: the gaming, overclocking, silent, SFF, homelab, aesthetic and retro labels need definition sources; silent and SFF are views over attributes, not tags (X-12). C9: card reader, video card = graphics card = グラフィックボード, M.2 vs NVMe, 天梯图 = performance ladder. C10: D1-6. C13: rename the platform ledger to the compatibility ledger and keep D1's "edges" as compatibility relations (X-2).

### D2 Phones, Tablets & Personal Devices

**D2-1 (high) Radios are divided by regional legal service.** Leaves D2.2.1 "Licence-free handheld radios" and D2.2.2 "Licensed handheld radios" hold the kinds FRS, PMR446, GMRS and Business (Part 90). Section 7 calls this an "exception" because "a radio certified for one is not authorised on the other" [S31]. These are regional standards. C11 says a region never names a sub-family or leaf, and C13 puts standards that set compatibility in the compatibility ledger. Licence status is also relative to country, so one radio can sit in both leaves (PMR446 is licence-free in Europe; GMRS needs a licence in the US). "Hobby radio (Funkgerät)" is a German retail umbrella [S11]; "hobby" is a motive, and the kind overlaps amateur and CB radio. Objects that would split: Baofeng UV-5R (amateur, often sold for GMRS), Motorola Talkabout (FRS, sold beside GMRS models), a Midland CB handheld. *Fix:* give D2.2 `leaf = null` (C5(b)) and kinds by form and mounting (C12): **handheld two-way radio (walkie-talkie)**; **vehicle-mounted two-way radio**; **base-station transceiver**; **pager**. The last three need the sourcing batch. Put the radio services (FRS, GMRS, PMR446, Part 90 business, amateur, CB, push-to-talk over cellular) in the compatibility ledger as `platform_type = radio service`, with `region` and `standard_ref` (FCC Part 95 [S29, S31]); each named form carries `radio_service`. Push-to-talk over cellular is a connectivity attribute (C10), not a kind.

**D2-2 (medium) "Rugged smartphone" is a spec, not a form.** Kind 5 is defined by IP and MIL-STD-810 certification [S04, S17], which C4(b) makes attributes. A rugged bar phone would sit in two kinds, and the kind has no attested named form. *Fix:* drop kind 5. Add attributes `ip_rating` (IEC 60529 [S22]) and `mil_std_810`, a `rugged` tag, and a generated view "rugged phones". Section 7's row then reads "rugged: view over IP and MIL-STD attributes".

**D2-3 (medium) Kind 20 contradicts the key.** "Tablet with detachable keyboard" rests only on a Wikipedia template entry [S35]. The step-24 note sends a device whose keyboard comes with it to D1, and a tablet with an optional keyboard to D2. *Fix:* drop kind 20 (the memo already offers this). Add the sentence "a tablet sold without a keyboard is a slate tablet; a keyboard cover sold for one tablet line is a D2 part (S3); a device sold with its keyboard is D1".

**D2-4 (medium) E-reader kinds split by size, display technology and operating system.** Kind 22 "Large-format document reader, 9 in and up" is by size (C6). Kind 24 "LCD e-reader" is by display technology (C10). Kind 23 "E-ink Android tablet" is by platform (C13). Note-taking e-paper tablets (reMarkable, Kindle Scribe) have no kind at all. Objects that would split: Kindle Scribe (large-format reader or e-ink tablet?), Boox Note Air4 C [S39], Boox Palma, Kobo Vox [S27]. *Fix:* kinds **e-reader** (made mainly to read: size, `panel` (Carta, Kaleido, LCD) and `platform` (Kindle, Kobo, Android) are attributes) and **e-paper writing tablet** (sold for stylus note-taking; attest it in the sourcing batch; Boox Note Air4 C [S39] is a first named form). An LCD device sold as an e-reader is an e-reader with `panel = LCD`.

**D2-5 (medium) Power-bank kinds rest on attributes.** "Built-in-cable power bank" (a feature) and "Laptop-class power bank" (output and capacity, C6 and C4(b)) are attributes. "Phone battery case" is made for one phone model, so it is a part (S3). *Fix:* give D2.7 `leaf = null`; kinds **power bank** and **magnetic power bank**. Keep the magnetic one only with the C10(b) argument: it attaches to the phone body and follows the MagSafe or Qi2 standard, and it needs a second source beyond idealo [S13]. Attributes: `capacity_wh` (FAA 100 Wh band [S21]), `output_w`, `built_in_cable`. Move the battery case to D2.8 (D2-8). "Laptop-class" becomes a view (`output_w` at or above an owner-set threshold).

**D2-6 (medium) A tracker kind named by user, and the pet case misses step 13.** Kind 41 "GPS or cellular tracker for vehicle, child or pet" is defined by user (C6: user is a tag). Step 13 (animal equipment) comes before step 24, so a tracker made to clip onto a pet's collar is B10. The memo's "B10 only if sold as pet equipment" points the same way but needs the key reason. Review B lists the GPS pet tracker as an open split (_review_B.md, B10 row). *Fix:* kind **GPS or cellular tracker**, with `user` tags. Precedence: made to attach to an animal → B10 (step 13); made to be fitted to a vehicle → G1 under S3 (X-11); otherwise D2.

**D2-7 (medium) The bike computer misses step 8, and a leaf is named by activity.** Section 9.6 files golf GPS devices and bike computers in D2 "by key order (step 24 before step 26)". A bike computer is made to be fitted to a bicycle, and step 8 (S3) is tried before step 24, so it is G1. Leaf D2.6.4 "Sport navigation devices" is named by activity. *Fix:* merge D2.6.4 into D2.6.1, renamed "GPS units" (kinds: handheld GPS, handheld GPS with two-way radio, golf GPS device). Send the bike computer to G1 under S3, pending the shared vehicle rule (X-11, owner question O-10).

**D2-8 (medium) No parts sub-family.** S3 sends phone cases, screen protectors, watch straps, watch chargers, styluses (Apple Pencil, which depends on the iPad line [S40]), tablet keyboard covers, e-reader covers and battery cases to D2. Best Buy and idealo shelve them as siblings of the devices [S01, S07, S13]. The tree has no place for them. *Fix:* add **D2.8 Parts and accessories made for one phone, tablet or wearable** (X-10), `leaf = null`, with kinds phone case, screen protector, watch strap, device charging dock or puck, stylus, tablet keyboard cover, e-reader cover, phone battery case. Phone-only gimbals, selfie sticks, clip-on lenses and clip-on game controllers join it (X-11, against D4 and D5). SIM cards need a ruling: the step-1 note sends access cards straight to step 19 (F5) before step 8 is tried (S1b), so the recommendation is F5 (owner question O-27).

**D2-9 (medium) Market tier mixes four kinds of scale.** Section 5 stores `market_tier` from Alibaba price ranges [S06], the Kimovil Range [S17], AnTuTu monthly segments [S18, S19] and maker ladders (iPad base, mini, Air, Pro [S40]; Garmin Entry, Advanced, Premium [S09]). *Fix* (X-3): Alibaba's bands are price, so the C4(d) price band covers them and they are not a tier; Kimovil's Range is a `platform_tag` (C4(c)); AnTuTu rank and score are tier entries with `scheme_type = measurement`, `region = CN` (the monthly charts are China data) and a date; maker ladders are C1 lines with `line_rank`. Drop the free label `market_tier` unless it names one of these schemes. `update_years` stays a dated attribute.

**D2-10 (low) Wrist devices.** "GPS sports watch (provisional)" has no test that tells it from a smartwatch. Garmin, idealo and Wikipedia separate them, while Geizhals and Kakaku merge them [S08, S13, S38, S12, S37]. Objects that would split: Garmin Forerunner 965 (AMOLED screen, apps), Apple Watch Ultra 3. The screenless band rests on one maker page [S08]. *Fix:* recommend one kind, **smartwatch**, with a `multisport_gps` attribute and a generated "sport watches" view, unless the owner wants the split (owner question O-25). "Smart band" and "fitness tracker" are one kind with a C9 alias row. Attest the screenless band (Whoop, CIRQA) from a second source or fold it into smart band with `display = none`.

**D2-11 (low) Usability: objects that would split.** Of 20 test objects (iPhone, Galaxy Z Fold, Z Flip, Nokia feature phone, iPad, Kindle Paperwhite, Kindle Scribe, Apple Watch, Fitbit Charge, Oura Ring, Ray-Ban Meta glasses, eTrex, inReach Mini, PLB, AirTag, Anker slab power bank, MagSafe battery, Motorola FRS radio, Baofeng UV-5R, Garmin Edge), two builders would split on six: Kindle Scribe (D2-4), Ray-Ban Meta (D2 smart glasses, D3 audio glasses or D4 camera; X-13), Motorola FRS and Baofeng (D2-1), the MagSafe battery with cable (D2-5) and Garmin Edge (D2-7). Applying the fixes above removes all six.

**D2-12 (low) Evidence.** Many kinds rest on one fetched page: screenless band [S08, a maker page], large-format and LCD readers [S27], e-ink tablet [S39], electronic organiser [S35], cellular push-to-talk and PMR446 [S31], magnetic and built-in-cable power banks [S13, S15], PLB [S28]. The radio evidence is US only (S29, S31). The historic phone forms are better attested than the memo claims: GSMArena's form-factor facet lists bar, flip, slide and swivel [S04] beside Wikipedia [S05].

C2: add `maker_bound` (all named forms here are maker-bound). C3: storage, colour and regional models are variants with `variant_type` configuration, colourway and regional model (X-5). C4: D2-9. C5(b): D2.2, D2.3 and D2.7 store `leaf = null`. C5(d): D2.8. C6: D2-4, D2-5 and D2-6. C7: "newest first" goes to a view (X-6). C8: gaming, rugged, minimal and luxury labels need sources. C9: walkie-talkie, Handy, 活動量計 = activity tracker, smart band = fitness tracker, phablet (a size label, not a kind), 電子辞書 = electronic dictionary.
