## 0. Category

**Code and name.** G2 Property & Structures (domain G, Vehicles & Property). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_7.md`, category list and placement key step 4.

**Membership test (step 4).** "**Land**; a **building or other permanent structure made to be occupied, entered or enclosed** (house, flat, garage, barn, stable block, shed, garden room, summerhouse, carport, in-ground pool, pergola, gazebo, decking, patio); shipping containers and portable cabins kept in place as stores or rooms; a vehicle or vessel made to **stay in one place as a dwelling** (houseboat, static caravan, tiny house on wheels); a burial plot or niche, however it is held?"

**Notes bound to step 4.** A building is filed as a whole. Its elements and products, installed or not, are filed by what they are: structural, envelope and finishing products (bricks, timber, roofing, windows, doors, stairs, flooring, tiles, wallpaper, fixed plumbing and wiring, fencing, acoustic panels) are C8; kitchen and wardrobe cabinetry is B1; carpets, blinds and curtains are B2; baths, showers, basins, toilets and hot tubs are B7; installed machines, lamps, safes and alarms keep their own category. Greenhouses and polytunnels are C2. Animal housing that is not a building a person can stand in (hutches, coops, kennels, stalls inside a building) is B10. Play structures, including treehouses, are E1. Pop-up gazebos are B1. The v0.7 notes add (K-60 to K-67): test the object as made (S5); a hull with its own propulsion and steering made to navigate (narrowboat, Dutch barge, cruising or pontoon houseboat), and a trailer, caravan or motorhome made for road towing or driving, are G1, however long they are kept in one place and whether or not someone lives in them; a dwelling made to stay in one place is G2 (a floating home or house barge without propulsion, a static caravan, lodge or park home, a park model, the tiny house on wheels); a park model RV is G2; commercial, industrial and institutional buildings are G2 in one leaf of other buildings by function, and market never divides them; a unit with its own sink, toilet and entrance is a dwelling, otherwise a garden room; a sauna cabin that stands as a building is G2 and a portable sauna B7; tents, yurts and tipis are E2 as made, whatever platform they stand on, and a platform or deck built for them is G2; a pergola, gazebo or decking kit is filed by the structure it makes, boards sold loose are C8; a garage building or a parking bay held as land is G2, a rented bay or a permit is OUT.

**Precedence sentences that touch G2.**
- Step 1 (OUT): "a licence, tenancy, permit, rental, timeshare week or timeshare points giving the person a specific place or space for their own use that they do not hold as land, a building or a burial plot, such as an allotment, mooring or berth, parking space or permit, storage unit or dedicated desk"; human remains; "the urn or box that holds remains is filed as that object".
- Step 11 (G1): "trailers and horseboxes, touring caravans and motorhomes" are vehicles; "every boat, canoe, kayak, bicycle and aircraft is G1". Step 4 names "houseboat" for G2; the step-4 note (K-60) settles where the two steps meet: a houseboat with its own propulsion and steering made to navigate is G1, a moored houseboat without propulsion is G2 (section 9).
- Step 14 (F4): coffins and caskets, cremation urns for people or pets and grave markers are F4, so the plot or niche is G2 and what is placed in or on it is F4.
- Step 25 (E1): "treehouses, playhouses and children's play equipment". Step 29 (C2): greenhouses, polytunnels, above-ground and inflatable pools. Step 30 (B1): pop-up gazebos. Step 30 (B7): hot tubs and portable saunas.
- S3: parts follow the whole, but "building products (step 4's note)" are never parts; so G2 has no Parts-and-accessories sub-family (section 3, section 9).
- S5: test the object, not the motive. Every G2 boundary sentence in section 9 tests the object as made, never how one owner uses it.
- Appendix A: region and culture, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, market (domestic, commercial), installation, power source and technology, material, origin (natural, cultured), how acquired (bought ready-made, built from a kit, handmade), motive and condition never divide a level. The owner's pointer adds: tenure and region are modifiers.
- C16: certificates, condition, provenance and facts about one particular object are not stored. Most G2 objects are unique and sold by place, so G2 records **facets** (the dimensions buyers filter on), not values for one property (section 10).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects

- **Agree.** Every census and portal opens with the physical relation of the dwelling to neighbours: free-standing, joined to one neighbour, in a row, or a unit inside a larger building (ONS, ACS, Japan, idealista, Chinese encyclopedias, Zillow, realestate.com.au). Every land source divides land by use (LandWatch, LandSale, Vantage, REINZ, ALUM). Every burial source separates in-ground from above-ground and urn spaces from casket spaces. Every relocatable-dwelling source separates what can be moved from what stays (tinyhomesnest, Bawtry, YSN, Lake Life).
- **Split on bungalows.** Rightmove treats a bungalow as its own type and keeps it out of detached, semi-detached and terraced results; ONS puts bungalows inside "whole house or bungalow" and divides by attachment; SeLoger offers plain-pied as a filter. The same object is a type, a sub-type or an attribute.
- **Split on "townhouse" and "unit".** realestate.com.au: a townhouse shares walls under strata; REINZ: a separate dwelling on a multi-title site; Zillow: a single-family subtype sharing at least one wall. REINZ "Unit" is attached on a multi-title site, realestate.com.au "Unit" is grouped with Apartment. Same word, different objects (alias table, section 10).
- **Split on mobile and static dwellings.** ONS and ACS fold caravans and mobile structures into a single "mobile" bin; Rightmove lists Mobile Home, Park Home, Caravan and House Boat as four types; Zillow separates mobile home, manufactured home, motor home, tiny home, houseboat and floating home; the UK trade separates static caravan, lodge and park home by build standard and site licence.
- **Split on composite holdings.** LandSale and PGG Wrightson sell farms, estates and lifestyle blocks as one product that is land plus a dwelling plus buildings; Rightmove lists Smallholding and Equestrian Facility next to houses; no source offers the land and the house as two objects. PGG Wrightson sells "Bare Land" and "Lifestyle with Dwelling" side by side, so the presence of a dwelling is a filter, not a different kind of land.
- **Split on commercial buildings.** The census treats a flat "in a commercial building" as a home; SeLoger and Rightmove sell offices and shops beside homes; the heritage thesaurus makes Commercial and Industrial top concepts; the Chinese source rates office towers (Grade A, 5A). None of the sources fetched here for owner-held property treats them as a different domain.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Dwelling: how it sits against neighbours, then whether you own the ground.** ONS asks "What type of accommodation is this?" with detached, semi-detached, terraced first (https://www.ons.gov.uk/census/census2021dictionary/variablesbytopic/housingvariablescensus2021/accommodationtype); idealista opens with one family against several; realestate.com.au defines House, Townhouse and Apartment by walls shared and by title (https://help.realestate.com.au/hc/en-us/articles/900007221983-How-to-search-for-different-property-types). Title and tenure (freehold, strata, condominium) follow as a modifier.
2. **Relocatable dwellings: mobility and legal class before size.** "The category matters more than the square footage" (tinyhomesnest); YSN's advice is "if you want to travel, buy a cruising houseboat; if you ... never intend to move, a floating home may fit better". The sources choose by code and licence, but under C17 a class a jurisdiction assigns never makes a kind; the tree therefore keeps the physical forms the sources name and puts the standard and licence beside them (section 4b).
3. **Land: use.** LandWatch's Property Types and Land Uses facets, LandSale's types, Vantage Land's uses, REINZ and PGG Wrightson's farm types and ALUM's primary classes are all use classes; price, place and area are facets beside them, not the divider.
4. **Outbuildings: what the building is for.** Dunster House and Hornbach name the building by use (office, summerhouse, shed, garage, carport, sauna, bike garage); material and roof shape are filters.
5. **Burial: in-ground or above, then capacity.** PlotListings' own advice puts "burial or cremation" first, "above-ground or in-ground" second and "for how many people" third.
6. **Price and place** are the dominant facets on every portal (SUUMO, LandWatch, realestate.com.au) but they are modifiers here. Maker is never the first choice for houses or land.

The evidence supports: physical relation or use first, then attachment or capacity, then price and place as facets.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: category G2, then at most four levels below it (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form), fixed level names (C5a). G2 has **no Parts-and-accessories sub-family** (C5d): the step 4 note sends every element of a building to C8, B1, B2 or B7, so nothing is received under S3 (CV-17 names G2 among the categories with none). *Shed and garden-building accessories (Bunnings "Shed Accessories and Attachments") are building products (C8), storage fittings (B1) or tools (C1); nothing is received under S3.*

### 3A. What is held, then the purpose or form of each part (decided)

- **Sub-family axis:** *what kind of thing is held*: ground (**Land**), a home (**Dwellings**), another building or structure (**Other buildings & structures**), a place for the dead (**Burial places**).
- **Leaf axis, stated per sub-family (C5c):**
  - Land: **land cover and principal use** (building land; cropland; grassland; permanent crops; woodland; water and wetland; mixed holdings). Land is filed by its principal land use whether or not it carries buildings; dwellings and farm buildings on it are the facet `improvements`.
  - Dwellings: **what is owned against the building around it** (a whole house; a unit inside a building; a whole building of two or more dwellings; a dwelling kept in place that is not a building).
  - Other buildings & structures: **what the building or structure is for** (vehicle shelter; storage and work; rooms for people; animal and crop buildings; shade and surface structures; pools; other buildings by function, the one leaf for commercial, industrial and institutional buildings, K-62).
  - Burial places: **no leaf level** (C5b: about 8 to 20 kinds); kinds are ordered by structure and position (section 8).
- **Kind axis, stated per leaf:** Houses by attachment to neighbouring dwellings (detached, semi-detached, terraced); Flats and maisonettes by the number of levels in the unit (one, two or more); Multi-unit residential buildings: one kind; Dwellings kept in place by physical form (static caravan, lodge or park home, park model, tiny house on wheels, moored houseboat, floating home); Land by crop, cover or holding; outbuildings by use-named building; Pools: one kind, with construction method the attribute `construction` (G-17); Other buildings by function: by the function the building is made for (K-62).
- **Named form:** a form of a kind that many builders or traditions make (end-of-terrace, riad, Dutch barn, penthouse): `maker_bound: no`; or a maker's model of a factory-built object inside one kind (a lodge, park-home, shed, garden-building, pool-shell or houseboat model): `maker_bound: yes`, harvested without a cap on one attested row each (CV-7, C2(f)). A maker's range spanning several kinds is a C1 lines row (G2-O1; section 6).
- **Facets, not levels:** house form, storeys, construction, build history, tenure, floor position, units in building, improvements, plot area, land grade, standard met, propulsion, capacity, allocation (section 10, facet schema).
- Example (kinds in bold): Dwellings > Houses > **semi-detached house** > *semi-detached villa*; Other buildings & structures > Rooms for people > **garden office**; Burial places > **columbarium niche** > *locker-type niche*.
- **Where it breaks, and how each break is settled.** (1) Composite holdings: a farm, ranch or lifestyle block is land, a dwelling and buildings sold as one product. Settled by the principal-use rule (G-13): it is filed once under its principal land use, with `improvements` as a facet. (2) Bungalow: a `house_form` value and named forms (detached bungalow), not a kind, though Rightmove treats it as a type (G-14). (3) A houseboat that cruises under its own engine is a vessel to YSN; under the step-4 note (K-60) a hull with its own propulsion and steering made to navigate is G1, and only the moored houseboat without propulsion is G2. (4) A private mausoleum is both a burial place and a building a person can enter; it sits in Burial places because buyers choose it as an interment option (PlotListings), not in Other buildings. (5) Smallholding against mixed farm differed only by scale and management; G-13 merges them into one kind, mixed holding, with area and enterprises as attributes.

### 3B. Portal list (the retail pattern)

- **Sub-family axis:** the filter value a portal shows (House, Flat, Bungalow, Land, Park Home, Garage, Plot, Caravan, House Boat); a single flat list, as at Rightmove (68 types) and LandSale (100+ types).
- Example kinds (13): terraced, semi-detached, detached, flat, maisonette, penthouse, bungalow, plot, smallholding, park home, house boat, garages, castle.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) The axes mix attachment (terraced), layout (maisonette), storeys (bungalow), position (penthouse), use (garages), regime (park home), tradition (riad) and size (studio) in one list, so a reader cannot predict a type from a rule. (2) Bungalow overlaps Detached and Semi-detached by Rightmove's own admission. (3) A "cottage" or "villa" is a style, which Zillow says is separate from type ("house style is based on the architectural elements"). (4) It cannot place a burial plot, a pool or a pergola at all.

### 3C. Use class first (planning and land-use pattern)

- **Sub-family axis:** the use class of the property: residential, agricultural, commercial, industrial, recreational, conservation (ALUM's six primary classes, the FISH top concepts, Rightmove's residential/commercial split).
- **Leaf axis:** the type within the class (house, flat; arable, pasture; shop, office).
- Example kinds (12): detached house, flat, arable land, pasture, woodland, vineyard, warehouse, shop, office, workshop, stable, garage.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) Market (domestic against commercial) is a Modifier in Appendix A, and a flat "in a commercial building" is still a home to the census. (2) A farmhouse on arable land sits in two classes. (3) A garage is residential accessory or commercial lock-up depending on the lease. (4) The scheme is built for mapping parcels (ALUM says it is about land use information for users across Australia) and has no home for a static caravan, a pool or a grave.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds.** Rightmove lists 68 residential types, LandSale says "100+ property types", the REINZ glossary lists 8 rural and 8 residential types, Historic England lists about 30 farm building kinds and PlotListings seven interment options. After this pass folds styles, regional names, house forms, position, size and legal class into named forms or facets, the estimate for G2 is **about 90 to 150 kinds**: Land 12 to 25, Dwellings 12 to 20, Other buildings & structures 50 to 85 (the farm-building and garden-building long tail), Burial places 10 to 20. Non-residential buildings are admitted (K-62) as one leaf, other buildings by function, with a few kinds (about 5 to 10); finer functions (bank, pub, chapel and the like) are named forms there, so the former estimate of 30 to 60 extra kinds is withdrawn.
- **Named forms.** The attested set is a few hundred and is now where most of G2's variety sits: Rightmove's styles (cottage, villa, mews, chalet, finca, cortijo, longère, gîte, trulli), the house forms (château, cave house, barndominium, riad), the Spanish and Chinese attachment words, the farm-building list, the regional burial forms, the Seattle dwelling terms, and position and history forms (penthouse, loft, studio). Product-model forms (a given shed, garden-building, lodge, park-home, pool-shell or houseboat model) could run to thousands; under the owner's depth-and-breadth rule they are admitted as maker-bound named forms, one attested row each, with no cap (CV-7, C2(f)), and ranges spanning kinds are lines (G2-O1; section 6). Non-maker forms are likewise admitted on one fetched page each, uncapped.
- **Where depth concentrates.** House named forms (68 Rightmove types, attachment and style vocabularies in every language), rural land (REINZ, PGG, LandSale, LandWatch each with 8 to 25 values), farm buildings (about 30 heritage kinds), garden buildings (Dunster, Hornbach), and the maker models, ranges and maker ledger of factory-built objects (garden buildings, lodges and park homes, moored houseboats and floating homes, pool shells).
- **Broad, medium, narrow leaves.** Broad: animal and crop buildings, rooms for people, houses (by named forms). Medium: dwellings kept in place, shade and surface structures, other buildings by function. Narrow: flats and maisonettes (two kinds), multi-unit buildings (one kind), mixed holdings (one kind), pools (one kind), building land.
- **Skip rule (C5b).** Burial places skips its leaf level. Dwellings holds about 12 to 20 kinds and could skip too, but keeps its four leaves because each states a different kind axis (attachment, levels, physical form).

## 4b. Choice level

The variant rule for this category is decided (G-12, see CV-35): *"For objects that are unique and sold by place (land, buildings, burial places), a kind stops where the sources' search filters and census or survey categories stop; a legal or licence class alone never makes a kind."* The second clause is also required by C17.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Decided: preference, with spec-ranked facets where a standard exists.** No source fetched ranks houses, land or burial places in a recognised tier order. Where a measured or standard-based ladder exists, it is a facet with its standard named (C4(c) class (i)), not a tier entry and never a level; no value for one particular property is stored (C16). A scheme enters on one fetched source (C4(e), CV-10).

- **Houses and flats.** Price per place, size and condition are the facets (SUUMO, LandWatch, realestate.com.au); there is no maker or brand tier. Era, condition and location are modifiers.
- **Land.** The UK Agricultural Land Classification grades land quality, "the higher the grading, the better and more versatile" (https://www.vantageland.co.uk/land-use.php). This is a measured ladder, so it is the facet `alc_grade` with the standard named; the data records the scheme, not a grade for any parcel. LandWatch carries parcel size bands and PGG Wrightson carries land-area bands: facets.
- **Dwellings kept in place.** Build standards separate the forms in practice: BS EN 1647 (holiday caravan), BS 3632 (residential park home), HUD code (US manufactured home), RV industry standards (tiny house on wheels, park model), IRC Appendix Q (tiny house on a foundation) (https://bawtrylodgecountrypark.co.uk/guides/lodge-vs-static-caravan-vs-park-home/, https://tinyhomesnest.com/types-of-tiny-homes/). They are the facet `standard_met`, not tiers and not kinds (X-5). The holiday, leisure and residential site licences, the Mobile Homes Act regime and US manufactured-home titling are classes a jurisdiction assigns and go to the C17 jurisdiction ledger.
- **Commercial buildings.** The only consensus-ranked tier found is Grade A and 5A for Chinese office towers (https://www.sohu.com/a/235399555_99894660). Non-residential buildings are admitted (K-62), but a particular building is an individual item, so no grade is ever stored on a building (C16); the grading is recorded only as a scheme in the C4(c) registry, with `kinds_served` = office building.
- **Price band.** The domain-wide C4(d) bands now run above 15,000 USD to "over 5,000,000" (CV-14: 15,000–50,000; 50,000–150,000; 150,000–500,000; 500,000–1,500,000; 1,500,000–5,000,000; over 5,000,000 USD). Almost every house, plot of rural land, houseboat and private mausoleum is above the old ceiling (SUUMO lists houses and land at tens to hundreds of millions of yen, YSN puts new luxury houseboats at $600,000 to $1,200,000+, U.S. News puts floating homes up to $1 million, and PlotListings puts a private mausoleum at $50,000 to $500,000+). Houses, plots and graves are unique and carry no list price that the data could hold (C16), so **price bands attach only to maker, line and maker-bound named-form rows for factory-built objects** (garden buildings, lodges and park homes, moored houseboats and floating homes, pool shells), as C4(d) states for G2. The memo's G2-only extension stays withdrawn.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Decided: incidental for land, houses, flats and burial places; identity-lite for factory-built objects, recorded in depth.** The maker is part of what is bought only where the object is a manufactured product; there, under the owner's depth-and-breadth rule, makers, lines and maker models are harvested without a cap.

- **Incidental.** Land, houses, flats and grave spaces are unique and sold by place. A builder, developer or architect may be named on a listing but buyers do not compare makers (Rightmove, SUUMO, LandWatch, realestate.com.au filters have no maker field).
- **Product makers (identity-lite).** Garden buildings are bought from kit makers and retailers (Dunster House, Hornbach, Bunnings are named on the pages fetched); pool shells from manufacturers who "sell through independent dealers" (Thursday Pools, https://thursdaypools.com/inground-pools); houseboats from builders (Stardust, Sumerset, Gibson, Fantasy, Lakeview, per YSN; their cruising houseboats are G1 under K-60, their moored houseboats G2, in the same ledger row); park homes and lodges from park-home makers (Prestige is named only on a search page, not fetched †). Buyers here compare the maker's range and models, which are lines and maker-bound named forms, never kinds.
- **Platform.** None. Build standards (BS 3632, EN 1647, HUD, IRC Appendix Q) are design standards, recorded as the `standard_met` facet, not a compatibility ledger (C13 is not needed for G2).
- **Maker ledger (C1).** G2 makers go in the one domain-wide maker ledger (CV-2), seeded from attested pages and existing catalogues and grown without a cap (CV-5; one fetched page admits a maker), covering every factory-built kind (dwellings kept in place, garden buildings and other kit-built outbuildings, pool shells, moored houseboats and floating homes); a maker's kinds may cross G1 and G2, and `known_for_kinds` is derived from the named-form ledger, not stored (CV-1). Core columns as in CONVENTIONS_v1 C1 (`maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `licensor_id`, `authority_id`, `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`). No G2 extension columns are needed. `maker_class` is multi-valued, from the merged C1 list: **house or manufacture** (pool-shell makers, park-home makers, garden-building makers), **kit supplier** (Dunster House and other kit makers, together with house or manufacture where they also manufacture), **builder** (houseboat and lodge builders that build to order or in small series), **converter** (a maker that builds on another maker's base, as in container or van conversions) and **retail own-label** (Bunnings and Hornbach own ranges). "Manufacturer-retailer" is not a class. House builders, developers, estate agents and cemeteries get no rows as makers of houses, land or graves, because no G2 kind or named form is compared by them (a cemetery is a place, not a maker); a builder that sells factory-built models enters like any other maker.
- **Lines table (C1).** Lodge, park-home, houseboat and pool-shell ranges are recorded as C1 lines rows, uncapped (G2-O1), with `kinds`, `line_rank` (the maker's own order of its ranges, for generated ladder views), `built_by_maker_id`, `licensor_id`, `years` and `sources`; garden-building ranges spanning several kinds are lines rows too. A range inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (C2(d)).
- **Variant ledger (C3).** Available but not expected to be filled at first: a lodge, shed or pool-shell model is a maker-bound named form (C2(f)); its sizes are attributes (C6), and size, trim or generation variants go in the C3 schema wherever a community compares them (CV-8).

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states (C7).**
- Houses: detached, semi-detached, terraced (the order of decreasing separation from neighbours, as ONS prints it).
- Flats and maisonettes: flat, maisonette (by number of levels).
- Dwellings kept in place: by permanence, from least to most fixed (moored houseboat, tiny house on wheels, park model, static caravan, lodge or park home, floating home), because the permanence of site and utilities is what the sources use to separate them (Lake Life, tinyhomesnest, Bawtry).
- Land leaves: building land, cropland, grassland, permanent crops, woodland, water and wetland, mixed holdings; kinds within a leaf canonical first, then alphabetical.
- Other buildings & structures leaves: vehicle shelter, storage and work, rooms for people, animal and crop buildings, shade and surface structures, pools, other buildings by function; kinds canonical first (garage, garden shed, summerhouse, barn, gazebo, office building), then alphabetical.
- Burial places (no leaves): ground burial plot, cremation plot, lawn crypt, mausoleum crypt, private mausoleum, columbarium niche, green burial plot, communal grave space. The first seven follow PlotListings' own order of increasing price, with the cremation plot after the ground plot as its in-ground urn counterpart.
- **Named forms (one rule, C7 as amended by CV-21):** non-maker named forms first, by first-attested year where a fetched source dates the form (Wikipedia, the heritage pages, ayagawa's 1999 for jumokuso), then alphabetically; then maker-bound named forms (the factory-built models of section 6), by maker in ledger `name` order, then first-introduction year, then alphabetically. Maker ladders (`line_rank`) are generated views, not stored order. The former "by region, then alphabetical" order for houses is withdrawn; region is a tag and a view.

Reason: the sources print canonical forms first (ONS, ACS, PlotListings) and C7 asks for it.

## 9. Boundaries

Closest cross-boundary objects and the precedence sentence for each. Sentences marked (new) were proposals of this memo; those decided as step-4 notes in CATEGORIES_v0_7 (K-60 to K-67) are marked (key v0.7), and the rest stand as G2 rules under the decisions cited. Every sentence tests the object as made (S5).

**G1 Vehicles (the largest boundary).** Objects: houseboat, narrowboat, motorhome, touring caravan, tiny house on wheels, static caravan, park model, horsebox, shipping container on a trailer.
- *Test the object as made (S5). A hull with its own propulsion and steering made to navigate (narrowboat, Dutch barge, cruising or pontoon houseboat), and a trailer, caravan or motorhome made for road towing or driving, are G1, however long they are kept in one place and whether or not someone lives in them. A dwelling made to stay in one place is G2: a floating home or house barge without propulsion, a static caravan, lodge or park home, a park model, and the tiny house on wheels that step 4 names.* (key v0.7, K-60; it replaces the memo's former sentence "a houseboat is G2 whether or not it has an engine". The G2 kind is therefore the moored houseboat, and the G1 memo holds the cruising houseboat.)
- *A narrowboat is G1.* (K-60; it is made to navigate canals under its own power. G1 holds it, as a named form of the cruising houseboat in the G1 memo.)
- *A tiny house on wheels is G2, as step 4 names it, however often it is towed.* (key v0.7, K-60; S5 forbids testing how it is used.)
- *A park model RV is G2.* (key v0.7, K-61; RVIA lists it among RV types on the G1 side, and the G1 memo states the same line.)
- *A factory-built home set on a permanent foundation is a detached house with `construction` = manufactured or modular; one kept on its chassis or on a pitch is a static caravan or a lodge or park home.* (G-16; Zillow and tinyhomesnest separate the forms by how they are set.)
- *A shipping container kept in place as a store or room is G2; one on a trailer or used to ship goods is G1 or C8 by what it is made for.* (restates step 4)

**C8 Building Materials & Hardware.** Objects: timber, roof panels, a porch canopy, shed kit panels, decking boards, fencing, a pergola kit, shed accessories.
- *A pergola, gazebo or decking kit is filed by the structure it makes; boards sold loose are C8.* (key v0.7, K-66.) By the same rule a shed or cabin sold as a kit is G2 as the building, and timber, boards and canopies sold loose as parts of a building are C8 (Dunster House sells "Porches and Canopies" as a product, which the note sends to C8).
- *Decking and patio laid are G2; decking boards and paving stones are C8.* (key text, restated)
- *Shed and garden-building accessories are building products (C8), storage fittings (B1) or tools (C1); nothing is received under S3.* (G2 rule; CV-17 gives G2 no parts sub-family; Bunnings "Shed Accessories and Attachments")

**Dwellings against outbuildings.** Objects: garden annexe, ADU, guest house, garden office with a toilet, log cabin, integral garage.
- *A unit with its own sink, toilet and entrance is a dwelling; otherwise it is a garden room.* (key v0.7, K-63; the test comes from the Japan Housing and Land Survey definition of a dwelling.) A dwelling of this kind is a detached house, with annexe, ADU and guest house as named forms; a garden building without them is a garden room (G2.3).
- *"Log cabin" names two objects: a garden log cabin is G2.3; a log house that meets the dwelling test is a detached house with `construction` = log.* (G2 rule; Dunster against Rightmove and LandWatch; alias table)
- *An integral garage is part of the house, which is filed as a whole (step 4); a garage building of its own is G2.3, attached or freestanding (C12 installation facet).* (G2 rule)

**Inside Dwellings.** Objects: one half of a duplex, a whole duplex, a flat in a block, a whole block.
- *File what is owned: a whole building of two or more dwellings is a multi-unit residential building; one dwelling of a pair or row is a semi-detached or terraced house; one unit in a block is a flat or maisonette.* (G2 rule)
- *A unit whose rooms are on two or more full floors is a maisonette, whatever its position (penthouse) or history (loft conversion); one on a single floor is a flat.* (G-15)

**Inside Land.** Objects: a farm with a house, a vineyard with a winery, an estate with tenanted farms, a smallholding.
- *Land is filed by its principal land use, whether or not it carries buildings; the dwelling and farm buildings on it are the facet `improvements` (dwelling, farm buildings, none). A holding with no principal use is a mixed holding, with area (`plot_area`) and enterprises (`enterprises`) as attributes. A house with a garden is filed as the dwelling.* (G-13)
- The former working cut between smallholding and mixed farm is withdrawn: it rested on size and management, not the object (G-13). Smallholding, lifestyle block, lifestyle farm, hobby farm, homestead and mixed farm are names of the one kind mixed holding (alias table).

**C2 Garden & Grounds, E1 Toys & Games, B10 Animal Equipment, B1 Furniture & Storage.** Objects: greenhouse, polytunnel, above-ground pool, inflatable pool, treehouse, playhouse, hutch, kennel, chicken coop, dovecote, stable inside a barn, pop-up gazebo, garden storage box.
- *A greenhouse and an above-ground pool are C2, an in-ground pool and a stable block a person can stand in are G2, a treehouse and a playhouse are E1, a hutch, coop or kennel is B10, and a pop-up gazebo is B1.* (restates notes to steps 4, 25, 29, 30) Equipment and chemicals made for a swimming pool, pool heat pumps included, are C2 (K-176).
- *A dovecote or granary that is a building a person can stand in is G2; a bird box or feeder is B10.* (restates the step 4 note)
- *A garden storage box that holds several objects and stands outdoors is B1; a shed a person can enter is G2.* (G2 rule)

**B7 Climate, Water & Energy.** Objects: hot tub, portable sauna, outdoor sauna cabin (Hornbach lists "Aussensauna" with garden houses), bathroom fittings.
- *A sauna cabin that stands as a building is G2; a portable sauna is B7.* (key v0.7, K-64.) A hot tub is B7 (step 4 note; B-26).

**E2 Sports, Fitness & Recreation.** Objects: tent, yurt, tipi, a platform built for one.
- *Tents, yurts and tipis are E2 as made, whatever platform they stand on; a platform or deck built for them is G2.* (key v0.7, K-65; "set as a dwelling" would be a use test.) The platform is filed as decking (G2.3 Shade and surface structures).

**F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects, F5 Documents.** Objects: casket, cremation urn, headstone and grave marker, burial deed, cemetery plan.
- *The plot, crypt or niche is G2; the casket, urn, headstone and marker placed in or on it are F4; the deed or certificate is F5.* (restates step 14 and step 19; ayagawa shows the Japanese "ippan-bo" is a plot plus a stone, which therefore splits into G2 and F4.)
- *A burial place is G2 however it is held (owned, leased or held on a use right), but a mooring, berth, allotment or parking permit is a right and OUT.* (restates steps 1 and 4; G-18: "however it is held" covers German Reihengräber and Japanese perpetual-care graves.)

**Rights OUT (step 1).** Objects: tenancy, timeshare week, mooring, berth, parking permit, floating-home co-op share (Lake Life: floating-home slips are "usually owned (co-op or condo)").
- *A share or membership that grants only use of a slip, pitch or space is a right and OUT; the floating home, static caravan or lodge standing on it is G2.* (G-18)
- *A garage building or a parking bay held as land is G2; a rented bay or a permit is OUT.* (key v0.7, K-67; Rightmove sells Parking and Garages. A freehold parking bay is G2.1 Land; its kind is not settled † and is listed in section 10.)

**Non-residential buildings (admitted, K-62).**
- *Commercial, industrial and institutional buildings are G2, in one leaf of other buildings by function; market never divides them.* (key v0.7, K-62.) A shop, office building, warehouse, industrial unit or hotel is filed by what the building is made for, as a kind of the leaf "Other buildings by function" under Other buildings & structures; market (domestic, commercial) never makes a sub-family (FISH divides by function; Appendix A makes market a modifier). A flat in a commercial building is still a flat (census).

## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Final structure (3A, decided).** G2 Property & Structures has four sub-families by what is held, with at most four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; CV-16) and no Parts-and-accessories sub-family (CV-17): **G2.1 Land**, with leaves by land cover and principal use (building land, cropland, grassland, permanent crops, woodland, water and wetland, mixed holdings), land filed by principal use with buildings as the facet `improvements`, and one kind "mixed holding" for holdings without a principal use (G-13); **G2.2 Dwellings**, with leaves by what is owned against the building around it: houses by attachment (detached, semi-detached, terraced; bungalow, courtyard, cave, castle and barn-type are `house_form` values with non-maker named forms such as riad and château, G-14), flat and maisonette by levels (penthouse, loft, studio named forms, G-15), one multi-unit residential building kind, and dwellings kept in place by physical form (static caravan, lodge or park home, park model, tiny house on wheels, moored houseboat, floating home; standards as `standard_met`, licences in C17, a factory-built home on a permanent foundation filed as a house, G-16), with every navigating hull, the narrowboat included, in G1 (K-60, K-61); **G2.3 Other buildings & structures**, with leaves by what the building or structure is for (vehicle shelter; storage and work; rooms for people, sauna cabins included, K-64; animal and crop buildings; shade and surface structures, platforms for tents and yurts included, K-65; pools, one in-ground swimming pool kind with a `construction` attribute, G-17; and other buildings by function, the one leaf for commercial, industrial and institutional buildings, K-62), with the dwelling test of K-63 between an annexe and a garden room; and **G2.4 Burial places**, with no leaf level (C5b), kinds by interment structure, held however it is held (G-18). Kinds stop where search filters and census categories stop, and a legal class never makes a kind (G-12). Tier type: preference, with spec facets (`alc_grade`, `standard_met`). Makers: incidental for land, houses, flats and graves; for factory-built objects one domain-wide maker ledger (CV-2) with lines rows for lodge, park-home, houseboat and pool-shell ranges (G2-O1) and maker-bound named forms for models, all uncapped (CV-5, CV-7); price bands (CV-14) only on those rows. The worked example tree has 55 kinds; the estimate is about 90 to 150 kinds.

**Remaining risks.** (1) The pool kind rests on two US dealers; if a non-US seller splits fibreglass, vinyl-liner and gunite pools, they become three kinds (G-17, level only, provisional until attested). (2) Several house named forms (riad, trullo, finca, cortijo, longère, masia, casona, mews) sit under detached house by the default rule because no fetched page states their attachment; a page that does may move them to another attachment kind. (3) The cut between a static caravan and a lodge or park home (single section against twin-unit or wider) is not stated on any fetched page. (4) The moored houseboat and floating home are close forms; the line between them rests on Seattle and US sources (YSN, Lake Life, U.S. News). (5) Burial places may hold more than the "about 12 kinds or fewer" that C5(b) allows without a leaf level if regional grave forms prove to be kinds rather than named forms. (6) The few kinds of other buildings by function are mostly unattested; finer functions are held as named forms, which keeps the leaf small but may need review once commercial portals are harvested. (7) House builders and developers get no maker rows because no G2 kind or named form is compared by them; if stage 3 finds volume builders selling standard house types by name, those would be maker-bound named forms under the depth-and-breadth rule, and the maker rule would need a note rather than a cap.

**Facet schema (CONVENTIONS_v1 attributes under C16; replaces the former attribute list).** G2 objects are mostly unique, so the data stores, per kind, the facets buyers filter on, with the standard named, and **no values for any particular property**. A facet value may be stored on a kind or named form only where it holds for every object of that kind or form (riad: `house_form` = courtyard; penthouse: `floor_position` = top).
