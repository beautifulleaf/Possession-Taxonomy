## 0. Category

**Code and name.** G2 Property & Structures (domain G, Vehicles & Property). Source: `framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md`, category list and placement key step 4.

**Membership test (step 4).** "**Land**; a **building or other permanent structure made to be occupied, entered or enclosed** (house, flat, garage, barn, stable block, shed, garden room, summerhouse, carport, in-ground pool, pergola, gazebo, decking, patio); shipping containers and portable cabins kept in place as stores or rooms; a vehicle or vessel made to **stay in one place as a dwelling** (houseboat, static caravan, tiny house on wheels); a burial plot or niche, however it is held?"

**Notes bound to step 4.** A building is filed as a whole. Its elements and products, installed or not, are filed by what they are: structural, envelope and finishing products (bricks, timber, roofing, windows, doors, stairs, flooring, tiles, wallpaper, fixed plumbing and wiring, fencing, acoustic panels) are C8; kitchen and wardrobe cabinetry is B1; carpets, blinds and curtains are B2; baths, showers, basins, toilets and hot tubs are B7; installed machines, lamps, safes and alarms keep their own category. Greenhouses and polytunnels are C2. Animal housing that is not a building a person can stand in (hutches, coops, kennels, stalls inside a building) is B10. Play structures, including treehouses, are E1. Pop-up gazebos are B1.

**Precedence sentences that touch G2.**
- Step 1 (OUT): "a licence, tenancy, permit, rental, timeshare week or timeshare points giving the person a specific place or space for their own use that they do not hold as land, a building or a burial plot, such as an allotment, mooring or berth, parking space or permit, storage unit or dedicated desk"; human remains; "the urn or box that holds remains is filed as that object".
- Step 11 (G1): "trailers and horseboxes, touring caravans and motorhomes" are vehicles; "every boat, canoe, kayak, bicycle and aircraft is G1". Step 4 names "houseboat" for G2, so the two steps meet at the cruising houseboat (section 9, OWNER: O1).
- Step 14 (F4): coffins and caskets, cremation urns for people or pets and grave markers are F4, so the plot or niche is G2 and what is placed in or on it is F4.
- Step 25 (E1): "treehouses, playhouses and children's play equipment". Step 29 (C2): greenhouses, polytunnels, above-ground and inflatable pools. Step 30 (B1): pop-up gazebos. Step 30 (B7): hot tubs and portable saunas.
- S3: parts follow the whole, but "building products (step 4's note)" are never parts; so G2 has no Parts-and-accessories sub-family (section 3, section 9).
- S5: test the object, not the motive. Every G2 boundary sentence in section 9 tests the object as made, never how one owner uses it.
- Appendix A: region and culture, era, price and quality tier, brand and maker, market (domestic, commercial), installation, power source and technology, material, origin (natural, cultured), how acquired (bought ready-made, built from a kit, handmade), motive and condition never divide a level. The owner's pointer adds: tenure and region are modifiers.
- C16: certificates, condition, provenance and facts about one particular object are not stored. Most G2 objects are unique and sold by place, so G2 records **facets** (the dimensions buyers filter on), not values for one property (section 10).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split the same objects

- **Agree.** Every census and portal opens with the physical relation of the dwelling to neighbours: free-standing, joined to one neighbour, in a row, or a unit inside a larger building (ONS, ACS, Japan, idealista, Chinese encyclopedias, Zillow, realestate.com.au). Every land source divides land by use (LandWatch, LandSale, Vantage, REINZ, ALUM). Every burial source separates in-ground from above-ground and urn spaces from casket spaces. Every relocatable-dwelling source separates what can be moved from what stays (tinyhomesnest, Bawtry, YSN, Lake Life).
- **Split on bungalows.** Rightmove treats a bungalow as its own type and keeps it out of detached, semi-detached and terraced results; ONS puts bungalows inside "whole house or bungalow" and divides by attachment; SeLoger offers plain-pied as a filter. The same object is a type, a sub-type or an attribute.
- **Split on "townhouse" and "unit".** realestate.com.au: a townhouse shares walls under strata; REINZ: a separate dwelling on a multi-title site; Zillow: a single-family subtype sharing at least one wall. REINZ "Unit" is attached on a multi-title site, realestate.com.au "Unit" is grouped with Apartment. Same word, different objects (alias table, section 10).
- **Split on mobile and static dwellings.** ONS and ACS fold caravans and mobile structures into a single "mobile" bin; Rightmove lists Mobile Home, Park Home, Caravan and House Boat as four types; Zillow separates mobile home, manufactured home, motor home, tiny home, houseboat and floating home; the UK trade separates static caravan, lodge and park home by build standard and site licence.
- **Split on composite holdings.** LandSale and PGG Wrightson sell farms, estates and lifestyle blocks as one product that is land plus a dwelling plus buildings; Rightmove lists Smallholding and Equestrian Facility next to houses; no source offers the land and the house as two objects. PGG Wrightson sells "Bare Land" and "Lifestyle with Dwelling" side by side, so the presence of a dwelling is a filter, not a different kind of land.
- **Split on commercial buildings.** The census treats a flat "in a commercial building" as a home; SeLoger and Rightmove sell offices and shops beside homes; the heritage thesaurus makes Commercial and Industrial top concepts; the Chinese source rates office towers (Grade A, 5A). None of the sources fetched here for owner-held property treats them as a different domain.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Dwelling: how it sits against neighbours, then whether you own the ground.** ONS asks "What type of accommodation is this?" with detached, semi-detached, terraced first (https://www.ons.gov.uk/census/census2021dictionary/variablesbytopic/housingvariablescensus2021/accommodationtype); idealista opens with one family against several; realestate.com.au defines House, Townhouse and Apartment by walls shared and by title (https://help.realestate.com.au/hc/en-us/articles/900007221983-How-to-search-for-different-property-types). Title and tenure (freehold, strata, condominium) follow as a modifier.
2. **Relocatable dwellings: mobility and legal class before size.** "The category matters more than the square footage" (tinyhomesnest); YSN's advice is "if you want to travel, buy a cruising houseboat; if you ... never intend to move, a floating home may fit better". The sources choose by code and licence, but under C17 a class a jurisdiction assigns never makes a kind; the tree therefore keeps the physical forms the sources name and puts the standard and licence beside them (section 4b).
3. **Land: use.** LandWatch's Property Types and Land Uses facets, LandSale's types, Vantage Land's uses, REINZ and PGG Wrightson's farm types and ALUM's primary classes are all use classes; price, place and area are facets beside them, not the divider.
4. **Outbuildings: what the building is for.** Dunster House and Hornbach name the building by use (office, summerhouse, shed, garage, carport, sauna, bike garage); material and roof shape are filters.
5. **Burial: in-ground or above, then capacity.** PlotListings' own advice puts "burial or cremation" first, "above-ground or in-ground" second and "for how many people" third.
6. **Price and place** are the dominant facets on every portal (SUUMO, LandWatch, realestate.com.au) but they are modifiers here. Maker is never the first choice for houses or land.

The evidence supports: physical relation or use first, then attachment or capacity, then price and place as facets.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: category G2, then at most four levels below it (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form), fixed level names (C5a). G2 has **no Parts-and-accessories sub-family** (C5d): the step 4 note sends every element of a building to C8, B1, B2 or B7, so nothing is received under S3. *Shed and garden-building accessories (Bunnings "Shed Accessories and Attachments") are building products (C8), storage fittings (B1) or tools (C1); nothing is received under S3.*

### 3A. What is held, then the purpose or form of each part (recommended)

- **Sub-family axis:** *what kind of thing is held*: ground (**Land**), a home (**Dwellings**), another building or structure (**Other buildings & structures**), a place for the dead (**Burial places**).
- **Leaf axis, stated per sub-family (C5c):**
  - Land: **land cover and principal use** (building land; cropland; grassland; permanent crops; woodland; water and wetland; mixed holdings). Land is filed by its principal land use whether or not it carries buildings; dwellings and farm buildings on it are the facet `improvements`.
  - Dwellings: **what is owned against the building around it** (a whole house; a unit inside a building; a whole building of two or more dwellings; a dwelling kept in place that is not a building).
  - Other buildings & structures: **what the building or structure is for** (vehicle shelter; storage and work; rooms for people; animal and crop buildings; shade and surface structures; pools; and, only if the owner admits them, non-residential buildings by function: OWNER: O2).
  - Burial places: **no leaf level** (C5b: about 8 to 20 kinds); kinds are ordered by structure and position (section 8).
- **Kind axis, stated per leaf:** Houses by attachment to neighbouring dwellings (detached, semi-detached, terraced); Flats and maisonettes by the number of levels in the unit (one, two or more); Multi-unit residential buildings: one kind; Dwellings kept in place by physical form (static caravan, lodge or park home, park model, tiny house on wheels, houseboat, floating home); Land by crop, cover or holding; outbuildings by use-named building; Pools by construction method (OWNER: O13).
- **Named form:** a form of a kind that many builders or traditions make (end-of-terrace, riad, Dutch barn, penthouse): `maker_bound: no`. Maker-bound product lines are not expected beyond the factory-built objects (section 6).
- **Facets, not levels:** house form, storeys, construction, build history, tenure, floor position, units in building, improvements, plot area, land grade, standard met, propulsion, capacity, allocation (section 10, facet schema).
- Example (kinds in bold): Dwellings > Houses > **semi-detached house** > *semi-detached villa*; Other buildings & structures > Rooms for people > **garden office**; Burial places > **columbarium niche** > *locker-type niche*.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) Composite holdings: a farm, ranch or lifestyle block is land, a dwelling and buildings sold as one product. Resolved by the principal-use rule: it is filed once under its principal land use, with `improvements` as a facet (OWNER: O5 confirms). (2) Bungalow: a `house_form` value and named forms (detached bungalow), not a kind, though Rightmove treats it as a type (OWNER: O3 confirms). (3) A houseboat that cruises under its own engine is a vessel to YSN but a dwelling to the key (OWNER: O1). (4) A private mausoleum is both a burial place and a building a person can enter; it sits in Burial places because buyers choose it as an interment option (PlotListings), not in Other buildings. (5) Smallholding against mixed farm still differs by scale (section 9; OWNER: O14).

### 3B. Portal list (the retail pattern)

- **Sub-family axis:** the filter value a portal shows (House, Flat, Bungalow, Land, Park Home, Garage, Plot, Caravan, House Boat); a single flat list, as at Rightmove (68 types) and LandSale (100+ types).
- Example kinds (13): terraced, semi-detached, detached, flat, maisonette, penthouse, bungalow, plot, smallholding, park home, house boat, garages, castle.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) The axes mix attachment (terraced), layout (maisonette), storeys (bungalow), position (penthouse), use (garages), regime (park home), tradition (riad) and size (studio) in one list, so a reader cannot predict a type from a rule. (2) Bungalow overlaps Detached and Semi-detached by Rightmove's own admission. (3) A "cottage" or "villa" is a style, which Zillow says is separate from type ("house style is based on the architectural elements"). (4) It cannot place a burial plot, a pool or a pergola at all.

### 3C. Use class first (planning and land-use pattern)

- **Sub-family axis:** the use class of the property: residential, agricultural, commercial, industrial, recreational, conservation (ALUM's six primary classes, the FISH top concepts, Rightmove's residential/commercial split).
- **Leaf axis:** the type within the class (house, flat; arable, pasture; shop, office).
- Example kinds (12): detached house, flat, arable land, pasture, woodland, vineyard, warehouse, shop, office, workshop, stable, garage.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) Market (domestic against commercial) is a Modifier in Appendix A, and a flat "in a commercial building" is still a home to the census. (2) A farmhouse on arable land sits in two classes. (3) A garage is residential accessory or commercial lock-up depending on the lease. (4) The scheme is built for mapping parcels (ALUM says it is about land use information for users across Australia) and has no home for a static caravan, a pool or a grave.

### Worked example tree under 3A (53 kinds)

Kinds are **bold**; named forms are in *italics*. The tree is a check on the structure, not the full kind list. Every name appears on a fetched page unless marked †.

- **G2.1 Land** (9 kinds; leaf axis: land cover and principal use)
  - Building land: **building plot** (Rightmove "Plot", LandSale "Building Plot", LandWatch "Homesite")
  - Cropland: **arable land** (Vantage, REINZ "Arable", PGG "Cropping")
  - Grassland: **grazing land** (REINZ, LandSale "Grazing Land", LandWatch "Pasture"; *paddock* (Vantage Land); *ranch* (LandWatch "Farms and Ranches", `region` = US); *drystock country* (REINZ, `region` = NZ))
  - Permanent crops: **orchard** (LandWatch, LandSale); **vineyard** (LandWatch, LandSale, PGG "Viticulture")
  - Woodland: **woodland** (Vantage, LandSale "Timberland", REINZ and PGG "Forestry" as aliases; facet `managed_for`: timber, amenity, conservation)

**Rules that keep one home (all in section 9).** File what is owned (whole house, unit, whole building). Land by principal use, buildings as `improvements`. ## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds.** Rightmove lists 68 residential types, LandSale says "100+ property types", the REINZ glossary lists 8 rural and 8 residential types, Historic England lists about 30 farm building kinds and PlotListings seven interment options. After this pass folds styles, regional names, house forms, position, size and legal class into named forms or facets, the estimate for G2 is **about 90 to 150 kinds**: Land 12 to 25, Dwellings 12 to 20, Other buildings & structures 50 to 85 (the farm-building and garden-building long tail), Burial places 10 to 20, with non-residential buildings (OWNER: O2) adding 30 to 60 if the owner admits them.
- **Named forms.** The attested set is a few hundred and is now where most of G2's variety sits: Rightmove's styles (cottage, villa, mews, chalet, finca, cortijo, longère, gîte, trulli), the house forms (château, cave house, barndominium, riad), the Spanish and Chinese attachment words, the farm-building list, the regional burial forms, the Seattle dwelling terms, and position and history forms (penthouse, loft, studio). Product-model forms (a given shed or lodge model) could run to thousands if admitted. Recommendation: admit only attested iconic or traditional forms; leave maker models to the maker ledger, and in practice leave them out (section 6).
- **Where depth concentrates.** House named forms (68 Rightmove types, attachment and style vocabularies in every language), rural land (REINZ, PGG, LandSale, LandWatch each with 8 to 25 values), farm buildings (about 30 heritage kinds), garden buildings (Dunster, Hornbach).
- **Broad, medium, narrow leaves.** Broad: animal and crop buildings, rooms for people, houses (by named forms). Medium: dwellings kept in place, mixed holdings, shade and surface structures. Narrow: flats and maisonettes (two kinds), multi-unit buildings (one kind), pools (three kinds or one), building land.
- **Skip rule (C5b).** Burial places skips its leaf level. Dwellings holds about 12 to 20 kinds and could skip too, but keeps its four leaves because each states a different kind axis (attachment, levels, physical form).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recommendation: preference, with spec-ranked facets where a standard exists.** No source fetched ranks houses, land or burial places in a recognised tier order. Where a measured or standard-based ladder exists, it is a facet with its standard named (C4b), not a tier entry and never a level; no value for one particular property is stored (C16).

- **Houses and flats.** Price per place, size and condition are the facets (SUUMO, LandWatch, realestate.com.au); there is no maker or brand tier. Era, condition and location are modifiers.
- **Land.** The UK Agricultural Land Classification grades land quality, "the higher the grading, the better and more versatile" (https://www.vantageland.co.uk/land-use.php). This is a measured ladder, so it is the facet `alc_grade` with the standard named; the data records the scheme, not a grade for any parcel. LandWatch carries parcel size bands and PGG Wrightson carries land-area bands: facets.
- **Dwellings kept in place.** Build standards separate the forms in practice: BS EN 1647 (holiday caravan), BS 3632 (residential park home), HUD code (US manufactured home), RV industry standards (tiny house on wheels, park model), IRC Appendix Q (tiny house on a foundation) (https://bawtrylodgecountrypark.co.uk/guides/lodge-vs-static-caravan-vs-park-home/, https://tinyhomesnest.com/types-of-tiny-homes/). They are the facet `standard_met`, not tiers and not kinds (X-5). The holiday, leisure and residential site licences, the Mobile Homes Act regime and US manufactured-home titling are classes a jurisdiction assigns and go to the C17 jurisdiction ledger.
- **Commercial buildings.** The only consensus-ranked tier found is Grade A and 5A for Chinese office towers (https://www.sohu.com/a/235399555_99894660). A particular building is an individual item, so no grade is ever stored on a building (C16). If the owner admits non-residential buildings (O2), the grading is recorded only as a scheme the category uses.
- **Price band.** The shared C4(d) bands stop at "over 15,000" USD, which almost every house, plot of rural land, houseboat and private mausoleum exceeds (SUUMO lists houses and land at tens to hundreds of millions of yen, YSN puts new luxury houseboats at $600,000 to $1,200,000+, U.S. News puts floating homes up to $1 million, and PlotListings puts a private mausoleum at $50,000 to $500,000+). Houses, plots and graves are unique and carry no list price that the data could hold (C16), so **price bands attach only to maker and line rows for factory-built objects** (garden buildings, lodges and park homes, houseboats, pool shells). The memo's G2-only extension is withdrawn in favour of the proposed domain-wide C4(d) amendment (15,000–50,000; 50,000–150,000; 150,000–500,000; 500,000–1,500,000; 1,500,000–5,000,000; over 5,000,000 USD). **OWNER: O15** (shared question: approve the amendment).

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommendation: incidental for land, houses, flats and burial places; identity-lite for factory-built objects.** The maker is part of what is bought only where the object is a manufactured product.

- **Incidental.** Land, houses, flats and grave spaces are unique and sold by place. A builder, developer or architect may be named on a listing but buyers do not compare makers (Rightmove, SUUMO, LandWatch, realestate.com.au filters have no maker field).
- **Product makers (identity-lite).** Garden buildings are bought from kit makers and retailers (Dunster House, Hornbach, Bunnings are named on the pages fetched); pool shells from manufacturers who "sell through independent dealers" (Thursday Pools, https://thursdaypools.com/inground-pools); houseboats from builders (Stardust, Sumerset, Gibson, Fantasy, Lakeview, per YSN); park homes and lodges from park-home makers (Prestige is named only on a search page, not fetched †). Buyers here compare the maker's range, but the range is a named form or a line, not a kind.
- **Platform.** None. Build standards (BS 3632, EN 1647, HUD, IRC Appendix Q) are design standards, recorded as the `standard_met` facet, not a compatibility ledger (C13 is not needed for G2).
- **Maker ledger (C1).** Recommend a **small** set of rows limited to the factory-built kinds (dwellings kept in place, garden buildings, pool shells, houseboats), in the one domain-wide maker ledger that the proposed C1 amendment describes, with `known_for_kinds` free to cross G1 and G2 (houseboat builders sit on both sides of O1). Core columns as in C1 (`maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags`, `price_band`, `sources`). No G2 extension columns are needed. Maker classes, from the C1 list: **house or manufacture** (Dunster House and other kit makers, pool-shell makers, park-home makers) and **retail own-label** (Bunnings and Hornbach own ranges). Houseboat and lodge builders that build to order or in small series would take the proposed class **builder** (OWNER: O16, shared C1 amendment; until then they are "house or manufacture"). The memo's former classes "manufacturer-retailer" (a sales channel) and "kit or product manufacturer" are dropped. Do not build rows for house builders, developers, estate agents or cemeteries (a cemetery is a place, not a maker).
- **Lines table (C1).** Not proposed. Lodge, park-home, houseboat and pool-shell ranges could be loaded as lines only if the owner wants brand depth here (OWNER: O17).
- **Variant ledger (C3).** Not needed for G2. A model code (a specific lodge or shed model) would be a named form if ever admitted, and nothing here justifies the depth.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Canonical form first, then the secondary key the sub-family states (C7).**
- Houses: detached, semi-detached, terraced (the order of decreasing separation from neighbours, as ONS prints it).
- Flats and maisonettes: flat, maisonette (by number of levels).
- Dwellings kept in place: by permanence, from least to most fixed (houseboat, tiny house on wheels, park model, static caravan, lodge or park home, floating home), because the permanence of site and utilities is what the sources use to separate them (Lake Life, tinyhomesnest, Bawtry).
- Land leaves: building land, cropland, grassland, permanent crops, woodland, water and wetland, mixed holdings; kinds within a leaf canonical first, then alphabetical.
- Other buildings & structures leaves: vehicle shelter, storage and work, rooms for people, animal and crop buildings, shade and surface structures, pools; kinds canonical first (garage, garden shed, summerhouse, barn, gazebo), then alphabetical.
- Burial places (no leaves): ground burial plot, cremation plot, lawn crypt, mausoleum crypt, private mausoleum, columbarium niche, green burial plot, communal grave space. The first seven follow PlotListings' own order of increasing price, with the cremation plot after the ground plot as its in-ground urn counterpart.
- **Named forms (one rule):** every G2 named form is `maker_bound: no`, so they go by first-attested year where a fetched source dates the form (Wikipedia, the heritage pages, ayagawa's 1999 for jumokuso), then alphabetically. This agrees with C7 for forms without a maker and with the proposed C7 amendment (non-maker forms first; OWNER: O18, shared). The former "by region, then alphabetical" order for houses is withdrawn; region is a tag and a view.

Reason: the sources print canonical forms first (ONS, ACS, PlotListings) and C7 asks for it.

## 9. Boundaries

Closest cross-boundary objects and the precedence sentence for each. Sentences marked (new) are proposals for the framework, not text of v0.6. Every sentence tests the object as made (S5).

**G1 Vehicles (the largest boundary).** Objects: houseboat, narrowboat, motorhome, touring caravan, tiny house on wheels, static caravan, park model, horsebox, shipping container on a trailer.
- *A vessel or vehicle made to stay in one place as a dwelling is G2; a vessel or vehicle made mainly to travel is G1, including a touring caravan, a motorhome and a boat made for cruising.* (restates step 4 and step 11)
- *A houseboat is G2 whether or not it has an engine, because the key names it; a boat made for cruising with cabin accommodation that is not made as a houseboat (a motor cruiser, a yacht) is G1.* (new; OWNER: O1. The review and the G1 memo's P4 propose instead: *"A hull with its own propulsion and steering made to navigate (narrowboat, Dutch barge, cruising or pontoon houseboat) is G1, however long it is kept in one place; a dwelling made to stay in one place, such as a floating home or house barge without propulsion, is G2."* The two memos disagree until the owner decides.) The memo's earlier wording "used mainly for cruising" tested use and is withdrawn.
- *A narrowboat is G1 (step 11, "every boat").* (new; it is made to navigate canals under its own power, and no reading of step 4 names it. OWNER: O1 asks the owner to confirm. G1 holds the kind.)
- *A tiny house on wheels is G2, as step 4 names it, however often it is towed.* (restates step 4; S5 forbids testing how it is used. The memo's earlier sentence, "G1 when it is a trailer used to travel", is withdrawn. OWNER: O9 confirms.)

- *A factory-built home set on a permanent foundation is a detached house with `construction` = manufactured or modular; one kept on its chassis or on a pitch is a static caravan or a lodge or park home.* (new; Zillow and tinyhomesnest separate the forms by how they are set; OWNER: O10 confirms.)
- *A shipping container kept in place as a store or room is G2; one on a trailer or used to ship goods is G1 or C8 by what it is made for.* (restates step 4)
