## 2. Findings by memo

Each finding gives severity, memo, evidence, the objects that would split, and an exact proposed fix. "Conforms to" names the convention the fix applies.

### F1 Media & Publications

**F1-1 (medium) Record kinds fuse diameter, speed and release type.**
- *Evidence.* The grooved-disc kinds are "12-inch LP record (33⅓ rpm)", "10-inch record", "7-inch single (45 rpm)", "12-inch single or maxi single (45 rpm)", "78 rpm shellac disc" and "flexi-disc". The memo itself reports that Discogs keeps Format as the carrier (Vinyl, Shellac, Flexi-disc, Lathe Cut), puts size and speed in the Description field, and states that "Album, EP and Single tags are independent of speed and media" (S01, S26). "LP" and "single" are release types; "10-inch" and "12-inch" are sizes; "78 rpm shellac" is a groove standard and a material. Three axes sit at one level.
- *Objects that would split.* A 10-inch shellac 78 (kind 41 or 44); a 12-inch 45 rpm audiophile album reissue (40 or 43); a 7-inch EP at 33⅓ (42, which says 45 rpm); a 10-inch vinyl LP (41, or 40 by "LP").
- *Fix.* Kinds by the groove standard, which decides the stylus and the playback deck: "microgroove vinyl record", "coarse-groove (78 rpm) record", "flexi-disc", "instantaneous disc (acetate or lathe cut)", "transcription disc (16-inch)". Diameter (7, 10, 12 inch), speed (33⅓, 45, 78) and release type (LP, EP, single, maxi single) become attributes, with Discogs' Format and Description fields as the attested standard. If the owner prefers diameter kinds because collectors shelve 7-inch singles apart, record that as a stated exception to C6 (see X11). Conforms to C5(c), C6.

**F1-2 (high) Japanese and comic formats have two homes, and the Franco-Belgian album has none.**
- *Evidence.* Kind 1 "Hardback book" lists "tankōbon A5, B5, B6 as the Japanese hardback trims [S04]", but the memo's own section 1.4 says ONIX B108 to B110 are "paperback size classes" (form detail). Kind 15 "Manga tankōbon" is a separate kind in F1.1.2. Kind 4 "Pocket paperback" lists "Japanese bunko A6 (B111)" as a named form, and kind 16 "Bunkoban (manga pocket reissue)" is a separate kind. Nothing says which leaf wins for a manga volume. The comics leaf rests on one US page for the edition ladder (CBR, S12) and on Japanese pages for manga; Fnac's BD department (S31) is cited only as a shelf name, and the bande dessinée album (hardcover, about A4, 46 to 64 pages) has no kind.
- *Objects that would split.* A Shueisha Jump Comics volume (kind 1 named form or kind 15); a manga bunko reissue (kind 4 named form or kind 16); a Tintin or Astérix album (kind 1 hardback or kind 12 collected hardcover); a Dark Horse softcover "omnibus" (kind 11, 13 or the book leaf).
- *Fix.* (a) Precedence: *"A comic or manga volume is filed in F1.1.2 whatever its binding or trim; F1.1.1 holds books that are not comics."* (b) Apply C11 inside F1.1.2: kinds by edition form, named generically: single-issue comic; collected softcover volume (named forms: US trade paperback, tankōbon, wide-ban); collected hardcover volume (named form: bande dessinée album, album cartonné); omnibus or compendium; pocket-size reissue (named form: bunkoban); premium large-format edition (named forms: kanzenban, sōshūhen, the Absolute and Gallery formats as maker lines, F1-6). Remove the tankōbon trims from kind 1. (c) Bunko and shinsho stay non-maker named forms of "pocket paperback" for prose only, region-tagged (C11). (d) Fetch a French or Belgian BD source and a second, non-US comics source before the ladder enters the data.

**F1-3 (medium) Cartographic kinds are split by content.**
- *Evidence.* Atlases are "reference atlas", "road atlas" and "street atlas" (by scope, S13); globes include "celestial globe" beside "desktop globe" and "floor globe". The memo's section 4b says maps stop "at physical form (folded, wall, booklet, tear-off)" and Replogle (S22) treats the depicted body as a "map style" filter. Scope and depicted body are what the map shows: content, which BRIEF and step 21 keep off the tree.
- *Objects that would split.* A 12-inch celestial desktop globe (37 or 39); an A–Z street atlas that also carries road maps (35 or 36); a moon globe (no kind).
- *Fix.* Atlas kinds by format, as for books: "bound atlas" (hard or soft cover) and "spiral-bound atlas", with `scope` (reference, road, street, thematic) as an attribute. Globe kinds by mount: "desktop globe", "floor globe", "hanging or suspended globe"; `body` (terrestrial, celestial, lunar, planetary), `map_style`, `illuminated` (C10) and diameter (C6) are attributes. A mini globe is a desktop globe with a size attribute. F1's owner questions 4 and 5 are then answered by the conventions.

**F1-4 (medium) Size classes are named forms and kinds.**
- *Evidence.* The named forms of kinds 3 and 4 are US trade (B102), UK trade (B106), UK A- and B-format (B104, B105), mass-market rack (B101), tall rack (B107) and Swedish pocket (B113, B114): trim sizes. Kind 20 "Digest-size magazine" differs from kind 19 only by trim (S27). Kind 1 offers folio, quarto and octavo as named forms (F1 question 6). C2(b) says sizes are attributes, not named forms; C6 says size is always an attribute with its standard. The memo's own choice-level evidence says "ONIX stops at BB and BC and puts the size classes in a second list" (S03, S04).
- *Fix.* Add `trim_class` with ONIX list 175 codes (B101 to B114) as the standard, and `format_size` with the ABAA height scale (S17, S18) for antiquarian books. Merge "digest-size magazine" into "magazine". Keep hardback, trade paperback and pocket paperback as kinds only if the owner accepts paper and binding, not trim, as what separates them (B&N S05: pulp paper, rack size); otherwise "paperback" is one kind with `trim_class`. Conforms to C2(b), C6.

**F1-5 (medium) A tier label sits on kinds, and pressing names are undecided.**
- *Evidence.* Section 5: quality "appears as a modifier 'fidelity class' or 'premium line' on the kind or named form". Section 4b calls HDCD and SHM-CD "named forms or pressing attributes"; the tree lists them as named forms of kind 48. C4(a) forbids a tier on a kind; C4(b) makes a measured spec an attribute with its standard named.
- *Fix.* Fidelity becomes attributes: `bit_depth`, `sample_rate`, `capacity` and `encoding` (HDCD is an encoding, C10) with the standard named where fetched (the CD and SACD specifications are not yet fetched; S24 gives the figures). SHM-CD is `pressing_material`. Record weight (180 g, S02) is an attribute. "Premium line" (Absolute, Omnibus, Masterworks) becomes C4 tier entries on the line row (F1-6), with CBR (S12) as the first source and a second region's source required by C4(e).

**F1-6 (medium) Maker ledger fields, lines and platforms.**
- *Evidence.* Section 6 proposes "maker, known-for kinds or lines, scene tags, tier, price band, country and era": the pre-convention field list. Edition lines span kinds: Marvel Masterworks appear in hardcover and softcover, Penguin Classics as pocket paperback, hardback and clothbound. Proprietary carriers are "already the platform; no separate maker level is needed", with no ledger.
- *Fix.* Use C1 core columns with maker_class values `publisher`, `imprint`, `record label`, `cartographic publisher` and `globe maker` (X7). A line that spans several kinds (Masterworks, Penguin Classics, Criterion Collection) is a C1 line row (review C's K4); a line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (C2(a)). Carrier systems become C13 compatibility rows (`platform_id`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`): Compact Disc (Philips and Sony), SACD, Blu-ray Disc (Blu-ray Disc Association), VHS (JVC), MiniDisc (Sony), Super 8 (Kodak), with D3 players and D4 projectors in `kinds_served`. Kinds stay generic and point to their row.

**F1-7 (medium) No parts sub-family.**
- *Evidence.* S3 sends to F1 every case, cover and stand made for one carrier: comic bags and backing boards, record inner and outer sleeves, CD jewel cases and replacement cases, slipcases and dust jackets sold alone, globe stands and meridians. The memo mentions only the globe stand (section 9, item 6).
- *Fix.* Add "F1.5 Parts and accessories made for one carrier" (C5(d), as extended by review C's K7), with leaves by the object served (book, comic, record, disc, globe). Boxes, crates and shelves that hold several items are B1.

**F1-8 (low) Evidence and attestation.**
- *Evidence.* "Fake book" is in the tree as kind 30 but "unattested on a fetched page" (S6 forbids it until attested). Flexi-disc (S01), zine (S27, a digest-size article that mentions UK fanzines in A5) and fine binding (S03 BG) each rest on one source. "Bound periodical volume" is placed in periodicals, while the cited HS note (S16 note 3) sends it to books. Periodicals have no retailer tree (section 10 says so). Several kind names are the memo's own groupings ("premium large-trim edition", "large-format manga reissue", "microcassette family").
- *Fix.* Hold the fake book and mook until a page is fetched. State that "bound periodical volume" departs from HS on purpose (one home with its issues), or move it to books as HS does. Fetch one periodical retailer or wholesaler tree (Japan 雑誌 and ムック, Presse-Grosso). Mark grouping names as such in the data (`label_source: memo`).

**F1-9 (low) "Fine-binding book (leather)" is a material kind.**
- *Fix.* Make `binding` (cloth, paper over boards, leather, fine binding) an attribute of the hardback kind; ONIX BG is cited as the attribute's code. Conforms to review C's K9.

**F1-10 (low) Boundaries that need the other side's sentence.**
- *Fix.* (a) Playaway-type players (F1 question 9): F1, because the work is published fixed on the device and the device plays only that work; D3 holds players of any content. (b) Pictorial and antique engraved maps: *"A map, including a single sheet from an atlas or a pictorial wall map, is F1; a print made as art that depicts a place is F2."* F2 needs the same sentence. (c) Tarot and oracle decks are F4 (step 14); a tarock pack for card play is E1 (review E). F1 should say both so that printed decks are not filed as books.

### F2 Art

**F2-1 (high) Ornamental figurines are under-built, and their makers have no ledger.**
- *Evidence.* Step 20 files "ornamental figurines of people and animals made in ceramic, glass, metal, stone, wood or resin, whatever the edition or price" in F2. The tree has one figurine kind, 47 "porcelain or ceramic figurine", under "Modelled and fired". Glass and crystal, resin, cast metal other than bronze, and carved figurines have no kind. Section 10 says "Ornamental figurines and animation cels have no retail or enthusiast page among the fetched sources" and "Lladró-type porcelain figurine makers, bronze foundries and poster printers are not evidenced". The maker ledger is designed for artists (role, ULAN). Yet figurines are where F2 has brand identity of the kind the owner asked for (manufactories and their numbered lines), and probably where many owned F2 objects sit by count (no fetched source measures this).
- *Objects that would split or have no home.* A Swarovski crystal figure; a Murano glass animal; a Willow Tree resin figure; a pewter or cold-painted metal figure; a Hummel (Goebel) figurine with its HUM number; a carved wooden or boxwood netsuke.
- *Fix.* Re-cut the sculpture sub-family by the material that makes the form (F2-6), so that figurines and sculpture share one axis: leaves Metal; Stone; Wood and other carved organic material; Ceramic (terracotta, earthenware and stoneware, porcelain and bone china); Glass and crystal; Resin, plastic and composite; Textile and soft; Found and mixed; Light. Kinds name the form within the material, with figurine and statue as forms where sources sell them apart (porcelain figurine, glass figurine, resin figurine, cast-metal figurine). Size and edition stay attributes (C6, Appendix A). Add the manufactory to the maker ledger (C1 maker_class `house or manufacture`), make its series C1 line rows (Lladró, Hummel, Royal Doulton figures, Swarovski Silver Crystal), and put model numbers (HUM, Lladró numbers) in the C3 variant ledger as `variant_code`. Fetch a figurine retailer or replacement dealer and a collectors' club before data entry. Owner decision: this structure, or a separate sub-family "Figurines" (section 5, question 20).

**F2-2 (medium) "Art poster" is a kind in a process axis.**
- *Evidence.* Kind 31 "art poster (offset-printed)" sits in the leaf "Photomechanical"; the leaf axis is "printing-process family". Posters are also made by stone lithography (the classic French posters), screenprint and inkjet. Kind 27 "offset lithograph" already exists under Planographic, and GA lists offset lithography as photomechanical, so offset work has two leaves.
- *Objects that would split.* A Mucha stone-lithograph poster (26 or 31); a screenprinted film or gig poster (28 or 31); an inkjet reproduction poster (30 or 31); an offset exhibition poster (27 or 31).
- *Fix.* Delete kind 31 and the Photomechanical leaf. A poster is filed by its process kind and carries `issue_form = poster` and `poster_size` (one-sheet, quad, B2) as attributes, with a generated "Posters" view. If the owner wants posters as their own kind (film-poster collecting is a market of its own), make "poster" one kind in a leaf "Posters and printed reproductions" whose axis is issue form, with process as an attribute and this precedence: *"A sheet printed in an open edition to be posted or sold as a poster is a poster, whatever the process."* Owner decision.

**F2-3 (medium) A use kind in the medium axis, and an edition leaf in the process axis.**
- *Evidence.* Drawing kind 17 "original illustration art" is defined by use (Christie's department), in a leaf whose axis is "drawing medium". The print leaf "Unique" holds only the monotype, which the memo admits "mixes the edition idea into a process axis". Kind 10 "mixed-media painting" overlaps kind 55 "collage".
- *Objects that would split.* An original comic page in pen and ink (16 or 17); a gouache book illustration (4 or 17); a painting with pasted paper (10 or 55).
- *Fix.* Delete kind 17; `use` (illustration, comic art, animation) becomes a tag with a view, and the animation cel is an open question for a kind under painting (acrylic on acetate) once a source is fetched. Rename the Unique leaf "Other and hybrid processes" (monotype, collagraph; chine-collé as an attribute). Precedence: *"A work whose image is built from pasted elements is a collage; a painting with added materials is mixed media."*

**F2-4 (medium) Aliases, brand processes and printers are named forms.**
- *Evidence.* Named forms include "giclée" (which the memo quotes Sotheby's as calling "a neologism used to market inkjet prints"), "serigraph" (a synonym for screenprint), "aquarelle" (watercolour), "C-print" (chromogenic print), and "Iris print; Piezograph and Imagegraph", which the memo says are printer and inkset trademarks (MTK-T, PCNY). "Monoprint" is a named form of monotype, but AAT lists monoprints and monotypes as separate nodes (AAT-PR).
- *Fix.* Move giclée, serigraph, aquarelle, C-print and Cibachrome (as the earlier name of Ilfochrome) to the C9 alias table. Iris, Piezograph and Imagegraph become a `printing_system` attribute (C10). Ilfochrome and Polaroid stay maker-bound named forms (`maker_bound: yes`, C2(a)). Monoprint becomes its own kind beside monotype, or an attribute, as AAT separates them.

**F2-5 (medium) Regional kind names (C11).**
- *Evidence.* Kind 6 is "nihonga"; kind 54 is "East Asian calligraphy", with the five scripts as named forms; section 7 mentions Arabic calligraphy but the tree has no home for it. C11 names a regional kind by its generic form, with the regional word as its name.
- *Fix.* "Mineral-pigment painting (nihonga)", with iwa-enogu in the alias table. "Brush and pen calligraphy" as the kind; East Asian script styles, Arabic calligraphy and Western calligraphy are named forms with a region tag; seal carving (篆刻) is a separate kind only if a fetched source sells it apart from calligraphy (ZY lists it as a class). Hanging scroll and handscroll stay format attributes, as F4 does for devotional scrolls (F4-1).

**F2-6 (medium) The sculpture leaves are the memo's own grouping.**
- *Evidence.* The leaves "Cast, Carved, Modelled and fired, Assembled and found, Soft and light" are not in any fetched source: AAT-SC is "sculpture by material", Singulart filters by type and material (SGL-SC), and HS97 ignores material. "Soft and light" joins two unrelated things; "welded steel" sits under Assembled; the memo itself warns that the build team "must not re-split a kind by material" while every kind is named by material.
- *Fix.* Use the material-family leaves of F2-1, which follow AAT-SC and SGL-SC and which the memo's own section 4b supports ("material is the form here"). Form (freestanding, relief, bust, wall object) stays an attribute (SGL-SC Types).

**F2-7 (low) Maker ledger and price bands.**
- *Fix.* C1 core columns, maker_class values `artist`, `print studio or atelier`, `print publisher`, `foundry`, `house or manufacture` (for figurines), multi-valued (X7), and the extension `authority_id` (ULAN). Known-for kinds are derived from named forms (C1). Seed with exemplars only, as the memo proposes. Use the C4(d) USD bands instead of Singulart's; the top band ("over 15,000") stays open.

**F2-8 (low) Evidence labels.**
- *Evidence.* Section 1.3 cites Mojarto's footer artists as fetched; section 6 calls the same fact "search excerpt only"; the TSV row says the footer list was fetched. Graphics Atlas' Timeline mode is from an excerpt. There is no enthusiast forum and no UK-only retailer (section 10 says so).
- *Fix.* Correct section 6. Fetch one printmaking or photography collector forum and one UK retailer before named forms are loaded.

**F2-9 (low) Boundaries that need a sentence.**
- *Fix.* (a) Taxidermy: Singulart lists taxidermy among sculpture materials (SGL-SC), and step 7 files taxidermy specimens in F6. *"A mount of one animal prepared as in life is F6, however it is presented; a work that combines animal parts with other materials into a composition is F2 (found and mixed)."* F6 needs the same sentence (F6-8). (b) Metal wall art and canvas prints (Kunstlager): pictures on a hung surface, F2, not B4. (c) Devotional images and photographs: see X3 and X4. (d) Antique map sheets: see F1-10(b).

### F3 Coins, Stamps, Medals & Trading Cards

**F3-1 (medium) The arcade token is in the tree and out of it.**
- *Evidence.* Leaf "Tokens for a service" lists "Arcade and game-machine token (S1; see section 9 for E1)". Section 9 (E1) says "casino and poker chips and tokens made for a game or gambling machine are E1".
- *Fix.* Delete the kind. A token made to be used up in one coin-operated game is that game's consumable and goes to E1 under S3. Numista's arcade-token node (S1) stays an alias row pointing to E1.

**F3-2 (medium) Stamp kinds mix issue class and postal service.**
- *Evidence.* The adhesive-stamp kinds are definitive, commemorative, special-issue, airmail and postage-due. The first three are issue classes (S7, S9, S10); airmail and postage due are services (S9 groups them). "Special stamp" (特殊切手, 特种邮票) is a Japanese and Chinese class that overlaps "commemorative" in other postal systems (S7, S9).
- *Objects that would split.* A commemorative airmail stamp; a Christmas stamp (US "special", UK "special", Japan "greeting"); a Chinese T-series stamp (special or commemorative); an official or semi-postal stamp (not placed).
- *Fix.* Two kinds by issue class: "definitive stamp" and "commemorative or special-issue stamp", with `issue_class` (commemorative, special, greeting) as an attribute in the issuer's own terms. `postal_service` (ordinary, airmail, postage due, official, semi-postal, express) becomes an attribute. Keep the stamp units (miniature sheet, booklet), stationery, covers and revenue stamps as the memo has them.

**F3-3 (medium) Trophy kinds include a material.**
- *Evidence.* The trophy kinds are cup, shield, plaque, figure or sculpture trophy, "crystal or acrylic award" and silverware bowl, tankard or salver (S17, S18). Crystal and acrylic are materials; the same retailers sell crystal cups and acrylic plaques.
- *Objects that would split.* A crystal cup; an acrylic plaque; a glass star award (crystal or figure).
- *Fix.* Kinds by form: cup trophy; shield; plaque; upright award (column, figure, star, block or obelisk on a base); bowl, tankard or salver; rosette or ribbon (S31 names award ribbons). `material` (metal, crystal, glass, acrylic, resin, wood) is an attribute. Championship belts and rings wait for the owner (framework conflict list).

**F3-4 (medium) The bullion-or-coin rule tests the market.**
- *Evidence.* Owner question 4 proposes "a coin struck to be priced by its metal weight ... is Bullion; a proof, commemorative or collector coin sold on its design and grade is Coins". How a coin is priced is the seller's market, which S5 and Appendix A (motive: investment) keep out of placement. The memo itself shows the same design in both worlds (Panda: S9; Britannia: S5).

### F4 Religious & Ceremonial Objects

**F4-1 (high) Generic and tradition-named kinds sit side by side (C11).**
- *Evidence.* The memo keeps "tradition-bound kinds ... as kinds because they are generic types in their own tradition". C11 (which refines C2(c)) says a regional or traditional name is a kind only when no generic kind already covers the form; where one exists, the regional name is a named form under it, tagged with the region. The memo's own evidence names the generic kinds: section 3.0 point 3 says "Prayer beads are the rosary, tasbih, mala and juzu", "the incense burner is the censer, the koro and the UNSPSC commodity", "the household shrine is the butsudan, the kamidana and the home temple", "the ritual bell is the altar bell, the ghanta, the singing bowl and the rin"; section 8 lists "generic kind (household altar, prayer beads, incense burner)" first in each leaf. Yet the tree has:
  - leaf "Prayer beads" with kinds rosary, tasbih, mala, juzu and no "prayer beads" kind;
  - "household altar", "butsudan" and "kamidana" as sibling kinds;
  - "incense burner", "censer" and "koro" as sibling kinds;
  - "altar bell", "rin" and "singing bowl" as sibling kinds (rin and singing bowl are both standing bowl bells);
  - "Buddhist statue", "saint statue" and "deity idol" as kinds split by the figure, although section 4b says "The *figure* is a tag (iconography)";
  - "thangka" (F4.1) and "devotional hanging scroll (kakejiku)" (F4.1) as two kinds of one form, a painted hanging scroll.
- *Objects that would split.* A Catholic rosary or a generic 108-bead mala (prayer beads or mala); a Shinto soreisha altar (household altar or kamidana or a butsudan named form); a koro on a butsudan (koro or incense burner); a rin (rin, altar bell or singing bowl); a Ganesha brass idol (deity idol or Buddhist statue); a Japanese Buddhist kakejiku of Amida (thangka-type or kakejiku).

  - prayer beads (named forms: rosary, chaplet, tasbih or misbaha, mala, juzu; rudraksha and bodhi seed become `material` variants under C3, because a named form cannot carry named forms);
  - household altar or shrine (named forms: butsudan, kamidana, soreisha, home mandir, home altar); butsudan styles (karaki, gold-leaf, modern, wall-hung) become a `style` attribute;
  - incense burner (named forms: koro, joss-stick pot, dhoop stand) beside a separate kind "swung censer (thurible)", because a censer hung on chains to be swung is a different form (AAT; S25);
  - standing bowl bell (named forms: rin, singing bowl), hand bell (named forms: altar bell, ghanta), slit drum (named form: mokugyo);
  - devotional statue, with iconography and tradition as tags;
  - devotional painting on panel (named form: icon) and devotional scroll painting (named forms: thangka, kakejiku, pata).
- *Owner decision.* C2(c) allows a traditional type to be a kind when a fetched source sells it as its own category, and Hasegawa (S16) sells butsudan in nine classes. If the owner wants butsudan depth, record "butsudan" as a stated C2(c) exception with its styles as named forms; otherwise apply C11 as above.

## 4. Cross-memo consistency

### 4.1 Boundaries where the memos disagree or only one side speaks

**X1 (high) Religious medals: F3 and F4 give opposite answers.**
- *Evidence.* F3 section 9: "an object made for use in a rite, such as a blessed devotional medal, is F4 ... a historical or commemorative medal that merely has a religious subject is F3." F4 section 9, item 8: "a devotional medal on a chain is A3; a loose commemorative or religious medal is F3 ... a devotional medal is a kind only if worn as an ornament". A loose Miraculous Medal is F4 to F3's memo and F3 to F4's memo. "Blessed" is a motive and a ritual act, not a property of the object (S5).
- *Proposed sentence (both memos):* *"A medal made for devotion (a saint's or scapular medal, a Miraculous Medal) is F4 under personal devotion aids when loose, and A3 when it is made with a chain or bail to be worn; a medal struck to commemorate or to reward, whatever its subject, is F3."* F4 adds the kind "devotional medal" to F4.2.

**X2 (high) Cards, scrip and postal covers between F3 and F5 use motive tests and contradict each other.**
- *Evidence.* F3: "a live payment, transit or gift card is F5, while an obsolete token or scrip collected for itself is F3"; "any envelope or card kept for its stamps and postal markings [is] F3"; the kind "Scrip or voucher (S1 'Trade vouchers')". F5: "a picture postcard is F5 used or not" (step 19); "telephone card" is an F5 kind with collector named forms; "a paper gift certificate redeemable only at its issuer is F5 while a money-substitute scrip used as currency is F3". The step 5 note already says "a card kept after it has expired or been used up is F5". "Collected for itself" and "kept for its stamps" test motive (S5).
- *Proposed sentences (both memos):* *"Payment, gift, telephone, transit, membership and key cards are F5 whether live, used or expired. A token of metal, plastic or other material issued to stand for money or a service is F3. Paper issued to circulate as money (scrip, emergency money, Notgeld) is F3; a voucher or gift certificate redeemable only with its issuer is F5. Postal stationery with a printed stamp, and a cover issued by a postal administration or prepared as a first-day cover, are F3; any other used envelope, letter or picture postcard is F5, whatever stamps it carries."* The last clause sends postal-history covers to F5; the owner may prefer F3 for any cover that has passed through the post (owner question 34).

**X3 (medium) Photographs: F2 and F5 use different axes and different tests.**
- *Evidence.* F2 divides photographs by process (gelatin silver, albumen as GA lists, chromogenic) and proposes "whether the print is signed, numbered or catalogued as an edition" as the F2 test. F5 divides photographic prints by format (carte de visite, cabinet card, real photo postcard, snapshot) and asks (O3) whether strangers' vernacular photographs, which step 19 does not cover ("of the owner's own life"), go to F5. A 19th-century albumen cabinet card is thus a process kind in one memo and a format named form in the other.
- *Proposed convention.* Every photographic print carries both `process` (F2's vocabulary, from GA) and `mount_format` (F5's named forms). Placement: *"A photographic print issued as an edition, or signed or stamped by its maker as a work, is F2; every other photographic print, slide or negative (snapshots, studio portraits, cartes de visite, cabinet cards, real photo postcards), whoever is shown, is F5."* This needs the framework to drop "of the owner's own life" from step 19 (framework conflict).

**X4 (medium) Devotional images: F2 and F4 both test intent.**
- *Evidence.* F2 boundary 4: "a picture or statue made for use in worship (icon, home-shrine image, devotional statue) is F4"; F2's question 7 asks about thangka formats. F4 boundary 7: "the maker's purpose does" decide, with an owner question on old-master altarpieces. Neither gives an object test, and the antiques trade lists icons under painting (F4 S22).
- *Proposed sentence.* *"The devotional forms are F4 by kind: icon (painted panel in the icon tradition), thangka and other devotional scroll paintings, altarpiece, devotional statue made for a shrine, crucifix. A painting or sculpture with a religious subject made in another form (an easel painting, a garden figure) is F2 or B4."* This files an old-master altarpiece in F4. The alternative, filing old altarpieces in F2 by age, would use era, an Appendix A modifier, and is not recommended.

**X5 (medium) Albums, binders and holders: three memos, three answers.**
- *Evidence.* F3: single holders follow the coin or card (S3); albums, binders and trays are B1. F6: domes and stands for one specimen are F6.10; trays and cabinets are B1. F5: empty photo albums are F5 (P6). F1: says nothing about sleeves, bags and jewel cases.
- *Proposed convention.* *"A holder, case, sleeve or stand made for one object follows it (S3) into that category's parts sub-family. An album, binder, stock book, box or tray made to hold several objects is B1 when empty, whatever it is made to hold. A filled photo album or scrapbook is F5 as a record; a blank scrapbook album is C4 (step 19 note)."* Changes: F5-1; F1-7; F3-5 (already B1 for albums).

**X6 (medium) Parts sub-family.**
- *Evidence.* Only F6 has one (F6.10). F3 has a parts leaf inside Medals and its holders in boundary text; F1 has none; F2, F4 and F5 have few S3 items.
- *Proposed.* Apply C5(d) as extended by review C's K7 to every F category that receives S3 items: F1.5, F3.10 and F6.10 now; F2, F4 and F5 only when an item is attested (a plinth for one sculpture, a rosary pouch, a mezuzah case is already a kind in F4.3 and stays there because it is the visible object).

**X7 (medium) Maker ledgers: six memos, five schemas.**
- *Evidence.* F1 uses the pre-convention template fields. F2 adds `role` and an authority identifier. F3 adds `role` with seven values and mixes issuers with makers. F4 proposes "maker, known-for kinds, scene or tradition tags, tier, country and era" or a plain column on named forms. F5 proposes an `issuer` field and a card-systems ledger. F6 uses C1 with a new maker_class `certifier`.
- *Proposed shared schema for domain F.* C1 core columns, with `maker_class` made multi-valued (a mint can also be a refiner; a publisher can issue cards and decks), and these F values added to C1's list: `publisher`, `imprint`, `record label`, `cartographic publisher`, `globe maker` (F1, F4 decks, F3 cards where the firm publishes); `artist`, `print studio or atelier`, `print publisher`, `foundry` (F2); `mint`, `refiner`, `banknote and security printer`, `medallist or engraver`, `card manufacturer`, `awards maker` (F3); `certifier or grader` (F3 graders PCGS, NGC, PMG, PSA, Beckett; F6 labs such as GIA; F1 comic graders if attested); `taxidermy studio` (F6). Extensions: `authority_id` (ULAN for artists; Numista, VIAF or ISNI where they exist), `certifications` (refiners' accreditation, already a C1 extension for A5). Issuing authorities (states, central banks, postal administrations, transit authorities) go in one **issuer table** shared by F3 and F5 (`issuer_id`, name, country, type, years, sources), linked to the region modifier; they are not makers.

**X8 (medium) Lines and named forms.**
- *Evidence.* F1 makes publisher lines named forms (Marvel Masterworks, DC Absolute, Replogle's National Geographic Collection), F3 makes card product lines named forms (Topps Flagship, BBM), F5 makes album lines named forms (Walther Fun, Goldbuch Florentine), F2 makes branded processes named forms (Polaroid, Ilfochrome). Some of these lines span several kinds.
- *Proposed.* One test for all F memos (review C's K4): a maker's line that spans several kinds is a C1 line row; a line or design inside one kind is a maker-bound named form. Topps Flagship, Penguin Classics and Lladró series are lines; Polaroid integral print, Rider-Waite deck and a Replogle globe model are named forms.

**X9 (medium) Condition and grading scales have no common place.**
- *Evidence.* F3 has Sheldon 70, PMG 70, PSA 10, Beckett and IBNS scales and puts grade "in the user's holding record", which does not exist. F6 has GIA scales (attributes), a seller's aesthetic score and Conchology's star system (platform tags). F1 notes Goldmine and book-condition scales "not researched". F4 has the kosher scroll grade. F5 has "unused" as a condition tag.
- *Proposed convention.* A **grading-scale registry**: `scale_id`, name, owner (standard body, grader or platform), `scale_type` (condition grade under Appendix A; measured spec under C4(b); platform label under C4(c)), `steps`, `kinds_served`, `sources`. Kinds link to the scales that apply; no grade value sits on a kind or named form; grade values belong to an owner's inventory, outside this taxonomy.

**X10 (medium) Sets, rolls and lots.**
- *Evidence.* F1 files box sets and combo packs by the primary carrier; F3 makes "coin roll or bag", "proof or mint set" and "complete set" kinds; F6 makes "study sets and lots" a sub-family; F4 makes "mass sets" and altar-fitting sets products only.
- *Proposed.* Adopt review C's K6 across F: a set of like items is filed as the item (`unit` or `quantity` attribute: coin roll, stamp block, bulk shells); a kit of unlike items is filed by its item of greatest value; owner-approved exceptions are kinds: mint proof and mint sets (F3), rock and mineral teaching set (F6), and, if the owner wants them, factory card sets (F3). F1's primary-carrier rule is the same rule applied to media.

**X11 (medium) Size against format.**
- *Evidence.* F1 makes record diameter, paperback trim, magazine digest size and film gauge kinds; F4 makes urn capacity kinds; F3 (medal size) and F5 (card format) make size an attribute; F6 makes `size_class` an attribute. C6 says size is always an attribute, with luggage as the only proposed exception.
- *Proposed addition to C6.* *"A size class names a kind only when it changes what the object can be used with, so that one size cannot be used in place of the other without different equipment (film gauge and projector; a 16-inch transcription disc; a 3-inch Mini-CD in a slot loader), and a fetched source sells the sizes apart."* By this rule film gauges stay kinds; 7, 10 and 12-inch records, paperback trims, digest magazines and urn capacities become attributes, unless the owner names them as exceptions beside luggage.

**X12 (medium) Regional and traditional names.**
- *Evidence.* C11 is applied correctly by F2 (ukiyo-e), F3 (New Year and zodiac issues) and F6 (Herkimer diamond, Itoigawa jadeite), partly by F1 (bunko named form, but tankōbon and bunkoban also kinds), and not by F4 (rosary, butsudan, koro, rin as kinds beside their generic kinds) or F6's pearls (Tahitian as a kind). F5 treats national ID designs as named forms although they are issuer differences, not regional forms.
- *Proposed.* Apply C11 as in F1-2, F2-5, F4-1 and F6-2, and use F6's two-region variety test (F6-6) as the tie-break when a regional or variety name could be either a kind or a named form: it becomes a kind only when fetched sellers in two regions give it its own category and no generic kind covers its form.

**X13 (low) Compatibility ledger.**
- *Evidence.* F1 (proprietary carriers) and F5 (transit card systems) describe platforms without the C13 ledger; F4's mezuzah fit is a size match, not a platform.
- *Proposed.* C13 rows for F1 carrier systems and F5 card systems; none elsewhere in F.

**X14 (low) Evidence status is not recorded in the sources files.**
- *Evidence.* All six TSVs have the columns url, source_type, region, what_it_shows; partial and failed fetches are noted in free text (F3 rows 6, 11, 13, 18; F4 rows 35 to 37), and excerpt-only facts are labelled only in the memos.
- *Proposed.* Add a column `fetch_status` (full, partial, nav-only, failed) to every S2 sources TSV through a tool, and list excerpt-only facts in a separate `S2_<CODE>_excerpts.tsv` so that no structural claim rests on a row that was not fetched.
