# Stage 2 domain review: F Culture, Records & Meaning (F1 to F6)

*Batch S2R_F. Cold, offline review (no web, no git) of memos framework/structure/F1.md to F6.md and their sources files verify/out/S2_F1_sources.tsv to S2_F6_sources.tsv, against BRIEF.md, framework/CATEGORIES_v0_6.md, framework/STAGE2_MEMO_TEMPLATE.md and the draft shared conventions C1 to C13 in framework/structure/_conventions.md. The conventions were written after these memos (F6 already applies C1 to C9), so every memo is also checked against them and the changes it needs are listed (section 3). Where the other domain reviews proposed a convention that bears on F (review C's K4 lines, K6 kits and sets, K7 parts; review E's trading-card and tarot sentences; review A's crown and prayer-bead questions), this review points to it rather than inventing a second one. This is a reviewer's judgement, not an owner decision. Severity: **high** = breaks a shared rule (BRIEF, Appendix A, a convention) or files the same object in two places across many objects; **medium** = one rule break, one contradiction or a clear split; **low** = wording, evidence or tidy-up.*

## 2. Findings by memo

Each finding gives severity, memo, evidence, the objects that would split, and an exact proposed fix. "Conforms to" names the convention the fix applies.

### F1 Media & Publications

**F1-1 (medium) Record kinds fuse diameter, speed and release type.**
- *Evidence.* The grooved-disc kinds are "12-inch LP record (33⅓ rpm)", "10-inch record", "7-inch single (45 rpm)", "12-inch single or maxi single (45 rpm)", "78 rpm shellac disc" and "flexi-disc". The memo itself reports that Discogs keeps Format as the carrier (Vinyl, Shellac, Flexi-disc, Lathe Cut), puts size and speed in the Description field, and states that "Album, EP and Single tags are independent of speed and media" (S01, S26). "LP" and "single" are release types; "10-inch" and "12-inch" are sizes; "78 rpm shellac" is a groove standard and a material. Three axes sit at one level.
- *Objects that would split.* A 10-inch shellac 78 (kind 41 or 44); a 12-inch 45 rpm audiophile album reissue (40 or 43); a 7-inch EP at 33⅓ (42, which says 45 rpm); a 10-inch vinyl LP (41, or 40 by "LP").
- *Fix.* Kinds by the groove standard, which decides the stylus and the playback deck: "microgroove vinyl record", "coarse-groove (78 rpm) record", "flexi-disc", "instantaneous disc (acetate or lathe cut)", "transcription disc (16-inch)". Diameter (7, 10, 12 inch), speed (33⅓, 45, 78) and release type (LP, EP, single, maxi single) become attributes, with Discogs' Format and Description fields as the attested standard. If the owner prefers diameter kinds because collectors shelve 7-inch singles apart, record that as a stated exception to C6 (see X11). Conforms to C5(c), C6.

**F1-2 (high) Japanese and comic formats have two homes, and the Franco-Belgian album has none.**
- *Evidence.* Kind 1 "Hardback book" lists "tankōbon A5, B5, B6 as the Japanese hardback trims [S04]", but the memo's own section 1.4 says ONIX B108 to B110 are "paperback size classes" (form detail). Kind 15 "Manga tankōbon" is a separate kind in F1.1.2. Kind 4 "Pocket paperback" lists "Japanese bunko A6 (B111)" as a named form, and kind 16 "Bunkoban (manga pocket reissue)" is a separate kind. Nothing says which leaf wins for a manga volume. The comics leaf rests on one US page for the edition ladder (CBR, S12) and on Japanese pages for manga; Fnac's BD department (S31) is cited only as a shelf name, and the bande dessinée album (hardcover, about A4, 46 to 64 pages) has no kind.
- *Objects that would split.* A Shueisha Jump Comics volume (kind 1 named form or kind 15); a manga bunko reissue (kind 4 named form or kind 16); a Tintin or Astérix album (kind 1 hardback or kind 12 collected hardcover); a Dark Horse softcover "omnibus" (kind 11, 13 or the book leaf).
- *Fix.* (a) Precedence: *"A comic or manga volume is filed in F1.1.2 whatever its binding or trim; F1.1.1 holds books that are not comics."* (b) Apply C11 inside F1.1.2: kinds by edition form, named generically: single-issue comic; collected softcover volume (named forms: US trade paperback, tankōbon, wide-ban); collected hardcover volume (named forms: bande dessinée album, US hardcover); omnibus or compendium; pocket-size reissue (named form: bunkoban); premium large-format edition (named forms: kanzenban, sōshūhen, the Absolute and Gallery formats as maker lines, F1-6). Remove the tankōbon trims from kind 1. (c) Bunko and shinsho stay non-maker named forms of "pocket paperback" for prose only, region-tagged (C11). (d) Fetch a French or Belgian BD source and a second, non-US comics source before the ladder enters the data.

**F1-3 (medium) Cartographic kinds are split by content.**
- *Evidence.* Atlases are "reference atlas", "road atlas" and "street atlas" (by scope, S13); globes include "celestial globe" beside "desktop globe" and "floor globe". The memo's section 4b says maps stop "at physical form (folded, wall, booklet, tear-off)" and Replogle (S22) treats the depicted body as a "map style" filter. Scope and depicted body are what the map shows: content, which BRIEF and step 21 keep off the tree.
- *Objects that would split.* A 12-inch celestial desktop globe (37 or 39); an A–Z street atlas that also carries road maps (35 or 36); a moon globe (no kind).
- *Fix.* Atlas kinds by format, as for books: "bound atlas" (hard or soft cover) and "spiral-bound atlas", with `scope` (reference, road, street, thematic) as an attribute. Globe kinds by mount: "desktop globe", "floor globe", "hanging or suspended globe"; `body` (terrestrial, celestial, lunar, planetary), `map_style`, `illuminated` (C10) and diameter (C6) are attributes. A mini globe is a desktop globe with a size attribute. Owner questions 4 and 5 are then answered by the conventions.

**F1-4 (medium) Size classes are named forms and kinds.**
- *Evidence.* The named forms of kinds 3 and 4 are US trade (B102), UK trade (B106), UK A- and B-format (B104, B105), mass-market rack (B101), tall rack (B107) and Swedish pocket (B113, B114): trim sizes. Kind 20 "Digest-size magazine" differs from kind 19 only by trim (S27). Kind 1 offers folio, quarto and octavo as named forms (owner question 6). C2(b) says sizes are attributes, not named forms; C6 says size is always an attribute with its standard. The memo's own choice-level evidence says "ONIX stops at BB and BC and puts the size classes in a second list" (S03, S04).
- *Fix.* Add `trim_class` with ONIX list 175 codes (B101 to B114) as the standard, and `format_size` with the ABAA height scale (S17, S18) for antiquarian books. Merge "digest-size magazine" into "magazine". Keep hardback, trade paperback and pocket paperback as kinds only if the owner accepts paper and binding, not trim, as what separates them (B&N S05: pulp paper, rack size); otherwise "paperback" is one kind with `trim_class`. Conforms to C2(b), C6.

**F1-5 (medium) A tier label sits on kinds, and pressing names are undecided.**
- *Evidence.* Section 5: quality "appears as a modifier 'fidelity class' or 'premium line' on the kind or named form". Section 4b calls HDCD and SHM-CD "named forms or pressing attributes"; the tree lists them as named forms of kind 48. C4(a) forbids a tier on a kind; C4(b) makes a measured spec an attribute with its standard named.
- *Fix.* Fidelity becomes attributes: `bit_depth`, `sample_rate`, `capacity` and `encoding` (HDCD is an encoding, C10) with the standard named where fetched (the CD and SACD specifications are not yet fetched; S24 gives the figures). SHM-CD is `pressing_material`. Record weight (180 g, S02) is an attribute. "Premium line" (Absolute, Omnibus, Masterworks) becomes C4 tier entries on the line row (F1-6), with CBR (S12) as the first source and a second region's source required by C4(e).

**F1-6 (medium) Maker ledger fields, lines and platforms.**
- *Evidence.* Section 6 proposes "maker, known-for kinds or lines, scene tags, tier, price band, country and era": the pre-convention field list. Edition lines span kinds: Marvel Masterworks appear in hardcover and softcover, Penguin Classics as pocket paperback, hardback and clothbound. Proprietary carriers are "already the platform; no separate maker level is needed", with no ledger.
- *Fix.* Use C1 core columns with maker_class values `publisher`, `imprint`, `record label`, `cartographic publisher` and `globe maker` (X7). A line that spans several kinds (Masterworks, Penguin Classics, Criterion Collection) is a C1 line row (review C's K4); a line inside one kind is a maker-bound named form (C2(a)). Carrier systems become C13 compatibility rows (`platform_id`, `owner`, `kinds_served`, `standard_ref`): Compact Disc (Philips and Sony), SACD, Blu-ray Disc (Blu-ray Disc Association), VHS (JVC), MiniDisc (Sony), Super 8 (Kodak), with D3 players and D4 projectors in `kinds_served`. Kinds stay generic and point to their row.

**F1-7 (medium) No parts sub-family.**
- *Evidence.* S3 sends to F1 every case, cover and stand made for one carrier: comic bags and backing boards, record inner and outer sleeves, CD jewel cases and replacement cases, slipcases and dust jackets sold alone, globe stands and meridians. The memo mentions only the globe stand (section 9, item 6).
- *Fix.* Add "F1.5 Parts and accessories made for one carrier" (C5(d), as extended by review C's K7), with leaves by the object served (book, comic, record, disc, globe). Boxes, crates and shelves that hold several items are B1.

**F1-8 (low) Evidence and attestation.**
- *Evidence.* "Fake book" is in the tree as kind 30 but "unattested on a fetched page" (S6 forbids it until attested). Flexi-disc (S01), zine (S27, a digest-size article that mentions UK fanzines in A5) and fine binding (S03 BG) each rest on one source. "Bound periodical volume" is placed in periodicals, while the cited HS note (S16 note 3) sends it to books. Periodicals have no retailer tree (section 10 says so). Several kind names are the memo's own groupings ("premium large-trim edition", "large-format manga reissue", "microcassette family").
- *Fix.* Hold the fake book and mook until a page is fetched. State that "bound periodical volume" departs from HS on purpose (one home with its issues), or move it to books as HS does. Fetch one periodical retailer or wholesaler tree (Japan 雑誌 and ムック, Presse-Grosso). Mark grouping names as such in the data (`label_source: memo`).

**F1-9 (low) "Fine-binding book (leather)" is a material kind.**
- *Fix.* Make `binding` (cloth, paper over boards, leather, fine binding) an attribute of the hardback kind; ONIX BG is cited as the attribute's code. Conforms to review C's K9.

**F1-10 (low) Boundaries that need the other side's sentence.**
- *Fix.* (a) Playaway-type players (owner question 9): F1, because the work is published fixed on the device and the device plays only that work; D3 holds players of any content. (b) Pictorial and antique engraved maps: *"A map, including a single sheet from an atlas or a pictorial wall map, is F1; a print made as art that depicts a place is F2."* F2 needs the same sentence. (c) Tarot and oracle decks are F4 (step 14); a tarock pack for card play is E1 (review E). F1 should say both so that printed decks are not filed as books.
