# C3 Plants & Living Cultures — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research memo, batch S2_C3. Research, not a decision: the owner approves structures later. Source ids [S1]–[S38] are the row numbers of `verify/out/S2_C3_sources.tsv`; every one is a page fetched in this batch. Call count: 15 searches and 38 fetches (caps 18 and 40). Evidence is strong for retail trees, collector groups and the cultivar-registration system; it is thin for living cultures (one spawn maker, one ferment-culture shop), aquatic plants (only society and marketplace headings) and non-Western markets other than Japan, Austria and Singapore (see section 10).*

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C3 Plants & Living Cultures, domain C (grow, make and maintain).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, placement key step 6): *a living plant or fungus, a living culture kept to grow or propagate (sourdough starter, kombucha culture, mushroom spawn), a fresh-cut plant, or a seed, bulb or spore for growing.* Category line: "living plants and fungi, fresh-cut flowers, seeds and bulbs, living cultures."

**Notes and precedence sentences in the framework that touch C3:**
- Step 6 note: cut vegetables, herbs, sprouts and microgreens sold to eat are step 3 (groceries, J4); a plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is C3 even when sold to eat (potted basil, living microgreens). Dried and artificial flowers are B4.
- Step 1 (outside scope) note: living cultures kept to grow or propagate (sourdough starter, kombucha and kefir cultures, mushroom spawn) are step 6 "even when what they make is eaten"; animals are OUT, so live feeders, eggs for hatching and aquarium fish are not here.
- Step 3 groceries: a plant sold still growing is C3; nuts, seeds and dried fruit, raising agents and dried yeast are J5; kefir as a drink is J3; vitamins and supplements are H2; bird seed and fat balls, hay and forage are B10.
- Step 7 (natural specimens): herbarium sheets are F6; dried flowers are B4 (so a pressed or dried plant is not C3).
- S3 and step 29: anything made for growing plants is C2 (planters and pots, plant care products, grow lights, compost, greenhouses), plant stands are B1. The plant is C3, the pot is C2.
- Step 14 note: "Christmas trees and ornaments" are B4 as festival decorations. This collides with step 6 for a fresh-cut or potted real tree (section 9 and 10).
- Step 27 (B9): tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products "in any form" are B9; step 6 is tried first, so a live plant or a seed for growing is C3 by key order (section 9).
- Step 4 (G2): land and its permanent structures are property; a tree standing on a plot is part of the land until it is sold as a plant.

### 1.2 Enthusiast societies and communities

- **Orchids, American Orchid Society [S6].** 16 alliances (Angraecum, Bulbophyllum, Catasetum, Cattleya, Coelogyne, Cymbidium, Dendrobium, Maxillaria, Oncidium, Phalaenopsis, Pleurothallid, Slipper, Stanhopea, Vanda, Zygopetalum, Miscellaneous) and an A–Z genus list. The unit of knowledge is the genus; alliances are a convenience grouping above it.
- **Aroids, International Aroid Society [S7].** The family Araceae has about 150 genera and over 4,500 species; the society's "popular genera" are Philodendron, Monstera, Anthurium, Alocasia, Colocasia, Syngonium, Dieffenbachia, Spathiphyllum, Aglaonema, Amorphophallus, each with notable species and named forms (Philodendron erubescens 'Pink Princess', Alocasia reginula 'Black Velvet', Alocasia amazonica 'Polly'). Collectors name the species.
- **Carnivorous plants, ICPS [S31].** Denomination classes are the genera in their widest circumscription, grouped by family (Sarraceniaceae, Droseraceae, Nepenthaceae, Lentibulariaceae and others).
- **Community [S36].** r/houseplants excludes plants that live outside full-time and cut flowers, sends plant trading to a separate community, and lists related communities for succulents, bonsai, terrariums and air plants: the community split is by horticultural group, not by indoor use alone.
- **Bonsai [S16, S17].** Enthusiast references divide bonsai by height class (mame, shohin, chuhin, omono; with komono, katade-mochi, imperial and the "hands" system), by trunk count (tan-kan to kyū-kan) and by style (formal upright, informal upright, slant, cascade, forest, raft, literati, windswept, broom). The Japanese tradition grows outdoor temperate trees; indoor bonsai use tropicals such as Ficus and jade.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- Rare aroid shops and guides [S9, S10] sell and rank by species and named form: Monstera deliciosa 'Albo Borsigiana', Anthurium regale, Philodendron Spiritus Sancti, Scindapsus pictus 'Mayari', crosses such as Philodendron Florida Beauty × Paraiso Verde Variegated. Variegation (chimeric, unstable) is a trait of the named form; humidity and difficulty are care columns. Stage (3-inch start, 6-inch pot, rooted or unrooted) is stated per listing [S9, S21].
- Etsy [S21] and Botachoku [S19] confirm that the tradable unit is a named form at a stage, with phytosanitary paperwork for cross-border sales, not a generic "houseplant".

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Harmonized System chapter 06 [S4].** 0601 bulbs, tubers, corms and rhizomes; 0602 other live plants, cuttings and slips, and mushroom spawn; 0603 cut flowers and buds; 0604 foliage, branches and grasses. The classifier divides by what is traded (propagule, living plant, cut flower, cut foliage), not by plant group, and files mushroom spawn with live plants.
- **Dutch trade [S23].** The VBN specifications are written for three product families: Cut Flowers, Houseplants and Garden Plants, with supplements per product group.
- **Cultivated-plant nomenclature [S24, S34, S8, S7, S31].** The ICNCP makes the cultivar the basic category, with Group and grex as the other categories; International Cultivar Registration Authorities (IAS for Araceae, ICPS for carnivorous plants, the RHS for orchid hybrids) keep registers. Orchid hybrids are named as grex (defined by parentage, e.g. Papilionanthe Miss Joaquim) with an optional Group and a clone epithet in single quotes. Trademarked commercial names stand beside cultivar epithets (Syringa 'Penda' BLOOMERANG) [S34].
- **Intellectual property [S32].** Plant patents, plant variety protection certificates and UPOV breeders' rights attach to named varieties; plant names can also be trademarks.
- **Quality marks [S37, S38].** AOS flower-quality awards (HCC 75–79, AM 80–89, FCC 90+ points) and the RHS Recommended: Award of Garden Merit (healthy, excellent performers, available, resistant, true to type).

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **RHS Plants by type [S25].** Alpines, annuals and biennials, aquatic plants, bulbs, cacti and succulents, carnivorous plants, climbers, conifers, ferns, grasses, heathers, hedges, houseplants, perennials, shrubs, trees; plus fruit, herbs and veg. Same mix of axes as Crocus [S1].
- **Wikipedia [S28, S33, S34].** "Ornamental plant": botanical forms (trees, shrubs, climbers, grasses, succulents, aquatics, perennials, annuals) versus non-botanical classifications (houseplants, bedding plants, hedges, cut-flower plants, foliage plants). "Houseplant" is defined by use, lists tropical and subtropical plants and succulents, and records name changes (snake plant now Dracaena trifasciata, formerly Sansevieria; string of pearls now Curio rowleyanus).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** (1) genus is the working unit for collectors and societies (AOS, IAS, ICPS, Mountain Crest, Engei); (2) the named form (cultivar, grex, selection) sits under genus or species, is registered by an authority and may be protected; (3) a plant is traded in states (bulb or seed, rooted plant, cut stem, culture) and trade bodies keep those apart (HS, VBN, Crocus, Interflora); (4) the specialist groups orchids, succulents and cacti, carnivorous plants, bonsai, aquatic plants, bulbs and fruit are recognised departments everywhere.
- **Split:** the general-plant tree. Crocus, RHS, PFLANZMICH, Engei and Takii all mix growth form, life cycle, propagule, crop use, habitat and setting in one list. Japan puts orchids, carnivorous and caudex plants inside "foliage", the West and Botachoku do not. Home Depot, Etsy and the RHS use a setting-first split (indoor plants against garden plants) that the owner's modifier rule forbids as a level. Aroids and orchids are grouped by family/alliance by their societies, but by "houseplant" by retailers.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Is it a living plant in a pot, a seed or bulb to plant, a cut stem, or a culture?** Trade classifications (HS 0601 to 0604 [S4]), trade specifications (VBN: cut flowers, houseplants, garden plants [S23]), Crocus (Bulbs, Seeds as top-level entries [S1]), Interflora (cut flowers separate from plants [S29]), Gardeners Dream (fresh cut against pot grown [S30]) and Etsy (seed packets mixed in only by search) all put this question first. It decides what the object is, how long it lives and how it is shipped.
2. **Which horticultural group or genus?** Orchid, aroid, succulent, carnivorous and bonsai buyers begin here (AOS alliances and genus list [S6], IAS popular genera [S7], Mountain Crest Types/Genera [S22], Botachoku category row [S19], Engei succulent genus list [S18]); mainstream buyers begin with a growth form (tree, shrub, perennial, climber) or a crop (apple, tomato).
3. **Species or named form.** Rare-plant buyers decide species then cultivar or variegated form [S9, S10, S21]; fruit buyers decide crop, then variety, then tree size and rootstock [S26, S27]; seed buyers decide crop then variety then seed type [S11, S12].
4. **Fit to the place and the owner.** Light, hardiness zone, pet safety, size, "easy to grow" [S1, S2, S26] are used as filters after the kind is chosen; they are setting and user modifiers.
5. **Stage and size.** Cutting, rooted start or established plant [S9]; pot size and plant height [S2, S9, S30]; tree size and rootstock [S26, S27]; bonsai size class [S15, S17].
6. **Occasion** (Valentine's, Mother's Day, Christmas, wedding) steers cut flowers and gift plants [S29, S30] but is a modifier.

No source starts with maker or price. The ordered choice is state sold, then group or genus, then species or named form, then size and stage.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: the maximum depth below the category is four (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). Region (hardiness zone, native range), setting (indoor, outdoor, balcony, aquarium), occasion, motive (collecting, gift), user (pet safe, beginner), price and rarity, origin (wild-collected, nursery-grown, tissue-cultured) and condition (variegation stability, plant stage) never split an inner level; they are tags, filters or ledger columns (Appendix A).

### Candidate A (recommended): state sold, then horticultural group, then genus or species, then named form

| Level | Name | The one stated axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | **What is sold**: a rooted living plant; a seed, bulb or other propagule; a fresh-cut stem, tree or arrangement; a living culture or fungus |
| 2 | Leaf | **Horticultural group** (for growing plants, an ordered key, first match wins); propagule type (for propagules); part cut (for cut plants); organism type (for cultures) |
| 3 | Kind | **Accepted botanical or crop name**: the genus by default, the species where buyers compare species (aroids, Hoya, Nepenthes, bonsai species, crops), the carrier for spawn |
| 4 | Named form | **Cultivar, Group, grex, hybrid, trade-named or variegated selection**, strain |

The ordered key for growing-plant leaves: (1) orchids; (2) carnivorous plants; (3) bonsai, a tree trained and sold as bonsai, where the form is the object; (4) cacti and other succulents; (5) aquatic and bog plants; (6) fruit and nut plants; (7) vegetable and herb plants; (8) tropical foliage and flowering plants (aroids, ferns, palms, Ficus, Hoya, calathea); (9) trees; (10) shrubs and climbers (roses, hydrangeas, conifers by habit); (11) perennials, grasses and garden ferns; (12) annuals, biennials and bedding. Specialist groups come first because their societies and registers (AOS, IAS, ICPS) already define them [S6, S7, S31].

**Where it breaks.**
1. *Tulip, three homes.* A dormant bulb (C3.2), a potted tulip in flower (C3.1; HS 0601 itself says "in growth or in flower" [S4]) and a cut tulip (C3.3). The rule that keeps one home: no roots and dormant = propagule; rooted in a medium and alive = growing plant; cut = cut. The same genus then appears in three sub-families, so a generated "by genus" view is needed.
2. *Air plants (Tillandsia).* Mountain Crest lists them under succulents [S22], Engei puts bromeliads in foliage plants [S18]; botanically they are epiphytic bromeliads. The ordered key puts them in leaf 8 (tropical foliage), which disagrees with one retailer.
3. *Citrus and other fruit plants kept as houseplants.* A compact Meyer lemon is a houseplant on Wikipedia [S33] and a fruit tree at Stark Bro's [S26]. Key order sends it to leaf 6 whatever the setting, as setting is a modifier.
4. *Bonsai against nursery trees.* A trained ficus is bonsai (leaf 3); a pre-bonsai or an untrained juniper is a tree or shrub (leaf 9 or 10). The test is whether the sale describes a trained bonsai form (style, size class, pot), which makes leaf 3 depend on the seller's description.
5. *Rose.* Cut rose (C3.3), bare-root or potted garden rose (leaf 10), a rose in a pot sold as a gift (leaf 10, "occasion" is a modifier).

### Candidate B: botanical lineage (family, genus, species, cultivar)

| Level | Name | Axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | Plant lineage (orchids, aroids, cacti and other succulent lineages, conifers, roses and other woody families, grasses, ferns, fungi and cultures) |
| 2 | Leaf | Genus |
| 3 | Kind | Species |
| 4 | Named form | Cultivar, Group, grex |

Example kinds (all on fetched pages): Orchidaceae: Phalaenopsis, Cattleya [S6]; Araceae: Monstera deliciosa, Philodendron gloriosum, Anthurium clarinervium, Alocasia reginula [S7]; Cactaceae: Mammillaria [S33]; Crassulaceae: Echeveria, Sempervivum, Kalanchoe [S22]; Nepenthaceae: Nepenthes; Droseraceae: Dionaea [S31]; Rosaceae: apple tree, pear tree [S26]; Pinaceae: Nordmann fir, Fraser fir [S30]; Asparagaceae: Dracaena trifasciata [S33]. Strength: one axis, one authority (the registers cite Kew's World Checklist [S8]); the societies work this way. Where it breaks: (1) *The state problem is not solved*: a tulip bulb, a potted tulip and a cut tulip share one leaf, and a buyer of cut flowers meets bulbs; the HS and VBN split of trade states [S4, S23] is lost. (2) *Names churn*: Sansevieria is now Dracaena, Senecio rowleyanus is now Curio, one orchid species had four names in four classification systems [S8, S33], so a tree keyed on current genus renames its own leaves. (3) *Edible crops and ornamentals share genera* (Citrus, Rosa, Malus), so buyers' crop departments [S1, S18, S20, S26] are split across lineages. (4) *Mainstream browsers cannot find their way*: no retailer navigates by family; only collector groups do.

### Candidate C: setting and use first (houseplants, garden plants, edible plants, cut flowers)

| Level | Name | Axis |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sub-family | Where or why it is kept: houseplants; garden plants; edible plants; cut flowers and trees; propagation material; cultures |
| 2 | Leaf | Growth form or horticultural group within the use |
| 3 | Kind | Genus or species |
| 4 | Named form | Cultivar, grex |

This is how Home Depot [S2], Etsy [S21], the RHS [S25, S35], Crocus ("Indoor plants") [S1], PFLANZMICH ("Zimmer", "Garten", "Balkon") [S3] and Wikipedia [S28, S33] present plants, so it is the shape a shopper expects. Where it breaks: Appendix A names setting (indoor, outdoor, garden) and motive as modifiers that never split a level. In practice (1) *Ficus, Citrus, orchids, succulents and bonsai* belong to several settings; (2) the same crop is a "garden plant" and an "edible plant" (blueberry, strawberry, lavender); (3) the HS and VBN split [S4, S23] shows the first split has to be state sold, not setting; (4) "houseplant" is defined by use [S33], so membership depends on the owner's home, not the object. It remains valuable as a generated view (indoor, outdoor, balcony) from tags.

**Comparison.** Candidate A follows trade classifications for level 1 and the societies and ICRA registers for levels 2 to 4; it keeps setting and use as tags. Candidate B is cleanest but ignores what buyers see first; Candidate C is the retail habit but breaks the modifier rule.

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (estimate, not counted).** About 500 to 800 if kinds are genus-level in specialist leaves and crop-level for edibles; 900 to 1,400 if species-level kinds are admitted for aroids, Hoya, Nepenthes and similar collector genera. Anchors from fetched pages: Araceae alone has about 150 genera and 4,500 species [S7]; AOS groups orchid genera in 16 alliances [S6]; Mountain Crest runs a long Types/Genera index for succulents alone [S22]; Stark Bro's carries 128 fruit-tree items across about a dozen crops [S26]; the True Leaf Asian-seed collection alone has 666 items across crop collections [S12]. Every added name needs an attested source (S6 in the framework), so the real count grows only as pages are fetched.
- **Named forms (estimate).** Tens of thousands worldwide. Anchors: PFLANZMICH advertises "15.000+ Sorten" and 5,000+ items [S3]; Crocus lists several thousand plants [S1]; a single rare-plant shop lists named crosses by the dozen [S9]; each ICRA runs a register [S7, S31]. Recommendation: named forms should be loaded as rows keyed to a register id (ICNCP epithet, grex, trade name), on demand, never pre-enumerated.
- **Where depth concentrates.** (1) Orchids (grex, Group, clone are three nested ranks below genus [S8]); (2) aroids and other collector tropical foliage (species plus variegated clones [S7, S9, S10]); (3) fruit plants (variety, rootstock, tree form [S26, S27]); (4) roses and bulbs (classes, divisions: hybrid tea or floribunda [S18], daffodil divisions [S1]); (5) seeds by crop and variety [S11, S12].
- **Leaf width.** *Broad:* tropical foliage plants, cacti and succulents, shrubs and climbers, perennials, annuals and bedding, vegetable and herb plants, seeds. *Medium:* orchids, trees, fruit and nut plants, cut flowers, bulbs. *Narrow:* carnivorous plants, bonsai, aquatic and bog plants, cuttings, cut trees, fungal spawn, ferment cultures.
- **Depth.** Four below the category fits all four sub-families. The only candidate for a fifth rank is genus above species; this memo recommends a *genus field* on every kind instead (section 8), because the cap is four.

## 4b. Choice level

**A kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.** Finer distinctions are named forms or attributes. The evidence differs by leaf, so the choice level is stated per leaf:

| Leaf or sub-family | Kind stops at | Below it (named form or attribute) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cacti & succulents | Genus (Echeveria, Haworthia, Lithops) | Cultivar and hybrid names; features such as "rosette" or "hanging" are tags | Mountain Crest keeps a Types/Genera index and genus pages [S22]; Engei's succulent feature is a list of genera [S18] |
| Orchids | Genus (Phalaenopsis, Cattleya, Paphiopedilum, Cymbidium) | Species, grex, Group, clone; flower-quality awards are scored per species or hybrid shown [S37] | AOS organises by alliance then genus [S6]; Engei gives Phalaenopsis its own category [S18]; RHS ranks grex, Group, clone [S8] |
| Tropical foliage (aroids, Hoya, Ficus) | Species | Cultivar, variegated or hybrid form ('Pink Princess', 'Albo', 'Tineke') | IAS names species with named forms [S7]; Soltech ranks by species and form [S10]; Botachoku gives a species or form its own page [S19]; Home Depot stays at common-name genus level for the mass market [S2] |
| Carnivorous plants | Genus | Species, cultivar (registered with the ICPS) | ICPS denomination classes are genera [S31] |
| Bonsai | Species group (juniper, pine, tropical) | Size class, style, age; pot is C2 | Bonsai Inc splits by species group then size [S15]; enthusiasts split by size and style [S16, S17] |
| Fruit and nut plants | Crop species (apple, pear, citrus, fig, blueberry) | Variety (named form); tree size and rootstock (attributes) | Stark Bro's subcategories are crops, filter is tree size [S26]; Chris Bowers compares rootstock and tree form [S27] |
| Vegetable and herb plants, seeds | Crop (tomato, daikon, lavender) | Variety; seed type heirloom, open-pollinated, hybrid (attribute) | Baker Creek and True Leaf collections are crops; seed type is a facet [S11, S12] |
| Trees, shrubs, perennials, annuals | Genus (Hydrangea, Camellia, Hosta, Petunia); rose by class (hybrid tea, climbing) | Cultivar, series, trade name | Engei and Takii pages are genus pages [S18, S20]; rose classes are Engei categories [S18] |
| Bulbs | Genus (tulip, daffodil, crocus) | Division, cultivar | Crocus filters daffodils by division [S1]; Engei bulb list by genus [S18] |
| Cut flowers | Flower genus (rose, lily, chrysanthemum) | Variety, colour (tag) | Interflora lists flower types [S29] |
| Cut trees | Species (Nordmann fir, Fraser fir) | Height, fresh-cut or pot-grown (attributes) | Gardeners Dream and Lowes facet by species [S30] |
| Fungal spawn | Carrier (plug, sawdust, grain) | Species or strain (shiitake, blue oyster, lion's mane) | North Spore explains the carrier trade-offs as the choice, then species lines [S13] |
| Ferment cultures | Culture type (sourdough starter, kombucha SCOBY, water kefir grains, milk kefir grains) | Strain or house line | Farmhouse Teas divides cultures by what they ferment [S14] |

Two rules follow. (1) Where a seller or society gives a *species* its own page or filter, and species differ in price or care, the species is the kind; otherwise the genus is the kind and species are named forms. (2) The genus is stored as a field on every kind, so "all Philodendron" or "all Tulipa" is a generated view and not a level.
