## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C3 Plants & Living Cultures, domain C (grow, make and maintain).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, placement key step 6): *a living plant or fungus, a living culture kept to grow or propagate (sourdough starter, kombucha culture, mushroom spawn), a fresh-cut plant, or a seed, bulb or spore for growing.* Category line: "living plants and fungi, fresh-cut flowers, seeds and bulbs, living cultures."

**Notes and precedence sentences in the framework that touch C3:**
- Step 6 note: cut vegetables, herbs, sprouts and microgreens sold to eat are step 3 (groceries, J4); a plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is C3 even when sold to eat (potted basil, living microgreens). Dried and artificial flowers are B4.
- Step 1 (outside scope) note: living cultures kept to grow or propagate (sourdough starter, kombucha and kefir cultures, mushroom spawn) are step 6 "even when what they make is eaten"; animals are OUT, so live feeders, eggs for hatching and aquarium fish are not here.
- Step 3 groceries: a plant sold still growing is C3; nuts, seeds and dried fruit, raising agents and dried yeast are J5; kefir as a drink is J3; vitamins and supplements are H2; bird seed and fat balls, hay and forage are B10.
- Step 7 (natural specimens): herbarium sheets are F6; dried flowers are B4 (so a pressed or dried plant is not C3).
- Framework rule S3 and step 29: anything made for growing plants is C2 (planters and pots, plant care products, grow lights, compost, greenhouses), plant stands are B1. The plant is C3, the pot is C2.
- Step 14 note: "Christmas trees and ornaments" are B4 as festival decorations. This collides with step 6 for a fresh-cut or potted real tree (section 9 and 10).
- Step 27 (B9): tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products "in any form" are B9; step 6 is tried first, so a live plant or a seed for growing is C3 by key order (section 9).
- Step 4 (G2): land and its permanent structures are property; a tree standing on a plot is part of the land until it is sold as a plant.

### 1.2 Enthusiast societies and communities

- **Orchids, American Orchid Society [S6].** 16 alliances (Angraecum, Bulbophyllum, Catasetum, Cattleya, Coelogyne, Cymbidium, Dendrobium, Maxillaria, Oncidium, Phalaenopsis, Pleurothallid, Slipper, Stanhopea, Vanda, Zygopetalum, Miscellaneous) and an A–Z genus list. The unit of knowledge is the genus; alliances are a convenience grouping above it.
- **Aroids, International Aroid Society [S7].** The family Araceae has about 150 genera and over 4,500 species; the society's "popular genera" are Philodendron, Monstera, Anthurium, Alocasia, Colocasia, Syngonium, Dieffenbachia, Spathiphyllum, Aglaonema, Amorphophallus, each with notable species and named forms (Philodendron erubescens 'Pink Princess', Alocasia reginula 'Black Velvet', Alocasia amazonica 'Polly'). Collectors name the species.
- **Carnivorous plants, ICPS [S31].** Denomination classes are the genera in their widest circumscription, grouped by family (Sarraceniaceae, Droseraceae, Nepenthaceae, Lentibulariaceae and others).
- **Community [S36].** r/houseplants excludes plants that live outside full-time and cut flowers, sends plant trading to a separate community, and lists related communities for succulents, bonsai, terrariums and air plants: the community split is by horticultural group, not by indoor use alone.
- **Bonsai [S16, S17].** Enthusiast references divide bonsai by height class (mame, shohin, chuhin, omono; with komono, katade-mochi, imperial and the "hands" system), by trunk count (tan-kan to kyū-kan) and by style (formal upright, informal upright, slant, cascade, forest, raft, literati, windswept, broom). The Japanese tradition grows outdoor temperate trees; indoor bonsai use tropicals such as Ficus and jade.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- Rare aroid shops and guides [S9, S10] sell and rank by species and named form: Monstera deliciosa 'Albo Borsigiana', Anthurium regale, Philodendron Spiritus Sancti, Scindapsus pictus 'Mayari', crosses such as Philodendron Florida Beauty × Paraiso Verde Variegated. Variegation (chimeric, unstable) is a trait of the named form; humidity and difficulty are care columns. Stage (3-inch start, 6-inch pot, rooted or unrooted) is stated per listing [S9, S21].
- Etsy [S21] and Botachoku [S19] confirm that the tradable unit is a named form at a stage, with phytosanitary paperwork for cross-border sales, not a generic "houseplant".

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Harmonized System chapter 06 [S4].** 0601 bulbs, tubers, corms and rhizomes; 0602 other live plants, cuttings and slips, and mushroom spawn; 0603 cut flowers and buds; 0604 foliage, branches and grasses. The classifier divides by what is traded (propagule, living plant, cut flower, cut foliage), not by plant group, and files mushroom spawn with live plants.
- **Dutch trade [S23].** The VBN specifications are written for three product families: Cut Flowers, Houseplants and Garden Plants, with supplements per product group.
- **Cultivated-plant nomenclature [S24, S34, S8, S7, S31].** The ICNCP makes the cultivar the basic category, with Group and grex as the other categories; International Cultivar Registration Authorities (IAS for Araceae, ICPS for carnivorous plants, the RHS for orchid hybrids) keep registers. Orchid hybrids are named as grex (defined by parentage, e.g. Papilionanthe Miss Joaquim) with an optional Group and a clone epithet in single quotes. Trademarked commercial names stand beside cultivar epithets (Syringa 'Penda' BLOOMERANG) [S34].
- **Intellectual property [S32].** Plant patents, plant variety protection certificates and UPOV breeders' rights attach to named varieties; plant names can also be trademarks.
- **Quality marks [S37, S38].** AOS flower-quality awards (HCC 75–79, AM 80–89, FCC 90+ points) and the RHS Recommended: Award of Garden Merit (healthy, excellent performers, available, resistant, true to type).

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **RHS Plants by type [S25].** Alpines, annuals and biennials, aquatic plants, bulbs, cacti and succulents, carnivorous plants, climbers, conifers, ferns, grasses, heathers, hedges, houseplants, perennials, shrubs, trees; plus fruit, herbs and veg. Same mix of axes as Crocus [S1].
- **Wikipedia [S28, S33, S34].** "Ornamental plant": botanical forms (trees, shrubs, climbers, grasses, succulents, aquatics, perennials, annuals) versus non-botanical classifications (houseplants, bedding plants, hedges, cut-flower plants, foliage plants). "Houseplant" is defined by use, lists tropical and subtropical plants and succulents, and records name changes (snake plant now Dracaena trifasciata, formerly Sansevieria; string of pearls now Curio rowleyanus).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** (1) genus is the working unit for collectors and societies (AOS, IAS, ICPS, Mountain Crest, Engei); (2) the named form (cultivar, grex, selection) sits under genus or species, is registered by an authority and may be protected; (3) a plant is traded in states (bulb or seed, rooted plant, cut stem, culture) and trade bodies keep those apart (HS, VBN, Crocus, Interflora); (4) the specialist groups orchids, succulents and cacti, carnivorous plants, bonsai, aquatic plants, bulbs and fruit are recognised departments everywhere.
- **Split:** the general-plant tree. Crocus, RHS, PFLANZMICH, Engei and Takii all mix growth form, life cycle, propagule, crop use, habitat and setting in one list. Japan puts orchids, carnivorous and caudex plants inside "foliage", the West and Botachoku do not. Home Depot, Etsy and the RHS use a setting-first split (indoor plants against garden plants) that the owner's modifier rule forbids as a level. Aroids and orchids are grouped by family/alliance by their societies, but by "houseplant" by retailers.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Is it a living plant in a pot, a seed or bulb to plant, a cut stem, or a culture?** Trade classifications (HS 0601 to 0604 [S4]), trade specifications (VBN: cut flowers, houseplants, garden plants [S23]), Crocus (Bulbs, Seeds as top-level entries [S1]), Interflora (cut flowers separate from plants [S29]), Gardeners Dream (fresh cut against pot grown [S30]) and Etsy (seed packets mixed in only by search) all put this question first. It decides what the object is, how long it lives and how it is shipped.
2. **Which horticultural group or genus?** Orchid, aroid, succulent, carnivorous and bonsai buyers begin here (AOS alliances and genus list [S6], IAS popular genera [S7], Mountain Crest Types/Genera [S22], Botachoku category row [S19], Engei succulent genus list [S18]); mainstream buyers begin with a growth form (tree, shrub, perennial, climber) or a crop (apple, tomato).
3. **Species or named form.** Rare-plant buyers decide species then cultivar or variegated form [S9, S10, S21]; fruit buyers decide crop, then variety, then tree size and rootstock [S26, S27]; seed buyers decide crop then variety then seed type [S11, S12].
4. **Fit to the place and the owner.** Light, hardiness zone, pet safety, size, "easy to grow" [S1, S2, S26] are used as filters after the kind is chosen; they are setting and user modifiers.
5. **Stage and size.** Cutting, rooted start or established plant [S9]; pot size and plant height [S2, S9, S30]; tree size and rootstock [S26, S27]; bonsai size class [S15, S17].
6. **Occasion** (Valentine's, Mother's Day, Christmas, wedding) steers cut flowers and gift plants [S29, S30] but is a modifier.

No source starts with maker or price. The ordered choice is state sold, then group or genus, then species or named form, then size and stage.

## 3. Candidate structures

Common to all three: the maximum depth below the category is four (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form; conventions C5(a)). Region (hardiness zone, native range), setting (indoor, outdoor, balcony, aquarium), occasion, motive (collecting, gift), user (pet safe, beginner), price and rarity, origin (wild-collected, nursery-grown, tissue-cultured) and condition (variegation stability, plant stage) never split an inner level; they are tags, filters or ledger columns (Appendix A, C6). Pot size and plant height are attributes (C6); no horticultural size standard has been fetched, so the standard to name is open †.

**Where it breaks.**
1. *Tulip, three homes.* A dormant bulb (C3.2), a potted tulip in flower (C3.1; HS 0601 itself says "in growth or in flower" [S4]) and a cut tulip (C3.3). The rule that keeps one home per object: no roots and dormant = propagule; rooted in a medium and alive = growing plant (a plug plant, liner or rooted tissue-culture plantlet is rooted, so it is a growing plant in its group); cut = cut. Only an unrooted cutting or division is a propagule. The same genus then appears in three sub-families as three different kinds (C14: each is a different object, not one kind in several leaves), so a generated "by genus" view is needed.
2. *Air plants (Tillandsia).* Mountain Crest lists them under succulents [S22], Engei puts bromeliads in foliage plants [S18]; botanically they are epiphytic bromeliads †. The tests settle it: leaf 4 excludes Bromeliaceae and leaf 8 names them, so Tillandsia is leaf 8 (this disagrees with one retailer).
3. *Citrus and other fruit plants kept as houseplants.* A compact Meyer lemon is a houseplant on Wikipedia [S33] and a fruit tree at Stark Bro's [S26]. Under C14(a) the kind goes to the leaf whose test defines it (leaf 6, grown for edible fruit) whatever the setting, as setting is a modifier; a generated view lists it under each use.
4. *Bonsai against nursery trees.* A trained ficus is bonsai (leaf 3); a pre-bonsai or an untrained juniper is a tree or shrub (leaf 9 or 10). The test is whether the sale describes a trained bonsai form (style, size class, pot), which makes leaf 3 depend on the seller's description.
5. *Rose.* Cut rose (C3.3), bare-root or potted garden rose (leaf 10, kind "Rose" with `rose_class`), a rose in a pot sold as a gift (leaf 10, "occasion" is a modifier).

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (estimate, not counted).** About 500 to 800 if kinds are genus-level in specialist leaves and crop-level for edibles; 900 to 1,400 if species-level kinds are admitted for aroids, Hoya, Nepenthes and similar collector genera. Anchors from fetched pages: Araceae alone has about 150 genera and 4,500 species [S7]; AOS groups orchid genera in 16 alliances [S6]; Mountain Crest runs a long Types/Genera index for succulents alone [S22]; Stark Bro's carries 128 fruit-tree items across about a dozen crops [S26]; the True Leaf Asian-seed collection alone has 666 items across crop collections [S12]. Every added name needs an attested source (framework rule S6), so the real count grows only as pages are fetched.
- **Named forms (estimate).** Tens of thousands worldwide. Anchors: PFLANZMICH advertises "15.000+ Sorten" and 5,000+ items [S3]; Crocus lists several thousand plants [S1]; a single rare-plant shop lists named crosses by the dozen [S9]; each ICRA runs a register [S7, S31]. Recommendation: named forms are loaded as rows keyed to a register id (ICNCP epithet, grex, trade name), on demand, never pre-enumerated (**OWNER:** Q14, section 10). Each named form carries `maker_bound` (C2(a)): *no* for an open cultivar, grex or clone that many nurseries grow; *yes* where a trademark, plant patent or PVP certificate ties it to one holder (BLOOMERANG [S34]; protection facts [S32]). Colours, pot sizes, leaf counts and SKUs are never named forms (C2(b)).
- **Where depth concentrates.** (1) Orchids (grex, Group, clone are three nested ranks below genus [S8]); (2) aroids and other collector tropical foliage (species plus variegated clones [S7, S9, S10]); (3) fruit plants (variety, rootstock, tree form [S26, S27]); (4) roses and bulbs (rose classes: hybrid tea or floribunda [S18], daffodil divisions [S1]; both are controlled attributes of one kind); (5) seeds by crop and variety [S11, S12].
- **Leaf width.** *Broad:* tropical foliage plants, cacti and succulents, shrubs and climbers, perennials, annuals and bedding, vegetable and herb plants, seeds. *Medium:* orchids, trees, fruit and nut plants, cut flowers, bulbs. *Narrow:* carnivorous plants, bonsai, aquatic and bog plants, cuttings, cut trees, fungal spawn, ferment cultures.
- **Depth.** Four below the category fits all four sub-families. The only candidate for a fifth rank is genus above species; this memo recommends a *genus field* on every kind instead (section 8), because the cap is four. Grex, Group and clone are ranks inside the named-form level (field `rank`, parent link), not further levels.

## 6. Makers and brands

**Recommended type: incidental at kind level; identity at named-form level** (the originator or registrant is part of what a collector seeks, but the nursery that sells the plant is not).

- Nurseries and shops (Crocus, Stark Bro's, Mountain Crest, Greenboog, Takii, Engei) are sellers; a kind such as "Echeveria" or "apple tree" is not sought by seller [S1, S22, S26]. Seller-owned selections do exist ("Stark Bro's Exclusives" facet, 25 products [S26]); they are rows in the **lines and collections table** (C1) with the seller as maker, not a maker tier, and named forms point to them.
- Breeders, hybridisers and registrants are attested by the registers: the IAS cultivar registry for Araceae [S7], the RHS orchid hybrid register with parentage statements [S8], the ICPS register with published descriptions [S31], all governed by the ICNCP [S24, S34]. These are the rows a maker ledger would hold.
- Commercial names and IP: trademarked names sit beside cultivar epithets (Syringa 'Penda' BLOOMERANG [S34]); plant patents and PVP certificates (20 years; 25 for vines and trees) restrict propagation [S32]. These legal classes go in the **jurisdiction ledger** (C17): `subject_id` (named form), `jurisdiction`, `scheme` (plant patent, PVP certificate, UPOV breeders' right, trademark), `class`, `criteria` (term in years), `source_url`, `date`. Other legal classes C3 meets go in the same ledger and have no fetched source yet †: phytosanitary certification for cross-border sales (seen only on a marketplace listing [S21]), protected-species trade limits (the over-harvesting remark in [S33]), and the regimes for live tobacco and cannabis plants (section 9, P8).
- No platform lock-in. A rootstock sets mature size and vigour [S27] but does not govern compatibility with other purchases; the compatibility ledger (C13) has no C3 rows.

**Maker ledger: recommended, small, for originators and registrants only.** It uses the core schema of C1: `maker_id`; `name`; `native_name`; `aliases`; `maker_class`; `country`; `place`; `founded`; `ended`; `status`; `parent_id`; `known_for_kinds`; `scene_tags` (with `core` or `adjacent` strength, C8); `price_band` (C4(d); usually empty for an originator); `sources` (at least one fetched URL). **OWNER:** `maker_class` needs a value "breeder or registrant" that C1 does not list (review K2; Q11). C3 extensions (optional category columns, C1): `role` (breeder, hybridiser, originator, registrant, selection owner), `register_ids` (ICRA or orchid register and the register's id). Patent and PVP status is not a maker column; it is a jurisdiction-ledger row on the named form (C17). Named forms point to the originator (`originator_maker_id`) and carry `epithet`, `rank` (cultivar, Group, grex, clone, hybrid, trade name), `parent_named_form_id`, `maker_bound`, `register_id`. Known-for named forms are derived from the named-form ledger, not stored in a column (C1). Not recommended: a ledger of retail nurseries, which would only restate Appendix A's "brand and maker" modifier for shops.

## 8. Order inside a leaf (C7)

- **Sub-families** follow trade logic: C3.1 growing plants, C3.2 seeds and propagules, C3.3 fresh-cut plants, C3.4 living cultures (the order of the framework category line, with the HS bulbs/plants/cut sequence kept within it [S4]).
- **Leaves of C3.1** follow the ordered key in section 3 (specialist groups first), because the key doubles as the tie-breaker rule.
- **Kinds inside a leaf:** the canonical kind first, then alphabetical by accepted botanical name (crop name where the kind is a crop), with the genus as a field so that genera stay adjacent. The canonical kind of a leaf is the one that its authority gives its own category (for example Phalaenopsis in Engei's orchid list [S18]); where a leaf has no attested canonical kind, order is alphabetical only. Reasons for the secondary key: every society and seller uses an A–Z genus or crop index (AOS letters A–Z [S6], Baker Creek A–Z [S11], Takii and Engei gojūon lists [S18, S20]); botanical names are language-neutral across the Japanese, German and English pages above. Common names (peace lily, snake plant, kochoran) are aliases in the alias table (C9). Because genera are renamed (Sansevieria to Dracaena, Senecio rowleyanus to Curio, orchid genera split and merged [S8, S33]), each kind carries a synonym list in that table and the accepted-name authority (Kew World Checklist, as the RHS orchid register uses [S8]).
- **Named forms inside a kind:** by maker (the originator or registrant, in the order of the maker ledger's `name`), then by first-introduction or registration year as attested, then alphabetically by epithet where no year is attested. For orchids, the rank nesting is kept: grex first, then Group, then clone under its parent [S8], each set ordered as above.
- **Variants** (if ever loaded) go by `variant_code` or year.
- **Spec attributes** (bonsai size class, tree size, rootstock) are ordered small to large wherever they are displayed as a list [S17, S26, S27].

## 9. Boundaries

The closest cross-boundary objects and the precedence sentence that settles each (the first four sentences restate the framework key; marked *proposal* where this memo adds a rule the framework lacks).

**C2 Garden & Grounds (equipment to grow plants).** Objects: planters and pots, bonsai pots, compost and potting mixes, plant food, stakes, grow lights, greenhouses, plant stands (B1).
- *P1.* The plant is C3 and anything made for growing it is C2 (framework rule S3, step 29): a bonsai tree is C3, its pot, wire, tools and soil are C2, a plant stand is B1.
- *P2 (proposal).* **OWNER: Q27 (and Q15, kits).** A planted pot, kokedama or terrarium sold as one unit is filed as the plant when the plant is the item of greatest value (the framework's set rule in step 3 covers groceries only); the planter kept after the plant dies is C2. This is the memo's current recommendation. The review (F14) found that C2's memo files any living plant in a container, basket, kokedama ball or terrarium as C3 whatever the container's value ("the empty container is C2"), so a cheap succulent in a costly glazed pot gets two answers; the owner is asked to choose one rule for both categories.

**B4 Decor (decorative objects).** Objects: dried, preserved and artificial flowers and plants, festival decorations, Christmas ornaments, dried wreaths.
- *P3.* A fresh-cut stem, bouquet, wreath or garland of living plant material is C3; a dried, preserved or artificial one is B4 (step 6 note, step 7 note). ## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure.** Candidate A: four sub-families by *what is sold* (growing plants, seeds and propagules, fresh-cut plants, living cultures), leaves by *horticultural group as recognised by a register or trade department* (the ordered key in C3.1 gives each leaf a written membership test, and key order only breaks ties), kinds by *accepted botanical or crop name* (genus by default, species in collector leaves, one kind for spawn), named forms by *cultivar, Group, grex, clone, hybrid, trade-named or variegated selection* (grex, Group and clone are nested ranks within the level). **Maximum depth: four below the category** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form); genus is a field, not a level. Setting, use, occasion, region, hardiness, user, origin and condition are tags and generated views. Tier is consensus-ranked awards as tier entries on named forms, with measured specifications as attributes (section 5). Makers are incidental at kind level and identity at named-form level, with a small originator-registrant ledger in the C1 schema (section 6); legal classes go in the jurisdiction ledger.

**Owner decisions needed** (every place in the memo marked OWNER: points to one of these).

4. **Bonsai** (Q3c). Leaf of its own (Candidate A) or a named form/attribute set on trees and shrubs? Retailers and the Japanese marketplace give it its own department [S5, S15, S19]; the framework test is the object, and a trained bonsai is an object different from an untrained tree.

6. **Framework clarifications** (Q45b): Christmas trees (P4), plant-and-pot sets and terrariums (P2), preserved plants (P3), sprouting and microgreen seed (P6), live tobacco and cannabis plants (P8).
