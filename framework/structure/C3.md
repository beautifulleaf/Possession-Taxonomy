# C3 Plants & Living Cultures — structure memo (research draft)

*Stage 2 research memo, batch S2_C3. Research, not a decision: the owner approves structures later. Source ids [S1]–[S38] are the row numbers of `verify/out/S2_C3_sources.tsv`; every one is a page fetched in this batch. Call count: 15 searches and 38 fetches (caps 18 and 40). Evidence is strong for retail trees, collector groups and the cultivar-registration system; it is thin for living cultures (one spawn maker, one ferment-culture shop), aquatic plants (only society and marketplace headings) and non-Western markets other than Japan, Austria and Singapore (see section 10).*

## 0. Category

**Code and name:** C3 Plants & Living Cultures, domain C (grow, make and maintain).

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, placement key step 6): *a living plant or fungus, a living culture kept to grow or propagate (sourdough starter, kombucha culture, mushroom spawn), a fresh-cut plant, or a seed, bulb or spore for growing.* Category line: "living plants and fungi, fresh-cut flowers, seeds and bulbs, living cultures."

**Notes and precedence sentences in the framework that touch C3:**
- Step 6 note: cut vegetables, herbs, sprouts and microgreens sold to eat are step 3 (groceries, J4); a plant sold still growing in a pot, tray or mat is C3 even when sold to eat (potted basil, living microgreens). Dried and artificial flowers are B4.
- Step 1 (outside scope) note: living cultures kept to grow or propagate (sourdough starter, kombucha and kefir cultures, mushroom spawn) are step 6 "even when what they make is eaten"; animals are OUT, so live feeders, eggs for hatching and aquarium fish are not here.
- Step 3 groceries: a plant sold still growing is C3; nuts, seeds and dried fruit, raising agents and dried yeast are J5; kefir as a drink is J3; vitamins and supplements are H2; bird seed and fat balls, hay and forage are B10.
- Step 7 (natural specimens): herbarium sheets are F6; dried flowers are B4 (so a pressed or dried plant is not C3).
- S3 and step 29: anything made for growing plants is C2 (planters and pots, plant care products, grow lights, compost, greenhouses), plant stands are B1. The plant is C3, the pot is C2.
- Step 14 note: "Christmas trees and ornaments" are B4 as festival decorations. This collides with step 6 for a fresh-cut or potted real tree (section 9 and 10).
- Step 27 (B9): tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products "in any form" are B9; step 6 is tried first, so a live plant or a seed for growing is C3 by key order (section 9).
- Step 4 (G2): land and its permanent structures are property; a tree standing on a plot is part of the land until it is sold as a plant.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

### 1.1 Retailers and marketplaces (US, UK, Germany, Austria, Japan, Singapore)

- **UK, Crocus [S1].** The Plants tree is a single list with counts: Perennials 1308, Bulbs 911, Shrubs 845, Seeds 561, Indoor plants 417, Connoisseurs collection 404, Climbers 347, Bedding plants 329, Trees 244, Fruit 239, Vegetables 237, Large plants for instant impact 213, Pond plants 197, Alpines & rockeries 175, Roses 158, Plants & pots 141, Grasses 117, Plant combinations 97, Herbs 94, Plants for gifts 63, Wildflowers 61, Hedging 58, Ferns 48, Conifers 45, Exotics 36, Bamboo 4. One list therefore mixes growth form (shrubs, climbers, trees), life cycle (bedding), propagule (bulbs, seeds), crop use (fruit, vegetables, herbs), habitat (pond, alpines), setting (indoor), use (hedging) and size. A second block of value facets (easy to grow, evergreen, fast growing, berries, climate resilient, native) runs across the whole tree, and daffodils are filtered by the RHS division number.
- **US, Home Depot Indoor Plants [S2].** Size tiers first (Large over 48 in, Small 24 in), then "Plant Types" by common name (peace lily, snake plant, bird of paradise, succulents, bonsai trees, palm, money tree, fern, monstera), then "Plant Features" (air purifying, cat safe, dog safe, low light, low maintenance, tropical). The kind a shopper names is the common name at genus or species level; safety, light and care are filters.
- **US, Mountain Crest Gardens [S22].** Succulent specialist: Collections (cactus, hens and chicks, sedum, echeveria, easy to grow, pet safe, cuttings and bare root, plants for collectors), Environment (house/office, outdoors), feature rows (hanging and trailing, miniature, rosettes, fuzzy, caudiciforms and bulbs, mimicry plants, strings of things, air plants), Color, and a "Types / Genera" index (Aloe, Echeveria, Haworthia, Kalanchoe, Sempervivum, Sedum, Senecio, Tillandsia and many more) for succulents, a second one for houseplants (Aglaonema, Alocasia, Pothos, Snake plants, Syngonium, Tradescantia). Genus is the working index; "over 1,000 unique varieties sold annually".
- **US, Stark Bro's (fruit) [S26].** Fruit trees by crop (apple 71, pear 13, citrus 12, cherry 8, nectarine 7, and apricot, avocado, banana, fig, guava, jujube, peach), with a Mature Tree Size facet (Semi-Dwarf 79, Dwarf 58, Standard 23, plus columnar) and a hardiness-zone lookup; the buyer's guide opens with tree size, not variety. A "Stark Bro's Exclusives" product-type facet (25) marks seller-owned selections.
- **US seeds, Baker Creek [S11] and True Leaf Market [S12].** Baker Creek: Bulbs, Flower Seeds, Herb Seeds, Vegetable Seeds and an A–Z index by crop (tomato, eggplant, zinnia). True Leaf: crop collections (daikon, turnip, watermelon) plus facets Seed Type (non-GMO 664, heirloom 441, organic 82, hybrid 186, open pollinated 462) and Intended Use (vegetable 505, herb 129, microgreens 81, fruit 58, sprouting 34, flower 29, cover crop, grain), with badges for heirloom, organic, AAS winner and container-suitable.
- **UK, specialist retail [S27, S30].** Chris Bowers sells apples by tree form (fan, espalier, stepover, cordon, supercolumn, dwarf) and rootstock (M27 miniature, M9 dwarf, M26 semi-vigorous, M106 vigorous, M111/M25 very vigorous). Gardeners Dream filters Christmas trees by Plant Type "Fresh Cut" (3) against "Pot Grown" (6) and by height 1–6 ft, with species named (Nordmann, Noble and Fraser fir; Norway and blue spruce).
- **UK, Interflora [S29].** Fresh cut flowers by flower type (roses, lilies, peonies, sunflowers), colour and luxury, with a separate Occasions tree (anniversary, wedding, thank you, sympathy) and a distinct "Gifts & Plants" block (All Plants, hampers). Occasion is a navigation axis, plants and cut flowers are different departments.
- **Germany and Austria [S3, S5].** PFLANZMICH (nursery): Hecken, Laubgehölze, Nadelgehölze, Bodendecker, Kletterpflanzen, Rhododendren, Bäume, Obstbäume, Stauden, Samen, Zwiebeln, Gemüse- und Kräutersaat, Rosen, with quick finders Garten / Balkon / Zimmer and "Raritäten". Etsy's "live house plants" mixes plants, bulbs and seed packets, and lists named forms with leaf count and phytosanitary certificate.

### 1.2 Enthusiast societies and communities

- **Orchids, American Orchid Society [S6].** 16 alliances (Angraecum, Bulbophyllum, Catasetum, Cattleya, Coelogyne, Cymbidium, Dendrobium, Maxillaria, Oncidium, Phalaenopsis, Pleurothallid, Slipper, Stanhopea, Vanda, Zygopetalum, Miscellaneous) and an A–Z genus list. The unit of knowledge is the genus; alliances are a convenience grouping above it.
- **Aroids, International Aroid Society [S7].** The family Araceae has about 150 genera and over 4,500 species; the society's "popular genera" are Philodendron, Monstera, Anthurium, Alocasia, Colocasia, Syngonium, Dieffenbachia, Spathiphyllum, Aglaonema, Amorphophallus, each with notable species and named forms (Philodendron erubescens 'Pink Princess', Alocasia reginula 'Black Velvet', Alocasia amazonica 'Polly'). Collectors name the species.
- **Carnivorous plants, ICPS [S31].** Denomination classes are the genera in their widest circumscription, grouped by family (Sarraceniaceae, Droseraceae, Nepenthaceae, Lentibulariaceae and others).
- **Community [S36].** r/houseplants excludes plants that live outside full-time and cut flowers, sends plant trading to a separate community, and lists related communities for succulents, bonsai, terrariums and air plants: the community split is by horticultural group, not by indoor use alone.
- **Bonsai [S16, S17].** Enthusiast references divide bonsai by height class (mame, shohin, chuhin, omono; with komono, katade-mochi, imperial and the "hands" system), by trunk count (tan-kan to kyū-kan) and by style (formal upright, informal upright, slant, cascade, forest, raft, literati, windswept, broom). The Japanese tradition grows outdoor temperate trees; indoor bonsai use tropicals such as Ficus and jade.

### 1.3 Collectors and the resale market

- Rare aroid shops and guides [S9, S10] sell and rank by species and named form: Monstera deliciosa 'Albo Borsigiana', Anthurium regale, Philodendron Spiritus Sancti, Scindapsus pictus 'Mayari', crosses such as Philodendron Florida Beauty × Paraiso Verde Variegated. Variegation (chimeric, unstable) is a trait of the named form; humidity and difficulty are care columns. Stage (3-inch start, 6-inch pot, rooted or unrooted) is stated per listing [S9, S21].
- Etsy [S21] and Botachoku [S19] confirm that the tradable unit is a named form at a stage, with phytosanitary paperwork for cross-border sales, not a generic "houseplant".

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **Harmonized System chapter 06 [S4].** 0601 bulbs, tubers, corms and rhizomes; 0602 other live plants, cuttings and slips, and mushroom spawn; 0603 cut flowers and buds; 0604 foliage, branches and grasses. The classifier divides by what is traded (propagule, living plant, cut flower, cut foliage), not by plant group, and files mushroom spawn with live plants.
- **Dutch trade [S23].** The VBN specifications are written for three product families: Cut Flowers, Houseplants and Garden Plants, with supplements per product group.
- **Cultivated-plant nomenclature [S24, S34, S8, S7, S31].** The ICNCP makes the cultivar the basic category, with Group and grex as the other categories; International Cultivar Registration Authorities (IAS for Araceae, ICPS for carnivorous plants, the RHS for orchid hybrids) keep registers. Orchid hybrids are named as grex (defined by parentage, e.g. Papilionanthe Miss Joaquim) with an optional Group and a clone epithet in single quotes. Trademarked commercial names stand beside cultivar epithets (Syringa 'Penda' BLOOMERANG) [S34].
- **Intellectual property [S32].** Plant patents, plant variety protection certificates and UPOV breeders' rights attach to named varieties; plant names can also be trademarks.
- **Quality marks [S37, S38].** AOS flower-quality awards (HCC 75–79, AM 80–89, FCC 90+ points) and the RHS Recommended: Award of Garden Merit (healthy, excellent performers, available, resistant, true to type).

### 1.5 Museums and reference works

- **RHS Plants by type [S25].** Alpines, annuals and biennials, aquatic plants, bulbs, cacti and succulents, carnivorous plants, climbers, conifers, ferns, grasses, heathers, hedges, houseplants, perennials, shrubs, trees; plus fruit, herbs and veg. Same mix of axes as Crocus [S1].
- **Wikipedia [S28, S33, S34].** "Ornamental plant": botanical forms (trees, shrubs, climbers, grasses, succulents, aquatics, perennials, annuals) versus non-botanical classifications (houseplants, bedding plants, hedges, cut-flower plants, foliage plants). "Houseplant" is defined by use, lists tropical and subtropical plants and succulents, and records name changes (snake plant now Dracaena trifasciata, formerly Sansevieria; string of pearls now Curio rowleyanus).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree:** (1) genus is the working unit for collectors and societies (AOS, IAS, ICPS, Mountain Crest, Engei); (2) the named form (cultivar, grex, selection) sits under genus or species, is registered by an authority and may be protected; (3) a plant is traded in states (bulb or seed, rooted plant, cut stem, culture) and trade bodies keep those apart (HS, VBN, Crocus, Interflora); (4) the specialist groups orchids, succulents and cacti, carnivorous plants, bonsai, aquatic plants, bulbs and fruit are recognised departments everywhere.
- **Split:** the general-plant tree. Crocus, RHS, PFLANZMICH, Engei and Takii all mix growth form, life cycle, propagule, crop use, habitat and setting in one list. Japan puts orchids, carnivorous and caudex plants inside "foliage", the West and Botachoku do not. Home Depot, Etsy and the RHS use a setting-first split (indoor plants against garden plants) that the owner's modifier rule forbids as a level. Aroids and orchids are grouped by family/alliance by their societies, but by "houseplant" by retailers.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

1. **Is it a living plant in a pot, a seed or bulb to plant, a cut stem, or a culture?** Trade classifications (HS 0601 to 0604 [S4]), trade specifications (VBN: cut flowers, houseplants, garden plants [S23]), Crocus (Bulbs, Seeds as top-level entries [S1]), Interflora (cut flowers separate from plants [S29]), Gardeners Dream (fresh cut against pot grown [S30]) and Etsy (seed packets mixed in only by search) all put this question first. It decides what the object is, how long it lives and how it is shipped.
2. **Which horticultural group or genus?** Orchid, aroid, succulent, carnivorous and bonsai buyers begin here (AOS alliances and genus list [S6], IAS popular genera [S7], Mountain Crest Types/Genera [S22], Botachoku category row [S19], Engei succulent genus list [S18]); mainstream buyers begin with a growth form (tree, shrub, perennial, climber) or a crop (apple, tomato).
3. **Species or named form.** Rare-plant buyers decide species then cultivar or variegated form [S9, S10, S21]; fruit buyers decide crop, then variety, then tree size and rootstock [S26, S27]; seed buyers decide crop then variety then seed type [S11, S12].
4. **Fit to the place and the owner.** Light, hardiness zone, pet safety, size, "easy to grow" [S1, S2, S26] are used as filters after the kind is chosen; they are setting and user modifiers.
5. **Stage and size.** Cutting, rooted start or established plant [S9]; pot size and plant height [S2, S9, S30]; tree size and rootstock [S26, S27]; bonsai size class [S15, S17].
6. **Occasion** (Valentine's, Mother's Day, Christmas, wedding) steers cut flowers and gift plants [S29, S30] but is a modifier.

No source starts with maker or price. The ordered choice is state sold, then group or genus, then species or named form, then size and stage.
