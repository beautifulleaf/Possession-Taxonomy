## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J2 Fish & Seafood.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_7, step 3, second branch): *fish and seafood, fresh, frozen, dried, smoked, salted or tinned, including shellfish and crustaceans sold alive, molluscs (snails included), roe and caviar? J2 Fish & Seafood.* Category line: "fish and seafood as bought, fresh, frozen or preserved, including live shellfish and crustaceans, roe and caviar." Under R-54 (key wording R-16(bb)) the live-animal exception also names edible snails, so the live-animal part of this line reads "live shellfish, crustaceans and edible snails sold for eating".

**Notes and precedence sentences that name or touch J2:**
- Step 1 (OUT list): a live animal is OUT "except shellfish and crustaceans sold alive to be eaten (oysters, mussels, clams, crabs, lobsters), which are groceries". R-54 / R-16(bb) amends this to "live shellfish, crustaceans and edible snails sold for eating are J2"; snails sold as pets or garden animals are OUT, like other animals. Curing, smoking, drying, salting, tinning, freezing or plain cooking of one main ingredient "does not make a food prepared"; "smoked and tinned fish" are named groceries. A prepared food is "made by cooking or combining ingredients into something eaten as it is" (ready meals, dumplings, sandwiches, salads and other prepared dishes): left to the Culinary taxonomy.
- Step 3 notes: a product is filed by what it is, not by what it contains: sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes and cooking fats are J5 "whatever they are made from (fish sauce, oyster sauce, stock cubes)". "Dried or cured meat and fish are groceries even when sold salted, roasted or as snacks." Soups, stews and curries in tins are ready meals. A set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value, and is OUT if that item is a prepared food. "A grocery that Culinary also describes (kimchi, smoked salmon, ham) is filed once here, as the product as bought, and Culinary links to it."
- Step 3 sentences added by decisions (v0.7): a spread, paste or dip eaten with other food (potted shrimp, taramasalata) is J5 whatever it is made from, and a pâté, terrine or galantine sold as a loaf or slice to be cut is J2 for fish (K-44); a shaped product made mainly of fish, seasoned but not coated, filled or sauced, is a grocery in J2 (fish-paste products), while coated (breaded or battered), filled or sauced products are prepared and OUT (K-45); a mix of kinds from one category goes under the kind that makes up half or more of its weight, otherwise under a final kind "mixed <class>" (K-46); snails are J2 molluscs (K-47); live fish sold to eat stay OUT, and shark fin and other protected-species goods are filed by what they are, their CITES status going to the C17 ledger (K-26).
- Step 3 pantry branch (J5): tinned and jarred fruit, vegetables, pulses and tomatoes, and "dried mushrooms and dried seaweed" are J5. J4 holds "edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen", "not dressed, sauced, fried or coated". J1 holds meat, poultry and game.
- Step 2 (H2): vitamins, supplements and sports nutrition are H2. Step 3 closing note: food made for animals, kept or wild, is B10 (including fish food); live aquarium fish are OUT (BRIEF.md).
- S3 (parts follow the whole) and S5 (test the object, not the motive) apply to nothing special here; S6 (every added name has an attested source) governs the kind list.
- Appendix A: region, brand and maker, price band and quality tier, occasion, motive, origin (natural, cultured), how acquired, condition and edition are modifiers. "Origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown)" is the modifier name for wild against farmed. Farmed, wild, Scottish and similar words are therefore tags and attributes, never part of a kind label.
- BRIEF.md: groceries are in, "judged by the quality of the ingredient (for example high-quality meat: breed, cut, grade, origin, age)". Grocery products that Culinary also holds "are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind". Culinary's matching leaves are SV-H4-1 Dried & Salted Fish & Seafood, H4-2 Smoked & Canned Fish, H4-3 Fermented & Pickled Seafood, H4-4 Roe & Caviar and H4-5 Fish-Paste Products (FRAMEWORK_v1.md, read only to plan links; the per-kind plan is in section 9, point 8).

### 1.2 Enthusiast and specialist-guide sources

- **Raw-fish culture (Japan-focused English reference).** Sushipedia treats tuna as eight species of which three dominate Japan's sushi and sashimi trade, and classes the flesh by fat into akami (lean), chūtoro (medium) and ōtoro (very fatty) [S36]. It notes that "tsuna" in Japanese means tinned tuna, not the fish [S36]. Its grade guide says "sashimi quality" is a trade and marketing term, not a legally defined standard [S35].
- **Specialist retailers' guides (USA).** Sashimi DC says "sashimi-grade" has no legal definition and cites a meta-analysis of 4,179 US seafood samples with 39.1% mislabelling, species substitution in 26.2% overall and 42.5% for the most fraud-prone species, tuna and red snapper [S37]. Fulton Fish Market says there is "no official, regulated standard" for the terms [S38].
- **Caviar and roe guides.** Roe is divided by source fish: sturgeon caviar (Beluga, Osetra, Sevruga, Kaluga), ikura by salmon species (chum, coho, king, pink), trout roe, tobiko (flying fish), masago (capelin), herring roe [S31][S32]. "Caviar" strictly means sturgeon roe; other roe is "roe" [S33]. Grade words (Grade 1, Grade 2, Premium, Royal, Imperial) are producer terms with no universal scale [S30][S34]; "Russian" and "Iranian" grade name curing styles [S34].

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS chapter 03** divides by form for fish: 0301 live, 0302 fresh or chilled, 0303 frozen, 0304 fillets and other meat, 0305 dried, salted or smoked; then by animal group: 0306 crustaceans, 0307 molluscs, 0308 other aquatic invertebrates, 0309 flours and meals fit for food. Crustacean and mollusc headings each hold every state (live, fresh, frozen, dried, salted, smoked, cooked). Canned, marinated, breaded and cooked preparations go to chapter 16 (canned tuna, canned crab meat, canned oysters, breaded squid rings are the examples) [S21][S23][S24]. Under 0302 the sub-headings are species: five tuna species each have their own [S22]. Customs headings are evidence only, not levels.
- **CITES and EU rules on caviar.** All sturgeon and paddlefish are listed; caviar containers carry a label with a three-letter species code (HUS Beluga, STE Sevruga, DAU Kaluga, MIX for pressed caviar of mixed species, a code for hybrids), source W or C, country, year; "pressed, pasteurized" and "malossol" caviar are all covered; in the EU and several countries the word "caviar" is restricted by law to sturgeon and paddlefish roe [S26].
- **FAO.** The sturgeon chapter lists three caviar types (Beluga, Osetra, Sevruga), grades (grade one, grade two, pressed "payusnaya"), Beluga grain categories 000, 00, 0 by size and colour, Osetra "royal", and the salt term malossol; it names salmon, trout, carp, pike, tuna, mullet, cod, lumpfish and flying-fish roes as "caviar substitutes" and cod roe sold fresh or smoked [S25].
- **MSC.** Two standards: the Fisheries Standard (wild capture) and the Chain of Custody Standard (the blue label), plus a seaweed standard with ASC [S27]. Waitrose and Ocado product names carry MSC and ASC marks [S2][S5].
- **Codex.** The list page and a Codex fish-standards webinar both returned only headers or background [S28][S29]; both are **not usable** as evidence (they are marked so in the source table, section 10.3). Codex standard titles (for example for canned tuna or canned sardines) are therefore not attested in this batch (coverage gap).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:** (1) The first split is the animal group (fish, crustaceans, molluscs, other invertebrates) or the form (live, fresh, frozen, dried, salted, smoked, tinned), and both are used by every source family [S1][S10][S14][S19][S21]. (2) Species is the identity below that: Fischkaufhaus A to Z, Rakuten genres, HS sub-headings, Woolworths guide [S10][S17][S22][S8]. (3) Roe and caviar are set apart as their own group by the retailers, FAO, CITES and the framework itself [S4][S12][S25][S26]. (4) Fresh against frozen keeps the same product names [S1][S2]; the sashimi-grade question turns on freezing and handling, not on a legal grade [S37][S38].
**Split:**
- **Form first or animal first.** Fischkaufhaus, Waitrose and the Chinese classes lead with form [S10][S2][S19]; Gyomu and Rakuten lead with animal group [S14][S17]; HS does both (form for fish, group for the rest) [S21].
- **Species groups.** Waitrose groups salmon with trout, Ocado groups trout with tuna and salmon alone; Ocado has both "Cod, Haddock & Pollock" and "White Fish" as sibling groups, Waitrose has one "Cod, Haddock & White Fish" [S1][S5]. No stable species-group level exists.
- **Fresh against frozen.** HS, the Chinese classes and the retail aisles separate them [S21][S20][S2]; product names do not [S1][S2].
- **Where coated and mixed products go.** Retailers keep breaded fish, fishcakes and ready-to-eat fish in the fish aisle [S1][S2][S7]; HS sends preparations to chapter 16 [S21].
- **Seaweed.** Rakuten and the Chinese classes keep dried seaweed with marine products [S18][S19]; the framework puts it in J4 and J5.
- **Freshwater and sea.** Chinese classes divide by origin habitat [S19]; the retailers do not.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence is mixed because retailers show navigation, not purchase order, and no fetched store exposes a species or form filter (Waitrose and Ocado filters are only Dietary & Lifestyle, own brands, offers, brands [S1][S5]). The departments themselves show the order:
1. **Storage state and form** (live, chilled, frozen, smoked, tinned): Waitrose splits chilled and frozen into separate departments [S1][S2]; Fischkaufhaus's form tabs come first [S10]; the Chinese classes and the HS fish headings are form-first [S19][S21].
2. **Species** (salmon, cod, tuna, prawn): every retailer has species or species-group categories [S1][S5][S10][S17], and the specialist guides assume the buyer chooses a species and then a cut [S36][S37].
3. **Cut or presentation** (whole, fillet, steak, loin, peeled, shell-on): "Fish Fillets" is a department child in both Waitrose aisles [S1][S2]; Fischkaufhaus has Ganze Fische against Premium Filets [S10]; HS gives fillets their own heading [S21].
4. **Quality questions** in the guides: Is it the species it says? (39.1% mislabelling [S37]); can it be eaten raw? (no legal grade [S37][S38]); what does the grade mean? (no universal scale [S30][S34]); wild or farmed, and is it certified? (MSC, ASC marks on product names [S2][S5]); how was it cured (malossol, pressed [S25][S34])?
5. **Maker** matters least: brands are a filter, not a navigation level [S5].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's limit of four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). "Fresh, frozen and live" is treated as a stored attribute `storage_state` in all three (reason in 4b; decided in J-5, with JC4 and CV-42).

### Candidate A (decided, J-4): form, then animal group, then species

- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: form in which the product is sold** (raw flesh or whole animal; smoked; salted, cured, dried or fermented; tinned, jarred or marinated; eggs; formed fish-paste products and pâtés). The source is the form-first navigation of Fischkaufhaus, the Waitrose aisle split, the Chinese classes and HS for fish [S10][S2][S19][S21]. A preserving process or a pack defines a sub-family here only because it makes a different product that buyers compare on its own (smoked, tinned, cured); a pack that keeps the same product is an attribute (JC4, CV-42). Formed fish-paste products and fish pâtés are a form of their own (K-45).
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis: animal group** (finfish, crustaceans, molluscs, other aquatic invertebrates), used only where a sub-family holds more than about 12 kinds (C5(b)); a smaller sub-family stores kinds with `leaf = null`. In the roe sub-family the leaf axis is the source fish group, the line CITES and EU law draw [S26]. In J2.6 the kinds are by product type and the sub-family has no leaf. The animal-group split is the one all families of source agree on [S14][S17][S19][S21]. Species groups (white fish, oily fish) are not a level because Waitrose and Ocado disagree on them [S1][S5]; they are a generated `species_group` view.
- **Level 3, kind. Axis: species or commercial type** (Atlantic salmon, sockeye salmon, cod, banana prawn, oyster, Beluga caviar, smoked salmon). Kind labels carry no modifier (no "farmed", "Scottish", "wild").
- **Level 4, named form.** The tuna fat cuts (J-1: every other fish cut is an attribute), cures and protected or traditional forms (ōtoro, kipper, Matjes), each with `maker_bound yes | no`. Specifications, grades and certifications are not named forms (C2(b)): they are attributes with the standard named (C4(b)) or tier entries (C4(a), (c)).
- **Sub-families:**
  - J2.1 Fresh, frozen & live seafood (leaves: finfish; crustaceans; molluscs; other invertebrates). A mix of seafood is not a leaf: see P6.
  - J2.2 Smoked fish & seafood (no leaf)
  - J2.3 Salted, cured, dried & fermented fish & seafood (no leaf, or leaf by animal group if it passes 12 kinds)
  - J2.4 Tinned, jarred & marinated fish & seafood (no leaf at first)
  - J2.5 Roe & caviar (leaves: sturgeon and paddlefish caviar; salmonid roe; other fish roe)
  - J2.6 Fish-paste products & pâtés (no leaf; kinds by product type; K-45)
  - No "Parts and accessories" sub-family: J has none (C5(d), CV-17).

### Candidate B: animal group, then form, then species

- Level 1 sub-family. Axis: animal group (finfish; crustaceans; molluscs; other invertebrates; plus roe and caviar as a fifth class, a stated exception).
- Level 2 leaf. Axis: form (raw fresh/frozen/live; smoked; salted/dried; tinned/marinated).
- Level 3 kind: species or commercial type. Level 4: named form.
- Examples (12 kinds): Finfish > Raw > Atlantic salmon, Cod, Bluefin tuna; Finfish > Smoked > Cold-smoked salmon, Smoked mackerel; Finfish > Tinned > Tinned tuna, Tinned sardines; Crustaceans > Raw > Tiger prawn, Lobster; Molluscs > Raw > Oyster, Scallop; Molluscs > Tinned > (tinned oysters, mussels).
- Support: HS (crustaceans and molluscs each one heading), Gyomu, Rakuten, the species-group lists [S21][S14][S17].
- Breaks: (1) *Mixed seafood* and *assorted tins* cross animal groups at the top level, so they need a sixth sub-family; (2) roe is an animal part, not an animal group, so the top axis is already mixed; (3) a smoked-salmon buyer compares across smokehouses, so the smoked kinds are spread over four sub-families instead of one aisle; (4) *dried sea cucumber*, *abalone*, *dried scallop* form tiny leaves under molluscs and echinoderms (each under 12 kinds).

### Candidate C: species group first, forms as kinds (the Ocado and Rakuten pattern)

- Level 1 sub-family: species group (salmonids; white fish; flatfish; oily fish; tuna and billfish; other marine fish; freshwater fish; prawns and shrimps; crabs and lobsters; bivalves; cephalopods; roe).
- Level 2 leaf: form (fresh or frozen, smoked, dried, tinned). Level 3 kind: species-and-form. Level 4: named form.
- Examples (10 kinds): Salmonids > Smoked > Cold-smoked salmon; Salmonids > Roe > Salmon roe; White fish > Raw > Cod, Haddock; Oily fish > Tinned > Tinned sardines; Tuna > Raw > Bluefin tuna; Tuna > Tinned > Tinned tuna; Prawns > Raw > Tiger prawn; Bivalves > Raw > Oyster, Mussel.
- Breaks: (1) the species-group boundaries are not stable across sources (Waitrose and Ocado group trout differently; Ocado has Cod/Haddock/Pollock and White Fish side by side) [S1][S5], and no authoritative list was reached to settle them; (2) dried goods such as sea cucumber, abalone and fish maw have no species group in the retail lists [S19]; (3) caviar by source fish does not fit a "salmonid" or "sturgeon" group without inventing a roe group; (4) a species with five forms appears in five places.

**Decided (J-4):** Candidate A, with the evidence that form leads in Fischkaufhaus, the Waitrose aisles and the Chinese classes [S10][S2][S19], animal group is the one stable second split, and species groups are views. Candidate B is the nearest alternative and stays the documented alternative; Candidate A is decided (J-4).

## 5. Quality and tier

**Recognised tier ordering: none that is spec-ranked; two consensus-ranked pockets; the rest is preference with certification attributes.** Choose: *preference*, with the pockets below. Tier never sits on a kind (C4(a)).

- **Caviar grade.** FAO describes grade one, grade two and pressed caviar, and for Beluga the grain categories 000 (light grey, dearest), 00 and 0, citing US Customs Service 1997; Osetra grade one includes "royal" (golden) [S25]. Seller guides say there is "no single universal consumer grading scale", that Grade 1, Premium, Royal and Imperial are producer terms, and that there is "no real oversight and regulations regarding the naming" [S30][S34]. So the ordering is **consensus-ranked within a producer, unranked across producers**. Storage, following C2(b), C4 and CV-40: (a) the FAO grain and colour categories 000, 00, 0 and "royal" are the attribute `grain_grade`, with the scheme named (FAO, quoting US Customs 1997 [S25]) and registered as a JC1 `standard` row whose value is attached to the kind or named form; (b) a producer's own grade (Grade 1, Premium, Royal, Imperial) is a maker's own ladder (C4(c)(iv)): `line_rank` in the lines table and a dated tier entry with `scheme` = the producer and the producer's line as `subject`, ranked only inside that producer and entered on one fetched source (C4(e)), or a `platform_tag` when a retailer names it; (c) neither is a named form and neither sits on the kind. Pressed caviar (payusnaya) stays a kind. Each grading scale is recorded as a scheme the category uses, never as a value for one tin (C16).
- **Tuna cut grades.** Akami, chūtoro and ōtoro are ordered by fat and texture in the Japanese trade [S36]. Consensus-ranked; they are cuts, so they are non-maker named forms under Bluefin tuna (and the other tunas where the trade uses the words), the one exception to "fish cut is an attribute" (J-1). Their stored order follows C7; the fat ladder is a generated view.
- **"Sashimi grade" or "sushi grade".** No legal definition in the US sources [S37][S38] and the Sushipedia guide calls it a trade and marketing term [S35]. Not a tier: stored as an attribute `raw_eating_asserted` (seller claim, dated, with the handling stated: frozen to a time and temperature, ikejime, never frozen).
- **Certifications.** MSC (wild capture, with a Chain of Custody mark) and ASC (farmed) are pass or fail marks [S27][S2][S5]. Per C4(b) they are attributes with the standard named, not tiers. They sit on the named form or product, not on the maker.
- **Premium own-label lines** (Waitrose No.1, M&S Collection) and platform tags such as "Responsibly Sourced" are retailer segment labels: `platform_tag`, not tier (C4(c)) [S1][S5].
- **Price band.** C4(d) bands are in USD per item. For groceries the price that shops show is per kilogram or per 100 g [S1][S5][S13], for example £2.11 per 100 g for herring in a jar and €399.80 per kg for whitefish caviar [S4][S12]. J2 carries the **unit-price band** (`unit_price_band`, USD per kg, all J2 products being solids, derived from a dated, sourced list price; bands set at first data load; JC2, CV-15). The C4(d) item band is kept only by J7 (JC2).
- Spec ladders that are measured on the object belong in attributes with the standard named: caviar salt level, size grades for prawns (counts per kilo), tin size. No fetched source gave a numeric size-grade scheme, so none is recorded here.

## 9. Boundaries

1. **J1 Meat & Poultry.** Closest cross-boundary objects: frogs' legs, softshell turtle, bullfrog (the Chinese list files them as "other freshwater animals" [S19]), edible insects, snails. *P-J1 (K-47): land and sea snails sold to eat (escargot, whelks) are molluscs and so J2.1; a live land snail sold alive for eating is J2.1 like live shellfish (step 1 exception, R-54), while a snail sold as a pet or garden animal is OUT; frogs' legs, turtle meat and edible insects are J1, whose branch now reads "meat, poultry, game and other land animals eaten (frogs, insects)".* A fish sausage or fish ham made mainly of fish belongs in J2 (J2.6 when shaped and not coated, filled or sauced, K-45); J1's P2 and this memo now agree.
2. **J5 Pantry Staples.** Closest objects: fish sauce, oyster sauce, shrimp paste, dashi stock, stock cubes, dried seaweed, pickled-fish pastes and dips (taramasalata), potted shrimp. *P-J5: A sauce, paste, dip, spread or stock made from fish or shellfish is J5 (framework step 3), and fermented seafood sold as a condiment (garum, or bagoong or jeotgal sold as a paste or sauce) is J5; salted whole fish or pieces sold to be cooked or eaten as the fish itself are J2.3, and whole or in-piece salted-fermented seafood sold to eat is J2.3 too (J-5; kind 69, provisional until attested).* Potted shrimp and taramasalata are J5 spreads whatever they are made from (K-44); a fish pâté or terrine sold as a loaf or slice to be cut is J2.6 (kind 68 †). ## 10. Recommendation (decided)

**Decided structure (one paragraph).** Candidate A (J-4). Category J2; sub-family by the form in which the product is sold (J2.1 fresh, frozen & live; J2.2 smoked; J2.3 salted, cured, dried & fermented; J2.4 tinned, jarred & marinated; J2.5 roe & caviar; J2.6 fish-paste products & pâtés); leaf by animal group where a sub-family passes about 12 kinds (finfish, crustaceans, molluscs, other invertebrates; for roe, the source fish group; none in J2.2, J2.4 and J2.6); kind by species or commercial type, or by product type in J2.6; named form for the tuna fat cuts, cures, and protected or regional forms. **Maximum depth: four levels below the category.** Storage state, cut (tuna fat cuts excepted), wild or farmed, certification, origin, size grade, pack medium, caviar grain grade and caviar cure are attributes; `scientific_name` is required; the grain grade is a JC1 `standard` row; a producer's caviar grade is a tier entry on its line. Tier is preference with certification attributes and two consensus-ranked ladders (tuna fat cuts, caviar grades). Price is the `unit_price_band`. The maker ledger is the shared one, uncapped, with `supply_role` values; scene and `tradition` tags carry a definition source; legal classes (the word "caviar", CITES, shark fin) are C17 rows. Fish sauces, pastes and spreads are J5; seaweed is J4 fresh and J5 dried; coated, filled and sauced fish is OUT; live fish are OUT.

**Remaining risks.**
- Sourcing is thin in places (no collector or museum page, no US, Spanish, French or Korean tree, Codex titles not rendered, GB/T 41545 only through a commentary page): species-group boundaries and the choice level for crustaceans rest on retailer lists. Every † below is attested at stage-3 harvest.
- The J2.6 sub-family, precedence P8 and kinds 63–68 are the memo's own placement of the K-45 decision (applied by default) and no fetched J2 page shows those kinds yet.
- "Mixed seafood" is stored with `leaf = null` in a sub-family that has leaves, which C5(b) does not strictly allow; the fallback is a one-kind leaf "several animal groups" (the K-102 precedent, R-11).
- Live edible snails: settled by R-54 (J2.1 with `storage_state = live`); the earlier OUT-by-default risk is closed. Whether a live snail is sold for eating or as a pet is judged from how it is sold (an edible species or grade offered as food), not from the species alone.
- Prawn trade names (banana, king) may cover several species; the unspecified-species kind is the fallback.
- The level of kind 44 (Schillerlocke) is provisional until attested (R-19, CV-27).
- Maker evidence is a hypothesis (no fetched page compares caviar houses or canneries); with no cap, the ledger grows by harvest.
