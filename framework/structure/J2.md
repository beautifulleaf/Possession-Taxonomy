## 0. Category

**Code and name:** J2 Fish & Seafood.

**Membership test** (CATEGORIES_v0_6, step 3, second branch): *fish and seafood, fresh, frozen, dried, smoked, salted or tinned, including shellfish and crustaceans sold alive, roe and caviar? J2 Fish & Seafood.* Category line: "fish and seafood as bought, fresh, frozen or preserved, including live shellfish and crustaceans, roe and caviar."

**Notes and precedence sentences that name or touch J2:**
- Step 1 (OUT list): a live animal is OUT "except shellfish and crustaceans sold alive to be eaten (oysters, mussels, clams, crabs, lobsters), which are groceries". Curing, smoking, drying, salting, tinning, freezing or plain cooking of one main ingredient "does not make a food prepared"; "smoked and tinned fish" are named groceries. A prepared food is "made by cooking or combining ingredients into something eaten as it is" (ready meals, dumplings, sandwiches, salads and other prepared dishes): left to the Culinary taxonomy.
- Step 3 notes: a product is filed by what it is, not by what it contains: sauces, condiments, stocks, pastes and cooking fats are J5 "whatever they are made from (fish sauce, oyster sauce, stock cubes)". "Dried or cured meat and fish are groceries even when sold salted, roasted or as snacks." Soups, stews and curries in tins are ready meals. A set, gift box or hamper is filed by its item of greatest value, and is OUT if that item is a prepared food. "A grocery that Culinary also describes (kimchi, smoked salmon, ham) is filed once here, as the product as bought, and Culinary links to it."
- Step 3 pantry branch (J5): tinned and jarred fruit, vegetables, pulses and tomatoes, and "dried mushrooms and dried seaweed" are J5. J4 holds "edible fungi and seaweeds, fresh or frozen", "not dressed, sauced, fried or coated". J1 holds meat, poultry and game.
- Step 2 (H2): vitamins, supplements and sports nutrition are H2. Step 3 closing note: food made for animals, kept or wild, is B10 (including fish food); live aquarium fish are OUT (BRIEF.md).
- S3 (parts follow the whole) and S5 (test the object, not the motive) apply to nothing special here; S6 (every added name has an attested source) governs the kind list.
- Appendix A: region, brand and maker, price band and quality tier, occasion, motive, origin (natural, cultured), how acquired, condition and edition are modifiers. "Origin (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown)" is the modifier name for wild against farmed.
- BRIEF.md: groceries are in, "judged by the quality of the ingredient (for example high-quality meat: breed, cut, grade, origin, age)". Grocery products that Culinary also holds "are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind". Culinary's matching leaves are SV-H4-1 Dried & Salted Fish & Seafood, H4-2 Smoked & Canned Fish, H4-3 Fermented & Pickled Seafood, H4-4 Roe & Caviar and H4-5 Fish-Paste Products (FRAMEWORK_v1.md, read only to plan links).

### 1.2 Enthusiast and specialist-guide sources

- **Raw-fish culture (Japan, with a German-language-run English reference).** Sushipedia treats tuna as eight species of which three dominate trade, and classes the flesh by fat into akami (lean), chūtoro (medium) and ōtoro (very fatty) [S36]. It notes that "tsuna" in Japanese means tinned tuna, not the fish [S36]. Its grade guide says "sashimi quality" is a trade and marketing term, not a legally defined standard [S35].
- **Specialist retailers' guides (USA).** Sashimi DC says "sashimi-grade" has no legal definition and cites a meta-analysis of 4,179 US seafood samples with 39.1% mislabelling, species substitution in 26.2% overall and 42.5% for the most fraud-prone species, tuna and red snapper [S37]. Fulton Fish Market says there is "no official, regulated standard" for the terms [S38].
- **Caviar and roe guides.** Roe is divided by source fish: sturgeon caviar (Beluga, Osetra, Sevruga, Kaluga), ikura by salmon species (chum, coho, king, pink), trout roe, tobiko (flying fish), masago (capelin), herring roe [S31][S32]. "Caviar" strictly means sturgeon roe; other roe is "roe" [S33]. Grade words (Grade 1, Grade 2, Premium, Royal, Imperial) are producer terms with no universal scale [S30][S34]; "Russian" and "Iranian" grade name curing styles [S34].

### 1.4 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **HS chapter 03** divides by form for fish: 0301 live, 0302 fresh or chilled, 0303 frozen, 0304 fillets and other meat, 0305 dried, salted or smoked; then by animal group: 0306 crustaceans, 0307 molluscs, 0308 other aquatic invertebrates, 0309 flours and meals fit for food. Crustacean and mollusc headings each hold every state (live, fresh, frozen, dried, salted, smoked, cooked). Canned, marinated, breaded and cooked preparations go to chapter 16 (canned tuna, canned crab meat, canned oysters, breaded squid rings are the examples) [S21][S23][S24]. Under 0302 the sub-headings are species: five tuna species each have their own [S22].
- **CITES and EU rules on caviar.** All sturgeon and paddlefish are listed; caviar containers carry a label with a three-letter species code (HUS Beluga, STE Sevruga, DAU Kaluga, MIX for pressed caviar of mixed species, a code for hybrids), source W or C, country, year; "pressed, pasteurized" and "malossol" caviar are all covered; in the EU and several countries the word "caviar" is restricted by law to sturgeon and paddlefish roe [S26].
- **FAO.** The sturgeon chapter lists three caviar types (Beluga, Osetra, Sevruga), grades (grade one, grade two, pressed "payusnaya"), Beluga grain categories 000, 00, 0 by size and colour, Osetra "royal", and the salt term malossol; it names salmon, trout, carp, pike, tuna, mullet, cod, lumpfish and flying-fish roes as "caviar substitutes" and cod roe sold fresh or smoked [S25].
- **MSC.** Two standards: the Fisheries Standard (wild capture) and the Chain of Custody Standard (the blue label), plus a seaweed standard with ASC [S27]. Waitrose and Ocado product names carry MSC and ASC marks [S2][S5].
- **Codex.** The list page and a Codex fish-standards webinar both returned only headers or background [S28][S29]. Codex standard titles (for example for canned tuna or canned sardines) are therefore not attested in this batch (coverage gap).

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and split

**Agree:** (1) The first split is the animal group (fish, crustaceans, molluscs, other invertebrates) or the form (live, fresh, frozen, dried, salted, smoked, tinned), and both are used by every source family [S1][S10][S14][S19][S21]. (2) Species is the identity below that: Fischkaufhaus A to Z, Rakuten genres, HS sub-headings, Woolworths guide [S10][S17][S22][S8]. (3) Roe and caviar are set apart as their own group by the retailers, FAO, CITES and the framework itself [S4][S12][S25][S26]. (4) Fresh against frozen keeps the same product names [S1][S2]; the sashimi-grade question turns on freezing and handling, not on a legal grade [S37][S38].
**Split:**
- **Form first or animal first.** Fischkaufhaus, Waitrose and the Chinese classes lead with form [S10][S2][S19]; Gyomu and Rakuten lead with animal group [S14][S17]; HS does both (form for fish, group for the rest) [S21].
- **Species groups.** Waitrose groups salmon with trout, Ocado groups trout with tuna and salmon alone; Ocado has both "Cod, Haddock & Pollock" and "White Fish" as sibling groups, Waitrose has one "Cod, Haddock & White Fish" [S1][S5]. No stable species-group level exists.
- **Fresh against frozen.** HS, the Chinese classes and the retail aisles separate them [S21][S20][S2]; product names do not [S1][S2].
- **Where coated and mixed products go.** Retailers keep breaded fish, fishcakes and ready-to-eat fish in the fish aisle [S1][S2][S7]; HS sends preparations to chapter 16 [S21].
- **Seaweed.** Rakuten and the Chinese classes keep dried seaweed with marine products [S18][S19]; the framework puts it in J4 and J5.
- **Freshwater and sea.** Chinese classes divide by origin habitat [S19]; the retailers do not.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

Evidence is mixed because retailers show navigation, not purchase order, and no fetched store exposes a species or form filter (Waitrose and Ocado filters are only Dietary & Lifestyle, own brands, offers, brands [S1][S5]). The departments themselves show the order:
1. **Storage state and form** (live, chilled, frozen, smoked, tinned): Waitrose splits chilled and frozen into separate departments [S1][S2]; Fischkaufhaus's form tabs come first [S10]; the Chinese classes and the HS fish headings are form-first [S19][S21].
2. **Species** (salmon, cod, tuna, prawn): every retailer has species or species-group categories [S1][S5][S10][S17], and the specialist guides assume the buyer chooses a species and then a cut [S36][S37].
3. **Cut or presentation** (whole, fillet, steak, loin, peeled, shell-on): "Fish Fillets" is a department child in both Waitrose aisles [S1][S2]; Fischkaufhaus has Ganze Fische against Premium Filets [S10]; HS gives fillets their own heading [S21].
4. **Quality questions** in the guides: Is it the species it says? (39.1% mislabelling [S37]); can it be eaten raw? (no legal grade [S37][S38]); what does the grade mean? (no universal scale [S30][S34]); wild or farmed, and is it certified? (MSC, ASC marks on product names [S2][S5]); how was it cured (malossol, pressed [S25][S34])?
5. **Maker** matters least: brands are a filter, not a navigation level [S5].

## 3. Candidate structures

All three keep the framework's limit of four levels below the category (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form). "Fresh, frozen and live" is treated as a stored attribute `storage_state` in all three (reason in 4b and open question 2).

### Candidate A (recommended): form, then animal group, then species

- **Level 1, sub-family. Axis: form in which the product is sold** (raw flesh or whole animal; smoked; salted, dried or fermented; tinned, jarred or marinated; eggs). The source is the form-first navigation of Fischkaufhaus, the Waitrose aisle split, the Chinese classes and HS for fish [S10][S2][S19][S21].
- **Level 2, leaf. Axis: animal group** (finfish, crustaceans, molluscs, other aquatic invertebrates), used only where a sub-family holds more than about 12 kinds (C5(b)); a smaller sub-family stores kinds with `leaf = null`. In the roe sub-family the leaf axis is the source fish group, the line CITES and EU law draw [S26]. The animal-group split is the one all families of source agree on [S14][S17][S19][S21]. Species groups (white fish, oily fish) are not a level because Waitrose and Ocado disagree on them [S1][S5]; they are a generated `species_group` view.
- **Level 3, kind. Axis: species or commercial type** (Atlantic salmon, sockeye salmon, cod, banana prawn, oyster, Beluga caviar, smoked salmon).
- **Level 4, named form.** Grades, cuts that buyers rank, cures and protected or traditional forms (ōtoro, kipper, "Imperial" Osetra), each with `maker_bound yes | no`.
- **Sub-families:**
  - J2.1 Fresh, frozen & live seafood (leaves: finfish; crustaceans; molluscs; other invertebrates; mixed seafood)
  - J2.2 Smoked fish & seafood (no leaf)
  - J2.3 Salted, dried & fermented fish & seafood (no leaf, or leaf by animal group if it passes 12 kinds)
  - J2.4 Tinned, jarred & marinated fish & seafood (no leaf at first)
  - J2.5 Roe & caviar (leaves: sturgeon and paddlefish caviar; salmonid roe; other fish roe)
  - No "Parts and accessories" sub-family: C5(d) applies to the A categories only.

**Where Candidate A breaks (edge objects):**
1. *Roe cured as something else* (smoked trout roe, salted cod roe, marinated ikura): form says "smoked" or "salted", animal part says "eggs". Resolved by a precedence sentence that roe is always J2.5 (section 9, P1); cure is an attribute.
2. *Tinned smoked mackerel*, *smoked fish in a retort pouch*: smoke or tin. Resolved by pack (P3).
3. *Mixed seafood* (frozen selection of prawns, mussels, squid [S2]): no single animal group. Needs the leaf "Mixed seafood" in J2.1, or the greatest-weight rule (P6).
4. *Cooked crab or cooked prawns*: raw sub-family but cooked. Plain cooking of one ingredient does not make a food prepared (step 1), so `cooked` is a presentation attribute.
5. *Dried fish that is also a cure by salting* (salt cod): salted and dried are one sub-family, so no conflict; fermented seafood (bagoong, jeotgal) and sauces cross into J5 (open question 6).

### Candidate B: animal group, then form, then species

- Level 1 sub-family. Axis: animal group (finfish; crustaceans; molluscs; other invertebrates; plus roe and caviar as a fifth class, a stated exception).
- Level 2 leaf. Axis: form (raw fresh/frozen/live; smoked; salted/dried; tinned/marinated).
- Level 3 kind: species or commercial type. Level 4: named form.
- Examples (12 kinds): Finfish > Raw > Atlantic salmon, Cod, Bluefin tuna; Finfish > Smoked > Cold-smoked salmon, Smoked mackerel; Finfish > Tinned > Tinned tuna, Tinned sardines; Crustaceans > Raw > Tiger prawn, Lobster; Molluscs > Raw > Oyster, Scallop; Molluscs > Tinned > (tinned oysters, mussels).
- Support: HS (crustaceans and molluscs each one heading), Gyomu, Rakuten, the species-group lists [S21][S14][S17].
- Breaks: (1) *Mixed seafood* and *assorted tins* cross animal groups at the top level, so they need a sixth sub-family; (2) roe is an animal part, not an animal group, so the top axis is already mixed; (3) a smoked-salmon buyer compares across smokehouses, so the smoked kinds are spread over four sub-families instead of one aisle; (4) *dried sea cucumber*, *abalone*, *dried scallop* form tiny leaves under molluscs and echinoderms (each under 12 kinds).

### Candidate C: species group first, forms as kinds (the Ocado and Rakuten pattern)

- Level 1 sub-family: species group (salmonids; white fish; flatfish; oily fish; tuna and billfish; other marine fish; freshwater fish; prawns and shrimps; crabs and lobsters; bivalves; cephalopods; roe).
- Level 2 leaf: form (fresh or frozen, smoked, dried, tinned). Level 3 kind: species-and-form. Level 4: named form.
- Examples (10 kinds): Salmonids > Smoked > Cold-smoked salmon; Salmonids > Roe > Salmon roe; White fish > Raw > Cod, Haddock; Oily fish > Tinned > Tinned sardines; Tuna > Raw > Bluefin tuna; Tuna > Tinned > Tinned tuna; Prawns > Raw > Tiger prawn; Bivalves > Raw > Oyster, Mussel.
- Breaks: (1) the species-group boundaries are not stable across sources (Waitrose and Ocado group trout differently; Ocado has Cod/Haddock/Pollock and White Fish side by side) [S1][S5], and no authoritative list was reached to settle them; (2) dried goods such as sea cucumber, abalone and fish maw have no species group in the retail lists [S19]; (3) caviar by source fish does not fit a "salmonid" or "sturgeon" group without inventing a roe group; (4) a species with five forms appears in five places.

**Recommendation:** Candidate A, with the evidence that form leads in Fischkaufhaus, the Waitrose aisles and the Chinese classes [S10][S2][S19], animal group is the one stable second split, and species groups are views. Candidate B is the nearest alternative and should be chosen if the owner wants the animal to lead (open question 1).

## 4. Depth and size

- **Generic kinds (estimate, from the lists seen):** raw finfish about 60 (Fischkaufhaus alone lists 34 species entries [S10]; HS 0302 lists dozens of examples [S22]); raw crustaceans about 15; raw molluscs about 20; other invertebrates about 8; smoked about 12 (Fiedlers has 11 smoked categories [S13]); salted, dried and fermented about 20 (Chinese dried class [S19]); tinned and marinated about 15; roe and caviar about 20 (sturgeon species with CITES codes [S26], plus roe of salmon, trout, char, whitefish, pike, lumpfish, capelin, flying fish, herring, cod, mullet [S12][S25][S31]). Total about 170 to 190.
- **Named forms (estimate):** about 80 to 120: tuna cuts, caviar grades and cures, smoked-fish cures (London cure, kipper, Bückling), protected-origin forms, and a few maker-bound lines.
- **Where depth concentrates:** finfish species (broad), roe and caviar (grades and cures), tuna (cuts), and the dried goods of the Chinese tradition [S19].
- **Broad leaves:** J2.1 finfish (about 60 kinds), J2.1 molluscs (about 20). **Medium:** crustaceans, roe by source, salted and dried, tinned. **Narrow, no leaf:** smoked (about 12), other invertebrates, fermented seafood if split out.
- **Maximum depth below the category:** four (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule applied:** a kind stops at the level where buyers actually compare quality or makers.

| Sub-family | Kind = | Below kind (named form or attribute) | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| J2.1 finfish | species | cut or presentation (whole, fillet, steak, loin, portion) is an attribute; wild or farmed, MSC or ASC, line-caught are attributes; tuna cut grades akami/chūtoro/ōtoro are named forms | Species is the A to Z axis at Fischkaufhaus and the genre axis at Rakuten [S10][S17]; HS gives each tuna species its own sub-heading [S22]; salmon species are priced and sold apart (sockeye, keta, coho) [S1][S5]; mislabelling concentrates in tuna and red snapper, so identity matters [S37]; fillet is a cross-cutting department child in both Waitrose aisles [S1][S2]; tuna cuts are compared like beef cuts [S36] |
| J2.1 crustaceans | commercial species or type (banana prawn, king prawn, langoustine, lobster, crab) | shell-on, peeled, cooked, head-on are attributes; count or size grade is an attribute (size grades not fetched here) | Woolworths separates green banana and green king prawns [S8]; Waitrose lists cooked and peeled prawns as an MSC line [S2] |
| J2.1 molluscs | animal type (oyster, mussel, scallop, squid) | species or cultivar and origin are named forms or tags | HS 0307 lists the types [S24]; origin names such as Shetland mussels sit on product names [S1] |
| J2.2 smoked | fish and smoke type (cold-smoked salmon, hot-smoked salmon, smoked mackerel) | cure and house style (London cure, oak, kipper) are named forms | Ocado and Waitrose separate smoked salmon from other smoked fish [S3][S6]; Fiedlers lists named cures [S13] |
| J2.3 salted, dried | product type (salt cod, dried scallop, dried sea cucumber) | grade and origin are attributes | Chinese classes list each [S19]; HS 0305.62 salted cod [S21] |
| J2.4 tinned | species/type (tinned tuna, tinned sardines) | pack medium (oil, brine, tomato, smoked) and species within tuna are attributes | The Chinese tinned class is divided by medium [S19]; Fischkaufhaus lists by fish [S11]; "tsuna" means tinned tuna in Japanese [S36] |
| J2.5 roe | source species (Beluga, Osetra, Sevruga, salmon, trout, lumpfish, tobiko) | grade (000, 00, 0; royal; Imperial) and cure (malossol, pressed, pasteurised) are named forms or attributes, because no universal scale exists | FAO and CITES by species [S25][S26]; guides: no universal grade [S30][S34]; roe types "not interchangeable" [S31] |

The choice level differs by sub-family: fish go as deep as species; shellfish stop at animal type; smoked and tinned goods are kinds at fish-and-process. Seller guides show the questions buyers ask (is trout roe caviar, is Grade 1 better, is it sashimi grade) [S33][S30][S37]; no "best X" list or forum thread was fetched, so comparison evidence is indirect.
