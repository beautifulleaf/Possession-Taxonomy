**One-line result.** Divide I3 first by what the plan gives access to (five sub-families: catalogue and play, online utility, connectivity, member places and passes, curated deliveries), then by the one axis each sub-family states for its leaf level (medium of the catalogue; for places, what the member does there), then by a **kind** that is a generic service type a buyer compares providers within (for example "music streaming subscription", "cloud storage and sync plan", "mobile phone plan", "multi-site membership", "member-selected menu plan"), then by **named form** = the provider's service or line (Spotify Premium, Xbox Game Pass, Deutschland-Ticket). **Seat structure (individual, duo, family, student), tier (Essential, Extra, Premium), term, delivery, region and price are plan attributes in a plan ledger (the C3 variant ledger with variant type `plan`), never levels.** Content genre, sport, audience and goods type never divide anything. Maximum depth below the category: sub-family, leaf, kind, named form (four levels), with the leaf level skipped in three of the five sub-families.

**Choice level:** the service type within which buyers compare providers (the medium for catalogues, the function for utilities, the connection for plans, the way of entering for places, the way of choosing for deliveries). **Tier type:** preference between providers, with maker-stated plan ladders inside one service (tier entries on plan rows) and measurable allowances (storage in GB, data in GB, speed in Mbps, term in months) kept as attributes. **Maker centrality:** identity (the provider is what is bought), with platform dependence for game, device-ecosystem and network plans (C13).

## 0. Category

**Code and name.** I3 Subscriptions, Memberships & Online Services (domain I, Software, Digital & Subscriptions).

**Membership test (placement key, step 5, third branch).** "a **subscription, membership or online utility service** giving ongoing or periodic access to (a) a catalogue of content or play: music and audio, audiobooks and podcasts, film and TV, text, news and magazines, comics, recorded and streamed classes, game catalogues, streaming and online play, single-game subscriptions and in-game battle and season passes; (b) an online utility, free or paid: email, messaging, cloud storage and backup, maps and navigation; (c) connectivity: mobile phone and broadband plans; (d) a shared place, club or venue open to members: gym, sports, social and hobby clubs, museums, parks and attractions, season tickets and passes for venues, events or travel valid for a period or a number of visits; or (e) a recurring delivery of goods chosen or curated by the provider (subscription boxes, wine and coffee clubs, meal-kit plans)?" Category line: "ongoing access to content and play catalogues, online utilities (email, messaging, cloud storage, maps), connectivity plans, member places and clubs, and curated deliveries; by kind of access, never by content."

**Notes and rules that already touch I3.**
- Step 5 note: these are divided by the kind of access they give, never by content. "Kind of access means the medium the catalogue holds (music and audio, audiobooks and podcasts, film and TV, text and news periodicals, comics, classes, games), how it is delivered (stream, download, physical delivery, entry to a place) and the seat structure (individual, family, student). The subject, genre, sport or audience of the content (sport, children, anime, crime, fitness, faith) never divides a category: a sports streaming service is film-and-TV streaming." (The words "and the seat structure" conflict with C6; the memo treats seat structure as a plan attribute. OWNER: amend the note, section 10, I3-Q5.)
- Bundles: "filed by the component it is named and sold for: Microsoft 365 (office software) is I1; iCloud+, Google One and Dropbox (storage) are I3; Apple One and Amazon Prime (catalogue access) are I3."
- A subscription box, club or meal-kit plan is filed as the plan; each item delivered is filed by what it is. "A repeat order at a fixed price with no curation, membership or access is only a way of buying the goods and is not filed."
- Shared access to a place open to all members is I3; a specific place for the person's own use is a right (step 1, OUT). A pass, membership or season ticket is I3 whatever its form (card, app, paper); a card kept after it has expired or been used up is F5. A playlist or library inside a service is part of that subscription.
- Step 5 first branch (I1): a hosted tool whose value is what the program does for the user (password managers, security and VPN software, AI assistants, Microsoft 365) is I1, even when subscribed. A hosted service whose value is the storage, catalogue, connection, place or play it gives access to is I3, even when an app is used to reach it.
- Step 1 (OUT): one-off services and recurring services performed for the person (cleaning, care, lessons and courses, repairs, energy and water supply); tickets or passes for a single event or journey; a social or entertainment platform account (social feeds, free video and content platforms); "a membership that gives only discounts, shipping or points"; money held as stored value, including "credits redeemable for a choice of items such as audiobook credits" and in-game currencies; insurance.
- Step 5 note and D5 note: game subscriptions and passes are I3 (catalogue and streaming subscriptions, online-play memberships, single-game subscriptions, in-game battle and season passes); games and their expansions are D5; cosmetic and consumable in-game items are I2.
- Step 19 note (F5): payment, access, membership and key cards and kept tickets are F5 as physical tokens once expired or used up.
- BRIEF.md (owner, 2026-10-17): "paid subscriptions and memberships are purchases that give enjoyment or quality of life. They are divided by the kind of access they give, never by content genre." Online services are in or out "by utility vs entertainment, not by price": utility services in whether free or paid; the internet as entertainment (social feeds, free video and content platforms) out; paid entertainment subscriptions in. Method: modifiers (region, price, era, motive, scene, tier) stay off the tree.

**Appendix A modifiers that bite hardest in I3** (never levels): region and culture, price band and quality tier, brand and maker (here the provider), user (child, student, senior, family, disabled), market (domestic, business), connectivity, how acquired (gift), edition (limited, trial), condition (active, expired).

**Gaps in the key that this research hit (details in sections 9 and 10; the framework-level ones are logged in framework/structure/_framework_conflicts.md).** (1) Goods rental subscriptions (clothes, furniture, watches, art, instruments), which the Japanese guide S10 treats as one of three subscription types, appear in none of branches (a) to (e). (2) Web and game-server hosting plans, which COICOP files with net storage (S01), are not named in branch (b). (3) Creator memberships (one creator's paid posts or channel) are not named. (4) Multi-venue class passes paid in credits (ClassPass, S21) sit between branch (d) and the step-1 exclusion of "credits redeemable for a choice of items". (5) The note names "seat structure" as part of the kind of access, while conventions C6 and Appendix A make user an attribute or tag. (6) Travel eSIM data packs bought for one trip are one-off services unless they renew. (7) The trade term "access" or "membership" (S27) means a perks-only plan, which step 1 puts OUT. (8) Libraries of production assets (stock images, fonts, sample packs: Adobe Stock, Envato Elements), which I2 sends to I3, are named in none of branches (a) to (e). (9) Car-sharing memberships and the physical SIM card or eSIM profile that carries a plan are not covered.

## 1. How the domain already divides itself

Claims cite the fetched pages by key. Where a page showed only a flat list, the memo says so. The domain has no single authority; it is described by statisticians (classifications), by operators (plan pages), by comparison sites (rankings by medium or by connection), by directories of boxes (by goods theme), and by code resellers (by platform and duration).

### 1.1 Standards bodies and trade classifications

- **COICOP 2018 (UN Statistics Division) [S01].** Splits the same objects by purpose of the spending, not by "subscription". Division 08 Information and communication: 08.3.1 fixed communication services, 08.3.2 mobile communication services, 08.3.3 Internet access provision and **net storage** services (the explanatory note names cloud storage, file hosting, web hosting and "subscriptions for email services"), 08.3.4 bundled telecommunication services (telephony, internet and TV packages), 08.3.9.1 TV and radio licences, 08.3.9.2 "subscription to audio-visual content, streaming services" (film and music streaming, cable, satellite, IPTV and pay-TV, online video recorders, video on demand). Division 09: 09.4.3.1 "rental of game software and subscription to online games" (kept apart from video streaming); 09.4.6.2 sporting services - practice, which includes "membership fees for sports clubs and fitness centres" and "membership fees of fishermen's and hunters' clubs"; 09.6.2 services of museums, libraries and cultural sites; 09.7.2 newspapers and periodicals, whose explanatory note includes "subscription for newspapers (home delivery)", "Internet subscription for newspapers", and the same for magazines. The introduction states the rule that fits I3: when the internet is used to rent a product "this activity is still a service and not the purchase of a product". Medium of the catalogue (audio-visual, games, press) and kind of connection are the divisions; the fetched excerpts show no class for curated boxes (they would sit with the goods) and none for member passes beyond sport clubs and museums.
- **NAICS 2022 (US Census Bureau) [S02].** Sector 51 Information separates by method of communication and nature of service: 516210 groups stand-alone streaming services, subscription video on demand, virtual pay-TV distributors, satellite radio networks and social networks with broadcast networks; 517 Telecommunications holds cable and satellite distribution, internet access and telecommunications resellers (517121, the nearest class for MVNOs); 518 holds computing infrastructure, data processing and web hosting; 519 holds web search portals, libraries and archives. NAICS therefore splits **catalogue provider** (516), **connection provider** (517) and **hosting provider** (518), the same three families the framework calls (a), (c) and (b). It has no member-place or box class in sector 51 (those are in other sectors, not fetched).

### 1.2 Retailers and plan pages, by region

- **Music and audiobook catalogues (Canada, US, UK) [S05, S24, S25].** Spotify sells one catalogue in four seat structures: Individual (1 account), Student (1 verified account), Duo (2 accounts, same address), Family (up to 6 accounts, same address); all Premium plans share one feature list, and audiobook hours are an extra on the music plan (15 hours a month on Individual, Duo and Family managers, none on Student) [S05]. Audible sells Premium (1 or 2 credits a month, or 12 or 24 on an annual plan, plus an unlimited Plus Catalogue) and Standard (one selection a month); a credit buys a title to keep, so the credit part is stored value (step 1) and the catalogue part is I3 [S24]. Kindle Unlimited and Everand are compared as two flat-fee reading libraries with different catalogues, concurrent-title limits and no family sharing [S25].
- **Bundles (US) [S06, S38].** Apple One sells Individual, Family and Premier plans that bundle iCloud+ storage (50 GB, 200 GB, 2 TB), Apple TV, Apple Music, Apple Arcade, and in Premier Fitness+ and News+, with sharing for up to five others [S06]. Amazon states that Prime bundles shipping, Prime Video, Amazon Music, Prime Reading, Photos and Gaming, and that a **Prime Shipping** plan without the digital benefits exists [S38]; the shipping-only plan is the perks-only case that step 1 excludes.
- **Game subscriptions (Canada, UK, US) [S07, S09, S37, S08].** Xbox Game Pass has Essential (50+ games, 5 hours cloud play), Premium (200+ games, 10 hours), Ultimate (400+ games, day-one releases, EA Play, Fortnite Crew, Ubisoft+ Classics, 15 hours) and a PC-only plan (300+ games) [S07]. PlayStation Plus has Essential (online multiplayer, monthly games, 100 GB cloud saves), Extra (adds a game catalogue) and Premium (adds classics, cloud streaming, trials) [S37]. Nintendo Switch Online crosses Individual (1 account) and Family (up to 8 accounts) with standard and Expansion Pack, the latter adding classic-game libraries and downloadable expansions [S09]; The Verge's fetched paragraph prices the Nintendo family plans [S08]. The three ecosystems sell **one kind of thing in a tier ladder**: online play first, then catalogue, then streaming and classics.
- **Video and "read and listen" (Japan, China) [S12, S31].** Selectra Japan ranks video services by monthly price, shows ad-supported, standard and premium tiers (Netflix from 890 yen with ads), and keeps a separate table for reading and listening libraries (Kindle Unlimited, Audible, d-magazine); it advises choosing by genre, price, simultaneous devices and trial [S12]. Chinese video services run a tier ladder by right (iQiyi Gold, Platinum, Diamond VIP; Tencent Video VIP and SVIP) with auto-renewing month, quarter and year terms and device limits [S31].
- **Connectivity (UK, Germany, India) [S14, S15, S16, S17].** Uswitch's SIM-only table filters by contract length (30-day rolling, 12, 18, 24 months), data, 5G, roaming and credit check, and flags whether the plan runs on a big-four network or an MVNO [S14]; its other pages split broadband by connection (fibre, copper, mobile, 5G, satellite) and by deal type (no-contract, 12-month, wireless, fixed-price, bundles with TV or home phone) [S15]. CHECK24 sells Handytarife as tabs for contract, eSIM, prepaid and travel eSIM, and filters by data volume, network (Telekom, Vodafone, o2) and contract duration [S16]. Jio sells prepaid fibre by validity (monthly, semi-annual, annual) and speed (30 Mbps to 1 Gbps) with free OTT video subscriptions bundled [S17].
- **Cloud storage (global, US) [S22, S23].** Google One sells storage tiers (15 GB free, 30 GB, 100 GB, up to 5 TB bundled with AI features) shared with up to five others [S22]. A buying guide compares Google One, iCloud+, OneDrive, Dropbox and Proton Drive by free storage, entry price, 200 GB / 1 TB / 2 TB breakpoints, family plans, bundled extras (Office apps, VPN) and the use (photos, documents, full backup) [S23].
- **Places and passes (UK, Scotland, Germany, Japan, US) [S18, S19, S20, S21, S28, S29].** The National Trust sells Joint, Family, Individual and Lifetime memberships, plus a short-visit Explorer Pass [S18]; the National Trust for Scotland sells Individual (adult, senior, young), Joint, Family (one or two adults) and Life memberships by month or year, with reciprocal access to other National Trusts [S19]. The Art Fund National Art Pass is Individual, Double or Under 30, annual or lifetime, with add-ons Plus One and Plus Kids and a named, non-transferable card [S20]. ClassPass sells monthly credit plans (8 to 125 credits) for booking classes across many studios [S21]. Germany's Deutschland-Ticket is a monthly-cancellable subscription for local and regional transport, not valid on ICE, IC or EC [S28]; JR's commuter and school passes run 1, 3 or 6 months on a named route, with Shinkansen, Green-car and off-peak variants [S29]. UK Railcards (nine types, one-third off fares for one or three years) are discount cards [S30], which step 1 excludes.
- **Deliveries (US) [S34, S35].** A winery club sells by wine style (Variety, Sweet), bottles per shipment (1 to 3) and fulfilment (monthly pick-up or quarterly delivery) [S34]. HelloFresh sells a plan by people served, recipes a week and a diet plan (Veggie, Fit and Wholesome, Pescatarian), with pause and skip [S35].

### 1.6 Where the sources agree and where they split

- **Agree.** (1) Medium of the catalogue is the first division of catalogue services: video, music, books and magazines, games are separate classes in COICOP [S01], separate ranking pages in Japan [S10, S11, S12] and separate tabs on game platforms [S07, S37]. (2) Connectivity is a separate family from content in COICOP, NAICS and every comparison site [S01, S02, S14, S16]. (3) The plan, not the service, carries seat structure and tier: Spotify, Apple One, Nintendo, National Trust, Art Fund all sell one service in several seat plans [S05, S06, S09, S18, S19, S20].
- **Split.** (1) Where cloud storage and email belong: COICOP puts them with internet access (08.3.3) [S01]; NAICS puts hosting in 518 and email with portals [S02]; the framework puts them in online utilities. (2) Whether games belong with video streaming: COICOP separates them (09.4.3.1) [S01], resale sites merge them [S32, S33], Japanese guides list games among entertainment subscriptions [S10]. (3) Whether pay-TV is streaming or connectivity: COICOP lumps cable, satellite and streaming together in 08.3.9.2 [S01]; NAICS puts the distribution network in 517 and the streaming service in 516 [S02]. (4) Bundles: Apple One and Amazon Prime mix media and storage or shipping [S06, S38]; JioFiber mixes broadband and OTT video [S17]. (5) Boxes: directories divide by goods theme and audience [S03, S04], while the delivery-mode guides [S10, S26, S27] divide by who chooses and how often.

## 2. The first axis people choose by

**Evidence.** Buyers choose in four steps, and the order is visible in how comparison sites are built.

1. **What kind of thing they want to get (medium or function).** Every comparison site opens with it: Japanese guides give a ranking page per medium (video, music, magazines, manga, clothes rental, perfume, furniture, flowers, hotels, sweets, toys) [S10]; Selectra separates video from "read and listen" libraries [S12]; Uswitch and CHECK24 separate SIM, broadband and bundles [S14, S15, S16]; cloud-storage guides compare only storage services [S23]; game platforms present one ladder [S07, S37]. This is the first axis, and it is the framework's own: "the medium the catalogue holds".
2. **Who uses it (seat structure).** On every maker page the plan choice is Individual, Duo, Family, Student, Joint, Young, Senior [S05, S06, S09, S18, S19, S20]. It is chosen second and never changes which service is bought.
3. **Which tier (features and allowances).** Essential, Extra, Premium; Gold, Platinum, Diamond; ads or no ads; GB; Mbps; contract months [S07, S31, S37, S12, S14, S17, S22].
4. **Price and trial** (Selectra: genre, monthly fee, simultaneous devices, trial [S12]; the tracker's "replaceability" and "cancellation difficulty" [S13]).

**Filter order on the major pages.** Uswitch: contract length, data, roaming, no-credit-check [S14]. CHECK24: data volume, network, contract duration [S16]. Jio: validity, then speed [S17]. Xbox and PlayStation: tier ladder on one page [S07, S37].

**Questions buyers ask** (from the pages' own FAQs): "Is the family plan cheaper for two people?" (Spotify's Duo and Family require the same address [S05]); "Can I keep what I bought if I cancel?" (Audible credits against catalogue titles [S24]; Kindle Unlimited titles vanish [S25]); "How much storage do I need for photos?" [S23]; "Which network does the MVNO use?" [S14, S16]; "Can I get it as one bill?" (bundles [S06, S38]).

**Consequence for the tree.** The first division must be by what the plan gives access to, not by seat, tier, price or genre. Content genre is the first axis on box directories [S03, S04] and an input on Selectra [S12], but the framework removes it.

## 3. Candidate structures

All three candidates keep the framework's constraints: one stated axis per level (per sub-family at the leaf, C5(c)), at most four levels below the category, no modifier as a level. They differ in the **level-1 axis**.

### 3.1 Candidate A (recommended): by what the plan gives access to

| Level | Axis | Values |
|---|---|---|
| Sub-family | what the plan gives access to | catalogue and play; online utility; connection; member place or pass; curated delivery |
| Leaf (only where needed) | catalogue: the medium of the catalogue; places: what the member does there | see 3.4 |
| Kind | the generic service type a buyer compares providers within (function, connection, way of entering, way of choosing) | see 3.4 |
| Named form | the provider's service or line (maker-bound), or a product defined by an agreement and sold by many operators (not maker-bound) | Spotify Premium; Deutschland-Ticket |
| Plan (variant ledger row, not a level) | seat structure, tier ladder rank, term, region, price | Duo; Ultimate; 12 months |

**Sub-families and leaf axes (C5(c)).**
- **I3.1 Catalogue and play access.** Leaf axis: medium of the catalogue (music and audio; audiobooks and podcasts; video; text, news and periodicals; comics; classes; games; production assets; bundles of several media). Kind axis inside a leaf: how the catalogue is offered (on-demand library, live or linear, periodical delivery, season pass) and, for periodicals, how many titles it holds (one title; a library of many titles).
- **I3.2 Online utility services.** No leaf level (six kinds, C5(b)). Kind axis: function of the service (store files, back up, mail, message, navigate, host).
- **I3.3 Connectivity plans.** No leaf level (seven kinds). Kind axis: the connection (mobile, mobile data, fixed line, satellite, home phone, bundle, travel eSIM).
- **I3.4 Member places and passes.** Leaf axis: what the member does there (use a facility or join a group; visit a site or collection; travel on a network; attend a series). Kind axis: the way of entering (one facility; a members' club; one body's network of sites; a scheme across many bodies' venues; one venue; a route; a series). The type of site or activity (heritage, museum, zoo, tennis, golf) is a tag, never a kind.
- **I3.5 Curated deliveries.** No leaf level (three kinds). Kind axis: who chooses what arrives (the provider curates; the member selects from a menu; a producer allocates its own goods). Goods rental (goods lent and returned) is not on this axis and is held for an owner ruling (I3-Q3).
- No "Parts and accessories" sub-family (C5(d)): a subscription has no fitted parts. Add-ons made for one service (Prime Video Channels, an audiobook add-on to a music plan, Plus One) are plan add-ons in the plan ledger (`addon_of`).

**Real kinds under it (14 of the 42 in 3.4):** music streaming subscription [S05, S06]; audiobook catalogue subscription [S24]; on-demand streaming video subscription [S12, S31]; e-book library subscription [S25]; game subscription [S07, S37, S09]; in-game season or battle pass [S07]; multi-service bundle [S06, S38]; cloud storage and sync plan [S22, S23]; mobile phone plan [S14, S16]; fixed-line broadband plan [S15, S17]; multi-site membership [S18, S19]; multi-venue pass scheme [S20]; period transit pass [S28, S29]; member-selected menu plan [S35].

**Why it fits the evidence.** It is the framework's own five-way split of step 5, and COICOP (communications / audio-visual / games / press / sport and culture [S01]), NAICS (catalogue provider / connection provider / hosting provider [S02]) and the comparison sites [S10, S12, S14, S16, S23] all divide first by service type.

**Where it breaks (edge objects).**
1. **Bundles that cross sub-families.** Apple One carries storage, video, music, games, fitness and news [S06]; JioFiber carries broadband and OTT video [S17]; Amazon Prime carries shipping and media [S38]. 2. **ClassPass.** A pooled pass for studios, paid in credits per booking [S21]. It reads as a facility membership (I3.4) but the credit mechanism looks like "credits redeemable for a choice of items" (step 1, OUT). 3. **Wine club at a winery.** A members' club with events, a monthly pick-up at the venue, and delivery [S34] is both a member place and a delivery. The key's own words ("wine and coffee clubs") put it in (e); the club's venue benefits are attributes.

An IPTV package sold by a broadband provider is the pay-TV package kind unless it is named for the line.

### 3.2 Candidate B: by how the plan is delivered

Level 1 = stream, download, physical delivery, entry to a place, network connection (the key's second list). Leaf = medium.

- Example kinds: streaming video; streaming music; downloadable e-books; mailed magazines; mailed boxes; gym entry; museum entry; mobile data.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) The same plan is delivered several ways: Spotify and Kindle Unlimited stream and download [S05, S25], Xbox Game Pass downloads and streams [S07], so one service would sit in two sub-families. (2) A newspaper subscription is print or internet in the same COICOP class [S01]. (3) Connectivity and cloud storage have no delivery mode other than "network". (4) Delivery is an attribute in every plan page seen [S05, S07, S24]. Verdict: use delivery as a plan attribute, with the shared domain I `delivery` value list (download; boxed or carrier; app store; web or hosted; self-hosted; portable; stream; physical delivery; entry to a place; network connection). Delivery is never a C13 row.

### 3.3 Candidate C: by commercial model

Level 1 = unlimited use, rental, regular delivery, perks-only membership (the Japanese three-way split and the "curation, replenishment, access" trade split [S10, S26, S27]).

- Example kinds: unlimited video; unlimited magazines; clothes rental; furniture rental; cosmetics box; flower delivery; discount club.
- **Where it breaks.** (1) Audible mixes credits (stored value) with an unlimited catalogue [S24]. (2) Telecom plans are "unlimited use" in the same bucket as Netflix, which no buyer compares. (3) The trade term "access" means a perks-only plan, which step 1 excludes [S27]. (4) A winery club is "regular delivery" and a "membership" at once [S34]. (5) Gym memberships and transit passes have no home. Verdict: keep the commercial model only inside I3.5 as the question who chooses what arrives, and hold rental as an open gap (I3-Q3).

## 4b. Choice level

**Rule.** A kind stops at the level where buyers compare quality or makers. For subscriptions the thing compared is the provider's service of one type, so the kind is the **service type within which providers are ranked**; everything finer (seat, tier, term, ad load, region, eligibility, print or digital delivery) is a plan attribute.

## 5. Quality and tier

**Is there a recognised tier ordering?** Not between providers. No standard, community consensus or measured benchmark ranks Spotify against Apple Music, or the National Trust against the Art Fund pass. Review sites rank services by their own scores and editors' picks, which are platform ratings, not tiers (C4(c)). **Choice: preference** at the kind level.

**What does exist, and where it goes.**
- **Maker-defined ladders inside one service.** PlayStation Plus Essential, Extra, Premium [S37]; Xbox Game Pass Essential, Premium, Ultimate and PC [S07]; Apple One Individual, Family, Premier [S06]; Netflix with ads, Standard, Premium [S12]; Disney+ Standard, Premium [S12]; iQiyi Gold, Platinum, Diamond and Tencent VIP, SVIP [S31]; Audible Standard and Premium [S24]; Nintendo standard and Expansion Pack [S09]. Each ladder is stated by the maker and ordered by what is added at each step. They are **tier entries** on the plan (variant) row of the named form, with `scheme = maker ladder: <named_form_id>`, the label as value and a `rank`, attested by the maker's page alone (exempt from C4(e), never compared across named forms), and never on the kind. A named form does not inherit a ladder from its maker. Where a maker's ladder mixes seat and content steps (Apple One Individual, Family, Premier), the seat is also recorded as plan attributes. OWNER: this uses the proposed C4(a) amendment of the domain I review (X3), which the owner has not yet approved; under C4(a) as printed, a tier entry sits only on a maker, line or named form.
- **Measurable allowances** are attributes with their unit (C4(b)): storage in GB or TB [S22, S23], data in GB and speed in Mbps [S14, S17], contract length in months [S14], simultaneous accounts [S05, S09], cloud-play hours a month [S07], bottles per shipment [S34], validity in days or months [S17, S29], credits per period (`credits_per_period`, for example ClassPass [S21]). They have no cross-provider ordering of their own except within one measure (more GB is more GB).
- **Catalogue size** ("50+", "200+", "400+ games" [S07]) is maker-stated, changes monthly, and is not comparable between services; keep it as a dated, sourced attribute on the plan.
- **Price band (proposed domain I rule for C4(d); OWNER: approval pending, I3-Q9).** The band is computed on each plan row from the **annualised list price of the plan as sold** (all seats included, `seat_count` recorded), in USD at a dated rate, using the C4(d) bands. A promotional first-year price is recorded but not used. One-time, perpetual, lifetime and multi-year terms use the full price, with the term recorded. A free plan gets the band "0 (free)". A named form's band is the band of its cheapest paid plan, plus a `has_free_plan` flag. Examples: Apple One Premier at $39.95 a month is $479.40 a year, the 150 to 500 band [S06]; Apple One Individual at $21.95 a month is $263.40 a year, the same band [S06]; National Trust for Scotland Life at GBP 1,860 [S19] goes to the band of its full price at a dated rate, term recorded. PlayStation Plus Essential at $59.99 a year would be the 50 to 150 band, but that figure came from a code marketplace [S32], which is a resale price and not a dated maker list price; † fetch the maker's list price and date before it enters the data.
- **Free plans** are in scope by the owner's rule on utilities (BRIEF.md): a free email or a 15 GB free cloud storage account [S22] is a plan row with price 0 and band "0 (free)".

## 6. Makers and brands

**Centrality: identity, with platform dependence.** The provider is what is bought: buyers choose Spotify or Apple Music, the National Trust or the Art Fund, giffgaff or Lebara [S05, S06, S14, S18, S20]. Comparison pages are lists of providers [S10, S12, S14, S16]. For three groups the maker also sets **compatibility** (C13): game subscriptions need the console or PC ecosystem (PlayStation Plus, Xbox Game Pass, Nintendo Switch Online [S07, S09, S37]); storage and bundles are favoured by the device ecosystem (iCloud+ for Apple users, Google One for Google users [S23]); and mobile plans depend on the host network, so MVNOs are listed by the network they use [S14, S16].

**Recommendation: one provider ledger for domain I (C1), with I3 extensions.**
- *One maker row per company across I1, I2, I3 and D5* (Apple, Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Sony, Nintendo and others appear in several). `known_for_kinds` is derived from the named-form ledger, not typed in.
- *Core columns (C1):* `maker_id`, `name`, `native_name`, `aliases`, `maker_class`, `country`, `place`, `founded`, `ended`, `status`, `parent_id`, `known_for_kinds`, `scene_tags` (empty, section 7), `price_band`, `sources`.
- *`maker_class` values (the shared domain I list, proposed; OWNER: approval pending, I3-Q12):* software publisher; community or foundation project; platform holder; storefront or marketplace (with a `resale` flag); game publisher; type foundry; format steward or standards body; registry operator; network operator; MVNO or reseller; membership body; venue, club or attraction operator; transport operator or association; aggregator; producer; curated-box company. How the I3 classes of the first draft map: platform owner (Sony, Microsoft, Nintendo, Apple, Google, Amazon) becomes **platform holder**; network operator is unchanged; MVNO or reseller (spusu, Lebara, giffgaff, congstar [S14, S16]) is unchanged; non-profit membership body (National Trust, National Trust for Scotland, Art Fund [S18, S19, S20]) becomes **membership body**; venue or club operator becomes **venue, club or attraction operator**; transport operator or association (JR, DB [S28, S29]) is unchanged; marketplace operator (Cratejoy [S04]) becomes **storefront or marketplace**; aggregator (ClassPass [S21]) is unchanged; resale marketplace (Eneba, G2A [S32, S33]) becomes **storefront or marketplace with `resale` = yes**, recorded for the resale view, not as providers of the service; Cooper's Hawk [S34] is a **producer**; HelloFresh [S35] and Birchbox [S26] are **curated-box company**. The first draft's "independent service" (a company whose own service is the product, such as Spotify, Netflix, Proton) has no class in the shared list. OWNER: add a class (for example "service provider") or place such companies under another class (I3-Q12).
- *I3 extension columns:* `host_network_id` (for MVNOs) and `markets_served` (a list of region tags, not a level). `platform_ids` is not a maker column: the C13 row's `owner` field links maker and platform.
- *Separate tables:* named forms (the service or line), plans (the variant ledger, section 10 under C3), tier entries (section 5).
- **Brand depth.** The owner's wish for brand depth is met by the ledger and by named forms. There is no subculture-label layer in I3.

## 8. Order inside a leaf

**Kinds: canonical form first, then fulfilment complexity.** Within a leaf, the form the average buyer holds comes first (on-demand library before live or linear before periodical delivery; one-title periodical before many-title library; mobile phone plan before data-only, fixed, satellite, bundle; the provider-curated box before the member-selected menu plan before the producer club), then the less common. The order is stated in the data, not by popularity counts (which change). Reason: the sources present the library form first and the other forms as variants (Selectra opens with on-demand video and keeps reading and listening as a second table [S12]; Uswitch opens with SIM-only and puts mobile broadband on a separate page [S14, S15]).

**Named forms (C7):** by provider name in ledger order, then by first-launch year as attested, then alphabetically.

**Plans (variant rows):** by seat count ascending (individual, duo, family [S05]), then by the rank of the maker's tier ladder in the maker's stated order (Essential, Extra, Premium [S37]; Individual, Family, Premier [S06]), then by term (rolling, annual, lifetime). Reason: makers state both orders themselves, so no outside ranking is needed.

## 10. Recommendation and open questions

**Recommended structure.** Five sub-families by what the plan gives access to, a leaf level only in I3.1 (medium of the catalogue) and I3.4 (what the member does there), kinds as service types within which providers are compared (42 in the worked tree, plus goods rental held for a ruling), and named forms as the provider's service or line (maker-bound) or an agreed product sold by many operators (not maker-bound). Seat structure, tier, term, delivery, region, price and eligibility are plan attributes in the plan ledger (C3 variant rows). **Maximum depth below the category: four levels** (sub-family, leaf, kind, named form), three where the leaf is skipped. Quality is preference between providers with maker ladders and measurable allowances as attributes. A provider ledger is needed (maker centrality: identity, with platform dependence).

**Convention conformance (C1 to C17).**
- **C1 maker ledger:** the provider ledger of section 6 follows the core schema, with one maker row per company across I1, I2, I3 and D5 and the shared `maker_class` list (proposed). Extensions `host_network_id` and `markets_served`; `platform_ids` removed (C13 `owner` links it). Lines and tier entries in separate tables.
