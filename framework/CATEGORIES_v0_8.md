## How to read this file

- **Levels.** Domain → category → department → shelf → kind → archetype → version → detail. Domains group neighbouring categories for reading; their names are never tests. Placement is always to a category, by the placement key.
- **The axis.** A short status filter (scope, animal, health, living plant, food and drink, property, home fixture, digital), then the object's **primary designed function**: what it is made to do or be for its owner. Never material, power source, price, brand, room, age, user, occasion, condition or motive; those are tags and views.
- **The key is authoritative.** Membership tests and precedence sentences restate it; where they differ, the key wins.
- **Shared rules.**
  - **S1 One home.** Every kind has one category: run the key and stop at the first *yes*. Cross-links point to neighbours; they never create a second home.
  - **S2 Generic kinds.** A kind is a generic type a person can own. Models, patterns and icons are archetypes under it; individual unique objects are never kinds.
  - **S3 Parts follow the whole.** A part, attachment, case or consumable made for one kind of object goes to that object's category. Never parts: furniture and storage for several objects (B1), anything worn (A), building supplies bought loose (C8), cleaning products (B6, vehicle care G1), fuels and standard cells (C8). Albums, binders and holders for collected objects follow what they hold (F3, F5).
  - **S4 Test the object, not the motive.** A signed football is a football; an antique chair is a chair; a commemorative plate is a plate.
  - **S5 Pursuits group only where owners shop that way.** Sports, crafts and faiths are separate taxonomies; a pursuit may group equipment (fishing tackle, climbing gear) but gets no department of its own without distinct equipment.
  - **S6 Attestation.** Every name has one fetched source showing it is or was made or sold.

## Scope

**In** — every generic type of object a person owns for personal life (hobby or quality of life):
- worn, carried, furnished, used, ridden, lived in, grown, displayed, played, collected objects;
- living plants and cultures (C3); **living animals** as possessions, recorded in the breeds and species register, never inside a category;
- real property and the fixtures built into a home (G2, G3);
- software, data-only items, NFTs and domains; subscriptions, memberships, passes, connectivity plans and online utility services, free or paid; **recurring and retained services** (cleaning, gardening, household staff, personal trainer, concierge, jet cards) (I);
- groceries as bought, graded by the quality of the ingredient, and **packaged prepared foods and drinks** (ready meals, frozen foods, dips, soups, baby food, bakery, sweets, snacks, soft drinks, water) (J1–J9);
- alcoholic drinks in sealed containers; tobacco, nicotine, cannabis and other plant products taken for effect (J7, J10);
- money, stamps and medals as objects; adult objects (neutral register).

**Out** —
- trade and institutional equipment (it drops out, or stays at kind level with no models);
- one-off services and tickets for a single event or journey (a kept stub is F5);
- bank balances and cryptocurrency holdings;
- **titles and genres**: films, TV, music, books, video and board-game titles belong to the entertainment and literature taxonomies; here only the object (carrier, format, edition, publisher or label line, condition, storage);
- **pursuits themselves** (sports, art forms, crafts, puzzles, faith practices);
- dishes and recipes (Culinary taxonomy); the internet as entertainment (free feeds and platforms).

*Why this order.* Status questions come first so that later functional questions never see animals, medicines, plants, food, property, fixtures or data. Inside groceries the narrow product families come before the J5 catch-all, and J10 and J7 precede J9 so that cannabis drinks and alcohol-free beer find their homes. Parts (21) come before function. Weapons, vehicles, farm and animal equipment, religious, money and paper objects precede the general play, worn and home steps that would otherwise catch them. E5 and E3 precede D so that consoles, VR headsets and studio gear are not read as general electronics; D precedes A5 and A so that smartwatches and smart rings stay devices. E1 and E2 precede the worn steps so that toy dress-up and sport-specific kit are filed first; work PPE (42) precedes clothing. Craft, garden and office precede the household steps; the C1 and C8 catch-alls come last.

## Categories

Each category has one membership test and the precedence sentences for its blurred edges. Departments are those of the live reader pages.

### A — Worn & Carried

**A1 Clothing** — Test: a garment, headwear, scarf, glove, belt, hosiery, uniform, costume or traditional dress worn on the body for everyday, formal or work wear.
- Garments, footwear and helmets made for one sport are E2; motorcycle riding gear is G1.
- Protective equipment worn against a work hazard (hard hats, respirators, hi-vis, chainsaw trousers) is C1.
- Religious dress and vestments, fetish wear and toy dress-up crowns and wands: A1, A1 and E1.
- Wigs, hairpieces and hair ties are H1.

**A2 Footwear** — Test: anything worn on the foot, including work, riding and traditional footwear, with shoe care.
- Footwear made for one sport (football boots, ski boots, climbing shoes) is E2.
- Shoe polish and shoe care are A2, not B6; orthotic insoles made to treat are H2.

**A3 Jewellery** — Test: an ornament worn on the body, hair or clothing, including amulets, piercing jewellery, traditional jewellery and jewellery parts.
- Smart rings are D2; medical ID jewellery is H2; watches are A5.
- Plain hair ties and clips are H1; loose gemstones and pearls are F6; patches sold to sew on are C4.

**A4 Bags & Accessories** — Test: a bag, wallet, small leather good, eyewear frame or sunglasses, luggage, umbrella, hand fan, baby carrier or other accessory carried on the person.
- All frames, sunglasses and reading glasses are A4; spectacle and contact lenses are H2.
- A case made for one object follows it (S3); tool bags are C1; cool bags B5.
- Quivers, chalk bags and hydration packs are E2; sword canes are E4.

**A5 Clocks & Watches** — Test: a watch, clock or timer whose main function is to show or measure time, with its straps, storage and parts.
- Smartwatches and hybrid smartwatches are D2; clock radios and smart displays D3.
- Chess and game clocks are E1; kitchen timers B5; a garden sundial is B4.

### B — Home

**B1 Furniture & Storage** — Test: movable furniture made to sit, lie, work at, display or store things, indoors or out, including nursery furniture, racks, boxes and organisers.
- Fitted kitchens, wardrobes and other fitted storage are G3.
- Workbenches and tool storage are C1; camp chairs and tables are E2; desk organisers C5.
- Albums and binders for collected objects follow what they hold (F3, F5).

**B2 Soft Furnishings** — Test: a household textile: rug or mat, bedding, blanket, cushion, curtain or blind, towel, or table and kitchen linen.
- Fitted flooring is G3; a hair towel is H1; furnishing fabric sold by the metre to sew is B2.

**B3 Lighting** — Test: a movable light (lamp, ceiling or wall light, decorative or outdoor light, torch, lantern) or a bulb, strip, shade or lamp part.
- Built-in fittings (recessed downlights, track, under-cabinet) are G3.
- Candles and candle holders are B4; studio and photo lights D4; grow lights C2; aquarium lights B10.

**B4 Decor** — Test: an object made to decorate a home or garden, or a celebration effect: vases, mirrors, wall decor, candles, home fragrance, incense, seasonal and folk decor, fireworks, artificial plants, garden ornaments, flags.
- Decorative lighting is B3; works of art and collectible figurines are F2.
- Incense made for a rite is F4; perfume worn on the body is H4.
- Decorative replica weapons are E4.

**B5 Kitchen & Dining** — Test: an object made to prepare, cook, brew, serve or store food and drink: cookware, kitchen knives, tools, appliances, coffee and tea equipment, tableware, glassware, preserving gear.
- Barbecues, smokers and pizza ovens are C2; camp stoves E2.
- Built-in and range cookers, fitted sinks and built-in wine cabinets are G3; freestanding ones stay B5.
- Pocket and utility knives are C1; fighting and hunting knives E4.

**B6 Cleaning & Laundry** — Test: equipment, tools and products made to clean a home or its textiles, or to collect waste.
- Shoe care is A2; vehicle care G1; adult-product cleaners H3; pool cleaners C2.
- Central vacuum and waste disposal units are G3; aquarium cleaning tools B10.

**B7 Portable Climate & Air** — Test: a movable appliance that heats, cools, moves, cleans, humidifies or dries the air of a room, or monitors indoor air quality.
- Built-in heating, cooling, ventilation, ceiling fans and heated towel rails are G3.
- Patio heaters and fire pits are C2; electric blankets B2; heated low tables B1.

**B8 Home Safety & Security** — Test: a movable object made to secure a home, its people and its contents, or to keep pests out: locks, safes, alarm kits, cameras, doorbells, fire and leak detectors, child safety, pest control.
- Door and window locks, multipoint and smart locks included, are B8.
- Installed intruder alarms, intercoms and window guards are G3; bicycle and vehicle locks G1.
- Skin-applied insect repellent is H1; GPS pet trackers B10.

**B10 Animal Equipment** — Test: equipment, food and supplies made to keep, feed, house, carry, train or care for animals, by animal (dogs, cats, fish and ponds, horses, birds, small animals, reptiles, bees).
- The animals themselves are in the breeds and species register.
- Farm-scale livestock equipment is C7; backyard flocks and leisure horses stay B10.
- Riders' helmets are E2 and riding boots A2; pet urns F4.

**C2 Garden & Grounds** — Test: equipment and supplies made for growing plants or keeping gardens, grounds and pools, or for cooking and heating outdoors.

**C3 Plants & Living Cultures** — Test: a living plant, fungus or culture kept to grow or propagate, a fresh-cut ornamental plant or Christmas tree, or a seed, bulb, spore or cutting for growing.

- Dried and artificial plants are B4; dried baking yeast is J5.

**C4 Craft & Art Supplies** — Test: a material, tool or machine for making things by hand, grouped by material: paint, paper, fabric, yarn, beads, clay, glass, resin, wax, leather, model paints.

**C5 Office & Stationery** — Test: an object made for writing, paper and desk work or calculating: pens, ink, notebooks, paper, seals and stamps, filing, calculators, collector machines (typewriters, slide rules).
