## How to read this file

**What is in scope.** Every generic type of object a person can own and keep: worn, carried, furnished, used, ridden, lived in, grown, displayed, played, collected, run as software or held as data, and the subscriptions, memberships and online utility services step 5 names. Media, games and digital content are here only as objects, carriers, formats, files and kinds of access; their genres, and the internet as entertainment, belong to separate taxonomies. Plants are in. Animals are out, but equipment for animals is in. **Groceries are in** (domain J): foods for people bought as ingredients or as raw, preserved or single-ingredient products, judged by the quality of the ingredient (breed, cut, grade, origin, age), and alcoholic drinks in sealed containers. Prepared foods and drinks (step 1) are out and left to the Culinary taxonomy; any packaged prepared kind that Culinary lacks is recorded in the review log, not filed here. A grocery that Culinary also describes (kimchi, smoked salmon, ham) is filed once here, as the product as bought, and Culinary links to it. **Tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products in any form, and other plant products taken for their effect**, are in (B9).

**Levels in stage 1.** Domain, then Category. Domains are a reading aid that groups neighbouring categories. **Domain names describe most of their members, not all; they are never tests.** Placement is always to a category, by the placement key.

**The axis: a short status filter, then designed function.** Steps 1 to 8 first sort objects by status: outside scope, human health, groceries, real property, software, data and subscriptions, living, natural specimen, or part of another object. From step 9 on, an object is filed only by its **primary designed function**: what it is made to do, or to be, for its owner. It is never filed by material, power source, technology, value, age, maker, brand, room or setting, installation, connectivity, market (domestic or commercial), the user's age, ability or gender, occasion, how it was acquired, its condition, or why the owner keeps it. Those are modifiers (Appendix A).

**The placement key is authoritative.** Category descriptions restate the key. Where they differ, the key wins.

**Shared rules for every category.**
- **S1 One home.** Every object type has one category. Run the placement key and stop at the first *yes*.
- **S1a Branches.** Branches inside a step are tried in the order printed; the first branch that fits wins. When a step's lead question is *yes* but no branch fits, the answer is *no*: continue to the next step.
- **S1b Notes bind.** A note in brackets inside a step is part of the key. It takes the objects it names out of that step and files them directly at the step or category it names; the steps in between are not tried. If the named step has branches and none fits, continue from the step after the named step. Where a note and a lead question disagree, the note wins.
- **S2 Generic types.** A kind is a generic type of object a person can own. Models, cuts and designs are named forms under a kind. An individual item is never a kind: a one-of-a-kind object is filed under the generic type it belongs to (an original painting under "painting").
- **S3 Parts follow the whole.** A component, attachment or consumable **made to be fitted to or used up in one kind of object** goes to that object's category: a graphics card to Computers, a lens to Cameras, guitar strings to Music, printer ink to Computers, a child car seat to Vehicles. **Cases, bags, covers and stands are parts only when they are made to hold, carry or protect one object (or one pair) at a time and travel or stand with it**: a phone case, a guitar case or stand, a golf bag, a camera bag, a watch strap. **Never parts:** furniture, racks, shelves, hooks, hangers, boxes and other storage that hold several objects or stand in a room as furniture, whatever they are made to hold (B1; safes are B8); anything worn on the body (step 17); building products (step 4's note); anything for growing plants (C2); laundry baskets are B6. **Cleaning products are B6 and fuels are C8, whatever they are used in**: dishwasher salt, descalers and cleaning tablets are B6; water-softener salt, filter cartridges and moisture absorbers are B7; mothballs and moth repellents are B8; contact-lens solution is H2; shoe and textile waterproofing and care sprays are B6; sex-toy cleaners are B6.
- **S3a Sets and kits.** A set of objects of one kind is filed as that kind. A mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed. A kit sold, standardised and compared as one object (first-aid kit, manicure set, tool kit, science experiment kit, mint and proof set, rock and mineral teaching set) is a kind. A furniture suite or set sold as one unit is filed by its pieces, each as its own kind, with a unit-of-sale attribute; there are no suite kinds.
- **S3b S3 readings.** These sentences bind like notes (S1b) and file the objects they name directly:
    - A cable with a proprietary connector made for one device follows it (S3); a cable with standard connectors (USB, HDMI, audio, instrument, microphone, patch, MIDI) is D1.
    - A generic charger (USB, GaN, Qi) is D1; a charger made for one device follows it (S3); a charger for standard-size cells is C8; a cordless-platform charger is C1.
    - A battery pack or charger made for a maker's cordless platform is C1, whatever tools or garden machines the platform runs. A battery made for one device follows it (a vape battery is B9). A loose standard-size cell, 18650 included, is C8.
    - Gimbals, selfie sticks, clip-on lenses, battery cases and clip-on game controllers made only for phones are D2 (S3); one made for cameras, consoles or computers that also takes a phone stays there.
    - Electronics made to be fitted to a vehicle (car audio, dashcam, mounted sat-nav, bike computer, OBD tracker) are G1 (S3), and guidance, auto-steer and monitor kits made for a tractor or farm machine follow it into C7; a portable sat-nav and a vehicle-mounted two-way radio are D2.
    - A roof box or rack made to be fitted to one vehicle is G1; a free-standing rack is B1.
    - A bracket, arm or mount made to hold one screen or speaker follows it (S3: D3, monitors D1, studio and stage speakers E3); a stand with shelves or storage is B1.
    - A holder, case, sleeve or stand made for one object follows it (S3), except passport covers, card and badge holders and sleeves for identity, payment and travel cards, which are A4 (step 17); sleeves, toploaders and deck boxes for playing or trading cards are E1 (step 25). An album, binder, stock book, box or tray made to hold several collected objects (stamps, coins, cards, photographs) is B1 when empty; ring binders, lever-arch and box files and letter trays for papers are C5 filing supplies (step 31). A filled photo album or scrapbook is F5; a blank scrapbook album is C4.
    - Welding wire and rods, solder, a grinding disc or a sanding sheet cut for one sander is C1 under S3; sandpaper and abrasive sheets and rolls for general use are C8.
    - Tools made to service a vehicle, bicycle or machine are C1; only parts, attachments and consumables follow the vehicle under S3.
    - A recorder sold only to run security cameras follows them (B8); a general network drive is D1.
    - A universal tripod is D4. A camera made only to fit a telescope is C6 (S3); a camera made for general photography as well is D4.
    - A toys-to-life figure is E1; its portal or reader is a D5 part. Toniebox-type figures are D3 parts under S3.
    - Studio backdrops are B2 and studio furniture B1; studio lights, stands (backdrop stands included) and flashes are D4.
    - Stage-lighting control and rigging made for lighting fixtures (DMX controllers, dimmer packs, lighting truss and clamps) are B3 under S3; general audio and video gear stays D3.
    - A dust extractor made for workshop machines is C1; a wet-and-dry vacuum is B6.
    - A watch chain, Albert or fob chain made to attach a pocket watch is A5 under S3; a decorative fob, seal or charm worn without a watch is A3.
    - A belt buckle, plain or decorative, is a part of a belt and is A1 under S3.
    - Cabinet knobs and pulls are C8; a lockset that includes a lock is B8.
- **S4 Modifiers never split the top level.** See Appendix A.
- **S5 Test the object, not the motive.** A signed football is a football; a collected teapot is a teapot; an antique chair is a chair; a commemorative plate is a plate.
- **S6 Attestation.** Every name added later has an attested source.

## Categories

### A — Worn & Carried
- **A1 Clothing** — garments and wearable accessories of any material: everyday, traditional and subculture dress, underwear, uniforms, costume, team and fan jerseys, protective clothing, helmets and personal protective equipment.
- **A2 Footwear** — anything worn on the feet, except footwear made for a sport (E2).
- **A3 Jewellery** — ornaments worn on the body, hair or clothing, including pins, badges, patches and worn amulets.
- **A4 Bags & Accessories** — bags and luggage of every kind, wallets, umbrellas, non-corrective eyewear, hand fans, keyrings, fashion canes, baby carriers.
- **A5 Clocks & Watches** — timepieces of every kind, worn or not.

### B — Home
- **B1 Furniture & Storage** — pieces and containers to sit, lie, work at, display, store and organise things, indoor or outdoor, fitted or not, including mattresses.
- **B2 Soft Furnishings** — bedding, rugs and carpets, blinds and curtains, cushions, throws, table linen, towels.
- **B3 Lighting** — lamps, light fittings, bulbs, lanterns and torches.
- **B4 Decor** — ornamental objects that are not pictures, sculpture or lamps, decorative lighting, festival and party decorations, garden ornaments, flags and signs.
- **B5 Kitchen & Dining** — everything made to prepare, cook, cool, store or serve food and drink, powered or not, domestic or commercial.
- **B6 Cleaning & Laundry** — everything made to clean, wash or dry, cleaning products, waste bins.
- **B7 Climate, Water & Energy** — heating, cooling and air treatment, domestic water, baths and sanitaryware, home energy equipment.
- **B8 Home Safety & Security** — locks, safes, alarms, extinguishers, security cameras, child safety, pest control.
- **B9 Smoking & Vaping** — tobacco, nicotine and cannabis products in any form, other plant products taken for a stimulant or psychoactive effect, and implements for smoking, vaping or preparing them.
- **B10 Animal Equipment** — housing, tack, carriers, feeders and feed, care, veterinary and toys for animals, kept or wild.

### C — Making, Growing & Work
- **C1 Tools & Workshop Equipment** — hand and power tools, workshop machines, ladders, measuring and testing tools for building and trades, generators, tool storage.
- **C2 Garden & Grounds** — tools, machines and supplies for growing plants and keeping grounds and pools.
- **C3 Plants & Living Cultures** — living plants and fungi, fresh-cut flowers, seeds and bulbs, living cultures.

- **C8 Building Materials & Hardware** — building products, hardware, electrical and plumbing supplies, household batteries, fuels, packing materials.

- **D1 Computers & Components** — computers, components, peripherals, storage, networking, point-of-sale equipment, loose electronic components (software is I1).

- **D5 Video Games** — consoles, handhelds, controllers, VR headsets, arcade machines, video games on any carrier or as downloads; game software divided by carrier type (platform is an attribute), never by genre.
