## The named-entry standard (apply to every kind in the department)
1. **One named level per kind**, stated in `nl` (one sentence, plain words): what one named entry is. For consoles and handhelds it is the **platform**: a distinct hardware system sold under its own name (PlayStation 2; Game Boy Advance). Regional names of the same platform are aliases (Mega Drive / Genesis; PC Engine / TurboGrafx-16), not separate entries. Revisions of a platform are versions, not entries (PS2 slim, Game Boy Advance SP, Mega Drive Model 2, Xbox Series S is a version of the Xbox Series X|S platform unless the reference lists it as its own platform). For product-family kinds (emulation handhelds, handheld gaming PCs, FPGA and clone consoles) the named level is the **product family or model line** (Anbernic RG35XX family; Steam Deck); the individual models are its versions. For LCD handhelds: a **series** (Game & Watch Silver); the individual games in it are versions.
2. **Completeness from a fetched reference list.** Where a reference lists the whole space (Wikipedia: List of home video game consoles; List of handheld game consoles; Home video game console generations; List of dedicated consoles; List of retro style video game consoles; Game & Watch; and similar), include **every entry in that list that falls in the kind's scope**, not a selection. Where no whole-space list exists (emulation handhelds, handheld PCs, FPGA/clone consoles), include every family named by at least one fetched specialist overview, and say so in `sel`. Write `sel` (one sentence): which reference(s) the list follows and any scope cut (e.g. "every home console in Wikipedia's List of home video game consoles, second generation onward; first-generation dedicated consoles are under Plug-and-play").
3. **Sections along the domain's own axis.** Each archetype gets `g`: its section label. Home consoles and handheld consoles: the console generation ("Second generation (1976–1992)", … "Ninth generation (2020– )") exactly as the fetched generations page names and dates them. Other kinds: the axis the reference uses (maker for emulation handhelds and handheld PCs; implementation FPGA / clone chip / emulation for clone consoles; series era for Game & Watch). Write the kind's `xg` (a few words naming that axis, e.g. "by console generation"). Within a section, order by release year (then name).
4. **Prominence is a badge, not a bucket.** Optional `role` on an archetype: "origin" (first of its kind or first of a lineage, per a fetched page) or "landmark" (named by a fetched page as best-selling, defining or most influential of its generation). Use it sparingly: at most about one in five entries. Everything else is a plain entry. There is no separate "models" list in this department: every former model either becomes an archetype (a platform or family in scope), a version of one, or is removed because it is out of scope (say which in the report). After this pass, no kind in this department has `m` or `r`.
5. **Every archetype**: `n`, `mk` (maker, exact brand-register name; add new makers to rebuild/out/E5_brands.json with country and founding year only if a fetched page gives them), `y` (release year), `g`, optional `role`, `a` (regional or other names), `d` (one sentence, what it is or why it matters, under 25 words), `s` (a fetched page: the platform's own page or the reference list row's page), `v` (versions: hardware revisions and notable special editions you can confirm on a fetched page; each {"n","y","d"} with `d` a few words), `dt` only if useful.
6. Keep each kind's `t`-level fields from the structure pass (`d`, `tt`, `see`) unless they now contradict the entries; update `tt` so its groups describe attributes, never named products.
