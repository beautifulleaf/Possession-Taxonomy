## What "organized like Stews" means (the model to copy)
Culinary's family "Stews, Curries & Braises" opens with one test of what belongs ("pieces cooked in a sauce or gravy that is eaten with them"), then splits into four sub-families, each stating ONE axis ("how the spice or chile base is built", "what thickens the sauce", "the cooking liquid", "the seasoning liquid"). Every leaf has a one-line definition with examples and its boundary ("…unlike chilli-oil poached dishes → D1-7"), and its kinds are grouped into sections along a stated axis. A reader sees the whole space at once and can tell why each thing sits where it does and where its neighbours went.

Today's Possession shelves often fail this. Example, E1 "Fidgets": no statement of what a fidget is versus a puzzle or a desk toy; kinds cut by mixed questions ("Clicker & button fidget" holds pop-its, which pop rather than click); one flat type list mixes shape, material and form ("Three-lobe · Two-lobe · Metal · Plastic · Spinner ring"); archetypes that are not named things ("Three-lobed bearing spinner", "Stress ball", "Magnetic push slider"); named classics missing; Rubik's Snake under fidgets though it is a twisty puzzle.
