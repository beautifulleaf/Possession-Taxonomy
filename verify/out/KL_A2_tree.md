# A2 Footwear: decided tree

Category: A2 Footwear (domain A, Worn and Carried). Membership test: footwear of any kind, including safety and steel-toe boots, anything worn on the feet; footwear made for one sport is not here.

Structure: sub-family (form family, tested on the object's construction), then leaf (only inside Shoes and Boots), then kind (the generic type a buyer can own and compare). Each object gets one kind. Where a sub-family has no leaf, the kind id has no leaf part.

Kind ids read A2.<sub-family>.<leaf, where used>.<kind number>.

## Rules that apply to every kind

- A kind is the generic type, not a maker model, colourway, size or material. These are attributes and never kinds: broguing (full, semi, quarter, longwing), toe and upper pattern (plain, cap, wingtip, wholecut), heel form (stiletto, block, kitten, wedge, platform), construction (cemented, Blake, Goodyear welt, stitch-down), upper material, width, colour, closure details, heating and lights.
- Safety footwear and therapeutic or diabetic footwear are not kinds: the object is filed by its form (closure and cut, or shaft height) like any other shoe; the safety class or therapeutic type is an attribute.
- A name for a setting or use ("house shoe", "room shoe", "work shoe", "school shoe") is not a form.
- A regional or traditional name is a kind only where no generic kind covers its form; otherwise the object goes under the generic kind that covers its form.

## Precedence sentences

- Footwear worn on the feet is in this category even if sold as fetish wear, vestment or costume; a shoe-shaped box, planter or souvenir that cannot be worn is not.
- A shoe is made for a sport only when it is built for the demands of one sport and sold as a performance model with sport-specific soles or spikes (football boots, spiked track shoes, climbing shoes, ski boots, skates, ballet, pointe, tap and dance shoes, cycling shoes with cleats, performance basketball and tennis models). Basketball- and tennis-lineage sneakers worn every day, running shoes, hiking boots, skate shoes and trainers are in this category, and the sport is a tag.
- Waders are made for angling and are not in this category.
- Riding boots are in this category: the boot made for riding (tall field or dress boot) and the riding-style boot made for everyday wear are both kinds in Boots, Knee and over-knee. Footwear for an animal is not in this category.
- Hosiery (socks, stockings, tights, leg warmers, gaiters, tabi socks) is not in this category. Anything worn over or in place of a shoe (spats, galoshes, overshoes, split-toe boots with a rubber sole) is.
- A medical walking boot that immobilises the ankle or foot is a brace and is not in this category; orthotic and therapeutic inserts are not in this category. An ordinary shoe in an adapted or comfort form (hook-and-loop closure, wide fit, cushioned footbed) stays here.
- Laces, removable insoles, heel grips, shoe trees and shoe horns made to be fitted into or used with one pair of shoes follow the shoe into this category (sub-family 7). Shoe care products (cream, polish, cleaner, brushes, waterproofing sprays) and racks, cabinets, boxes and bags that hold several pairs are not.
- A shoe with lights or heating stays here; a separate sensor or insole whose main function is sensing or tracking is not.
- A historical style made or sold as an original or replica goes under its nearest generic kind, and is a kind of its own only if it is sold as its own category.

## 1 Sneakers and athletic-style shoes (no leaf)

- A2.1.01 Low-top sneaker: lace-up athletic-style shoe cut below the ankle bone.
- A2.1.02 High-top sneaker: lace-up athletic-style shoe whose upper rises over the ankle bone.
- A2.1.03 Slip-on sneaker: laceless athletic-style shoe with elastic panels or a stretch collar.
- A2.1.04 Running shoe: cushioned road-running shoe.
- A2.1.05 Trail running shoe: running shoe with a lugged outsole for off-road ground.
- A2.1.06 Walking shoe: cushioned shoe made for walking.
- A2.1.07 Skate shoe: shoe with a durable upper and flat grip sole made for skateboarding.
- A2.1.08 Chunky (dad) sneaker: sneaker with an oversized, sculpted midsole as the main design feature.
- A2.1.09 Minimalist (barefoot) shoe: very thin, flexible, low or zero-drop sole shaped to the foot, including five-toe designs.
- A2.1.10 Hiking shoe (low): low-cut lace-up shoe made for walking on rough ground, with a flexible lugged sole.
- A2.1.11 Water shoe (aqua shoe): drainable shoe made for use in and beside water.

A safety trainer is filed by form (usually a low-top sneaker).

## 2 Shoes (closed, non-athletic)

Leaf axis: closure and vamp cut. Dress-shoe kinds are set by lacing and cut.

Leaf 2.1 Fastened: laces or a buckle close the shoe over a covered instep.
- A2.2.1.01 Oxford: fastened shoe with the lace eyelets stitched under the vamp (closed lacing).
- A2.2.1.02 Derby: fastened shoe with the lace eyelets stitched on top of the vamp (open lacing).
- A2.2.1.03 Monk strap: fastened shoe closed by a buckled strap across the instep instead of laces.
- A2.2.1.04 Saddle shoe: lace-up shoe with a contrasting saddle-shaped band across the instep.

Leaf 2.2 Slip-on: no fastening; the vamp covers the instep.
- A2.2.2.01 Loafer: laceless low shoe with a moccasin-style construction and a closed heel.
- A2.2.2.02 Boat (deck) shoe: low shoe with a siped non-marking sole, usually with a leather lace running round the upper.
- A2.2.2.03 Moccasin: shoe made of a soft sole-and-upper wrap with a plug or apron at the toe.
- A2.2.2.04 Espadrille: canvas or fabric shoe on a rope (jute) sole or wedge.
- A2.2.2.05 Mule: any backless shoe with a closed toe; a backless shoe with a loafer upper is a mule.
- A2.2.2.06 Clog: slip-on with a thick rigid sole of wood or moulded material, backed or backless.

Leaf 2.3 Court cut and strap: a low-cut vamp leaves the instep open; the shoe is held by its cut or by a strap.
- A2.2.3.01 Court shoe (pump): low-cut closed-heel shoe with a high or low heel and no fastening.
- A2.2.3.02 Slingback: court-cut shoe held by a strap round the back of the heel.
- A2.2.3.03 Mary Jane: low-cut shoe with a strap across the instep.
- A2.2.3.04 Ballet flat: flat or near-flat low-cut shoe with a thin sole and a rounded, shallow vamp.
- A2.2.3.05 Peep-toe shoe: court-cut shoe with an opening at the toe.

## 3 Boots

Leaf axis: shaft height of the kind's canonical form. A kind keeps its name when one model departs from the canonical height.

Leaf 3.1 Ankle (shaft to the ankle bone)
- A2.3.1.01 Ankle boot (bootie): short boot ending at or just above the ankle, any closure.
- A2.3.1.02 Chelsea boot: ankle boot with elastic side gussets and no laces.
- A2.3.1.03 Chukka (desert boot): ankle boot with two or three eyelet pairs and an unlined or soft construction.

Leaf 3.2 Mid (above the ankle, below mid-calf)
- A2.3.2.01 Service and dress lace-up boot: lace-up boot on a plain, sleeker last, without a heavy lugged or wedge work sole.
- A2.3.2.02 Work boot: lace-up boot with a heavy lugged or wedge outsole made for labour, usually Goodyear-welted or stitch-down.
- A2.3.2.03 Engineer boot: pull-on boot with a strap and buckle at the instep and the top, no laces.
- A2.3.2.04 Combat (military) boot: lace-up boot made to a military service pattern.
- A2.3.2.05 Hiking boot: lace-up boot made for walking on rough ground: padded collar, flexible lugged sole; not made for labour.
- A2.3.2.06 Motorcycle boot: boot with reinforced ankle and shin and a gear-shift area, made for riding a motorcycle.
- A2.3.2.07 Sheepskin-lined pull-on boot: pull-on boot of sheepskin with a fleece lining and a soft, moulded sole.
- A2.3.2.08 Split-toe work boot (jika-tabi): boot with a separated big toe and a rubber sole, fastened by hooks or a zip up the lower leg.

Leaf 3.3 Calf (to the calf, below the knee)
- A2.3.3.01 Cowboy (western) boot: pull-on boot with a stitched shaft, pointed or rounded toe and angled heel in the western pattern.
- A2.3.3.02 Rain boot (wellington): waterproof rubber or synthetic boot made for wet ground.
- A2.3.3.03 Snow and winter boot: insulated, waterproof boot with a lugged sole made for cold and snow.

Leaf 3.4 Knee and over-knee
- A2.3.4.01 Knee-high boot: boot with the shaft reaching to the knee.
- A2.3.4.02 Over-the-knee (thigh) boot: boot with the shaft above the knee.
- A2.3.4.03 Equestrian riding boot: tall field or dress boot made for riding (smooth sole, spur rest, close fit).
- A2.3.4.04 Riding-style boot: tall boot in the equestrian pattern made for everyday wear.

Test between the two riding kinds: what the boot is made for (riding features against everyday wear).

## 4 Sandals and open footwear (no leaf)

- A2.4.01 Flip-flop (thonged flat sandal): flat sandal held by a strap between the toes.
- A2.4.02 Slide: backless sandal with a band or straps over the front of the foot.
- A2.4.03 Flat strap sandal: flat sandal held by straps across and round the foot.
- A2.4.04 Heeled or wedge sandal: sandal on a heel or wedge.
- A2.4.05 Sports and outdoor sandal: sandal with adjustable straps on a treaded sole.
- A2.4.06 Gladiator sandal: flat or low sandal with multiple straps up the foot or leg.
- A2.4.07 Woven leather sandal (huarache): sandal with a woven-leather upper.
- A2.4.08 Raised wooden sandal (geta): sandal on a raised wooden base with teeth.

A regional name is a kind only where no generic kind covers its form: thonged flat sandals and flat strap sandals go under kinds 01 and 03.

## 5 Slippers (soft, unstructured footwear without a rigid outsole; no leaf)

- A2.5.01 Closed slipper: backed slipper.
- A2.5.02 Open (backless) slipper: slipper without a back.
- A2.5.03 Slipper boot: soft boot without a rigid outsole.

A backless shoe on a rigid sole is a slide (sandals) or a mule (shoes), not a slipper. A soft boot without a rigid outsole is a slipper boot; a sheepskin-lined boot on a moulded outsole is a boot.

## 6 Over-footwear (worn over another shoe to cover the foot; no leaf)

- A2.6.01 Overshoe and galosh: waterproof covering worn over a shoe.
- A2.6.02 Spat: cloth or leather covering for the instep and ankle worn over a shoe.

## 7 Parts and accessories made for one pair of shoes (no leaf)

- A2.7.01 Shoelace.
- A2.7.02 Removable insole, non-medical.
- A2.7.03 Heel grip.
- A2.7.04 Shoe tree.
- A2.7.05 Shoe horn.
