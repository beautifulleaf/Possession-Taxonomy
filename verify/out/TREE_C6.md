# C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments: decided tree

Category: C6 Optical & Scientific Instruments (domain C, Making, Growing & Work). Membership test (key step 23): an instrument made to observe, magnify or measure for observation, study or science: binoculars, telescopes, microscopes, magnifiers and loupes (head-worn included), eclipse glasses, astronomical models and sky instruments, weather instruments and windsocks, air-quality monitors, laboratory glassware and instruments, and science-education kits (experiment programme), together with parts and accessories made for one such instrument (S3). A measuring tool made for another task goes with that task (kitchen B5, postal C5, building and trade C1, body H1, golf and hunting rangefinders and navigation compasses E2); rifle scopes are E4, spectacles that correct vision and low-vision aids are H2, a universal tripod and a camera made for general photography are D4. Structure: sub-family (what the instrument is made to do with its subject), then leaf (the axis stated per sub-family; none in C6.4), then kind (the design principle, the form in which the lens is held, the mechanism, the vessel or device, or the part type). Modifiers (tier, price band, aperture and size class, maker, scene, era, user, market, material, connectivity, mount) are attributes, tags and views, never kinds. A kind marked † is named only by the key, a review or a decision, or rests on a claim not yet shown on a fetched page, and needs a fetched page before it enters the data.

Ids (R-58): C6.n is a sub-family and C6.n.m a leaf; a kind is C6.n.m.kk, or C6.n.kk where a sub-family has no leaf.

## C6.1 Optical & astronomical instruments

Leaf axis: instrument type. Sub-family test: look through it or model the sky.

### C6.1.1 Binoculars & monoculars

Kind axis: prism design or form. Magnification x aperture size class, ED glass, prism glass grade, coating, waterproofing, stabilisation and rangefinding are attributes.
- C6.1.1.01 Porro-prism binoculars: twin-barrel binoculars with Porro prisms, giving an offset, Z-shaped body.
- C6.1.1.02 Roof-prism binoculars: twin-barrel binoculars with roof prisms, giving a straight, slim body.
- C6.1.1.03 Giant binoculars: binoculars of 70 to 150 mm aperture, used on a tripod.
- C6.1.1.04 Opera glasses: small Galilean-design binoculars for the theatre and opera.
- C6.1.1.05 Monocular: single-tube handheld viewing instrument, not a telescope on a mount.

### C6.1.2 Night-vision & thermal viewers

- C6.1.2.01 Night-vision viewer: viewer that intensifies available light to show a scene in the dark (rule T(b), C6-O1: provisional until attested). †
- C6.1.2.02 Thermal-imaging viewer: viewer that shows a scene by the heat it gives off (rule T(b), C6-O1: provisional until attested). †

### C6.1.3 Astronomical telescopes

Kind axis: optical design. Aperture, glass class (achromat, ED, APO), GoTo or manual, and the `mount` of a complete set (alt-az, Dobsonian, equatorial) are attributes.
- C6.1.3.01 Refractor telescope: telescope that gathers light with a lens objective.
- C6.1.3.02 Newtonian reflector telescope: telescope that gathers light with a primary mirror and a flat secondary mirror at the side of the tube; the Dobsonian is a named form of it, not a kind (C-18).
- C6.1.3.03 Schmidt-Cassegrain telescope: catadioptric telescope with a corrector plate and a folded mirror light path.
- C6.1.3.04 Maksutov-Cassegrain telescope: catadioptric telescope with a meniscus corrector and a folded mirror light path.
- C6.1.3.05 Solar (H-alpha) telescope: telescope made to observe the Sun in hydrogen-alpha light (rule T(b), C6-O1: provisional until attested). †
- C6.1.3.06 Smart (imaging) telescope: telescope with a sensor and no eyepiece that shows the sky on a screen or app (rule T(b), C6-O1: provisional until attested).

### C6.1.4 Spotting scopes

- C6.1.4.01 Spotting scope: compact single-tube telescope for daytime viewing of distant things, used on a tripod or rest.

### C6.1.5 Microscopes

Kind axis: principle. Mono, bino or trinocular head, objective grade and illumination are attributes; antique stand forms are named forms.
- C6.1.5.01 Compound light microscope: microscope with an objective and an eyepiece in series, for thin specimens on a slide.
- C6.1.5.02 Stereo (dissecting) microscope: low-power microscope with two optical paths giving an upright three-dimensional view of solid objects.
- C6.1.5.03 Digital microscope: microscope with its own sensor that shows the image on a screen or computer (rule T(b), C6-O1: provisional until attested).
- C6.1.5.04 Pocket microscope: small handheld microscope carried in the pocket. †
- C6.1.5.05 Polarizing microscope: microscope that lights the specimen with polarized light, for minerals and crystals.

### C6.1.6 Magnifiers & loupes

Kind axis: form in which the lens is held. Power, lens material, illumination and use (reading, jewellery, stamps, botany) are attributes or tags.
- C6.1.6.01 Handheld magnifier: magnifying lens on a handle held in the hand.
- C6.1.6.02 Pocket (folding) loupe: small loupe that folds into a case or holder to be carried.
- C6.1.6.03 Stand magnifier: magnifier on a desk, floor or neck-cord stand.
- C6.1.6.04 Head-worn magnifier: loupe or magnifier on a headband or clip, also called a binocular loupe (K-135, R-13).

### C6.1.7 Solar viewing glasses

- C6.1.7.01 Eclipse viewing glasses: glasses with a solar filter made for looking at the Sun (K-135).

### C6.1.8 Astronomical models & sky instruments

- C6.1.8.01 Orrery: mechanical model of the Sun, planets and moons (C-18). †
- C6.1.8.02 Astrolabe: instrument that models the sky for finding the positions of stars and the time (C-18). †
- C6.1.8.03 Planetarium projector: projector that shows the night sky on a dome or wall (C-18). †

## C6.2 Weather instruments

Leaf axis: quantity measured, with one leaf for stations that measure several (C14(c)). The sub-family reads the air and is C6 for a household or a farm alike.

### C6.2.1 Temperature & humidity

Paired because retailers sell them together.
- C6.2.1.01 Thermometer (air): instrument that reads the air temperature indoors or outdoors; a working Galileo thermometer is this kind.
- C6.2.1.02 Hygrometer: instrument that reads the humidity of the air.

### C6.2.2 Pressure

Kind axis: mechanism.
- C6.2.2.01 Cistern barometer: mercury barometer whose column stands in an open cistern.
- C6.2.2.02 Angle (diagonal) barometer: mercury barometer whose tube is bent or sloped to lengthen the scale.
- C6.2.2.03 Aneroid barometer: barometer that reads pressure from a sealed flexible capsule.

### C6.2.3 Wind

- C6.2.3.01 Anemometer: instrument that measures wind speed.
- C6.2.3.02 Wind vane: instrument that shows the wind direction.
- C6.2.3.03 Windsock: tapered fabric sleeve that shows wind direction and strength.

### C6.2.4 Precipitation & radiation

- C6.2.4.01 Rain gauge: instrument that collects and measures rainfall.
- C6.2.4.02 Pyranometer: instrument that measures solar radiation.

### C6.2.5 Multi-quantity stations

- C6.2.5.01 Weather station: instrument set that measures several weather quantities together; `sensor_suite`, `sensor_count`, `link`, `shield` and display type are attributes (C14(c)).

### C6.2.6 Air quality

- C6.2.6.01 Air-quality monitor: instrument that measures pollutants or particles in the air (K-147). †

## C6.3 Laboratory apparatus

Leaf axis: what the item does to a sample (hold or measure it; weigh, separate, warm, cool, enclose or dissect it).

### C6.3.1 Vessels & volumetric ware

Hold, mix, heat, culture or measure volume. Glass or plastic, volume and tolerance class are attributes.
- C6.3.1.01 Beaker: wide cylindrical open vessel with a spout, for holding, mixing and heating liquids.
- C6.3.1.02 Erlenmeyer flask: conical flask with a narrow neck, for mixing and heating.
- C6.3.1.03 Volumetric flask: flat-bottomed flask with a long neck and a single mark, for making a solution to an exact volume.
- C6.3.1.04 Round-bottom flask: round flask for heating and boiling liquids.
- C6.3.1.05 Test tube: small tube closed at one end, for holding or heating a small sample.
- C6.3.1.06 Graduated cylinder: tall cylinder with a volume scale, for measuring liquid volume.
- C6.3.1.07 Pipette: tube for drawing up and delivering a measured amount of liquid.
- C6.3.1.08 Burette: graduated tube with a tap, for delivering liquid in controlled amounts.
- C6.3.1.09 Petri dish: shallow lidded dish for culturing cells or microbes.

### C6.3.2 Sample-handling equipment

Device type. Capacity and temperature range are attributes.
- C6.3.2.01 Laboratory balance: balance made to weigh samples in a laboratory.
- C6.3.2.02 Centrifuge: machine that spins samples to separate their parts.
- C6.3.2.03 Laboratory incubator: chamber that holds samples at a set warm temperature.
- C6.3.2.04 Laboratory oven: oven for drying or heating samples at a set temperature.
- C6.3.2.05 Laboratory freezer: freezer or ultra-low-temperature unit made for cold storage of samples (C-18; R-28 includes laboratory refrigerators). †
- C6.3.2.06 Fume hood: ventilated cabinet that draws fumes from work on samples away from the user (C-18). †
- C6.3.2.07 Dissection set: set of instruments for dissecting specimens.

## C6.4 Science-education kits

Leaf axis: none; the sub-family holds one kind, so the data stores `leaf = null` (C5(b)).
- C6.4.01 Science experiment kit: kit made to teach science through an experiment programme; `discipline` is an attribute (chemistry, physics and electricity, biology, earth and space, crystals and materials and others as sold) with a generated view (C-11, K-149).

## C6.5 Parts and accessories made for one instrument

Leaf axis: instrument served (S3; the closing sub-family, exempt from the level-1 axis, C5(d), CV-17).

### C6.5.1 Telescope parts

- C6.5.1.01 Telescope eyepiece: eyepiece made for a telescope.
- C6.5.1.02 Alt-azimuth mount: telescope mount that moves up-down and left-right.
- C6.5.1.03 Equatorial mount: telescope mount with an axis aligned to the Earth's axis for following the sky.
- C6.5.1.04 Solar filter: filter made to fit a telescope or binocular for safe viewing of the Sun.
- C6.5.1.05 Astronomy imaging camera: camera made only to fit a telescope (K-15).

### C6.5.2 Microscope parts

- C6.5.2.01 Microscope slide: slide made to hold a specimen under a microscope.
- C6.5.2.02 Microscope camera: camera or adapter made to fit only one kind of microscope (S3 reading as in K-15). †

## Precedence inside C6

- The kind is the instrument's design, not its use, size, maker or price: binocular size class (8x42, 10x50), ED glass, prism glass grade, stabilisation and `rangefinder` are attributes, so a stabilised or rangefinding binocular is filed by its prism design (C6.1.1.01 or C6.1.1.02); a "best 8x42" list is a generated view (C-18, K-148).
- The Dobsonian is a non-maker named form under the Newtonian reflector telescope (C6.1.3.02), with `mount` an attribute (alt-az, Dobsonian, equatorial); a maker's model of the design is a maker-bound named form of the same kind (C-18).
- Smart telescopes (C6.1.3.06), solar telescopes (C6.1.3.05), digital microscopes (C6.1.5.03), night-vision viewers (C6.1.2.01) and thermal viewers (C6.1.2.02) are kinds under rule T(b), provisional until attested (C6-O1, CV-24). A telescope or microscope with a built-in sensor made to observe is C6, not D4, because step 23 is tried before step 24.
- Parts follow the whole (S3): a mount made for a telescope is C6.5.1.02 or C6.5.1.03, an eyepiece C6.5.1.01, a solar filter C6.5.1.04 and a slide C6.5.2.01; a universal tripod is D4. A camera made only to fit a telescope is C6.5.1.05 and one made to fit only one kind of microscope is C6.5.2.02; a camera made for general photography as well is D4 (K-15). A complete telescope sold with its mount is filed by its optical design, with `mount` an attribute.
- Magnifiers are kinds by the form in which the lens is held (C6.1.6.01 to C6.1.6.04); `use` (reading, jewellery, stamps, botany) is a tag and `illumination` an attribute. A head-worn loupe or magnifier on a headband or clip, a binocular loupe, is C6.1.6.04 (K-135, R-13). A magnifier made and sold as a low-vision aid is H2; any other magnifier or loupe is C6 whoever uses it (K-38). Opera glasses are C6.1.1.04 and eclipse viewing glasses C6.1.7.01 (K-135).
- A rangefinding binocular is C6 (filed by prism design, C6.1.1.01 or C6.1.1.02); golf and hunting laser rangefinders are E2 and building laser measurers C1 (K-148).
- A barometer is filed by mechanism (cistern C6.2.2.01, angle C6.2.2.02, aneroid C6.2.2.03). An instrument that measures several weather quantities together is a weather station (C6.2.5.01), whether for a household or a farm; one that measures a single quantity goes to the leaf of that quantity. An air-quality monitor is C6.2.6.01 (K-147).
- A wind vane, windsock, thermometer, barometer or hygrometer that works is C6 however ornamental, including a Galileo thermometer (C6.2.1.01) and a weather house; a decorative replica that cannot measure is B4. A weather station, barometer or thermometer built into a clock whose main function is the time is A5; where the readings are the main function and the clock is an extra it is C6.
- A laboratory balance is C6.3.2.01 and a kitchen scale is B5 (K-167); fume hoods (C6.3.2.06) and laboratory freezers (C6.3.2.05) are C6, laboratory furniture is B1; instruments that measure a sample for study or science are C6 and instruments that measure dimensions, electrical quantities or material properties for building, trade, workshop or electronics work are C1 (K-147). Glass versus plastic, volume and tolerance class do not make a kind in C6.3.1.
- A planetarium projector, orrery or astrolabe is C6.1.8.03, C6.1.8.01 or C6.1.8.02 (C-18); a star atlas, planisphere or globe is F1; a sundial is A5 (R-28).
- A kit made to teach science through experiments is C6.4.01 whatever its parts and whatever its discipline; a programmable robot or coding toy made for play is E1, and a board or component kit sold without an experiment programme is D1 (K-149, R-22).
- A child's telescope or microscope that magnifies is C6 in its design kind (C6.1.3 or C6.1.5); an imitation with no working optics, which cannot do the real object's job, is E1 (C-18, CV-32).
