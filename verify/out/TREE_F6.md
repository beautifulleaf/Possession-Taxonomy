# F6 Natural History & Specimens: decided tree

Category: F6 Natural History & Specimens (domain F, Culture, Records & Meaning). Membership test (placement key step 7): a natural object, not made or shaped by people beyond cleaning, cutting, polishing or preserving, kept as a specimen for study, display or collection: minerals, rocks, fossils, meteorites, shells, element samples, unset gemstones and loose pearls (natural, cultured or laboratory-grown, rough or cut), skeletal and taxidermy specimens, pinned insects, herbarium sheets; a preserved or emptied specimen kept as a specimen is F6 (K-80); holders, domes and stands made for one specimen follow it here (S3, K-9). Not here: living animals, eggs for hatching and living plants (step 1, C3), human remains and anatomical specimens (OUT, K-79, R-3), relics and rudraksha beads (F4), tanned hides and leather (C4, B2), natural materials sold for a use (firewood C8, garden stone C2, sponges, loofahs and pumice H1, animal chews B10, craft feathers, fleece and craft shell assortments C4, dried and stabilised flowers B4), drilled gem and pearl beads (C4) and strung or mounted ones (A3), gem simulants (A3 or C4 by form, K-77), casts, replicas and carved figures (F2 sculpture, E1 toy or kit, B4 decor; K-77, F-25), stone lamps (B3), refined bullion bars, rounds and coins (F3), works combining animal parts with other materials (F2, K-76), books about specimens (F1), lenses, UV lamps and rock hammers (C6, C1), and albums, trays, drawers and cabinets that hold several specimens (B1 when empty). Structure (Candidate A, substance first, decided; memo sections 3, 4b, 9, 10, 12 and 14): sub-family by which natural substance the object is, in museum-and-trade order (F6.1 to F6.8; F6.9 is retired and its id is not reused), then the closing parts sub-family F6.10 (C5(d), CV-17, exempt from that axis), then leaf by one stated axis per sub-family (F6.4 Meteorites, F6.8 Element samples and F6.10 Parts have `leaf = null`), then kind (the trade name at which sellers open a page and buyers compare: mineral species or major variety, rock type, fossil group, meteorite class, gem species or variety, shell family or popular group, specimen type, element form), then named form (variety or trade name, taxon, named fall, shell species, pearl trade name; never maker-bound, uncapped, one fetched page each; not listed here). The F6.1 and F6.5 kinds of one species (Amethyst, Garnet, Tourmaline, Opal; Corundum with Sapphire and Ruby) are two kinds by an object test (cut state) and share one `species_id` and alias row (C9). Attributes, tags, ledgers and views, never kinds or leaves: `presentation` (natural specimen, specimen on matrix, rough, tumbled, polished, slab, sphere, egg, faceted, cabochon; faceted and cabochon pieces fall under the F-4 test into F6.5), mineral `size_class` (thumbnail, miniature, small cabinet, cabinet, large cabinet), locality and mine, geological age and formation, `origin_type` (natural, cultured, laboratory-grown), `treatment` on coloured-gemstone and diamond kinds (untreated, unheated, heated, filled, diffused, other treatment; the treatments the trade applies are listed on the kind row, one stone's treatment never stored), UV reaction and fluorescence (C10), gem form (single, pair, lot, faceted, cabochon, rough), `quantity` (single, pair, lot, set of like specimens; Gem Rock pairs and lots are not kinds), `meteorite_form` (individual, cut slice, etched slice, fragment; fragment provisional), `part` (tooth, bone, claw), `strunz_code` on each mineral kind, element identity (a closed list of 118), colour, carat, shape, clarity, geographic origin, the GIA and other scales as registry rows (F6-SC1 to SC7), the C17 jurisdiction ledger (CITES, ivory and wildlife regimes; a generated legal flag on the kind's page), motive tags (`use:` healing, education, decor, curio, gift, birthstone) and scene tags; certificates, lab reports, condition, provenance and collection names of one piece are not stored (C16). Tier: never on a kind; no tier entry on present evidence (section 5). Maker role (maker_roles.tsv, provisional): none for the whole category ("specimens are chosen by species and locality"); the two exceptions are maker rows of class `certifier or grader` (GIA, SSEF, Gübelin, AGL, AIGS, GFCO) and `taxidermy studio`; no maker tab; the gem-lab view and the named-fall view of meteorites are generated (display only, section 14). Display groups (navigation proposal, display only, never moving a home; no group ids issued): Minerals and crystals (F6.1, shown A to Z by species, Strunz class leaves as a secondary grouping); Fossils and amber (F6.3, its five leaves as level-2 groups); Meteorites and impact glass (F6.4); Gemstones and pearls (unset) (F6.5, level 2 diamonds, coloured gemstones, pearls); Shells and coral (F6.6); Rocks and teaching sets (F6.2); Taxidermy, bones and preserved specimens (F6.7, its six leaves as level-2 groups); Element samples (F6.8); Display domes and stands for one specimen (F6.10). Quality markers (display; judged on the piece, its named form or a registry scale, never stored per piece and never a tier on a kind; marker order provisional, one guide each): minerals by colour, then form (crystal habit, matrix presentation), condition (damage and disclosed repair), luster and clarity or transparency, locality, provenance, then size class; fossils by authenticity, then completeness, surface detail and preservation, natural against repaired or restored, matrix quality, legal sourcing with identification, then size; meteorites by class and official name (Meteoritical Society), then `meteorite_form`, seller guarantee, then known fall or find history; coloured gemstones by identity (species, natural not laboratory-grown), then treatment (untreated and unheated, then heated, then other treatments), geographic origin, visual quality (colour, clarity grade VVS, VS, SI, cut, life and fire), then carat and calibrated size; diamonds by the GIA shape, colour, clarity, cut and carat; pearls by the GIA seven value factors, luster first. Pending kinds: the memo marks 12 kinds † or holds them until a fetched page shows them (F6.1.4.06, F6.5.2.02, F6.5.3.01, F6.5.3.02, F6.6.4.02, F6.7.2.03, F6.7.2.04, F6.7.2.05, F6.7.3.02, F6.7.4.01, F6.7.6.01, and the section 14 new kind F6.5.2.10 "provisional until a page shows cut opal sold"); the two F6.10 holders the memo lists as not yet attested (F6.10.02, F6.10.03) are † too; their ids are allocated here in memo order, each line ending in †, and a † kind enters the data only when a fetched page attests it. Leaves F6.7.4 and F6.7.6 hold only † kinds and are provisional with them. Provisional levels (CV-27, "prov": level provisional, not inclusion): Ruby (F6.5.2.02), Spinel (F6.5.2.04), Barite (F6.1.7.01). The mineral leaves and their order are provisional until the quartz-family placement under Strunz is read (F-24); the pearl kinds are provisional until one page divides pearls into saltwater and freshwater (F-24).

Ids (R-58): F6.n is a sub-family and F6.n.m a leaf; a kind is F6.n.m.kk, or F6.n.kk where a sub-family has no leaf.

## F6.1 Minerals
Leaf axis: chemical class (Strunz, 10 classes, in Strunz order, with the class wording the memo takes from S13; decided in F-24, provisional until the quartz-family placement is read). Kinds are uncut species or major varieties; each mineral kind carries a `strunz_code`. A loose faceted, cabochon or cutting-rough stone is F6.5; every other piece (crystal, cluster, specimen on matrix, geode, tumbled stone, slab, sphere, egg, palm stone) is here under its species, even when the species is a gem (F-4). Inside a leaf kinds are shown by Strunz division and then A to Z (section 8); an A to Z view is generated. Enter at harvest (no id yet): the further species and varieties sellers list (about 250 to 450 kinds; section 4).

### F6.1.1 Elements
- F6.1.1.01 Native gold: native gold sold as a mineral specimen (nugget, crystal or wire, on or off matrix); a refined bar, round or coin sold by weight is F3.

### F6.1.2 Sulfides
- F6.1.2.01 Pyrite: iron sulfide mineral specimen (cubes, clusters, suns, on or off matrix).

### F6.1.3 Halides
- F6.1.3.01 Fluorite: calcium fluoride mineral specimen (crystals, clusters, octahedra, specimens on matrix, polished pieces).

### F6.1.4 Oxides

- F6.1.4.01 Quartz: uncut quartz specimen that no separate quartz kind covers (rock crystal points and clusters, tumbled and polished quartz).
- F6.1.4.02 Amethyst: uncut purple quartz specimen (geode, point, cluster, tumbled stone, slab, sphere); a faceted or cabochon amethyst is F6.5.2.05.
- F6.1.4.03 Agate: uncut or polished banded chalcedony specimen (nodule, geode, slab, tumbled stone).
- F6.1.4.04 Jasper: uncut or polished opaque microcrystalline quartz specimen (rough, tumbled stone, slab, sphere); a cut cabochon of jasper falls under the F-4 test into F6.5 when attested.
- F6.1.4.05 Opal: opal specimen, matrix piece or uncut nodule; a loose opal cut en cabochon or faceted, or opal rough sold as cutting material, is F6.5.2.10.

## F6.3 Fossils and fossil resin
Leaf axis: the kind of organism or trace the fossil was (invertebrate, vertebrate, plant, trace and microbial, fossil resin). Amber and ammolite stay here in every cut state, so a cabochon of either is not F6.5 (F-4). An unworked fossil is F6 whatever it is kept for (F-17). Enter at harvest (no id yet): the further organism groups of the fossil sellers (about 60 to 90 kinds; section 4).

### F6.3.1 Invertebrates
- F6.3.1.01 Ammonite: fossil ammonoid shell, whole, split, polished or on matrix, including unworked stones kept for a religious tradition (F-17).
- F6.3.1.02 Trilobite: fossil trilobite, prepared on or off matrix.
- F6.3.1.03 Belemnite: fossil belemnite guard or other straight cephalopod.
- F6.3.1.04 Echinoderm fossil: fossil echinoderm (crinoid, starfish, sea urchin, blastoid).
- F6.3.1.05 Ammolite: gem-quality iridescent ammonite shell material, rough or cut (F-4).

### F6.3.2 Vertebrates
- F6.3.2.01 Fossil shark tooth: fossil tooth of a shark or ray.
- F6.3.2.02 Dinosaur fossil: fossil tooth, bone or claw of a dinosaur (`part` attribute).
- F6.3.2.03 Fossil fish: fossil fish, whole or partial, usually on matrix.

### F6.3.3 Plants
- F6.3.3.01 Petrified wood: fossil wood replaced by silica or other minerals, rough, slab or polished.

### F6.3.4 Trace and microbial

### F6.3.5 Fossil resin

- F6.3.5.02 Copal: young, partly fossilised tree resin, sold beside amber.

## F6.4 Meteorites and impact glass

Kinds follow the class lists, shown in the AMNH class order; individual, slice or fragment is `meteorite_form`; a named fall is a named form and a fragment of it an individual. The chondrite and achondrite split of the sellers is covered by these kinds (section 14).
- F6.4.01 Iron meteorite: meteorite made mainly of iron-nickel metal.
- F6.4.02 Pallasite: stony-iron meteorite of olivine crystals in iron-nickel metal.
- F6.4.03 Carbonaceous chondrite: chondrite rich in carbon compounds and water-bearing minerals.
- F6.4.04 Ordinary chondrite: the common stony meteorite of chondrules (H, L, LL groups).
- F6.4.05 Lunar meteorite: achondrite meteorite ejected from the Moon.
- F6.4.06 Martian meteorite: achondrite meteorite ejected from Mars.
- F6.4.07 Tektite and impactite: natural glass or rock formed by a meteorite impact (tektites, impact glass, impactites).

## Precedence inside F6

- One home per object: a variety, trade name, taxon, named fall, shell species or pearl trade name is a named-form row under one kind and never a second home; presentation, size class, locality, origin, treatment, quantity and motive never decide a home (S5). Healing stones, birthstones and decor pieces are the same objects as collector pieces and are filed by species (`use:` tags, generated views).
- Cut state is the one presentation value that decides a home (F-4): a loose stone that is faceted or cut en cabochon, or rough sold as cutting material by weight, is F6.5; every other piece (crystal, cluster, specimen on matrix, geode, tumbled stone, slab, sphere, egg, palm stone) is F6.1 under its species, even when the species is a gem. So a faceted amethyst is F6.5.2.05 and an amethyst geode or sphere F6.1.4.02; a ruby crystal in marble matrix is F6.1.4.06 and a cut ruby F6.5.2.02; a facet-grade tourmaline rough sold by weight is F6.5.2.07 and a tumbled garnet F6.1.9.02; a cut or cabochon opal is F6.5.2.10 and an opal specimen F6.1.4.05. The two kinds of one species share one `species_id` and alias row (C9).
- Amber and ammolite are F6.3 in any cut state: a cabochon of Baltic amber is F6.3.5.01 and a cut ammolite F6.3.1.05, never F6.5 (F-4).
- Amethyst, agate, jasper and opal are kinds of their own (F6.1.4.02 to F6.1.4.05), not named forms of quartz (F6.1.4.01); Herkimer diamond, smoky quartz, rutilated quartz and rose quartz are named forms under quartz (F-23, CV-27). Diamond (F6.5.1.01) and fancy-colour diamond (F6.5.1.02) are two kinds because they are graded on separate scales (F-23).
- A rock that is also a gem or mineral (lapis lazuli, turquoise, jade) has one home for the uncut piece: F6.1 under its species where sellers treat it as a species, with a rock alias; a cabochon or faceted piece goes to F6.5 by the cut-state test.
- A pearl is filed under its habitat kind, saltwater (F6.5.3.01) or freshwater (F6.5.3.02); South Sea, Tahitian, Akoya, keshi and Kasumiga are region-tagged named forms, and natural or cultured is an attribute (F-24).
- A stone only cut and polished into a slab, sphere, egg, palm stone or tumbled stone stays F6 under its species; a stone lamp is B3, a carved animal F2 or B4, a pendant A3, and a cast, replica or carved figure is filed by what it is (F2 sculpture, E1 toy or kit; F-25, K-77). A gem simulant is not a gemstone (A3 or C4 by form, K-77).
- An unworked stone or fossil is F6 whatever it is kept for: an unworked salagrama is a named form under ammonite (F6.3.1.01) with a tradition tag and an F4 generated view; a stone worked or mounted as a shrine object is F4, and a relic housed in a reliquary or sealed and documented as a relic is F4 (F-17, R-3).
- A native-metal specimen sold as a specimen is F6 (native gold F6.1.1.01); a refined bar, round or coin sold by weight is F3; a refined element sold as a cube, ampoule or crystal for its identity is F6.8 (F6.8.01 to F6.8.04).
- A set of like specimens is the specimen with a `quantity` or `unit` attribute (100 tumbled quartz pebbles are F6.1.4.01; 48 pieces of one rock are that rock); a mixed set is filed by its item of greatest value, with its contents listed; a kit sold, standardised and compared as one object is a kind, and the rock and mineral teaching set (F6.2.4.01) is one, also when it holds fossils or minerals (S3a, K-1). A rock, mineral, fossil or insect set is F6 even when sold as a science-education kit (step 7 before step 23); the lens, UV lamp or hammer used with specimens is C6 or C1.
- A single shell with its species identified is F6.6; an unsorted assortment of shells sold for weddings, classrooms and crafts is C4; an undrilled loose stone or pearl is F6, a drilled gem or pearl bead sold for stringing C4, and a stone mounted, drilled and wired as a pendant or a pearl strung A3 (K-78).
- A mount of one animal prepared as in life is F6.7.1 however it is presented; a work that combines animal parts with other materials into a composition is F2 (K-76). A preserved or emptied specimen kept as a specimen is F6 (K-80): a dead shell, a coral skeleton (F6.6.4.01), an emptied egg shell (F6.7.2.03) and a pinned insect (F6.7.3.01) are F6, while a living animal, a living coral and an egg for hatching are out of scope (step 1), and human remains and anatomical specimens are OUT (K-79, R-3). The legal class of a species is a C17 ledger row, never a home.
- A pressed or mounted plant specimen on a sheet is F6.7.4.01; a living plant is C3; dried flowers and preserved (stabilised) plants are B4 (K-74); fossil plants and petrified wood are F6.3.3.01.
- A natural material sold for a use is filed by that use (pumice H1, an antler chew B10, garden boulders C2, firewood C8, craft feathers C4); the same material sold as a labelled specimen is F6 (an antler F6.7.2.04, a feather F6.7.3.02).
- A dome, stand, case or box made to hold one specimen and stand with it is F6.10 (F6.10.01 to F6.10.03) under rule S3 (K-9, CV-17); a frame fixed permanently to one specimen is part of that specimen; an album, tray, drawer or cabinet made to hold several specimens is B1 when empty.
- A book or atlas about specimens is F1; the specimen is F6.
