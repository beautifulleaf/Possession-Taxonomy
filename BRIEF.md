# Possession taxonomy — working brief

Agreed with the owner on 2026-10-17.

## Scope
- **Fresh domain.** The old Possessions taxonomy is not used or consulted.
- **In:** every kind of object a person can own and keep, including plants (houseplants, bonsai, garden plants), property (houses, land, structures, boats and vehicles as dwellings), digital goods (game items, domains, software licences, NFTs and other tokens), consumables kept or collected rather than used up (wine cellars, perfume collections, cigars), money as collectible objects (coins, banknotes), and adult objects (clinical, descriptive register).
- **Groceries are in (owner, 2026-10-17):** food and drink bought as ingredients and raw or minimally processed products, judged by the quality of the ingredient (for example high-quality meat: breed, cut, grade, origin, age). Dishes and prepared foods stay with the Culinary taxonomy.
- **Software and media (owner, 2026-10-17):** types of software are in, by what the software does (for example a password manager), however it is licensed or delivered. Media, games and digital content are in only as objects: carriers, formats and files. Literary, game, music and film/TV genres, and the internet as entertainment, belong to separate taxonomies; they never divide categories here.
- **Subscriptions and streaming are in (owner, 2026-10-17):** paid subscriptions and memberships are purchases that give enjoyment or quality of life. They are divided by the kind of access they give, never by content genre.
- **Out:** animals of any kind (pets, livestock, aquarium fish). Their equipment and housing are in (an aquarium tank, a saddle, a dog bed). Bank balances and other non-object financial positions are out.
- **Level of generality (owner, 2026-10-17):** a kind is a generic type of object someone has one of. Specific product models, cuts and iconic designs are named forms under a kind. Individual unique items (a particular card, a particular painting) are out.
- **Depth:** areas with very deep variety must be captured in depth — for example clothing, including the brands of different subcultures and scenes.
- **Quality:** some areas have clear tiers of quality set by brand or specification (for example many PC components); others are personal preference. The structure must allow both.

## Method (owner, 2026-10-17)
- **Stage 1:** a preliminary top-level category pass first. **Stage 2:** each category is researched separately to find the best structure for it, and the owner approves each structure. Brand handling, tiers, order axis and depth are decided per category in stage 2, not in advance.
- **Shared contract across all categories:** one home per object; kinds are generic types; modifiers (region, price, era, motive, scene, tier) stay off the tree as tags or views; every added name has an attested source; a maximum depth even where level names differ; data in tables with generated pages; resumable sessions.
- Cross-domain edges: Possession files the object as owned and kept, and groceries as ingredients bought, graded by quality; Culinary files dishes and what is eaten or drunk as a dish; Pleasure files the practice.

## Owner decisions on edge calls (2026-10-17)
- Pet food and animal feed are in, with animal equipment.
- Vitamins, dietary supplements and protein powders are in (filed under health unless the owner says otherwise).
- Tickets for a single event or journey, and one-off services, are out: the enjoyment is the experience, not the ticket. A kept ticket stub is in as a keepsake paper.
- Cryptocurrency holdings are out, like a bank balance; NFTs are in.
- Online services and accounts are in or out by **utility vs entertainment, not by price**: utility services (email, cloud storage, messaging, password vaults, maps and similar) are in whether free or paid; the internet as entertainment (social feeds, free video and content platforms) belongs to a separate taxonomy. Paid entertainment subscriptions are in as purchases.
- Grocery products that the Culinary taxonomy also holds (cheese, cured meats, fruit, tea, coffee, wine) are described here as bought, with their quality grade, and link to the Culinary kind.
- Cannabis and nicotine products are in, with tobacco.
- Baby and child goods have no category of their own; each goes to its functional home.

## Owner decisions on stage 2 (2026-10-17)
- **Stage-2 answers delegated to Claude:** "Your judgment for the answers." Claude answers the shared-convention, framework and per-category questions of framework/structure/STAGE2_OWNER_QUESTIONS.md and STAGE2_SUMMARY.md. The answers are in framework/structure/decisions/ (D1 shared and framework, D2 categories A–E, D3 categories F–J), each marked as delegated. The owner may overturn any of them; until then they bind like owner decisions.
- **No separate sourcing pass:** the owner questioned the need for sourcing passes compared with the Culinary taxonomy. Claims marked † in the stage-2 memos are attested when kinds are harvested in stage 3, as in the Culinary taxonomy. A decision that rests on an unverified fact is marked "provisional until attested".
- **Depth and breadth are the point of the research** (owner): "The research in this taxonomy will primarily be about depth and breadth". Examples given: under puzzles, the Japanese puzzle-box makers; fashion with its subcultures and brands; fragrances. So maker ledgers, lines and named forms have no per-category cap: they are seeded from attested lists and grow as far as the attested evidence goes. Makers, artisans, houses, workshops, perfumers and schools are recorded in ledgers off the tree, not as extra kinds. Scene and subculture tags are used wherever communities exist.
- **One source proves existence; no two-source rule** (owner): unlike Culinary and Pleasure, where research checked that a thing existed, here "if something is able to be bought it is real", and needing two sources "would diminish real, one of a kind items". A kind, named form, line or maker enters on one fetched page showing it is or was made or sold (a maker's or shop page, a catalogue, an auction or marketplace listing, a museum or trade record). A second source or a second region is never a condition of inclusion. Region or source counts may still help decide the level (kind against named form) or the weight of a tier scheme, but never whether an item is in. Individual objects (one particular painting or watch) stay out, as above.
- **Use existing catalogues first** (owner): "depending on category there will possibly be sources that already exist that we can utilize rather than searching blindly". Stage 3 harvests each category from the catalogues, databases, registries and community wikis that already list its makers, lines and named forms (for example perfume databases, record and game databases, coin and stamp catalogues, collector registries). Open web search fills the gaps. A source census per category comes before harvesting.
- **Board game genres belong to a future Interactive Entertainment taxonomy** (owner), together with video game genres and the internet as entertainment. Here a board, card or tabletop game is filed as an object (its physical form, publisher, edition and components). Game genres, mechanics and themes (worker placement, deck-building, eurogame against ameritrash) are never a level, kind or scene here. At most they are a link field to that future taxonomy.

## Owner decisions on navigation (2026-10-17, after seeing the pilot)
- **Scene-first navigation for scene-driven categories** (owner: the pilot "may miss the mark in how someone would actually navigate consumerist subcultures"; "brands in different clusters would make more sense as a primary way of navigating clothing"). For clothing, and likewise for other scene-driven categories (footwear, fragrance, watches and similar), the main way in is scene clusters → the brands in each (core or adjacent; a brand may sit in several scenes) → their signature pieces. The garment-type tree stays underneath as the "what is it" index and the home for placement; scenes stay tags in the data (one home per object holds), but they are the primary navigation, not an afterthought view.
- **D1 is restructured to shopper paths** (owner: D1 "is awkwardly split out in ways that don't match what someone would seek out"). D1's sub-families are rebuilt around how people buy: computers; PC parts as a builder sees them; peripherals and desk setup; printing and scanning; external storage and media; networking; cables and adapters; electronics and maker parts. Kinds keep their content; ids are reissued once with a crosswalk, and D1 gets a kind-level blind test.
- **Regional coverage of clothing brands** (owner, 2026-10-17): "make sure all regions of the world are adequately represented … some may be genuinely having a lot more brands but want to make sure the brands in each region are represented." Clothing brands are harvested region by region worldwide (Africa, the Middle East, South, Southeast, East and Central Asia, Europe east and west, the Americas, Oceania), from sources local to each region and in its languages; coverage is measured per region and per country, and a thin region is a gap to close, not a reflection of the world.
- **Coverage proportional to the world's distribution** (owner, 2026-10-17, a general principle for every category): "In the end, it should be proportional to the world's distribution of brands, so we can tell the Anglosphere being that low is off." Each category's brand (maker) coverage per country and region is compared with an outside reference of how that category's brands are spread across the world, built from fetched sources. A country well below its expected share is a gap to close with depth passes; every region still gets represented however small its share. Balance is reached by adding brands where coverage is thin, never by removing attested ones.

## Owner decision on country data (2026-10-24, session 5)
- **No country backfill** (owner): "I'm not sure the country backfill is worth doing, this is supposed to be organized for consumers and people wouldn't necessarily organize on that but rather the type of clothes … like genres of fashion." Country is never a way in: categories are navigated by the type of object and, where scenes exist, by genre (fashion scenes and styles). Dedicated passes that only look up makers' countries are stopped; a country is recorded when a harvested page states it.
- **Regional harvests stay, per-country balancing goes** (owner, 2026-10-24): makers are still harvested region by region so every part of the world is found, but the depth passes that chase each country's expected share (coverage proportional to the world's distribution, 2026-10-17) are no longer run. Coverage reports may still be generated for information.
- **Where regional maker harvests run** (owner, 2026-10-24): region-by-region maker harvests run only where shoppers choose by maker and the makers are a long local tail: clothing and the other scene-driven categories (footwear, fragrance, cosmetics, jewellery, watches), dairy, tinned fish and caviar houses, animal equipment, video-game studios, consumer and design lighting, and later wine and spirits, tea and coffee, beer, furniture, toys and crafts. They do not run where a few global brands dominate or the makers are invisible to shoppers: computers and components, phones, cars, large appliances, software, optics and instruments, fresh fruit and vegetables and fresh fish (there, varieties and protected origins matter, not growers). Makers already attested stay. Even where makers are harvested by region, countries are not the way the category is divided: the region is only a way of finding makers.
- **Maker roles** (owner, 2026-10-24, from a proposal by Claude): each category gives makers one of three roles. *Way in*: the maker is what shoppers choose (clothing and the other scene-driven categories, craft and collector goods, instrument makers, art, wine estates); full maker ledger, maker pages, harvested as deep as sources go. *Owner only*: shoppers choose a model, and a maker enters only as the owner of a named form or line (computers, phones, cameras, audio, appliances, optics, lighting, animal equipment, software); no standalone maker lists or regional maker harvests. *None*: commodities and producers invisible to the shopper (fresh produce, fresh fish and meat, eggs, building materials); a maker appears only through a branded product. For video games only hardware makers are recorded (game studios are not, since titles are out of scope). For groceries (the categories graded by quality of the ingredient), origin and grade replace the maker step: breed, cut, variety, appellation and protected origin, farm type and grade lead, and makers appear only through branded products. Makers already in the ledger are kept but not shown where their category's role hides them. The table is framework/structure/maker_roles.tsv.

## Owner decision 2026-10-28: pursuits belong to other taxonomies
- **Pursuits are out; their equipment is in, organised by object.** Separate taxonomies will cover all Physical, Creative, Intellectual and Spiritual pursuits (sports, art forms and crafts, puzzles and games as activities, faith practices). This taxonomy does not delineate every sport, art form, craft or practice. It covers the objects a person owns for them, organised by what the object is (its form, mechanism, maker, archetype), with a link field to the pursuit taxonomy. A pursuit may still group equipment where owners shop that way (fishing tackle, climbing gear), but a pursuit with no distinct equipment gets no department, shelf or kind of its own here.
- **Religious objects form first (owner 2026-10-28).** F4 is grouped by what the object is (images and altars, prayer aids, scriptures and holders, ritual vessels and tools, light and incense, divination, funeral goods). The faith tradition is a tag on each kind; the traditions register stays as a browse view. This replaces the earlier rule that faith traditions are F4's primary browse axis.
- **Fixed lights and locks (owner 2026-10-28).** Built-in light fittings (recessed downlights, track lighting, under-cabinet lights) move from B3 Lighting to G3 Home Fixtures; B3 keeps lamps, torches, bulbs and anything movable. Door and window locks stay in B8 Home Safety & Security (compared by security grade, bought as hardware); G3's multipoint lock moves to B8.
- **Packaged prepared foods are in (owner 2026-10-28).** Ready meals, frozen meals and pizza, dips, soups and baby food bought packaged are products here, in a J5 department organised by product type and brand; the recipes and dishes themselves stay in the Culinary taxonomy.

## Owner decisions 2026-10-31 (session 13): named entries, market layer, boundaries
- **Named entries come from a canon, never a selection.** Each kind states what one named entry is (`nl`) and which reference its list follows (`sel`); big complete canons are preferred over a picked subset ("big canons are preferred instead of an arbitrary selection"). Prominence is a badge (origin, landmark, classification rank), not a separate list. Pilot: video game consoles (every platform from the reference lists, by generation; revisions as versions).
- **Treatments by kind of field:** (1) a field classification exists → use it as sections and list it completely; (2) a hobby reference database names model lines → line as entry, references/generations as versions; (3) a brand runs its own catalogue (LEGO) → the brand is one entry, versions only for what changes the object (LEGO System, Duplo, Technic), no themes or sets ("the LEGO brand stands for itself"); (4) no canon but a market → a few traceable originators plus a market layer; (5) commodity → the kind is the end; (6) content → link to the owning taxonomy.
- **Object, not content.** Themes, licences, titles and jigsaw images are content. Art (F2) keeps the physical object (process, edition, support, authentication, condition, provenance); artists, works and movements belong to the Visual Entertainment taxonomy.
- **Market layer for groceries (and later other markets).** What separates groceries from the Culinary taxonomy is market analysis (owner's example: US soft drinks are dominated by Coca-Cola, PepsiCo and Keurig Dr Pepper; internationally there are independents and a craft/indie scene). Tiers: owner groups and their brands, national champions, independents and craft. Share figures only from fetched pages, with year; tiers rest on public ownership and published rankings.
- **Fresh food** has a certification layer (halal, kosher, organic, welfare: a certifier register, tags on products) and a supplier market (premium online butchers and fishmongers, halal/kosher delivery, exotic fruit boxes); delivery services themselves are I3, linked to J.
- **Wine, beer, spirits:** wine by appellation (complete canon) with classified producers as versions and grape as a second view; beer kinds by style with market tiers; spirits by protected category → distillery → expression with owner groups above.
- **Non-alcoholic offerings highlighted:** an alcohol-level attribute with market legal bands; alcohol-free versions stay with their parent (Heineken 0.0 under Heineken); alcohol-free-only products (zero-proof spirits, NA specialist brewers) in a "No & low" department in J7; a cross-category "No & low" view over J7 and J9; mocktails stay in Culinary.
- **Clothing:** garment tree as home; patterns as named entries by origin; brand register sectioned by market; scenes remain the way in (17 October decision), citing their sources.
- **AI** is software: model families with generations as a canon (per lab), apps in I1, plans in I3, open-weight model files as owned data.
- **Market list leads for market-shaped groceries (owner 2026-10-31):** in Soft Drinks & Water (and categories that work the same way: beer, confectionery and snacks), brands are the named entries and the department is organised by market (tier, then owner group or home region); flavours are versions or attributes, since the Culinary taxonomy already describes soft-drink flavours. No catch-all flavour kinds.
