== Brick cheese True https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick_cheese 
Brick cheese is a cheese originating in Wisconsin, United States, made in brick-shaped form due to (originally) using actual bricks to form the shape. The color ranges from pale yellow to white with an orange rind. When unaged, this cheese has a sweet, mild flavor and is suitable for melting; after aging it has a stronger smell and a nuttier taste.

== Limburger True https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburger 
Limburger, commonly known as Limburg (in southern Dutch contexts Rommedoe, and in Belgium Herve cheese), is a cheese that originated in the Herve area of the historical Duchy of Limburg, which had its capital in Limbourg-sur-Vesdre, now in the French-speaking Belgian province of Liège. The cheese is especially known for its strong smell caused by the bacterium Brevibacterium linens. Herve has been produced since the 15th century.

== Taleggio cheese True https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taleggio_cheese 
Taleggio (Italian: [taˈleddʒo]; Lombard: talegg, taegg or taècc) is a semisoft washed-rind Italian cheese named after Val Taleggio, Italy. The cheese has a thin, edible rind and a strong aroma, but its flavour is comparatively mild with an unusual fruity tang. The rind is a pinkish-brown, and the interior is creamy and pale yellow. It has a protected designation of origin so that only such cheese produced in the Lombardy or Piedmont regions of Italy may be designated as Taleggio.

== Milleens True https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milleens 
Milleens is an Irish farmhouse cheese, made on a farm on the outskirts of Eyeries, County Cork, Ireland.  First made in 1976, Milleens was originally produced by Norman and Veronica Steele, and is now produced by their son.  Milleens cheese matures within four to ten weeks after it is produced.  It is sold in large and small rounds - the latter called "dotes" - and with a washed rind.  In 1997, it was the winner of the Supreme Champion at the British Cheese Awards held at Chelsea Barracks.
